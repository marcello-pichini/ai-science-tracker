What new mathematical results has AI helped discover?

FunSearch found explicit constructions that improved lower bounds for cap sets. AlphaEvolve searched for constructions across 67 problems and the authors reported improvements on several. The AlphaProof Olympiad result illustrates a different task, solving and proving competition problems with known answers.

Editorial review 22 Nov 2026. Selected examples compare constructions, competition solutions and proposed proofs.

What the systems produced and how it was checked

FunSearch produced larger cap-set constructions

A language model proposed programs and an evaluator checked the resulting sets for the required property. An explicit larger set raises the known lower bound on the maximum size. An exact maximum also requires an upper bound that matches the construction.

Read the FunSearch paper · FunSearch catalog record

AlphaEvolve improved some known constructions

The study authors used automated scoring to guide an evolutionary search over programs. Across 67 problems, they report that the system recovered the best known constructions in most cases and improved several. The preprint distinguishes individual constructions from instances where a pattern was generalized to a formula. The scope of each result depends on the construction and its justification.

Read the AlphaEvolve preprint · AlphaEvolve catalog record

AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 solved competition problems

Google DeepMind reports that the systems solved four of six problems from the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad. Two mathematicians scored the solutions at 28 of 42 points. The team translated the problems into formal language by hand, and some solutions took up to three days. This measures performance on a specific problem set under the team's stated conditions.

Riemann and Navier–Stokes proof announcements

Riemann hypothesis

The hypothesis says that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part ½. In August 2026, Alpöge and Ralph Furman posted a preprint on a lower bound for the proportion of zeros that are simple and lie on the critical line, building on work by Claude. The lower bound concerns a proportion of zeros. The hypothesis concerns every nontrivial zero.

In November 2026, Levent Alpöge announced that he and Claude had found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and its generalization to Dirichlet L-functions. His announcement describes their collaboration and presents the argument in Lean. At the editorial check, the Clay Mathematics Institute still listed the Riemann hypothesis as unsolved. The announced proof remains a claim in this dossier.

Read the earlier lower-bound preprint · Read Alpöge’s announcement · See the Clay problem statement and status

Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness

OpenAI reports that agents using an internal model produced a proof and a Lean formalization of finite-time singularity for three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes flow under smooth external forcing. OpenAI identifies this as the breakdown alternatives C and D in the official problem formulation. The forcing is part of the result as stated.

On 11 September 2026, the Clay Mathematics Institute described the problem as apparently settled in its response to the announcement and referred readers to its evaluation process. That response and the released proof artifacts serve different purposes. The catalog record tracks the result as a proposed solution.

Read OpenAI’s announcement and proof links · Read Clay’s response · Navier–Stokes catalog record

How to interpret the evidence

What counts as a new mathematical construction?

If a paper gives an explicit set of a certain size and proves it meets the rules, that set is evidence that the optimum is at least that large. To establish the optimum, mathematicians still need an upper bound that rules out anything larger. This distinction explains how an AI-assisted construction can be a new result even while the underlying optimization problem remains open.

AlphaEvolve also illustrates a separate jump in scope. A pattern that holds for several inputs can suggest a formula. The proof must explain why the formula works for every input in the stated domain. Read each claim with its own conditions and verification artifact.

Read the AlphaEvolve study

What does a Lean proof actually check?

Lean can check whether a formal conclusion follows from the definitions and assumptions in the encoded statement. That is one part of reviewing a result. Readers also need to match the encoded statement to the problem posed in the mathematical literature and identify any additional assumptions or narrower scope.

This matters for both announcements. The Riemann claim concerns every nontrivial zero. The earlier lower-bound preprint concerns a proportion of those zeros. For Navier–Stokes, the announced result uses smooth external forcing, a condition readers can compare directly with the breakdown alternatives in the prize problem.

Read the Riemann proof announcement

When is a Millennium Prize problem considered solved?

Clay lists the Riemann hypothesis as unsolved. In its September 2026 response to the Navier–Stokes announcement, Clay described the problem as apparently settled and pointed to its evaluation process. Its problem page still shows an active status.

The prize rules require publication in a qualifying outlet, at least two years since publication and general acceptance in the international mathematics community before Clay considers a proposal. This is a useful distinction when reading reports about proof files, researcher reactions and prize decisions.
