What has AI helped discover in science?

AI has helped researchers find antibiotic candidates, model molecular structures, identify planet candidates, improve forecasts and construct new algorithms. The evidence varies by study.

Start here

medicine2020

AI model flags an antibiotic, halicin, that works in lab and mouse tests

A computer model trained on about 2,500 molecules screened roughly 6,000 compounds and picked halicin, which killed many resistant bacteria in the lab and cleared A. baumannii infections in mice within 24 hours.

Tested in a new experiment

biology2024

AlphaFold 3 predicts how proteins, DNA, RNA and small molecules fit together

AlphaFold 3 predicts the joint 3D structures of proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules and ions; its developers report at least 50% greater accuracy than existing methods for protein interactions with other molecules.

Checked on existing data

astronomy2021

ExoMiner AI statistically validates 301 more Kepler planets

Researchers used a neural network called ExoMiner to sort through leftover planet candidates from the Kepler telescope and statistically validated 301 new planets, none of them Earth-like.

Checked on existing data

climate2023

GraphCast produces global 10-day weather forecasts

GraphCast is an AI weather model that makes a 10-day global forecast in under a minute on one chip, and it beat ECMWF's HRES forecast system on over 90% of 1,380 tested targets.

Checked on existing data

mathematics2025

AlphaEvolve uses Gemini to evolve better algorithms and solutions

Google's AlphaEvolve lets Gemini models propose code that automatic checkers score and improve; it matched the best known result on about 75% of more than 50 open math problems and improved about 20%.

Checked on existing data

humanities2024

Prize-winning AI methods read parts of a burned Herculaneum scroll

A Vesuvius Challenge submission used AI models to find ink in CT scans of a burned scroll and recovered over 2,000 characters, about 5% of the first scroll; papyrologists judged it to yield the most text.

Independently or prospectively checked
