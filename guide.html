How to use this site

A short tour of the catalog: where to start, how to narrow it down, and how to share what you find.

The pages

  • Discoveries: the catalog of AI-enabled scientific results, with charts, filters and a list by year.
  • Models: model releases from leading labs and the model families named in discoveries.
  • Labs and families: one profile per organization or model family, with its timeline, discoveries and releases.
  • Vuln discoveries: public vulnerability discoveries with documented AI involvement, disclosure context and fix evidence. This is separate from the scientific catalog and is not a live patch inventory.
  • Dataset and API: download the catalog as JSON or CSV, schemas and a read-only static API.
  • Contributors: who maintains the catalog, how to contribute and how contributors are credited.
  • Methodology: what is included, how entries are verified and how relevance is scored.

Start with the timeline

The blue banner on the Discoveries page shows one mark per catalog entry, placed by date; height represents editorial relevance. Hover over a mark to preview it, or select it to open the entry. Where several entries share a date or appear close together, choose from the nearby entries; their actual dates are preserved. With the keyboard, focus the chart, use the left and right arrows, then press Enter. The Models scatter plot also supports keyboard exploration and a chooser for nearby releases.

Narrow the catalog

  • Fields: the buttons under “Explore by field” filter by science field; the number is the discovery count.
  • Charts: click a row (field or organization) or a year in “By the numbers” to filter the list; click it again to clear. Aggregate charts describe the full catalog, even when the list is filtered.
  • Data snapshot: collection views validate the actual loaded file bytes against one component manifest. If files differ during deployment, loading retries once; a persistent mismatch shows Retry and downloads instead of stale search results. The visible snapshot and component hashes describe the data used by the view.
  • Entity names: reviewed exact aliases group MIT/Massachusetts Institute of Technology and source-attributed AISLE labels. Complete original labels remain on records; an AISLE vendor group does not identify a specific model, version or autonomous role. Unknown affiliations are separate from institutions.
  • Filter bar: search text, organization, year, model family, minimum relevance and sort order. “Clear filters” resets everything.
  • Copy filter link: every filter is stored in the address, so you can share or bookmark the exact view.

Read an entry

  • Each entry gives the date, the organization, a summary with the evidence limits, the AI technology and an editorial relevance score from 20 to 100 in 13 possible steps (open the source and editorial details to see impact, validation and novelty). It is not an evidence grade or a precise measure of scientific value.
  • “Original source” opens the original paper, preprint or institutional announcement; “Paper” opens a separately linked paper when available. Figures are reported by sources and are not independently verified.
  • “Source checked” means we checked the source page, not that the finding was replicated. The expandable details work with touch and keyboard, show the source type and editorial subscores, and are included when printing. A missing paper link does not mean no paper exists.
  • The organization name and the family line lead to profiles; the family line also links to the Models page.

Read the annual chart

Choose catalog-entry counts or mean editorial relevance. The accompanying table shows both and the annual sample size. These figures describe this selected, incomplete collection, not worldwide discovery rates. The most recent catalog addition is not a scientific review date. The evidence and coverage tables show which entries have structured annotations and how primary source types vary by year; neither is an evidence-quality ranking.

Suggest a correction or discovery

“Suggest a correction” on an entry opens a GitHub draft with its ID and source. “Suggest a discovery” in the catalog and profile footers opens a separate form requiring an original source and announcement date. GitHub sign-in is required and submitted reports are public: do not include personal information. Every suggestion is reviewed before any catalog change; opening a form does not submit it.

Models and profiles

On Models, click a year or lab to filter releases, or a family to open its card. A family is linked to a discovery when the discovery names it, so the link identifies a family, not the exact version used. On Labs and families, pick any row to see that profile.

Share and print

  • Copy link on an entry copies a link that shows a preview card on social networks and opens the entry.
  • Use your browser’s Print or “Save as PDF” on Discoveries, Models or Vuln discoveries to include every matching entry or release, including rows not yet loaded on screen. Discovery and vulnerability evidence details are expanded for print; the page restores your reading state afterwards. Your current filters apply.

Good to know

  • Light and dark themes follow your device settings.
  • Coverage is manual and incomplete; absence of a result does not mean it did not happen.
  • Model data comes from Epoch AI under CC BY 4.0.

Follow a question across collections

Open Dossiers to read focused reviews that compare what AI contributed, what researchers tested and where the evidence ends. Start with AlphaFold or AI-discovered antibiotics.

Use Search for literal names, topics, DOI and CVE identifiers. Results show the matching fields and separate collection counts. Model inventories and notable releases can overlap; their totals must not be added.

Discovery cards separate publication and test context, source checks, and result/AI/limits. “Your selection” counts every matching entry before pagination; test and publication filters are shared in the URL and retained in JSON/CSV exports.

Family profiles explain name-based scientific associations and offer a bounded two-family comparison. Security links group cases citing the exact same report without merging vulnerabilities. Read the antibiotic evidence dossier for a dated synthesis, or model progress to compare exact models, uncertainty, benchmarks and recorded release rhythm.
