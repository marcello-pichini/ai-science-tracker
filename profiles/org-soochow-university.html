26 Aug 2026Soochow University; Zhengzhou University; Fudan University; Hefei National Laboratory

Physics-informed retrieval-augmented language model guides synthesis of five previously unreported inorganic crystals

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers built PIRAG-LM, which combines a structured knowledge base of experimental synthesis routes, physics-based chemical/structural/thermodynamic similarity retrieval, and LLM route reasoning. In prospective tests, it guided synthesis of five compounds previously unreported in experimental literature: BaMo0.3In0.7O2.95, BaNb0.4In0.6O2.9, Hg[B(CN)4]2, CoCo(CN)6 and SrNb2Fe2(PO4)6. Powder X-ray diffraction patterns were consistent with target phases. This is one paper-level synthesis-planning result, not five separate entries.

Technology: PIRAG-LM, physics-informed retrieval-augmented language model, with DeepSeek-V4 used in the structured synthesis-knowledge-base extraction workflow

28 May 2026Soochow University

Machine learning identifies a candidate NEC expression signature with experimental checks

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: RF+XGBoost screening identified SLC26A3, CCL20 and CXCL5 as a candidate expression signature for neonatal necrotizing enterocolitis, with retrospective expression validation and new mouse and cell-model expression checks. The study used 46 training and 24 test samples; the reported 0.979 AUC is training performance, with 0.917 and 0.957 in two small test cohorts. Geneformer and virtual-knockout analyses generated regulatory hypotheses; causality, protein-level diagnostic performance and clinical benefit remain unestablished.

Technology: Random forest; XGBoost; Geneformer

29 Sep 2024Soochow University; Xuzhou Medical University; Nantong University; Nanjing Medical University

Learned mutation score associates with survival in checkpoint-treated gastroesophageal cancer

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An XGBoost mutation score separated observed survival groups in public cohorts of checkpoint-treated gastroesophageal cancer. The source used 123 patients for initial fitting and two additional cohort groups of 42 and 66 for model comparison. Because those comparison cohorts helped select the final model, the result is an exploratory prognostic association rather than untouched external validation or evidence of treatment benefit.

Technology: XGBoost mutation-feature selection and boosted survival-risk modeling

Editorial relevance 33 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The learned feature-selection and boosted model combined tumor mutation profiles into the represented survival-associated score; immune enrichment analyses alone are not the admission basis.
Test and result
The paper defined overall survival in checkpoint-treated gastroesophageal cancer as the endpoint. A 123-person MSK cohort supplied initial prognostic-gene filtering. It compared 297 algorithm combinations using cohort groups of 42 and 66 and selected the highest mean test-cohort concordance. The selected XGBoost procedure retained twelve mutation features, with a training-derived median score threshold separating survival groups across the represented cohorts.
Limitation / still unknown
The purported validation cohorts were used for final model selection and therefore do not establish an untouched external test. Participant overlap between public source cohorts was not independently reconstructed. Small cohort groups, extensive model searching and differing sequencing panels increase optimism and transportability concerns. Survival among treated patients does not identify benefit relative to no treatment or distinguish predictive treatment-response effects from general prognosis. Drug-sensitivity predictions and cell-line experiments are outside this admitted case, and no therapeutic efficacy is claimed.

14 Aug 2024Soochow University; Xi’an Jiaotong University

Machine learning identifies predicted zero- and extreme-thermal-expansion monolayers

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: High-throughput calculations and symbolic regression identified mechanical descriptors that classify positive- versus negative-thermal-expansion 2D crystals, then used those descriptors to predict monolayers with near-zero thermal expansion (2D Invar) and unusually large positive or negative expansion (anti-Invar). The paper reports multiple specific predicted crystals and explains the phonon/deformation-mode basis. The materials are computational predictions rather than experimentally synthesized monolayers.

Technology: High-throughput calculations combined with symbolic regression machine learning

Editorial relevance 53 materials Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned interpretable relationships between in-plane tensile stiffness, out-of-plane bending stiffness and thermal-expansion class, then used them to screen 2D-crystal databases for predicted Invar and anti-Invar materials.
Test and result
High-throughput calculations and symbolic regression identified mechanical descriptors that classify positive- versus negative-thermal-expansion 2D crystals, then used those descriptors to predict monolayers with near-zero thermal expansion (2D Invar) and unusually large positive or negative expansion (anti-Invar).
Limitation / still unknown
The identified materials and thermal-expansion properties are from computational/high-throughput predictions and were not experimentally validated in this paper. This is a new 2D monolayer property result, distinct from previously cataloged bulk high-entropy Invar alloys.
