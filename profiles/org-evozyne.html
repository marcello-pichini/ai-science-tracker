ProT-VAE designs synthetic enzymes with measured activity and high-temperature stability

The transformer–variational-autoencoder ProT-VAE generated synthetic enzyme sequences that were tested experimentally. Among 190 designed human phenylalanine-hydroxylase sequences, 69 had measurable activity and 19 exceeded wild-type activity, with the best at 2.5× wild type. A designed γ-carbonic anhydrase had a measured melting temperature of 116 °C (61 °C above the most thermostable sequence previously reported by the authors) and remained active in 23% methyl diethanolamine at pH 11.25 and 93 °C.

Technology: Protein Transformer Variational AutoEncoder (ProT-VAE), using ProtT5nv transformer representations and a family-specific variational autoencoder

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The transformer–variational-autoencoder ProT-VAE generated synthetic enzyme sequences that were tested experimentally.
  • Still unknown: The reported outcomes are laboratory expression/activity/stability tests on selected synthetic sequences; most generated candidates were not active or did not express. The 0.8× activity comparison is relative to bovine α-carbonic anhydrase under the stated assay, and the 61 °C comparison is against the previously reported thermostable γ-CA referenced by the authors. Industrial relevance is a proposed application, not demonstrated process performance.
  • AI role: The generative model learned protein sequence–function patterns, produced candidate enzyme sequences conditioned in a learned latent space, and guided selection for synthesis. The authors measured enzyme function and stability experimentally; AlphaFold was used only for structure predictions of selected designs.
