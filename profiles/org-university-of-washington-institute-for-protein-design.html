16 Jan 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Computer-designed proteins neutralize snake toxins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Small, stable proteins designed with AI bind and neutralize three-finger toxins from cobras and mambas in the lab; in mice, they yield 80–100% survival against lethal neurotoxin doses, depending on the toxin. Laboratory and animal studies only; traditional antivenoms remain central.

Technology: AI protein design (RFdiffusion)

18 Sep 2024University of Washington Institute for Protein Design; University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center; Broad Institute; Massachusetts General Hospital; University of Connecticut; Seoul National University; collaborating institutions

Deep learning identifies previously unreported protein interactions in human bacterial pathogens

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A RoseTTAFold2-Lite pipeline combined residue coevolution, deep-learning complex prediction and AlphaFold modelling to screen protein pairs across 19 human bacterial pathogens. In a validation set selected using the deep-learning pipeline, co-immunoprecipitation detected four of six predicted interactions. Three pairs lacked prior direct interaction evidence: E. coli UbiE–YcaR, P. aeruginosa PA4105–PA4106, and P. aeruginosa WbpG–HisF2. The PA4105–PA4106 structural similarity to proteins in 3-thiaglutamate biosynthesis and the WbpG–HisF2 result provide functional hypotheses, not established native-pathogen mechanisms.

Technology: RoseTTAFold2-Lite integrated with direct coupling analysis and AlphaFold complex modelling

Family AlphaFold: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
RoseTTAFold2-Lite used residue–residue coevolution and predicted complex structures to rank candidate protein pairs in a proteome-wide screen. The authors selected six pairs from the final deep-learning pipeline for co-immunoprecipitation tests; four yielded positive interaction signals. Three were described as lacking previously reported direct interaction evidence, including UbiE–YcaR, PA4105–PA4106 and WbpG–HisF2. The models enabled the experimental target selection; they did not themselves establish physical interactions.
Test and result
In a validation set selected using the deep-learning pipeline, co-immunoprecipitation detected four of six predicted interactions.
Limitation / still unknown
The positive interaction evidence comes from heterologous co-immunoprecipitation/pull-down assays, with two independent experiments per positive pair, rather than native-pathogen systems. The validation subset was small (four positives among six AI-selected candidates); most of the thousands of predicted interactions remain untested, and false positives and false negatives are expected. Proposed roles for PA4105–PA4106 and UbiE–YcaR remain hypotheses; this paper does not establish their physiological function or a therapeutic target.

5 Sep 2024University of Washington Institute for Protein Design; and Department of Biochemistry; Howard Hughes Medical Institute; University of California, Davis; Korea Institute of Science and Technology; Sungkyunkwan University

AI-accelerated virtual screening identifies small-molecule binders to KLHDC2 and Nav1.7

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The OpenVS platform combined target-specific neural-network active learning with RosettaVS physics-based docking to search billion-scale compound libraries. The study reported seven low-micromolar KLHDC2 degron-pocket binders and four Naᵥ1.7 VSD4 binders. KLHDC2 hit C29 competed with a degron peptide at IC50 about 2.9 μM and its predicted pose was supported by a 2.0 Å crystal structure; the strongest Naᵥ1.7 hit, Z8739902234, had IC50 1.3 μM in whole-cell electrophysiology.

Technology: OpenVS active-learning platform with target-specific feed-forward neural-network triage, coupled to RosettaVS physics-based docking and RosettaGenFF-VS scoring

Editorial relevance 60 chemistry Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The target-specific neural network used docking-labeled data to guide which molecules were prioritized for additional expensive docking over iterative multi-billion-compound screens; RosettaVS/RosettaGenFF-VS performed physics-based docking and pose/affinity scoring. The AI-guided screen nominated molecules that were synthesized and experimentally tested. Experiments confirmed KLHDC2 degron displacement and a crystal structure matching the predicted pose, plus Naᵥ1.7 binding/inhibition in patch clamp. The paper attributes discovery to the combined AI-accelerated and physics-based workflow, not to a generative model alone.
Test and result
The study reported seven low-micromolar KLHDC2 degron-pocket binders and four Naᵥ1.7 VSD4 binders.
Limitation / still unknown
Early ligand-hit discovery only: binding/inhibition was measured in biochemical competition, biolayer interferometry and cell electrophysiology assays, not in animals or patients. The paper states further optimization is needed to improve KLHDC2 compounds toward nanomolar potency. KLHDC2 C29’s crystal structure is experimental support for one predicted binding pose; target modulation, therapeutic efficacy and safety were not established. The reported target hit rates apply to manually filtered, synthesized subsets, not the full billion-molecule library.

19 Dec 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Designed proteins bind peptides with the highest affinity reported

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, researchers designed proteins that bind helical peptides such as glucagon, neuropeptide Y and parathyroid hormone, with what the authors consider the highest reported affinity for a computer-generated biomolecule. They detect low concentrations of peptides in human serum and withstand heat; a parathyroid-hormone biosensor produced a 21-fold increase in bioluminescence. The press release gives no affinity values.

Technology: RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN

16 Sep 2022University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

ProteinMPNN: protein sequences designed in about one second

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural network generates the amino-acid sequence for a given protein shape in about one second, over 200 times faster than the best previous software, without specialist tuning. A related study shows that proteins designed with ProteinMPNN fold correctly much more often, including nanoscale rings and complex assemblies. Researchers describe it as the beginning of machine learning in protein design.

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.
