18 Apr 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design; and UCLA

Serine hydrolases designed from scratch

RFdiffusion backbones and the PLACER network for catalytic-cycle ensembles produced de novo serine hydrolases with kcat/Km up to 4.3e3 M^-1 s^-1 and crystal structures within 1 Å of the designs, in five folds not seen in this enzyme family. Design success rates not checked.

Technology: RFdiffusion and PLACER

16 Jan 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Computer-designed proteins neutralize snake toxins

Small, stable proteins designed with AI bind and neutralize three-finger toxins from cobras and mambas in the lab; in mice, they yield 80–100% survival against lethal neurotoxin doses, depending on the toxin. Laboratory and animal studies only; traditional antivenoms remain central.

18 Sep 2024University of Washington Institute for Protein Design; University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center; Broad Institute; Massachusetts General Hospital; University of Connecticut; Seoul National University; collaborating institutions

Deep learning identifies previously unreported protein interactions in human bacterial pathogens

A RoseTTAFold2-Lite pipeline combined residue coevolution, deep-learning complex prediction and AlphaFold modelling to screen protein pairs across 19 human bacterial pathogens. In a validation set selected using the deep-learning pipeline, co-immunoprecipitation detected four of six predicted interactions. Three pairs lacked prior direct interaction evidence: E. coli UbiE–YcaR, P. aeruginosa PA4105–PA4106, and P. aeruginosa WbpG–HisF2. The PA4105–PA4106 structural similarity to proteins in 3-thiaglutamate biosynthesis and the WbpG–HisF2 result provide functional hypotheses, not established native-pathogen mechanisms.

Technology: RoseTTAFold2-Lite integrated with direct coupling analysis and AlphaFold complex modelling

5 Sep 2024University of Washington Institute for Protein Design; and Department of Biochemistry; Howard Hughes Medical Institute; University of California, Davis; Korea Institute of Science and Technology; Sungkyunkwan University

AI-accelerated virtual screening identifies small-molecule binders to KLHDC2 and Nav1.7

The OpenVS platform combined target-specific neural-network active learning with RosettaVS physics-based docking to search billion-scale compound libraries. The study reported seven low-micromolar KLHDC2 degron-pocket binders and four Naᵥ1.7 VSD4 binders. KLHDC2 hit C29 competed with a degron peptide at IC50 about 2.9 μM and its predicted pose was supported by a 2.0 Å crystal structure; the strongest Naᵥ1.7 hit, Z8739902234, had IC50 1.3 μM in whole-cell electrophysiology.

Technology: OpenVS active-learning platform with target-specific feed-forward neural-network triage, coupled to RosettaVS physics-based docking and RosettaGenFF-VS scoring

19 Dec 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Designed proteins bind peptides with the highest affinity reported

Using RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, researchers designed proteins that bind helical peptides such as glucagon, neuropeptide Y and parathyroid hormone, with what the authors consider the highest reported affinity for a computer-generated biomolecule. They detect low concentrations of peptides in human serum and withstand heat; a parathyroid-hormone biosensor produced a 21-fold increase in bioluminescence. The press release gives no affinity values.

Technology: RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN

16 Sep 2022University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

ProteinMPNN: protein sequences designed in about one second

A neural network generates the amino-acid sequence for a given protein shape in about one second, over 200 times faster than the best previous software, without specialist tuning. A related study shows that proteins designed with ProteinMPNN fold correctly much more often, including nanoscale rings and complex assemblies. Researchers describe it as the beginning of machine learning in protein design.

Technology: ProteinMPNN

The network predicted about 2,000 new sequences as foldable; over 100 were produced in the lab, and for three, nuclear magnetic resonance and X-ray crystallography confirmed the predicted shapes. Only three proteins were characterized in detail; using the method to create useful functions (drugs, enzymes) is a future goal.

15 Jul 2021University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

RoseTTAFold: accessible prediction of protein structures

A three-track neural network (sequence, residue interactions, and 3D structure) computes a structure in about ten minutes on a single gaming computer. Baker’s group predicted hundreds of new structures, including little-known proteins in the human genome. The web server received over 4,500 requests in the previous month. The page does not state limitations.
