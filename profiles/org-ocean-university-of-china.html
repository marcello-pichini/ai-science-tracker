ETopt-guided screening identifies thermophilic beta-agarases

ETopt combines sequence, structural and protein-language-model features in an ensemble XGBoost model to predict enzyme optimum temperatures. The authors report wet-lab screening that identified two novel thermophilic beta-agarases. The draft is limited to those source-identified enzyme findings; model benchmarks and other screened enzyme classes are not counted as separate discoveries. 10 May 2025Ocean University of China; University of Sydney; University of Tasmania; Laoshan Laboratory

Machine learning reconstructs deep-time crustal thickness and links thickening episodes with porphyry deposits

A machine-learning mohometry model estimated paleo-crustal thickness from 66 geochemical proxies across subduction, collision and intraplate settings, extending chemical reconstruction beyond arc-related rocks. Tested reconstructions for southern Tibet and South China agreed with independently constrained crustal histories (R² = 0.937; RMSE = 4.3 km). The authors report that reconstructed thickening episodes correlate with porphyry-ore formation in ancient orogens.

Technology: Machine-learning mohometry regression model with feature-importance filtering

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A machine-learning mohometry model estimated paleo-crustal thickness from 66 geochemical proxies across subduction, collision and intraplate settings, extending chemical reconstruction beyond arc-related rocks.
  • Still unknown: The ancient thickness histories and porphyry association are model-derived reconstructions, not direct measurements of past Moho depth or proof that thickening caused ore formation. Validation is reported in southern Tibet and the South China Block; R²/RMSE summarize the model data and do not remove uncertainty from proxy assumptions, tectonic context or the geological record. The study reports a correlation that may inform exploration, not a validated mineral-deposit prediction system.
  • AI role: The regression model learned relationships between whole-rock geochemical measurements and Moho depth from a global database spanning subduction, collision and intraplate tectonic settings. It estimated ancient crust thickness from records that may be missing elements and enabled reconstruction beyond arc magmas; feature-importance filtering highlighted geochemical proxies. Those model-derived reconstructions supported the paper’s comparison of crust-thickening episodes with porphyry-deposit timing.
