10 May 2025Ocean University of China; University of Sydney; University of Tasmania; Laoshan Laboratory

Machine learning reconstructs deep-time crustal thickness and links thickening episodes with porphyry deposits

A machine-learning mohometry model estimated paleo-crustal thickness from 66 geochemical proxies across subduction, collision and intraplate settings, extending chemical reconstruction beyond arc-related rocks. Tested reconstructions for southern Tibet and South China agreed with independently constrained crustal histories (R² = 0.937; RMSE = 4.3 km). The authors report that reconstructed thickening episodes correlate with porphyry-ore formation in ancient orogens.

Technology: Machine-learning mohometry regression model with feature-importance filtering

Those model-derived reconstructions supported the paper’s comparison of crust-thickening episodes with porphyry-deposit timing.
