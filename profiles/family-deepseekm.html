27 Oct 2026Shanghai AI Laboratory; Fudan University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; University of Science and Technology of China; Tsinghua University

Unchanged agent instructions could appear to improve during skill selection

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 16 tuned instruction sets, held-out task success averaged only 0.1 percentage points above the starting instructions. In replay tests, the same acceptance logic often selected apparent improvements even when every candidate kept the original instructions.

Technology: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GPT-6 Luna; model-driven skill optimizers

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned agents performed each task under original and edited natural-language instructions. Their repeated scores supplied the null replay and the held-out comparison.
Test and result
The authors tested three optimizers on three benchmarks. Their no-change replay draws task-success probabilities from a fitted beta-binomial posterior, simulates each candidate as another run of the seed instructions, and applies the original acceptance rule. Small development sets had at most 30 tasks.
Limitation / still unknown
The no-change result concerns the studied acceptance rules and resampling design. An aggregate near-zero test result does not establish equivalence of every skill or model. The null replay assumes episodes are independent conditional on each task’s success probability; its prior is fitted using seed development and test episodes, so the replay is a model-based diagnostic rather than a newly executed unchanged-instruction optimization trial.

27 Oct 2026Infinite Ascent

Paper trading agents echoed peer wording without a detectable immediate trade shift

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a seven-day paper trading study with 200 agents across four model families, later-starting board agents reused peers' phrases 6.8 percentage points more often when they could see a fresh post. A timing-based comparison found no detectable same-morning shift in matching trades, with an estimate of 2.4 points and a 95% interval of −4.1 to 9.3.

Technology: Opus 5.5; GPT-6.1 Sol; Grok 4.6; DeepSeek V4 Pro

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Four learned model families ran separate paper trading accounts and produced peer messages while retaining independent decisions. Frozen views and time-stamped paper orders made phrase and order comparisons possible.
Test and result
Timing-based comparisons found 6.8 percentage points more same-morning eight-word phrase reuse for pairs with an opportunity to read a recent peer post (95% interval 1.7–11.8, 68 board group mornings). In a separate comparison subtracting timing differences in isolated groups, same-direction orders in the same stock differed by 2.4 points (95% interval −4.1 to 9.3).
Limitation / still unknown
The study used one group per communication regime and model family, with only seven trading days and simulated fills at live prices. Timing was not randomized. The interval leaves room for some short-run effects and says nothing about market impact.

26 Oct 2026Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Judge-labeled invalid coding traces were longer in two related models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Code-generation reasoning traces labeled invalid by a model-judge ensemble averaged 17,163 tokens for DeepSeek-R1-0528, compared with 7,102 for valid-labeled traces. Its distilled 8B variant showed the same direction. These labels concern reasoning coherence and are explicitly distinct from whether the final code passes functional tests.

Technology: DeepSeek-R1-0528; DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B

Family DeepSeek: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
DeepSeek R1 models generated traces; DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-3-Flash and GLM-5.1 judged their validity, while learned semantic annotators classified reasoning actions.
Test and result
Researchers collected traces under matched prompting and decoding. Three model judges each rated trace validity three times; confidence-weighted votes labeled high-confidence cases and two human annotators adjudicated the remainder. The reported length contrast groups these trace-validity labels, not final code correctness.
Limitation / still unknown
Validity judgments include coherence and unnecessary detours, which may themselves relate to trace length; the contrast does not establish a causal effect of long reasoning or a functional-code failure rate. The models share a lineage, selected complete traces exclude incomplete or unparsable responses, and verbal traces need not faithfully reflect internal reasoning.

26 Oct 2026Independent researcher Marios Tsatsos

Language-model safety probes transferred despite shifts in learned directions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across four language-model families, the directions fitted to separate benign and harmful requests shifted by about 17° to 35° after distillation or instruction tuning. Applying the base probes unchanged shifted AUC by −0.011 to +0.027 on XSTest and −0.015 to +0.053 on an independent prompt set.

Technology: Fitted linear activation probes; Qwen2.5, Llama 3.1 and distilled DeepSeek-R1 variants

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Fitted activation classifiers estimated directions and classification performance on base and successor models. Their transfer separated change in direction from change in discrimination.
Test and result
Linear classifiers trained on the four base models were evaluated unchanged on two matched successors per family. Transfer was compared with the same probe’s performance on the base model.
Limitation / still unknown
Similar observed accuracy does not establish equivalence, and XSTest fold intervals are too narrow. On OR-Bench the probes flagged 29% to 42% of benign prompts that sounded harmful.

Language models trusted incorrect reassurances more than incorrect warnings in a defensive audit

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In tests of a prerecorded defensive audit dataset, incorrect reassurances moved language-model verdicts toward agreement more than incorrect warnings did. A prespecified check on seven additional model configurations found a mean asymmetry of 0.16, with a 95% interval of 0.10–0.24. This measures verdict behavior under controlled evaluation conditions.

Technology: DeepSeek; Qwen; GLM; Kimi audit models

Family DeepSeek: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Eleven language model configurations independently evaluated prerecorded audit examples and supplied verdicts that were compared under paired evidence conditions.
Test and result
The study analyzed 16,572 evaluations on 916 matched pairs across eleven configurations. The seven-configuration confirmation used prerecorded samples and paired comparisons against valid evidence.
Limitation / still unknown
The confirmation was conducted by the same team. The data and decision conditions are controlled and do not measure real-world incidence.

22 Oct 2026University of Wisconsin-Madison; University of Oxford

Unacknowledged suggestions influenced AI reasoning through question and filler tokens

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers found that activation changes at questions and filler tokens carried part of an unacknowledged suggestion’s influence in two language models. Swapping those native activations partially shifted the models’ next-token predictions toward the predictions made without the suggestion.

Technology: Gemma-4-E4B-it, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B

Family DeepSeek: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fitted residual stream editor identified candidate locations where hidden representations preserve a suggestion’s influence, which researchers tested with independent native activation patching.
Test and result
The two-target patch recovered 16.2% of the counterfactual distribution shift in Gemma and 19.3% in the DeepSeek model, measured by the study’s KL-divergence recovery metric. Equal-size random token controls recovered less.
Limitation / still unknown
The experiment concerns held-fixed responses under the tested suggestions. It establishes partial mediation at the selected locations, with effects that differ by model.

26 Aug 2026Soochow University; Zhengzhou University; Fudan University; Hefei National Laboratory

Physics-informed retrieval-augmented language model guides synthesis of five previously unreported inorganic crystals

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers built PIRAG-LM, which combines a structured knowledge base of experimental synthesis routes, physics-based chemical/structural/thermodynamic similarity retrieval, and LLM route reasoning. In prospective tests, it guided synthesis of five compounds previously unreported in experimental literature: BaMo0.3In0.7O2.95, BaNb0.4In0.6O2.9, Hg[B(CN)4]2, CoCo(CN)6 and SrNb2Fe2(PO4)6. Powder X-ray diffraction patterns were consistent with target phases. This is one paper-level synthesis-planning result, not five separate entries.

Technology: PIRAG-LM, physics-informed retrieval-augmented language model, with DeepSeek-V4 used in the structured synthesis-knowledge-base extraction workflow

Family DeepSeek: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 materials Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
PIRAG-LM retrieved synthesis precedents by chemical, structural and thermodynamic similarity and proposed synthesis methods, precursors and processing conditions. Experts evaluated routes and refined practical parameters; researchers synthesized and characterized the five target phases. The final route class, precursor logic and main processing sequence generally followed model recommendations.
Test and result
Researchers built PIRAG-LM, which combines a structured knowledge base of experimental synthesis routes, physics-based chemical/structural/thermodynamic similarity retrieval, and LLM route reasoning.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a source-checked preprint (not treated as peer-reviewed here). The reported new-compound claims refer to absence from experimental literature and prospective synthesis; PXRD matches support target phase formation, but the paper does not establish broad application performance or independent replication. Experts selected materials and adjusted operational conditions; the system did not autonomously run experiments. Five compounds remain bundled as one paper-level finding.
