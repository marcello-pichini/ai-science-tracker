24 Feb 2026Pennsylvania State University; Georgia Institute of Technology

Deep-learning tracking of mantle simulations finds continental-margin subduction zones persist longer

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fully convolutional network segmented subduction zones in two-dimensional mantle-convection simulations using temperature, vertical velocity and fineness fields. Across the modeled runs, zones forming near continental margins were generally longer-lived and less sensitive to internal heating than oceanic zones, supporting the hypothesis that continental lithosphere can stabilize subduction during early-Earth-like conditions. This is a computational-model result rather than direct observation of ancient Earth.

Technology: Attention-based fully convolutional network with custom segmentation loss

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Provided threshold-free, spatially contextual tracking of evolving subduction zones in simulation outputs; the resulting tracked histories enabled statistical comparison of lifetimes and sensitivity to mantle convective vigor by tectonic setting.
Test and result
A fully convolutional network segmented subduction zones in two-dimensional mantle-convection simulations using temperature, vertical velocity and fineness fields.
Limitation / still unknown
The physical inference comes from two-dimensional numerical mantle-convection models and their selected parameter space, not empirical reconstruction of Earth's early subduction history. The AI performs segmentation/tracking; the tectonic contrast is a statistical result from model outputs.

1 May 2025Pennsylvania State University; University of Minnesota

LIMITS-guided peptide design yields selective anti-tuberculosis host-defense peptides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The Penn State/University of Minnesota team used the Lead Informed Machine Interrogation of Therapeutic Sequences (LIMITS) workflow to train random-forest and neural-network predictors on approximately 100 host-defense peptides, using lead mutational scans and biophysical sequence features. Experimentally tested neural-network-designed MAD5.1 and MAD5.3 showed reported MICs of 5 μM against M. tuberculosis H37Rv, selectivity indices >16 against an eight-organism non-mycobacterial panel, and mammalian-cell toxicity indices >100. The study identifies peptide length as a potentially underappreciated activity determinant.

Technology: LIMITS (Lead Informed Machine Interrogation of Therapeutic Sequences), using random-forest and feed-forward neural-network predictors

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Lead-candidate mutational scanning informed feature selection and training on a limited ~100-peptide dataset; the models iteratively selected candidate anti-TB peptide sequences for synthesis and bacteriological testing. NN-predicted MAD5 sequences were assayed against virulent and avirulent M. tuberculosis strains and counter-screened against other bacteria and mammalian cells.
Test and result
Experimentally tested neural-network-designed MAD5.1 and MAD5.3 showed reported MICs of 5 μM against M. tuberculosis H37Rv, selectivity indices >16 against an eight-organism non-mycobacterial panel, and mammalian-cell toxicity indices >100.
Limitation / still unknown
This is preclinical in-vitro peptide evidence, not animal efficacy or clinical treatment. The paper reports that MAD5.3 did not produce a statistically significant reduction in H37Rv viability after seven days of culture despite its MIC and selectivity/toxicity metrics; do not generalize the headline activity to every candidate or assay. MAD2.2 was moderately hemolytic. The 5 μM H37Rv MIC and high indices apply to selected peptides and stated assay conditions.

22 Nov 2024Pennsylvania State University

Time-series model tests sepsis prediction with an indeterminate option

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An attention-based LSTM evaluated hospital-record sepsis labels retrospectively. External-dataset AUC was 0.971 six hours before recorded onset; withholding uncertain classifications reduced false positives. This does not establish improved care or prospective non-ICU performance.

Technology: Attention-based long short-term memory neural network; conformal prediction

Editorial relevance 40 medicine PreprintScientific article Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The LSTM learns laboratory trajectories; conformal sets flag unfamiliar inputs.
Test and result
MIMIC-IV development and eICU-CRD retrospective evaluation. The November2024 preprint already reports this result; peer-reviewed version followed in May2025.
Limitation / still unknown
External labels used diagnosis strings rather than Sepsis-3. About 17% were indeterminate. Missingness eligibility used the six-hour window even for earlier forecasts. ICU-onset cases were included; preprocessing isolation is incompletely documented. No measured workflow benefit.

12 Sep 2024Pennsylvania State University; Utah FORGE

Deep learning expands the Utah FORGE DAS microearthquake catalog and resolves stimulation structure

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A ResNet-based DASEventNet model applied to continuous distributed acoustic sensing from the April 2022 Utah FORGE Well 16A (78)-32 stimulation manually verified 5,749 microearthquakes absent from the Silixa STA/LTA catalog; 45 of 5,794 model-positive windows were false detections on manual review. The study reports events down to Mw −1.80 versus −1.14 for STA/LTA and a broader Stage 3 seismicity distribution consistent with fracture geometry seen in an independent deep-borehole geophone catalog. This is a local event-catalog and stimulation-geometry result, not a direct DAS-based event-location measurement.

Technology: DASEventNet (ResNet-50 convolutional neural network)

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Trained on manually checked DAS event and noise windows, the network detected weak microearthquake waveforms in continuous seven-day DAS recordings. It expanded the catalog and enabled a denser spatiotemporal characterization of the hydraulic stimulation seismicity.
Test and result
A ResNet-based DASEventNet model applied to continuous distributed acoustic sensing from the April 2022 Utah FORGE Well 16A (78)-32 stimulation manually verified 5,749 microearthquakes absent from the Silixa STA/LTA catalog; 45 of 5,794 model-positive windows were false detections on manual review.
Limitation / still unknown
Original JGR: Solid Earth paper reports manual review of model-positive events and comparison with conventional Silixa detections. Model predicts event presence but does not estimate event locations or magnitudes from DAS alone; timing association and location/magnitude comparison use an independent deep-borehole geophone catalog with an assumed 0.2 s matching window. Fiber placement limits direct geometry inference. Detection of cataloged events is not independent proof of every physical interpretation.

17 Jun 2021Pennsylvania State University; Syracuse University

Machine-learning unmixing links acid rain and subsurface flowpaths to watershed CO₂ drawdown

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) was applied to stream-chemistry time series from three watersheds to infer solute endmembers and their changing contributions without predefining endmember chemistry. Combined with geological interpretation and independent geochemical evidence, the model separated shallow acid-rain-affected flow from deeper pyrite-weathering contributions. The analysis found that acid rain can reduce a watershed’s long-timescale CO₂-sequestration capacity and that omitting sulfide oxidation/acid-rain reactions overestimates weathering-driven CO₂ drawdown.

Technology: Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) machine-learning blind-source-separation model applied to stream chemistry

Editorial relevance 53 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The NMF model simultaneously inferred endmember chemical signatures and per-sample mixing proportions from solute ratios without preset endmember compositions. Researchers interpreted those patterns using watershed geology, sulfur isotopes, weathering profiles and calcite saturation, then estimated mineral-weathering rates and CO₂ sequestration. The ML decomposition enabled source separation where conventional endmember assumptions were uncertain; it did not directly measure a global carbon flux.
Test and result
Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) was applied to stream-chemistry time series from three watersheds to infer solute endmembers and their changing contributions without predefining endmember chemistry.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an inference from historical stream chemistry and watershed-specific geology, not a direct measurement of global CO₂ sequestration or a causal intervention. The NMF endmembers were interpreted as subsurface flowpaths using geological knowledge and supporting geochemical evidence; the inferred acid-rain sulfate export at Shale Hills includes a 19–31-year lag estimate. The result applies to three studied watersheds and the weathering timescales considered. Endmember counts and interpretations depend on the model setup and watershed reaction-front assumptions.
