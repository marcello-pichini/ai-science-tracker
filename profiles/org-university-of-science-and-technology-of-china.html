13 Jul 2026University of Science and Technology of China; Purple Mountain Observatory; Chinese Academy of Sciences

Transformer-based SDSS search identifies two previously unreported optical–UV tidal disruption events

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A PCA-enhanced Transformer classifier screened 298,195 SDSS DR7 spectra and selected 14 TDE-like cases for secondary review. The authors report two newly identified TDEs: SDSS J124225.39+642919.0, whose GALEX UV source was present by the spectrum date (before 11 February 2002), and SDSS J152459.70+045423.1, whose TDE-H+He spectrum coincided with a Catalina optical outburst in 2007–08. The first is reported as the earliest optical–UV TDE then known; one other model-selected source was a previously reported likely TDE.

Technology: PCA-enhanced Transformer TDE spectrum classifier

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The Transformer classifier screened preprocessed SDSS galaxy spectra and selected TDE-like events for follow-up assessment. From roughly 298,000 spectra it produced 14 cases above the selection threshold; after checking spectral features against archival GALEX or Catalina variability and other diagnostics, the authors reported two previously unreported TDEs and one previously reported likely TDE. Reported test-set precision and recall were 0.88 and 0.99, respectively.
Test and result
A PCA-enhanced Transformer classifier screened 298,195 SDSS DR7 spectra and selected 14 TDE-like cases for secondary review.
Limitation / still unknown
The source is an arXiv preprint whose arXiv record says accepted by The Astrophysical Journal; no journal DOI was supplied in the source checked. The TDE classifications are supported by spectral and contemporaneous archival photometric evidence, not new contemporaneous follow-up observations. The event dates predate 2020, but this record uses the 2026 source-publication date for the since-2020 catalog period. The paper also reports a third previously known likely TDE, which is not counted as a new discovery.

4 Apr 2026University of Science and Technology of China; Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

Weakly supervised deep learning maps seamount morphology patterns associated with tectonic setting

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A weakly supervised deep-learning analysis combining satellite-derived gravity and bathymetry produced a global seamount inventory and reported systematic morphology differences: broad edifices along fast-spreading ridges and steeper forms near intraplate hotspots. The authors interpret these differences as reflecting geodynamic controls on volcanic construction. Many detections rely on indirect gravity/bathymetry data rather than shipborne mapping, so individual feature identifications and causal interpretations require further validation.

Technology: Weakly supervised deep-learning detector with vision foundation model, iterative pseudo-label refinement, and elliptical Gaussian fitting

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Detected and morphologically characterized seamount features in global gravity and bathymetry data despite sparse labels, enabling the cross-setting comparison that revealed tectonically organized morphology patterns.
Test and result
A weakly supervised deep-learning analysis combining satellite-derived gravity and bathymetry produced a global seamount inventory and reported systematic morphology differences: broad edifices along fast-spreading ridges and steeper forms near intraplate hotspots.
Limitation / still unknown
The publisher abstract describes numerous previously undocumented features but does not establish shipborne confirmation for every mapped detection. The reported morphology-setting relationship is an observational pattern and should not be read as proof of a unique causal mechanism.

21 Aug 2023University of Science and Technology of China; Chinese Academy of Sciences

Active-learning search experimentally realizes a high-zT Cu₁₋ₓAgₓGaTe₂ thermoelectric

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Compressed-sensing symbolic regression and active learning screened the Cu₁₋ₓAgₓGaTe₂ thermoelectric series and guided synthesis of selected compositions. The published study reports a p-type Cu₀.₄₅Ag₀.₅₅GaTe₂ sample with a measured thermoelectric figure of merit zT of about 1.90 at 770 K, using measured heat capacity in the analysis. This is one paper-level materials result from a targeted search, not a claim that every composition in the series is new.

Technology: SISSO compressed-sensing symbolic regression within an active-learning workflow

The model guided candidate selection; the researchers synthesized and characterized the samples and measured the properties.

1 May 2023University of Science and Technology of China; Mengcheng National Geophysical Observatory

Machine-learning classification shows rigidity-corrected moment release is similar across earthquake depths

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random-forest classification of source-time-function characteristics for 3,675 global Mw > 5.5 earthquakes showed that depth-dependent rigidity explains much of the differences in rupture duration, length and stress drop between shallow, intermediate and deep earthquakes. Once medium rigidity is corrected, the analyzed events exhibit similar moment-release processes, supporting a constant-strain-drop interpretation across depths and host-rock types.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning classification and feature-importance analysis

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Compared source-time-function features across depth classes and identified surrounding-medium rigidity as the principal explanatory feature, enabling the rigidity-corrected cross-depth comparison.
Test and result
Random-forest classification of source-time-function characteristics for 3,675 global Mw > 5.5 earthquakes showed that depth-dependent rigidity explains much of the differences in rupture duration, length and stress drop between shallow, intermediate and deep earthquakes.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a global observational analysis of 3,675 moderate-to-large earthquakes. Its constant-strain-drop conclusion is a supported scaling interpretation, not proof that every earthquake rupture mechanism is identical; depth class and source-time-function estimates have sampling limitations.
