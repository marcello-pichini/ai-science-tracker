29 Sep 2026University of Pittsburgh

FOCAL couples interpretable ML to regulatory networks and finds NFATC2-IRF8 interplay

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: FOCAL couples state-specific and dynamic gene regulatory networks with outcome-supervised latent factors learned by interpretable machine learning to prioritize regulatory subnetworks. In B and T cells it prioritized subnetworks underlying established states and transient episodes preceding them. It uncovered an NFATC2-IRF8 interplay in activated B cells, validated by in vitro and in vivo genetic perturbations, where the two factors restrain plasmablast differentiation and promote germinal center fate. Preprint; mouse and cell systems.

28 Apr 2025University of Pittsburgh

Deep-network encoding models reveal a complexity hierarchy in auditory cortex

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Intracerebral recordings from 20 epilepsy-surgery patients listening to natural sounds were modelled with representations from layers of a deep neural network pretrained to categorise sounds. The depth of the best-fitting layer tracked anatomy, with shallow, middle and deep layers mapping to core, lateral belt and parabelt regions, with smooth gradients within regions. Sites that began encoding later had higher representational complexity independent of region. The network serves as an analysis lens in a small patient sample.

Technology: Encoding models using layers of a pretrained sound-categorisation deep neural network

AI contribution

Test and result

Limitation / still unknown

19 Feb 2024University of Pittsburgh

SLIDE links islet T-cell clone expansion to immune gene programs

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: SLIDE, an interpretable latent-factor interaction method, was applied to paired single-cell RNA-seq and T-cell receptor data from islet-derived CD4 T cells in NOD mice. The analysis identified latent components associated with clonal expansion and grouped inhibitory-receptor and intracellular signaling/apoptosis-related genes among the associated programs. The result is an inference from existing multiomic data, not a causal perturbation; it is a distinct T1D application in the same article as the separately catalogued systemic-sclerosis analysis.

Technology: SLIDE interpretable latent-factor interaction discovery and exploration

AI contribution
SLIDE uses interpretable machine learning to identify interactions among latent factors in high-dimensional omics data and was applied to connect T-cell gene programs with clonal expansion.
Test and result
The method was applied to paired single-cell RNA-seq/TCR-seq from islet-derived CD4 T cells in NOD mice; the analysis reports latent components associated with clone expansion and compares the findings against an independent published dataset and alternative methods.
Limitation / still unknown
The inferred gene programs are associations from existing data; the T1D analysis does not prospectively perturb the proposed pathways or establish causality.

27 May 2023University of Pittsburgh; University of Pittsburgh Medical Center

Learned symptom clusters track heart-failure emergency outcomes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Fitted k-means partitioned nine recorded symptoms in438 emergency patients with prior heart failure into three groups. The indigestion group had adjusted odds ratios of1.8 for both acute heart-failure exacerbation and30-day adverse cardiac events compared with the group lacking indigestion and dyspnea. These are exploratory same-cohort associations, not a validated triage intervention.

Technology: K-means clustering

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Fitted k-means learns symptom-based patient partitions; the resulting assignments enable clinical outcome comparisons. PCA performed poorly and was not the retained partition.
Test and result
A secondary analysis of prospectively collected emergency presentations used438 patients and nine binary symptoms. Silhouette analysis selected three clusters of178,100 and160 patients. Two reviewers adjudicated clinical outcomes; regression adjusted for selected baseline variables.
Limitation / still unknown
The same selected cohort was used to discover and test clusters, with univariate-informed adjustment. Confidence intervals for the reported odds ratios round to1.0; no independent replication or causal interpretation is established. Outcomes include a heterogeneous composite; clustering is not proof of distinct disease mechanisms or improved emergency decisions.

24 Nov 2021University of Pittsburgh

Machine learning links neighborhood context to acute coronary diagnoses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random forest evaluated acute coronary syndrome in 2,400 emergency chest-pain patients using demographics, medical history and neighborhood measures. A held-out 720-patient test supported an AUC of 0.71, while regularized regression retained a neighborhood association after adjustment. Diagnoses were independently adjudicated. These single-city observational results do not establish causal neighborhood effects, individual income effects or benefit from model-guided triage.

Technology: Random forest; regularized logistic regression

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Limitation / still unknown

15 Jan 2020University of Pittsburgh; UPMC

Quantification of Retinal Nerve Fibre Layer Thickness on Optical Coherence Tomography with a Deep Learning Segmentation-Free Approach

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A ResNet34 model predicted retinal nerve fibre layer thickness directly from unsegmented OCT scans. In three held-out sets, predictions tracked conventional measurements on clean scans and more closely matched same-day good-quality references on scans with segmentation errors or other artefacts. This retrospective proof of concept did not test patient outcomes.

Technology: ResNet34 deep-learning model predicting retinal nerve fibre layer thickness from unsegmented optical coherence tomography scans

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fine-tuned ResNet34 deep-learning model predicts global retinal nerve fibre layer thickness directly from unsegmented OCT B-scans.
Test and result
The full study evaluated 25,285 B-scans from 1,338 eyes of 706 subjects in three test sets. Correlations with reference measurements were 0.983 on clean scans, 0.972 versus 0.829 for conventional segmentation on scans with segmentation errors, and 0.940 versus 0.640 on scans with other artefacts.
Limitation / still unknown
Retrospective test; references were conventional measurements or same-day good-quality scans. No downstream patient benefit was evaluated.
