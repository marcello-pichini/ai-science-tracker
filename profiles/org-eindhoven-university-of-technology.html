Joint molecular modeling identifies seven low-micromolar kinase hits beyond its training chemistry

A joint molecular model combined bioactivity prediction and molecule reconstruction to estimate how unfamiliar a candidate structure was to its training distribution. In a prospective screen of commercial compounds against PIM1 and CDK1, unfamiliarity-informed selection led to seven compounds with low-micromolar potency and limited similarity to training molecules. The authors also evaluated the metric across 33 experimentally labelled bioactivity datasets.

Technology: Joint molecular model (JMM): semi-supervised molecular property-prediction and reconstruction model using a SMILES encoder; reconstruction loss defines the unfamiliarity score

The study screened only a small selected set per target after computational ranking. It does not establish the unfamiliarity measure as universally superior or demonstrate animal or clinical benefit.
  • AI role: The model used reconstruction-based unfamiliarity alongside predicted bioactivity and prediction uncertainty to rank molecules for a prospective virtual screen. Researchers tested selected commercial compounds against PIM1 and CDK1; seven compounds showed low-micromolar potency. AI supported prioritization and selection, while wet-lab assays established activity.
