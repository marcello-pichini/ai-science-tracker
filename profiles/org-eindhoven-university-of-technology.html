20 Oct 2026Eindhoven University of Technology

AI-guided simulations rank two zeolites differently as CO₂ pressure changes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: RaspAssistant compared three literature-based adsorption models against existing MFI zeolite measurements, selected the one with the lowest aggregate error and used it to simulate CO₂ adsorption in 197 pure-silica structures. JBW had the highest simulated uptake at 1 kPa, while GIS led at 10 and 100 kPa and had the highest calculated working capacity from 1 to 100 kPa. These are model-dependent rankings of known structures. The reference measurements were for MFI.

Technology: RaspAssistant; GPT-5.2; GPT-5.4

Family GPT-5: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The GPT-5.2 supervisor and GPT-based specialists compared candidate force fields with reference measurements, selected the model for the larger screen, coordinated simulations and analyzed pressure-specific rankings.
Test and result
Three force-field combinations were compared against existing MFI adsorption isotherms at three temperatures. The selected model was used for 2,955 adsorption calculations on 197 structures. The outcome is relative simulated uptake and working capacity in the specified screening study.
Limitation / still unknown
Quantitative transfer from MFI to other frameworks was not established. The screened candidates were not retested experimentally in this case study, and reported descriptors could not identify a pore-scale mechanism. Prior work already considered GIS and JBW for carbon capture.

22 Apr 2026Eindhoven University of Technology; Centre for Living Technologies (TU/e, WUR, UU and UMC Utrecht)

Joint molecular modeling identifies seven low-micromolar kinase hits beyond its training chemistry

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A joint molecular model combined bioactivity prediction and molecule reconstruction to estimate how unfamiliar a candidate structure was to its training distribution. In a prospective screen of commercial compounds against PIM1 and CDK1, unfamiliarity-informed selection led to seven compounds with low-micromolar potency and limited similarity to training molecules. The authors also evaluated the metric across 33 experimentally labelled bioactivity datasets.

Technology: Joint molecular model (JMM): semi-supervised molecular property-prediction and reconstruction model using a SMILES encoder; reconstruction loss defines the unfamiliarity score

Editorial relevance 53 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model used reconstruction-based unfamiliarity alongside predicted bioactivity and prediction uncertainty to rank molecules for a prospective virtual screen. Researchers tested selected commercial compounds against PIM1 and CDK1; seven compounds showed low-micromolar potency. AI supported prioritization and selection, while wet-lab assays established activity.
Test and result
The authors also evaluated the metric across 33 experimentally labelled bioactivity datasets. The reported wet-lab candidate assays directly test the molecular-model predictions within the study.
Limitation / still unknown
The kinase finding is a prospective in-vitro hit-identification result, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy. Seven low-micromolar hits were reported across PIM1 and CDK1; CDK1 was described as independent of the study’s earlier dataset analysis. The study screened only a small selected set per target after computational ranking. It does not establish the unfamiliarity measure as universally superior or demonstrate animal or clinical benefit.

30 Jan 2026Eindhoven University of Technology; Amsterdam UMC; Leiden University Medical Center; Angiogenesis Analytics

Ultrasound neural maps identify pathology-defined prostate cancer tissue

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A three-dimensional CNN and multilayer perceptron combined quantitative ultrasound features to map clinically significant prostate cancer. Development involved 250 patients, followed by 77 later-enrolled patients; reported voxel-level AUC was 0.884 in that later group. Pathology registration and a surgical-enriched cohort limit interpretation. This does not establish patient-level diagnostic accuracy, biopsy benefit or equivalence to MRI-guided care.

Technology: Three-dimensional convolutional neural network; multilayer perceptron

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Three CNN feature branches learn ultrasound patterns and an MLP combines them into voxel-level cancer probability maps.
Test and result
Patient-partitioned development and temporally separate same-centre testing, using reconstructed prostatectomy pathology or negative MRI/biopsy references.
Limitation / still unknown
Lesion/patient-level clinical performance and treatment benefit remain unestablished; entire-dataset feature standardization and registration uncertainty limit a strictly untouched-test interpretation.
