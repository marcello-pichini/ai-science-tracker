27 Oct 2026The University of Manchester; University of Rajshahi; Varendra University

Verifier feedback raised both correct and incorrect model verdicts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: On 32 software verification tasks, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B averaged 22.0 correct verdicts with verifier feedback versus 12.7 without it. Incorrect verdicts also rose from one to seven. A deterministic validation baseline returned 26 correct verdicts and left six unresolved.

Technology: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct; verifier output interpretation

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The trained coding model interpreted formal-verification evidence, allowing a direct comparison of verdicts under controlled feedback conditions.
Test and result
The fixed assertion set was evaluated with and without feedback for three seeds. Correct, incorrect and unresolved decisions were counted separately.
Limitation / still unknown
The subset was small and selected from one verification benchmark. The deterministic baseline included an additional validation step and is not an equivalent raw-feedback condition.

27 Feb 2026The University of Manchester; National Astronomical Research Institute of Thailand

Random-forest target selection supported nine millisecond pulsar discoveries

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random-forest classifier ranked unidentified Fermi-LAT sources, from which the team selected 79 targets for MeerKAT UHF observations. The survey reported nine new millisecond pulsars, all detected in coherent tied-array beams; seven were also confirmed as Fermi-LAT counterparts through gamma-ray pulsations. This entry covers only the nine MSP discoveries. Six slower pulsars were also reported, but five were found in a wider incoherent beam and are not included in the AI-linked result. The authors note earlier optical redback-candidate identification for PSR J1910−5320 and single-pulse reports in the J0657−4657 beam; no claim is made that these were the first observations at all wavelengths.

Technology: Random Forest classifier used to rank unidentified Fermi-LAT sources as pulsar-like candidates

Editorial relevance 53 astronomy Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A random-forest model ranked unassociated Fermi-LAT sources for target selection; radio searches of 79 selected targets yielded the nine coherent-beam MSP discoveries.
Test and result
The survey observed 79 random-forest-selected Fermi-LAT sources and reports nine new MSPs, all detected in coherent tied-array beams; seven were confirmed as Fermi-LAT counterparts through gamma-ray pulsations.
Limitation / still unknown
The nine-MSP record is limited to the coherent-beam result. The paper also reports six slow pulsars, but five were detected in the wider incoherent beam and are not included in this AI-linked claim. The authors note an earlier optical redback-candidate identification for J1910−5320 and single-pulse reports from the J0657−4657 beam; the entry does not claim priority across wavelengths or treat those reports as prior radio-pulsar discoveries.

21 May 2024The University of Manchester; Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy; South African Radio Astronomy Observatory; INAF-Osservatorio Astronomico di Cagliari; University of Cape Town; Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics; Leibniz University Hannover; Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics; Western Sydney University

PICS-assisted survey reported seven new Small Magellanic Cloud pulsars

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A MeerKAT survey of the Small Magellanic Cloud reported seven new radio pulsars. Its pipeline partially classified folded candidates with PICS, a machine-learning system, and applied score and signal-to-noise thresholds before volunteer visual review and follow-up. The paper does not identify each pulsar’s PICS score, so this is an AI-assisted survey-level result rather than a claim that the model independently discovered all seven sources. The paper also reports pulsar–nebula associations; the later timing paper follows this cohort and is not counted again.

Technology: Pulsar Image-based Classification System (PICS), a machine-learning classifier for folded pulsar candidates

Editorial relevance 47 astronomy PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
PICS partially classified folded candidates and filtered the candidate set before human visual classification.
Test and result
The survey reports seven new SMC radio pulsars; candidate triage included PICS filtering followed by human review and observational follow-up.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper does not report per-pulsar PICS scores; it does not establish model-only detection or that PICS was essential for each individual pulsar.
