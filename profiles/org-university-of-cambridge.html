29 Oct 2026University of Cambridge; California Institute of Technology

GPT-6 Astra resolves the spectral isometry conjecture for arbitrary unital C*-algebras

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The paper proves that every unital surjective complex-linear spectral isometry between unital C*-algebras is a Jordan isomorphism; without unitality, such a map is a central-unitary multiple of a Jordan isomorphism. The proof establishes that entire one-parameter groups of unital spectral isometries are algebra automorphisms, then reduces the general case to the commutative case. The authors report that the result was formally verified in Lean.

Technology: OpenAI GPT-6 Astra

10 Jul 2026University of Cambridge; and collaborators

Multiscale machine-learning microscopy identifies robust paleomagnetic carriers in meteorite WIS 91600

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A multiscale microscopy study of carbonaceous chondrite WIS 91600 used a deep-learning classifier to segment magnetic remanence-carrying ensembles and quantify their volume and texture. Combined chemical mapping, 3D nanotomography, and diffraction showed fine matrix-hosted magnetite has paleomagnetic recording potential comparable to magnetite framboids but is less susceptible to strong interactions; transient heating also reduced the contribution of matrix pyrrhotite through its vacancy-ordering state. This is one meteorite case study, and the mineralogical conclusions draw on the full microscopy and diffraction workflow, not AI alone.

Technology: UNET segmentation, SIGMA interactive machine-learning mineral analysis, and few-shot image segmentation

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution

Test and result
A multiscale microscopy study of carbonaceous chondrite WIS 91600 used a deep-learning classifier to segment magnetic remanence-carrying ensembles and quantify their volume and texture.
Limitation / still unknown
The source is a single meteorite case study. The AI classified/quantified image ensembles, while mineral identities and remanence interpretation use additional microscopy, chemical mapping, nanotomography, and diffraction; extrapolation to other carbonaceous chondrites requires more samples.

20 May 2024University of Cambridge; Politecnico di Torino; Uppsala University

Machine learning guided multi-metric optimization of photocatalytic CO2 reduction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Bayesian optimization guided experiments on a five-component self-assembled photocatalytic micelle system for CO₂-to-CO reduction. The authors report that optimized conditions increased measured CO yield from 1.48 ± 0.24 to 5.72 ± 0.12 μmol h⁻¹, with improved turnover and quantum-yield metrics while maintaining CO selectivity. The article’s narrative fold-change (4.2×) is inconsistent with the displayed means (about 3.9×), so this record gives the means and omits the fold claim. The findings apply to the tested aqueous micelle system and conditions.

Technology: Bayesian optimization for experimental selection; random-forest models and SHAP for later analysis

17 Apr 2024University of Cambridge; US National Institutes of Health, National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases; A*STAR; Indiana University School of Medicine

Iterative machine learning identifies potent inhibitors of alpha-synuclein secondary nucleation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A structure-based iterative-learning workflow combined prior α-synuclein fibril-binding hits, a junction-tree variational autoencoder, a random-forest regressor and Gaussian-process residual model to rank compounds for repeated testing. Prospective iterations produced multiple molecules inhibiting seeded α-synuclein aggregation in vitro; lead I4.05 had a reported kinetic inhibitory constant (KIC50) of 0.52 μM (95% CI 0.45–0.59 μM), compared with extrapolated values of 18.2 μM for its parent and 36.4 μM for Anle-138b in the authors’ assay. Leads also showed activity in lipid-induced and spontaneous aggregation assays.

Technology: Iterative structure-based learning workflow using a junction-tree variational autoencoder, random-forest regressor and Gaussian-process residual model

Editorial relevance 53 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The machine-learning model ranked a ZINC-derived evaluation set for potency using earlier assay measurements, then researchers tested and fed results from successive rounds back into the model. It proposed compounds for experimental testing; aggregation assays measured outcomes. Initial fibril binders came from an earlier docking-and-screening study, so this paper reports iterative optimization rather than an entirely independent start.
Test and result
A structure-based iterative-learning workflow combined prior α-synuclein fibril-binding hits, a junction-tree variational autoencoder, a random-forest regressor and Gaussian-process residual model to rank compounds for repeated testing.
Limitation / still unknown
Evidence is biochemical/in-vitro inhibition of α-synuclein aggregation, including seeded, lipid-induced and spontaneous assays. The paper does not establish animal efficacy, neuronal protection or clinical benefit. The KIC50 and comparison values are assay-specific; no random-molecule comparator was tested, and model QSAR R² was described as modest. This is related to, but not the same paper/result as, the earlier docking-screen study that supplied starting compounds.

26 Mar 2024University of Cambridge; Max Planck Institute for Astronomy; Gemini Observatory/NSF NOIRLab; INAF; collaborators

Three spectroscopically confirmed z≈6 quasars found through contrastive learning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A self-supervised contrastive-learning search of Dark Energy Survey imaging isolated a group of 11 sources that included seven known z≈6 quasars. Follow-up Gemini-South and archival NTT spectroscopy confirmed three additional quasars at redshifts 5.94–6.07. One source, J0109−5424, shows evidence of a foreground object and is a lensed-quasar candidate, but its multiple-image status requires high-resolution imaging.

Technology: Self-supervised contrastive-learning neural network trained on Dark Energy Survey imaging data

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Grouped sources into a learned embedding-space island that prioritized four unclassified candidates; spectroscopy confirmed three as quasars, including one source missed by conventional color selection because foreground light contaminated the photometry.
Test and result
A self-supervised contrastive-learning search of Dark Energy Survey imaging isolated a group of 11 sources that included seven known z≈6 quasars.
Limitation / still unknown
The three quasar identifications are spectroscopically confirmed, but the possible gravitational lens interpretation of J0109−5424 remains unconfirmed pending high-resolution imaging. The reported 91% efficiency refers to ten quasars among the eleven-source island, including seven previously known objects; three of the four previously unclassified targets were confirmed quasars. It is not a universal completeness estimate.

24 Oct 2023University of Cambridge; and Cambridge University Hospitals; Barts Health NHS Trust; Medical University of Vienna; collaborating institutions

Integrated radiogenomic machine learning predicts neoadjuvant chemotherapy response in metastatic ovarian cancer

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The IRON ensemble combined baseline clinical data, CA-125, ctDNA and CT radiomics from primary and metastatic lesions to estimate tumor-volume change during neoadjuvant chemotherapy for high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma. In an independent 42-patient Barts cohort, the integrated model achieved AUC 0.78 for RECIST 1.1 response classification versus 0.47 for a clinical-only model; radiomics-containing scores significantly correlated with volume change in internal holdout. The study also measured spatial heterogeneity, with omental lesions showing better response than pelvic/ovarian disease.

Technology: IRON ensemble combining elastic net, support-vector regression and random forest

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Pretreatment clinical, blood and multi-lesion radiomic features predicted volumetric response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Held-out and external evaluation showed radiomics added predictive information beyond clinical variables.
Test and result
In an independent 42-patient Barts cohort, the integrated model achieved AUC 0.78 for RECIST 1.1 response classification versus 0.47 for a clinical-only model; radiomics-containing scores significantly correlated with volume change in internal holdout. The model used historical Cambridge and Barts cohorts with baseline and post-treatment imaging, including an external retrospective test set.
Limitation / still unknown
Model analysis of two prospective observational cohorts (training n=72, internal holdout n=20, external n=42), not a decision trial. External ctDNA unavailable and imputed to training means. Sample size limits generalizability; treatment benefit from model-guided decisions was not tested.
