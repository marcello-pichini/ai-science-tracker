16 Dec 2024Fraunhofer Institute for Algorithms and Scientific Computing (SCAI); University of Bonn; Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School; Boehringer Ingelheim

Deep learning identifies externally replicated fast and slow cognitive-progressor groups in early Alzheimer dementia

Deep learning clustered cognitive and functional trajectories in 283 people with early dementia probably due to Alzheimer disease into fast- and slow-progressing groups. The trajectory groups were externally validated and independently replicated in a 2,779-person dementia cohort. A classifier using cross-sectional data at diagnosis predicted progression group in external validation with AUC 0.70 ± 0.01.

Technology: Deep-learning clustering of longitudinal cognitive/functional trajectories, followed by machine-learning classification from cross-sectional diagnosis data

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: The trajectory groups were externally validated and independently replicated in a 2,779-person dementia cohort.
  • Still unknown: These are prognostic progression groups, not established biological disease subtypes or treatment-response predictors. The classifier had modest external discrimination (AUC 0.70 ± 0.01). Trial-size reduction and cost savings were estimated by emulating hypothetical enrichment and were not demonstrated in an actual clinical trial. The study does not show improved patient outcomes.
  • AI role: Deep learning grouped patients using multivariate disease trajectories; the authors then trained a classifier to predict those progression subgroups from measurements available at diagnosis. The model-supported fast/slow progression groups were externally validated and independently replicated in a separate dementia cohort.
