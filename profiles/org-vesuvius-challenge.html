2 Nov 2026Vesuvius Challenge

Writing seen in PHerc. 306B for the first time in 2000 years

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Vesuvius Challenge awarded a $50K First Letters Prize for scroll PHerc. 306B, where the winning submission reached the 10-character target and the papyrology team agreed on a reading, tentatively a Greek phrase with parallels in Aristotle and Philodemus. Ink was detected by multiplying two models (TRACE v3B and a fine-tuned A-hn). Letters remain faint, the readable area is small, and the work relied on hundreds of hours of manual labeling.

Technology: ML ink-detection models (TRACE v3B, A-hn) with VC3D GrowPatch

Editorial relevance 53 humanities Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine-learning ink-detection models (TRACE v3B and a fine-tuned A-hn) produced the ink maps; the readings and prize review were done by humans.
Test and result
The reading was checked by the papyrology team, which agreed on the letters with the winning submission, and was compared against 25 community submissions and a 10-character target; the page describes no independent replication.
Limitation / still unknown
The reading is partial and uncertain, letters remain faint, training on machine-generated labels could compound errors (not assessed), and the Grand Prize is not yet awarded.

25 Jun 2026Vesuvius Challenge; EduceLab and ESRF

A Herculaneum scroll read end to end without opening

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Machine-learning ink detection on synchrotron scans let papyrologists read about 22 columns of a likely Stoic ethics text from scroll PHerc. 1667. The text is fragmentary because only the inner core survives, and humans transcribed and reviewed every reading.

Technology: Machine-learning ink detection with virtual unwrapping

The text is fragmentary because only the inner core survives; systematic recovery across the library is described as future work.

5 Feb 2024Vesuvius Challenge

Vesuvius Challenge: the first Herculaneum scroll can be read

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The $700,000 prize went to a submission with 11 additional columns and more than 2,000 characters (about 5% of the first scroll), using models to detect ink and automated segmentation. Papyrologists evaluated 15 anonymized columns and recovered more text from the winning submission. Segmentation remains largely manual and costly.

Technology: Ink detection with vision models and automated segmentation

Editorial relevance 93 humanities Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Models detected ink in CT scans (a TimeSformer-based model was strongest), and an automated tool produced the segmentation of the papyrus surfaces.
Test and result
Papyrologists independently evaluated 15 anonymized columns, and the winning submission yielded the most text for every reviewer, meeting the stated criteria.
Limitation / still unknown
The comprehensive study is not yet published, only rough draft transcriptions exist, segmentation is largely manual, and about 5% of the first scroll has been read.

12 Oct 2023Vesuvius Challenge

Vesuvius Challenge: first letters read from an unopened Herculaneum scroll

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: For the first time, a complete word (‘porphyras’, purple) was read from inside a never-opened carbonized scroll; a model trained on crack patterns yielded 13 letters independently and unanimously annotated by papyrologists. The letters are faint and lack context; other readings are not ruled out.

Technology: Machine-learning models for ink detection

Editorial relevance 87 humanities Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine-learning models trained on ink crackle patterns (and a second model with pseudo-labeling) read letters from the scroll.
Test and result
A model trained on crack patterns produced 13 letters, which papyrologists independently annotated with varying confidence; the page links only to code and no paper.
Limitation / still unknown
The letters are faint and lack context, word boundaries are unclear, and the page states other readings are not ruled out and the papyrological team was still investigating.
