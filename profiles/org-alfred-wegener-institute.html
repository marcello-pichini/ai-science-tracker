Observation-trained machine learning halves estimated Arctic winter sea-ice surface cooling

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural network trained on observations from five Arctic field campaigns reduced ERA5 errors in the Arctic sea-ice surface energy budget and removed ERA5’s wintertime near-surface warm bias of about 4 K. The corrected estimates imply about 50% weaker winter surface cooling and 80–120 cm seasonal thermodynamic ice growth, closer to SMOS–CryoSat thickness increases than ERA5’s 150–200 cm estimate.

Technology: Feed-forward neural network product SEBai

Editorial relevance 60 climate Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The network learned the relationship between ERA5 atmospheric states and observed fluxes from MOSAiC, SHEBA, AO2018, N-ICE2015 and ARTofMELT campaigns, then bias-corrected hourly Arctic sea-ice surface fluxes for 1994–2024 to derive a revised regional climatology and ice-growth estimates.
Test and result
A neural network trained on observations from five Arctic field campaigns reduced ERA5 errors in the Arctic sea-ice surface energy budget and removed ERA5’s wintertime near-surface warm bias of about 4 K.
Limitation / still unknown
Validation uses leave-one-campaign-out cross-validation and multiple field campaigns, but direct flux observations remain sparse in space and time. The climatology applies to ice-covered Arctic regions north of 70°N with sea-ice concentration above 80%; authors do not recommend extrapolation to marginal ice zones or coastal regions. The ice-growth values are thermodynamic estimates rather than direct pan-Arctic measurements.
