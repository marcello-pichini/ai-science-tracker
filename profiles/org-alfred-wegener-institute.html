Observation-trained machine learning halves estimated Arctic winter sea-ice surface cooling

A neural network trained on observations from five Arctic field campaigns reduced ERA5 errors in the Arctic sea-ice surface energy budget and removed ERA5’s wintertime near-surface warm bias of about 4 K. The corrected estimates imply about 50% weaker winter surface cooling and 80–120 cm seasonal thermodynamic ice growth, closer to SMOS–CryoSat thickness increases than ERA5’s 150–200 cm estimate.

Technology: Feed-forward neural network product SEBai

The ice-growth values are thermodynamic estimates rather than direct pan-Arctic measurements.
  • AI role: The network learned the relationship between ERA5 atmospheric states and observed fluxes from MOSAiC, SHEBA, AO2018, N-ICE2015 and ARTofMELT campaigns, then bias-corrected hourly Arctic sea-ice surface fluxes for 1994–2024 to derive a revised regional climatology and ice-growth estimates.
