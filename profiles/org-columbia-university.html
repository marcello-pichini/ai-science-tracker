30 Apr 2026Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Harvard University

AI-guided design enables E. coli ribosomal proteins without isoleucine

Researchers used computational protein design and generative AI to recode ribosomal proteins in E. coli so they function without isoleucine, then assembled an engineered strain that remained viable and genomically stable over more than 450 laboratory generations. The result shows a reduced amino-acid alphabet can support core translation machinery in this bacterium; the organism still uses isoleucine elsewhere.

Technology: Protein design models including ESM2, MSATr, ProteinMPNN and AfDesign

8 Jan 2025Columbia University; Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence; collaborating institutions

GET and interaction assays link a leukemia-risk PAX5 variant to stronger NR2C2 binding

The General Expression Transformer (GET) model inferred cell-type-specific transcription-factor motif interactions from regulatory data and nominated a lymphocyte-specific interaction between PAX5 and nuclear-receptor-family factors. AlphaFold multimer modeling and PAX5 proximity-labeling in a B-ALL cell line supported interaction with NR2C2; the leukemia-associated PAX5 G183S variant showed increased PAX5–NR2C2 proximity signal. The variant-specific expression profile was enriched for predicted NR/3 and shared PAX5/NR/3 target genes, suggesting a possible regulatory mechanism for the germline risk variant.

Technology: GET (General Expression Transformer) foundation model for cell-type-specific regulatory grammar; AlphaFold2/AlphaFold Multimer for structural hypothesis assessment

14 Dec 2022Columbia University; ETH Zurich; Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research (WSL)

Unsupervised seismic clustering reveals distinct drainage signals at Gornergletscher

An unsupervised analysis of five weeks of seismic records from Gornergletscher, Switzerland, separated cryoseismic signals into clusters associated with the 2007 Gornersee lake flood and with afternoon/evening meltwater flow. One high-energy, broadband noise cluster began about two days before the reported onset of subglacial flooding; the authors suggest it captures the earlier initiation of englacial and subglacial drainage and improves the flood chronology.

Technology: SpecUFEx unsupervised feature extraction: non-negative matrix factorization, hidden Markov modeling, principal component analysis, and k-means clustering
