22 Sep 2026Stanford University; Boston University; University of Auckland; Colorado State University

Machine learning attributes post-2015 emissions to intensification of Europe’s recent heat extremes

A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology. It found >95% probability that anthropogenic emissions since the 2015 Paris Agreement intensified Europe’s summer temperature extremes in 2021–2025; for the June 2025 heatwave, regional mean temperature is estimated to have been 0.34 °C lower under 2015 cumulative emissions, with >99% probability.

Technology: Generative diffusion model for spatially explicit temperature fields

16 Sep 2026Stanford University; collaborating institutions

Paper2Agent integrates perturbation datasets to prioritize GPR137 at a psoriasis risk locus

Paper2Agent-generated agents used AlphaGenome to prioritize GPR137 as the top predicted gene affected by psoriasis-associated variant rs887314 in CD4+ T cells, then integrated that prediction with existing MPRA-coupled scCRISPRi and CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq data. A cross-screen correlation of gene-expression signatures supported GPR137 as the probable causal gene under stimulated conditions (Spearman rho 0.613 at 8 h and 0.630 at 48 h; FDR < 0.05), but not at rest. The paper presents this as activation-dependent computational evidence.

Technology: Paper2Agent-generated AlphaGenome, MPRA-coupled scCRISPRi and CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq agents, coordinated through an AI co-scientist

28 Feb 2026Stanford University; Anthropic

Claude's Cycles: a Hamiltonian cycle decomposition found by Claude

Knuth's note reports that Claude Opus 4.6 found an explicit decomposition of the arcs of the m^3-vertex digraph into three directed Hamiltonian cycles for odd m, an open problem Knuth posed. Knuth's proof of the construction was reportedly formally verified in Lean by Kim Morrison. Caveat: sources disagree on the even case, which an independent arXiv paper (2603.24708) and a GPT-5.4 Pro proof treat differently.

Technology: Claude Opus 4.6

5 Jan 2026Stanford University and collaborating institutions

AI-generated virtual spatial proteomics reveals lung-cancer tumor–immune niches associated with immunotherapy response

HEX, a pathology-image AI model, predicts spatial expression of 40 protein biomarkers from routine H&E slides. In retrospective NSCLC cohorts, combining HEX-derived virtual spatial proteomics with histology improved prognosis prediction and, in a separate 148-patient immune-checkpoint-inhibitor cohort, improved response prediction over H&E-only and virtual-proteomics-only models. The authors interpreted virtual maps as showing co-localized T-helper and cytotoxic T-cell niches among responders and immunosuppressive macrophage/neutrophil aggregates among non-responders.

Technology: HEX (H&E to protein expression), using a pathology foundation-model backbone; MICA (multimodal integration via co-attention)

4 Sep 2025Stanford University; Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia – Osservatorio Vesuviano; University of Naples Federico II

AI-enhanced seismic catalog exposes a ring-fault system at Campi Flegrei

A retrained machine-learning workflow applied to continuous seismic records from 2022–2025 expanded the Campi Flegrei earthquake catalog from about 12,000 to more than 54,000 events. The more complete locations delineated a sharply defined, shallow caldera ring-fault system, illuminated previously unclear northern faults, and identified very shallow hybrid events likely associated with the hydrothermal system. The authors report no direct signature of upward magma migration in the analyzed data.

Technology: Retrained machine-learning seismic detection and phase-picking workflow developed at Stanford

14 Aug 2025Stanford University and collaborators

Decoding inner speech with a brain-computer interface

In four people with severe motor and speech impairments, researchers found imagined-speech signals in the motor cortex and decoded them with machine learning. They also tested filters and a trigger phrase to limit unintended decoding. This is a proof of principle with implanted electrodes: free, rapid inner speech cannot be decoded accurately, and attempted speech remains more accurate.

Technology: Brain-computer interface and machine learning

8 May 2025Stanford University; Cedars-Sinai Medical Center; University of California, Los Angeles

Deep learning maps previously unknown disease signals in ECGs across two health systems

Researchers independently trained PheWASNet on ECG and electronic health-record data from Stanford Health Care and Cedars-Sinai to assess 1,243 disease phenotypes. In held-out patient splits at both centers, the study reported previously unrecognized ECG associations for disorders including respiratory failure, neutropenia and menstrual disorders (AUCs 0.86, 0.83 and 0.84 at Stanford, respectively). Cross-condition model-output analysis found that 35 of 37 strongly detectable non-cardiac phenotypes shared signals with only four other disease outputs, and simple ECG measurement models explained some findings.

Technology: PheWASNet, a multi-task deep-learning model based on a one-dimensional EfficientNet architecture

30 Apr 2025Stanford University

Regional deep-learning phase picking reveals 24 additional earthquakes in a Gorda Ridge swarm

A long-window regional phase picker and phase-association workflow detected 34 earthquakes in a 29 February 2024 swarm near the Gorda Ridge, offshore northern California: all 10 reported by the USGS plus 24 additional events. The detections were possible from stations roughly 300 km from the swarm, beyond the distance range represented by standard local-event pickers. This is one paper-level catalog result, not 24 separate discoveries.

Technology: SKYNET deep-learning P/S phase picker with GENIE graph-neural-network phase association

21 Feb 2025Stanford University, Oklahoma Medical Research Foundation, Duke University, University of Pennsylvania and collaborating institutions

Mal-ID maps disease-associated immune-receptor patterns across infections and autoimmune conditions

Mal-ID analyzed B-cell and T-cell receptor repertoires from 593 people, including patients with COVID-19, HIV, systemic lupus erythematosus and type-1 diabetes, influenza-vaccination recipients and healthy controls. Its combined models classified held-out immune states and highlighted disease-associated receptor patterns; interpretation identified distinct autoreactivity features in lupus and type-1 diabetes and antigen-associated receptor sequences in COVID-19. The authors also evaluated classification on independent COVID-19 cohorts.

Technology: Mal-ID (MAchine Learning for Immunological Diagnosis), an ensemble using repertoire feature models and ESM-2 protein-language-model embeddings

2 Dec 2024Stanford University; Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics; Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory; collaborators

Representation learning uncovers the extragalactic X-ray transient XRT 200515 in Chandra archives

A representation-learning pipeline searched archived Chandra event data and identified XRT 200515, a previously unnoticed extragalactic fast X-ray transient in the Large Magellanic Cloud. Its light curve has a sharp sub-10-second hard burst followed by an approximately 800-second oscillating tail with spectral softening. The paper discusses possible origins including a low-energy giant magnetar flare or a Type I burst from a faint low-mass X-ray binary; the source's physical nature is unresolved.

Technology: Unsupervised representation-learning pipeline using sparse autoencoders, dimensionality reduction and density-based clustering

Generative agents built from interview transcripts simulate 1,000 people

LLM agents built from participants' full interview transcripts reproduced their General Social Survey answers at about 85% of the rate at which participants reproduced their own answers two weeks later, a normalized score. They also matched personality and economic-game results (normalized correlations of about 0.80 and 0.66) and replicated four of five behavioural experiments. Caveat: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed, and the results are normalized rather than raw accuracy.

Technology: Generative agent architecture with LLM and full interview transcripts

14 Jul 2021Stanford University and Chan Zuckerberg Biohub collaborators

Machine learning reveals a nitrogen-assimilation strategy in Bacteroides

A microbiome metabolomics study used random-forest models to associate bacterial metabolite profiles with taxonomy and found a previously undescribed nitrogen-assimilation pattern in Bacteroides/Bacteroidetes. Follow-up growth experiments tested 60 Bacteroidetes strains with asparagine or glutamine as the sole nitrogen source and supported the predicted phenotype; the study also reports a distinct reduced-sulfur utilization result in B. thetaiotaomicron.

Technology: Random-forest models over metabolomics profiles

25 Mar 2020Stanford University; Cedars-Sinai Medical Center

EchoNet-Dynamic: video-based AI for left ventricular ejection fraction

A deep learning model trained on echocardiogram videos segmented the left ventricle with a Dice coefficient of 0.92 and estimated ejection fraction with a mean absolute error of 4.1% (6.0% on an external dataset from another health system). Prospective evaluation with repeated human measurements found its variance comparable to or lower than that of human experts. Caveat: training data come from one institution's echocardiograms, with external testing on a single additional system.

Technology: EchoNet-Dynamic (video deep learning model)
