19 Nov 2026Stanford University

Calibrated AI detectors estimate rising AI-assisted drafting in four state courts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Stanford researchers applied the learned EditLens detector with ScoreMix calibration to 127,518 complaints from four U.S. county court systems. Their selected settings estimated rising AI-like drafting, reaching about 38% in Santa Clara and 36% in San Francisco in 2026 for non-debt-collection complaints. Calibration used documented hallucination filings and assumed their detector-score distribution represented other AI-assisted writing. Detector settings, imperfect controls, case mix and selection of conspicuous AI mistakes limit the estimates. These are population estimates, not proof that any particular filing used AI, fabricated citations or affected a judicial outcome. November 19 is the institutional announcement date; the working paper posting day is unestablished.

Technology: EditLens (Llama-3.2-3B); ScoreMix calibration

22 Sep 2026Stanford University; Boston University; University of Auckland; Colorado State University

Machine learning attributes post-2015 emissions to intensification of Europe’s recent heat extremes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology. It found >95% probability that anthropogenic emissions since the 2015 Paris Agreement intensified Europe’s summer temperature extremes in 2021–2025; for the June 2025 heatwave, regional mean temperature is estimated to have been 0.34 °C lower under 2015 cumulative emissions, with >99% probability.

Technology: Generative diffusion model for spatially explicit temperature fields

Test and result
A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology.
Limitation / still unknown

The paper emphasizes uncertainty from internal variability/model spread and reports robust regional-mean effects, while grid-cell-level changes are more limited. The June 2025 value is a median modeled counterfactual estimate.
