Autonomous ML search and experiments find Fe–Co–Ir/Pt alloys above the Slater–Pauling magnetization limit

A game-tree search using Gaussian-process predictions and first-principles calculations proposed dilute Ir and Pt additions as a counterintuitive way to raise Fe–Co magnetization. Combinatorial sputtering and magnetic measurements experimentally found Fe–Co–Ir and Fe–Co–Pt compositions with magnetization exceeding the conventional Fe₃Co₁ Slater–Pauling limit; the highest-moment points reported in the ternary maps were near Fe₈₁Co₁₅Ir₄ and Fe₈₅Co₁₁Pt₄. The study’s calculations attribute the effect to Ir/Pt-induced increases in local Fe and Co moments.

Technology: Autonomous materials search using a Gaussian-process regression model with game-tree search and an upper-confidence-bound acquisition rule, coupled to KKR-CPA first-principles calculations

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: A game-tree search using Gaussian-process predictions and first-principles calculations proposed dilute Ir and Pt additions as a counterintuitive way to raise Fe–Co magnetization.
  • Still unknown: The highest-moment composition suggested by the autonomous search (Fe₈₂Co₁₃Ir₄Pt₁) was not itself the maximum in the measured ternary composition spreads; the Fe–Co–Ir and Fe–Co–Pt maps identified different peak compositions. The finding is based on sputtered, annealed thin-film composition spreads and room-temperature MOKE/SQUID measurements, not bulk ingots or device testing. The eight-element search space was narrowed by preliminary calculations. KKR-CPA results assume ideal disordered bulk states at 0 K, unlike the film measurements.
  • AI role: The autonomous system iteratively selected compositions in an eight-component disordered-alloy search using ab-initio magnetic-moment estimates and a game-tree Gaussian-process model balancing exploration and exploitation. After about 43 days it proposed Fe₈₂Co₁₃Ir₄Pt₁; that hypothesis directed combinatorial synthesis and testing of Fe–Co–Ir and Fe–Co–Pt spreads. The follow-up measurements found compositions surpassing the prior Fe₃Co₁ benchmark and motivated theoretical analysis of impurity-enhanced Fe/Co local moments.
