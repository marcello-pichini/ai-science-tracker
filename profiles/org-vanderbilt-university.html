30 Jun 2026Vanderbilt University

An expanded document audit found training matches for 35.2% of test pages

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An expanded audit of the RVL-CDIP benchmark found training-set matches for 14,076 test pages, or 35.20%, and 14,151 validation pages, or 35.38%. The search used learned image matching and counted near-duplicates that shared a document template as well as copies. A smaller 2023 analysis had already identified substantial duplication.

Technology: CLIP, SuperPoint, LightGlue

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned CLIP embeddings screen scanned documents for likely matching pairs, and SuperPoint and LightGlue match their visual structure before final duplicate classification.
Test and result
Learned CLIP embeddings proposed likely cross-split matches before SuperPoint and LightGlue visual matching and a text-similarity check. The calibration set contained 403 matched pairs among 1,600 candidate pairs, with 82.9% recall at the chosen threshold.
Limitation / still unknown
The audit includes same-template pages and may miss matches. The earlier 2023 sample-based estimate of about 32% uses a different method and is not a baseline for measuring a change in duplication.

MutexaGPT-guided cold-adapted amylase variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The MutexaGPT multi-agent large language model system translated a cold-adaptation design request into molecular simulations and linker-variant proposals for bidomain amylase. The authors report experimentally validated variants with 1.7-fold and 3.7-fold activity enhancement at 0 °C. The preprint appeared on 19 June 2026; the peer-reviewed journal version followed on 10 September 2026.

Technology: Multi-agent large language model system coordinating high-throughput molecular dynamics simulations and mutation design

Editorial relevance 60 biology PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
LLM agents clarify the design request, translate it into computational plans and mutation patterns, and explain simulation outputs. High-throughput molecular dynamics metrics guide the proposed variants; experimental assays test their activity.
Test and result
The primary article reports two MutexaGPT-guided bidomain amylase linker variants with experimentally measured 1.7-fold and 3.7-fold activity enhancement at 0 °C. The Methods define relative activity using the 0 °C to 45 °C activity ratio.

The experimental result is specific to the tested bidomain amylase and variants; broader enzyme-family transfer and independent reproduction are not established.

26 Mar 2026Vanderbilt University

Machine-learning-guided genome mining links an Amycolatopsis BGC to dipyrimicins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning model that predicted antibacterial activity from biosynthetic-gene-cluster sequences prioritized Amycolatopsis azurea DSM 43854. Bioactivity-guided fractionation recovered dipyrimicins A and B from this strain, and genome analysis linked them to a previously uncharacterized dip BGC with a 76% predicted antibacterial probability; broth-microdilution assays confirmed activity against several test strains.

Technology: Machine-learning predictor of antibacterial activity from biosynthetic gene clusters

Editorial relevance 47 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model ranked biosynthetic clusters and prioritized A. azurea DSM 43854 for experimental work. Researchers performed bioactivity-guided fractionation, isolated the compounds, linked them to the previously uncharacterized dip BGC, and tested antibacterial activity. The dip BGC was absent from the model’s training set, providing an out-of-training-set test of the prioritization approach.
Test and result
Bioactivity-guided fractionation recovered dipyrimicins A and B from this strain, and genome analysis linked them to a previously uncharacterized dip BGC with a 76% predicted antibacterial probability; broth-microdilution assays confirmed activity against several test strains.
Limitation / still unknown
Dipyrimicins themselves had been reported previously from a related Amycolatopsis strain; the paper’s new result is their isolation from A. azurea DSM 43854 and first linkage to its BGC, not discovery of the molecules as novel compounds. Reported antibacterial potency was moderate and tested against a few strains (for dipyrimicin A, MICs included 32 µg/mL for B. spizizenii and 64 µg/mL for S. aureus; >128 µg/mL for P. aeruginosa). Proposed functions of some tailoring genes remain speculative.
