Machine-learning-guided genome mining links an Amycolatopsis BGC to dipyrimicins

A machine-learning model that predicted antibacterial activity from biosynthetic-gene-cluster sequences prioritized Amycolatopsis azurea DSM 43854. Bioactivity-guided fractionation recovered dipyrimicins A and B from this strain, and genome analysis linked them to a previously uncharacterized dip BGC with a 76% predicted antibacterial probability; broth-microdilution assays confirmed activity against several test strains.

Technology: Machine-learning predictor of antibacterial activity from biosynthetic gene clusters

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Bioactivity-guided fractionation recovered dipyrimicins A and B from this strain, and genome analysis linked them to a previously uncharacterized dip BGC with a 76% predicted antibacterial probability; broth-microdilution assays confirmed activity against several test strains.
  • Still unknown: Dipyrimicins themselves had been reported previously from a related Amycolatopsis strain; the paper’s new result is their isolation from A. azurea DSM 43854 and first linkage to its BGC, not discovery of the molecules as novel compounds. Reported antibacterial potency was moderate and tested against a few strains (for dipyrimicin A, MICs included 32 µg/mL for B. spizizenii and 64 µg/mL for S. aureus; >128 µg/mL for P. aeruginosa). Proposed functions of some tailoring genes remain speculative.
  • AI role: The model ranked biosynthetic clusters and prioritized A. azurea DSM 43854 for experimental work. Researchers performed bioactivity-guided fractionation, isolated the compounds, linked them to the previously uncharacterized dip BGC, and tested antibacterial activity. The dip BGC was absent from the model’s training set, providing an out-of-training-set test of the prioritization approach.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13122635/
