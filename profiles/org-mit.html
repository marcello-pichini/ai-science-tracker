22 Jun 2026MIT, Karolinska Institutet, and collaborators

A1: deep learning identifies an antibiotic candidate against gonorrhea

A screen of about six million compounds found candidates active against resistant Neisseria gonorrhoeae. A1 reduced bacteria in animal models, and proteomics, genetics, and biochemistry confirmed its target, alanine racemase. These are preclinical results, published in Science Translational Medicine.

3 Oct 2025MIT

Enterololin: AI helps clarify an antibiotic and its mechanism

An AI screen of over 10,000 molecules identified enterololin, and DiffDock predicted its binding to LolCDE within minutes, confirmed by resistant E. coli mutants, RNA-seq, and CRISPR. The mechanism was clarified in about six months versus 18–24 with traditional methods. In mice, in Crohn's-like inflammation models, healing was faster than with vancomycin. Early stage; no human testing.

Technology: Machine-learning screening and DiffDock

14 Aug 2025MIT

Antibiotics designed with generative AI against gonorrhea and MRSA

Over 36 million possible compounds were screened computationally: candidate NG1 killed resistant Neisseria gonorrhoeae in a dish and in a mouse model, while DN1 cleared a MRSA skin infection in mice. Only 2 of 80 fragment candidates could be synthesized; these are preclinical results.

28 Jul 2025MIT

An autonomous platform speeds research on polymer mixtures

The platform tests up to 700 mixtures a day (96 at a time), guided by a genetic algorithm: hundreds of mixtures outperformed the individual components, and the best retained 73% enzyme activity, 18% more than the best component. Application is limited to enzyme thermal stability, replenishment is still manual; published in Matter.

Technology: Genetic algorithm with robotics

17 Dec 2024MIT

Boltz-1: an open model for biomolecular structures

A fully open MIT model predicts 3D structures of proteins and other biomolecules and, according to its developers, matches AlphaFold3's accuracy; unlike AlphaFold3, its training pipeline is also released. The comparison is developer-reported; the group calls it a starting point for the community.

8 Apr 2024MIT

AI identifies semapimod against dormant bacteria

A machine-learning system selected semapimod, a known anti-inflammatory drug, for activity against dormant Gram-negative bacteria. MIT reports laboratory activity against E. coli and A. baumannii and an effect that sensitizes these bacteria to other antibiotics; the result is preclinical and does not demonstrate efficacy in patients.

20 Dec 2023MIT

A new class of antibiotic candidates against MRSA

A deep-learning model trained on about 39,000 compounds screened about 12 million commercial compounds; of about 280 purchased and tested, two from the same class reduced MRSA burden tenfold in two mouse models, with very low toxicity to human cells. Results from plates and mice; toxicity was partly predicted computationally.

25 May 2023MIT and McMaster University

Abaucin: a targeted antibiotic against Acinetobacter baumannii

A model trained on about 7,500 compounds screened 6,680 new compounds in under two hours; of 240 tested in the laboratory, nine were antibiotics, including abaucin, which was effective against wound infections in mice. Activity is highly narrow (no effect on the other bacteria tested) and still preclinical.

20 Jan 2023MIT, Mass General Cancer Center and Chang Gung Memorial Hospital

Sybil: lung-cancer risk prediction up to six years from a low-dose CT

The model predicts risk from a single CT with a six-year C-index of 0.75 (NLST), 0.81 (MGH) and 0.80 (CGMH), and a one-year ROC area between 0.86 and 0.94. Retrospective validation on three independent datasets; trained only on smokers, with prospective testing and testing in nonsmokers as next steps.

Technology: Sybil (neural network on CT scans)

28 Jan 2021MIT

Mirai: future breast cancer risk from mammograms

Mirai, trained on over 200,000 exams, was evaluated on hospital data from the United States, Sweden, and Taiwan. In a comparison of high-risk groups at Massachusetts General Hospital, it identified nearly twice as many future diagnoses as Tyrer-Cuzick; it does not demonstrate reduced mortality.

Language models identify viral regions less prone to immune escape

Analysis of influenza, HIV, and coronavirus sequences identifies regions of surface proteins less likely to mutate to escape immune responses, including the S2 subunit of spike. These are computational leads for studying possible vaccine targets, not validated vaccines.

A model trained on volunteer recordings recognized 98.5% of coughs associated with confirmed infections in the test reported by MIT. This is an early result on data collected online, with health status also self-reported; it is not a diagnosis or prospective clinical validation.

20 Feb 2020MIT

Halicin: deep learning identifies an antibiotic

A model trained on about 2,500 molecules screened the roughly 6,000 compounds in the Broad Institute’s Drug Repurposing Hub and identified halicin, which killed many resistant bacteria in the lab and cleared A. baumannii infections in mice within 24 hours. Cell-culture and mouse results, not human studies; the mechanism remains preliminary.

Technology: Graph neural network for predicting antibacterial activity
