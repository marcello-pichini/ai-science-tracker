24 Oct 2024German Climate Computing Center (DKRZ); Met Office; University of New South Wales; Freie Universität Berlin

AI reconstruction extends the 1911 French heatwave signal into September

A partial-convolution U-Net trained on CMIP6 climate simulations and transferred to European station-based extreme indices reconstructed the poorly observed 1911 heatwave. The model estimates that in parts of southern France, more than 40% of September days exceeded the 90th percentile of maximum temperature, extending the heatwave signal into fall. The reconstructed pattern is similar to 20th Century Reanalysis Version 3 and aligns spatially with higher French mortality in 1911; the HadEX3 gridded record does not show the same pronounced heat.

Technology: Climate Reconstruction AI (CRAI), a U-Net with partial convolutions trained on CMIP6 historical simulations and transferred to the HadEX-CAM European extreme-index dataset

23 Aug 2024German Climate Computing Center (DKRZ); Max Planck Institute for Meteorology; Center for Climate Physics, Institute for Basic Science; Pusan National University

Machine learning detects volcanic fingerprints and suggests a northern extratropical signal for the unidentified 1809 eruption

A convolutional neural network trained on idealized Earth-system-model simulations classified eruption occurrence and broad latitude band from global surface-temperature anomalies in the first post-eruption summer. Applied to reanalysis and observational climate records, the method identified major historic events and assigned a northern-extratropical footprint to the unidentified 1809 eruption. The same analysis found that some smaller events leave weaker, less distinguishable temperature signals.

Technology: Convolutional neural network with explainable-AI relevance mapping
