23 Apr 2025University of California San Diego; collaborating biomedical research institutions

AlphaFold structure analysis helps reveal PHGDH transcriptional activity linked to Alzheimer’s amyloid pathology

AlphaFold-based structural analysis highlighted a helix-helix-turn-helix subdomain in PHGDH that resembles a TALE homeodomain, prompting tests of a previously unrecognized DNA-binding and transcriptional function. Experiments supported PHGDH binding to promoters and activation of IKKα and HMGB1 in astrocytes, independently of its metabolic enzyme activity. In mouse models and human brain organoids, PHGDH expression promoted amyloid pathology; a blood-brain-barrier-permeable inhibitor of its transcriptional function reduced amyloid pathology and improved measured behavioral deficits in mice.

Technology: AlphaFold structure prediction, followed by structural comparison of the predicted PHGDH fold

Learned promoter models identify human-specific transcription motifs

Models trained on promoter activity measurements helped characterize a human-specific downstream promoter variant and select synthetic motifs with species-specific activity. In-vitro transcription assays tested the predicted preferences with human and Drosophila extracts. These are gene-regulation and construct-assay findings, not demonstrated effects in organisms or disease.

9 Sep 2021University of California San Diego

COMPOSER sepsis model flags unfamiliar cases instead of guessing

COMPOSER, a deep-learning early sepsis predictor, reached AUCs of 0.925-0.953 in ICU and 0.938-0.945 in ED across six cohorts (515,720 patients) from two US health systems. About 20% of non-septic and 8% of septic prediction windows were marked indeterminate, and the model gave a median early warning of 12.2 hours in ICU and 2.1 hours in ED before the first antibiotics order. The abstract reports no prospective deployment or patient-outcome results.

Technology: COMPOSER deep-learning model with uncertainty-based indeterminate output

Boolean-network learning prioritizes an IBD organoid barrier target

Learning from transcriptomic networks helped prioritize PRKAB1 for experimental follow-up. An agonist improved a barrier-resistance endpoint in about 80% of diseased patient-derived organoids in the tested sample. This is preclinical target-response evidence, not treatment of patients or clinical efficacy. The network direction incorporates sample labels and does not independently establish causal disease progression.

20 Apr 2021University of California San Diego

Bayesian optimization and graph learning identify two synthesized ultra-incompressible hard materials

A Bayesian optimization workflow using symmetry-constrained crystal relaxation and the MEGNet graph neural network screened 399,960 transition-metal boride and carbide compositions. The study identified MoWC₂ (P6₃/mmc) and ReWB (Pca2₁), then synthesized both by in-situ reactive spark-plasma sintering; the measured hardness was reported to agree with model and DFT predictions. The result is limited to these two synthesized compounds and the authors’ tested synthesis conditions.

Technology: BOWSR (Bayesian Optimization With Symmetry Relaxation) with the MEGNet graph neural network energy model

1 May 2020University of California San Diego; Duke University

Machine-learning-guided synthesis realizes four single-phase refractory carbide compositions

A random-forest model ranked new chromium-containing multicomponent carbide compositions by predicted entropy-forming ability. After density-functional calculations and experimental synthesis, X-ray diffraction identified four previously unreported single-phase rock-salt compositions: CrMoNbVWC₅, CrMoNbTaWC₅, CrMoTaVWC₅, and CrMoTiVWC₅. The first three were high-EFA selections; CrMoTiVWC₅ was a medium-EFA composition and required extended annealing to reach chemical homogeneity.

Technology: Random-forest models using chemical attributes, with and without CALPHAD thermodynamic features

22 Jan 2020University of California San Diego; and collaborators

Interpretable multi-omics machine learning reveals unexpected drug mechanisms in Huntington disease models

An interpretable machine-learning model integrated transcriptomic, epigenomic, metabolomic and proteomic data from Huntington-disease cell models to identify previously unknown mechanisms for protective compounds. The analysis predicted autophagy activation for one compound group and mitochondrial-respiration inhibition for another; follow-up imaging, biochemical and energetics assays experimentally confirmed these effects in the cell model.

Technology: Interpretable network-optimization model integrating multi-omics over a molecular-interaction network
