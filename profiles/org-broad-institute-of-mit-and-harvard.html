HiDDEN finds malignant-like cell signals in early multiple-myeloma precursor samples

HiDDEN refines sample-level case labels at single-cell resolution. In human bone-marrow single-cell RNA-seq data, its labels tracked manual neoplastic-cell annotations in mixed precursor samples and inferred malignant-like cells in three low-purity MGUS samples that the original manual analysis had labeled as containing only healthy cells. The inferred cells were enriched for previously described malignancy-associated gene signatures. In a separate mouse demyelination dataset, HiDDEN identified an early endothelial subpopulation with expression changes associated with angiogenesis, blood–brain-barrier breakdown and extracellular-matrix responses.

Technology: HiDDEN, a machine-learning framework for refining disease/condition labels at single-cell resolution

23 Oct 2024Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard; The Jackson Laboratory; Yale University

Machine-guided synthetic regulatory DNA drives cell-type-specific expression across cell lines and vertebrates

The CODA platform used the Malinois deep-learning model and sequence-search algorithms to design synthetic cis-regulatory elements (CREs). In massively parallel reporter assays, 94.1% of the synthetic sequences met the authors’ cell-type-specificity criterion, versus 73.6% of model-selected natural sequences and 40.6% of chromatin-mark-selected natural sequences; synthetic sequences also had higher measured target/off-target separation across K562, HepG2 and SK-N-SH cells. In transgenic zebrafish, two of three liver-directed and two of three neuron-directed CREs showed the intended activity. One neuron-directed sequence, synN1, retained cortical and neuronal expression in mice, including postnatal neocortical layer 6 and subplate neurons.

Technology: CODA (Computational Optimization of DNA Activity) with Malinois deep neural network and AdaLead, simulated annealing, and Fast SeqProp sequence search/design algorithms

4 Aug 2022Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard; Harvard University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Brigham and Women's Hospital

Machine learning generates active daunomycin-binding synthetic nucleic-acid polymers outside selected sequence families

The study combined in-vitro selection with a conditional variational autoencoder trained on selection data to generate high-affinity highly functionalized nucleic-acid polymer (HFNAP) sequences. Experimentally generated CVAE sequences bound daunomycin with reported dissociation constants of 9–26 nM despite having no apparent primary-sequence similarity to experimentally selected polymers; competition selections found selected generated variants performed as well as or better than sequences obtained after multiple rounds of high-stringency selection.

Technology: Conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) trained on in-vitro selection-derived HFNAP sequence/fitness data
