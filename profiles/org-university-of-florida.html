23 Nov 2026University of Florida

Evolutionary optimization designs a simulated low-voltage photonic junction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint used NSGA-II evolutionary optimization to vary doping-mask geometry and jointly reduce modulation voltage–length product and optical loss in a silicon microring junction. The resulting C-shaped profile reached a simulated VπL of about 0.39 V·cm, compared with 0.44–0.72 V·cm for representative profiles listed by the authors. Carrier and optical simulations fed a four-bit photonic converter model. These are computational device-design results under stated process assumptions; no fabricated junction, measured bandwidth or demonstrated neural-network acceleration was reported.

Technology: NSGA-II multiobjective evolutionary optimization

23 Nov 2026University of Florida; D’Or Institute for Research and Education

Deep learning on lung-screening CT estimates spirometry in two external cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A three-dimensional convolutional neural network trained on 10,371 low-dose CT reconstructions from 5,975 NLST participants was fused with 20 densitometry and tracheal geometry features to predict FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC. Applied frozen to an external screening cohort (n = 268) and COPDGene (10,302 scans), it detected abnormal spirometry with AUROC 0.898 and 0.913, and concordance correlation coefficients were 0.80-0.88. Agreement was lower with overestimation in a COPD subgroup, the reference spirometry differed (pre- versus post-bronchodilator), and prospective utility is unproven.

Technology: 3D convolutional neural network fused with CT-derived densitometry and tracheal features, predicting FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A 3D CNN plus quantitative CT features estimated spirometric parameters from existing chest CT.
Test and result
A frozen model was evaluated on two external cohorts (n = 268 and 10,302 scans) against reference spirometry.
Limitation / still unknown
Prospective clinical utility; performance across populations, since sensitivity and specificity differed at the same decision rule; full text not reviewed.

11 Nov 2026University of Florida

MRI radiomics supports individualized Duchenne disease-trajectory reconstruction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: XGBoost combined baseline MRI radiomics and clinical features to estimate muscle fat-fraction trajectory parameters in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. The final dataset included 98 unique participants, with 68 soleus and 64 vastus-lateralis analyses; held-out reconstructed trajectories had reported mean absolute errors of 0.04 and 0.06. Targets came from a previously fitted nonlinear mixed-effects model, rather than directly observed trajectory parameters. The retrospective analysis identified candidate progression subgroups but did not establish treatment response, prospective prediction or external generalizability.

Technology: XGBoost; MRI radiomics

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Participant-level 80:20 train/test split; XGBoost estimates NLME-derived trajectory descriptors from baseline radiomic/clinical features; reconstruction compared with observed fat fraction. Derived targets can shrink sparse subject trajectories; this is not new prospective clinical utility.
Test and result
Held-out participant trajectory reconstruction compared with observed longitudinal fat fractions.
Limitation / still unknown
Independent cohort validation, robustness of derived targets and benefits for clinical trial design remain unestablished.

27 Jan 2026University of Florida; Oak Ridge National Laboratory; Quantum Formatics

Machine-learning screening and experiments identify two new superconductors, Be₂HfNb₂ and Be₂Hf₂Nb

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An AI-accelerated workflow screened more than 1.3 million material structures, combining a graph-neural-network model of superconducting properties with density-functional calculations and stability filters. The researchers experimentally synthesized and confirmed superconductivity in Be₂HfNb₂ and Be₂Hf₂Nb, reporting two previously unreported compounds among 741 computationally screened stable candidates. The two compounds were confirmed in the Be₂Nb₃ substitution series; other listed candidates remain computational predictions.

Technology: BEE-NET equivariant graph neural networks in an AI-accelerated high-throughput screening workflow

Editorial relevance 60 materials Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Predicted Eliashberg spectral functions and superconducting transition temperatures to prioritize candidate structures, followed by DFT stability calculations and experimental synthesis/characterization by the research team.
Test and result
An AI-accelerated workflow screened more than 1.3 million material structures, combining a graph-neural-network model of superconducting properties with density-functional calculations and stability filters.
Limitation / still unknown
The AI model accelerated candidate selection but did not itself perform the synthesis; DFT and laboratory confirmation were essential. The paper reports an overall screening precision of 86%, so most candidate predictions were not experimentally verified in this study.

23 Dec 2020University of Florida

Machine-learning variable selection supports 90-day heart-failure risk prediction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 3,189 patients hospitalized for heart failure, machine-learning variable selection supported a logistic model for 90-day acute-heart-failure readmission or death. LASSO selection and logistic regression yielded a reported AUC of 0.760, compared with 0.744 for logistic regression alone. This single-centre retrospective analysis used internal cross-validation; external performance and benefits from model-guided care were not tested.

Technology: LASSO; gradient boosting; random forest; support vector machine

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
