Macrophage-derived features predict thyroid cancer lymph-node status in retrospective cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Machine-learning combinations using SPP1-positive macrophage-derived molecular features classified lymph-node status in papillary thyroid cancer cohorts. The selected glmBoost/random-forest model had AUC 0.998 in training, 0.706 in held-out TCGA data and 0.657 in a combined GEO cohort. Those latter cohorts contributed to model prioritization, so the results are cross-cohort assessments rather than untouched external validation. Single-cell discovery involved three patients. Separate cell and small mouse experiments supported SPP1 as a therapeutic candidate; they do not validate clinical prediction or establish a human treatment benefit.

Technology: glmBoost and random forest; 111 combinations of 12 learned model strategies

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned model combinations use macrophage-associated expression features to classify nodal status; glmBoost/random forest was selected through cross-cohort comparison. The represented claim is this clinical cohort result, not an autonomous AI discovery of SPP1 biology.
Test and result
Patient-level 70/30 TCGA partition; features selected in training and 111 model combinations compared. Selected model AUCs were 0.998/0.706/0.657 across training, held-out TCGA and Meta-PTC. Meta-PTC includes 21 pN0 and 50 pN1 tumors; model prioritization used held-out/meta-cohort performance.
Limitation / still unknown
The three-patient single-cell discovery cohort and model-selection use of evaluation cohorts limit generalization. Prospective clinical benefit is unestablished. Separate SPP1-inhibitor cell and mouse experiments are ancillary preclinical evidence, not validation of a frozen clinical predictor.

12 Nov 2026Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine; Suzhou Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital

Exposure-oriented machine learning prioritizes nobiletin as a colorectal cancer candidate

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Four machine-learning models gave a composite score to 47 exposure-related parent compounds profiled by serum UPLC-QTOF-MS/MS, and nobiletin ranked first (score 0.77). Nobiletin inhibited CT26 proliferation (24-h IC50 70.8 uM) and suppressed tumour growth in syngeneic CT26 mice without body-weight loss. Abstract only; model names, ablation and mouse numbers were not read, and only the top-ranked compound was tested.

Technology: Four machine-learning models producing a composite prioritization score

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine learning ranked candidate compounds.
Test and result
In vitro proliferation and EdU assays and a syngeneic CT26 mouse tumour model.
Limitation / still unknown
Model names, mouse numbers; activity of lower-ranked compounds; full text not reviewed.
