19 Nov 2026Stanford University

Calibrated AI detectors estimate rising AI-assisted drafting in four state courts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Stanford researchers applied the learned EditLens detector with ScoreMix calibration to 127,518 complaints from four U.S. county court systems. Their selected settings estimated rising AI-like drafting, reaching about 38% in Santa Clara and 36% in San Francisco in 2026 for non-debt-collection complaints. Calibration used documented hallucination filings and assumed their detector-score distribution represented other AI-assisted writing. Detector settings, imperfect controls, case mix and selection of conspicuous AI mistakes limit the estimates. These are population estimates, not proof that any particular filing used AI, fabricated citations or affected a judicial outcome. November 19 is the institutional announcement date; the working paper posting day is unestablished.

Technology: EditLens (Llama-3.2-3B); ScoreMix calibration

2 Nov 2026Vesuvius Challenge

Writing seen in PHerc. 306B for the first time in 2000 years

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Vesuvius Challenge awarded a $50K First Letters Prize for scroll PHerc. 306B, where the winning submission reached the 10-character target and the papyrology team agreed on a reading, tentatively a Greek phrase with parallels in Aristotle and Philodemus. Ink was detected by multiplying two models (TRACE v3B and a fine-tuned A-hn). Letters remain faint, the readable area is small, and the work relied on hundreds of hours of manual labeling.

Technology: ML ink-detection models (TRACE v3B, A-hn) with VC3D GrowPatch

Editorial relevance 53 humanities Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine-learning ink-detection models (TRACE v3B and a fine-tuned A-hn) produced the ink maps; the readings and prize review were done by humans.
Test and result
The reading was checked by the papyrology team, which agreed on the letters with the winning submission, and was compared against 25 community submissions and a 10-character target; the page describes no independent replication.
Limitation / still unknown
The reading is partial and uncertain, letters remain faint, training on machine-generated labels could compound errors (not assessed), and the Grand Prize is not yet awarded.

27 Apr 2026University of New Mexico; New Mexico Consortium; Utah State University; Louisiana State University

Simulated training data locates archaeological tar-kiln-like sites in Louisiana, field check finds WWII features

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Machine learning models trained on simulated landscape data flagged 9 of 12 known targets (model 1) and all 12 (model 2, with 686 false positives among 709 remaining predictions) in Kisatchie National Forest, Louisiana; a model trained on real South Carolina tar kilns found 11 of 12. Field visits to 11 targets and augering of 2 found none of the expected tar-kiln features, and the authors concluded the structures more likely relate to a WWII military training site. Evidence caveat: only 12 known targets, and field validation is qualitative.

Technology: Machine learning classifiers trained on simulated archaeological sites (with a real tar-kiln-trained comparison model)

Editorial relevance 67 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Mask R-CNN models trained on simulated lidar-embedded objects produced predictions, a rule-based script removed likely natural mounds, and human archaeologists reviewed and chose field targets.
Test and result
Models trained on simulated objects flagged known tar-kiln-like targets, and 11 targets were visited in the field with 2 augered; no expected tar-kiln features were found, and the authors concluded the features are most likely associated with military training exercises.
Limitation / still unknown
Access to all targets was limited by dense understory, and further historical research is needed to confirm the function of the objects.

23 Jul 2025Google DeepMind

Aeneas: restoration, dating, and provenance of Latin inscriptions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The model restores texts with 73% top-20 accuracy for gaps up to ten characters (58% when length is unknown), attributes provenance with 72% accuracy across 62 Roman provinces, and dates inscriptions within 13 years; it was trained on over 176,000 inscriptions. In a study with 23 historians on 60 inscriptions, results were best when parallels and predictions were used together. Published in Nature.

Technology: Aeneas

Editorial relevance 60 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Aeneas restored text, attributed provinces, estimated dates and retrieved parallels; historians evaluated outputs and combined them with their own knowledge.
Test and result
Corpus of 176,861 Latin inscriptions. Aeneas: 72% geographical accuracy over 62 provinces; dating within 13 years on average; restoration character error rate 40.5% with known length. 23 historians on 60 inscriptions, solo versus with Aeneas parallels and predictions.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper notes a small dataset, survival bias, only about 5% of inscriptions with images, and weaker results where data are scarce. Training data include earlier editors' restorations. Other languages are suggested, not tested.

17 Jul 2025University of Bologna

AI-guided CORONA imagery search finds four archaeologically verified tells in Abu Ghraib

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fine-tuned deep-learning model trained with 1960s CORONA satellite images flagged eight previously unrecognized tell candidates in Iraq's Abu Ghraib district. Archaeologists investigated all eight; four contained surface ceramic sherds confirming archaeological remains and enabling dating. The AI suggestions led the field team to inspect locations that routine surveys would not have visited.

Technology: MANet deep convolutional segmentation network with attention, transfer learning and two-stage fine-tuning

Editorial relevance 53 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Researchers retrained the existing neural model using CORONA historical imagery and generated probability heatmaps that archaeologists used to select previously unrecognized locations for field reconnaissance. Eight AI-suggested sites were investigated; four were confirmed through collected and studied ceramic sherds. The authors state the four locations would not have been investigated in routine field surveys without the model's suggestions.
Test and result
A fine-tuned deep-learning model trained with 1960s CORONA satellite images flagged eight previously unrecognized tell candidates in Iraq's Abu Ghraib district.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an arXiv preprint. The results are limited to mounded tell sites in Abu Ghraib and depend on a small training set (208 images) and archaeologist-selected field follow-up. Four of eight AI-suggested sites were confirmed; the other four should not be described as archaeological finds. The source reports that the four confirmed sites were destroyed or no longer visible on the ground, with sherds establishing their presence and dates.

9 Mar 2022Google DeepMind

Ithaca: restoring and dating ancient Greek inscriptions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural network reconstructs missing text in damaged Greek inscriptions, identifies their provenance and dates them: 62% accuracy in restoration, 71% in location, and dating within 30 years of reference ranges. With Ithaca, historians improved from 25% to 72% correct restorations. It is intended to assist historians.

Technology: Ithaca (deep neural network)

Editorial relevance 67 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Neural network restored missing text, identified provenance and dated Greek inscriptions.
Test and result
Restoration (62%), location (71%) and dating were checked on held-out dated inscriptions, and a historian study rose from 25% to 72% correct restorations with the tool.
Limitation / still unknown
Accuracy is far from perfect, dating is a range within about 30 years, and the model was trained only on Greek inscriptions.
