Machine-learning feature selection identifies student-performance factors in Nepal

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A study analyzed 1,021 survey responses from engineering students reflecting on their first year at colleges affiliated with Tribhuvan University in Nepal. A feature-selection vote including Random Forest and AdaBoost selected 21 candidate academic, psychometric and demographic factors, triangulated with 11 focus-group themes and expert review. The reported set included adaptation, motivation, residence and travel-time factors alongside academic measures. These context-dependent selections do not establish causal effects, externally validated predictive performance or transfer beyond the sampled institution system.

20 Nov 2026City University Malaysia

Vocabulary app with Qwen tutoring shows a short-term score advantage in eight classes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A twelve-week teaching study randomized four matched pairs of university tutorial classes to a vocabulary application or non-adaptive word lists. The application bundled Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct examples, wav2vec2.0 pronunciation feedback and rule-based review scheduling. Among 92 completers from 100 allocated students, class-adjusted posttest scores favored the application by 6.6 points on a 100-point test (95% CI 1.3–11.9). This is a prospective comparison of the complete instructional package, not an isolated effect of AI. Eight classes, repeated test items, complete-case analysis and no delayed-retention measure limit generalization.

Technology: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; wav2vec 2.0 speech model; VocabPilot EDU 2.1.4

19 Nov 2026Stanford University

Calibrated AI detectors estimate rising AI-assisted drafting in four state courts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Stanford researchers applied the learned EditLens detector with ScoreMix calibration to 127,518 complaints from four U.S. county court systems. Their selected settings estimated rising AI-like drafting, reaching about 38% in Santa Clara and 36% in San Francisco in 2026 for non-debt-collection complaints. Calibration used documented hallucination filings and assumed their detector-score distribution represented other AI-assisted writing. Detector settings, imperfect controls, case mix and selection of conspicuous AI mistakes limit the estimates. These are population estimates, not proof that any particular filing used AI, fabricated citations or affected a judicial outcome. November 19 is the institutional announcement date; the working paper posting day is unestablished.

Technology: EditLens (Llama-3.2-3B); ScoreMix calibration
