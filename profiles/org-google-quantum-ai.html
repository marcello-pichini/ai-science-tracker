A quantum computer that learns from its own errors

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A reinforcement-learning agent tuned over 1,000 control parameters on the Willow chip: logical stability was 3.5 times higher under injected drift, with a further 20% reduction in logical error after expert calibration, and error below one per thousand cycles in the surface code. Drift was injected artificially, and scalability was shown only in simulation; published in Nature.

Technology: Reinforcement learning for quantum control

Editorial relevance 73 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An RL agent used error-detection events as a learning signal and adjusted frequencies, amplitudes and phases during the computation without stopping it for recalibration.
Test and result
On the Willow chip, drift was injected artificially into control parameters; RL steering raised logical stability 3.5-fold, and a further 20% error reduction followed expert calibration; scalability was tested only in simulation.
Limitation / still unknown
The drift was artificial rather than natural; scalability evidence is simulation-only; environmental decoherence cannot be fully prevented; fuller results need tighter integration.
