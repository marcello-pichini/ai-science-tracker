24 Nov 2026Sorbonne Université; CNRS; ESPCI Paris; Institut Polytechnique de Paris; Sapienza University of Rome

Generative models guide ribozymes that retain activity at high temperature

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A November 24, 2026 preprint reports laboratory-tested catalytic RNA designs that remain active at 60°C. A learned evolutionary sequence model combined with an independent RNA thermodynamic score generated variants that preserved function more often than either score alone. Across 2,500 designed variants from ten starting sequences, the jointly favored group had a 72.5% high-temperature activity hit rate, compared with 7.8% for the evolutionary-score group and 0.4% for the physical-score group. The result is limited to one ribozyme family and the study’s assay.

Technology: Edge-activation direct coupling analysis (DCA); learned Potts sequence model combined with RNA thermodynamics

Editorial relevance 60 biology PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An edge-activation DCA model learned family-specific sequence constraints from an alignment of 817 natural sequences. Combining its generative prior with a separately derived thermodynamic score guided designs whose catalytic activity was then measured.
Test and result
Laboratory activity measurements compared 1,500 jointly favored designs with 500 evolutionary-score and 500 physical-score designs, pooled across ten starting sequences. High-temperature activity hit rates were 72.5%, 7.8% and 0.4%, respectively, under the study’s reference-normalized threshold.
