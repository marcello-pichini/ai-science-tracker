19 May 2026Google Research

Empirical Research Assistance writes expert-level scientific code across six domains

Google's Empirical Research Assistance (ERA), a Gemini-based tree-search system, reaches expert-level performance on six benchmarks spanning genomics, public health, satellite imagery, neuroscience, time-series forecasting and mathematics. Its state-level U.S. hospital admission forecasts for flu, COVID-19 and RSV rank at or near the top of CDC leaderboards. Results are computational and checked against public benchmarks and leaderboards, with no wet-lab validation; exact benchmark scores were not visible in the sources read.

Technology: Empirical Research Assistance (ERA), Gemini-based tree search

4 Dec 2025University of Oxford; UK AI Security Institute; London School of Economics; Stanford University; MIT

The levers of political persuasion with conversational AI

In three large experiments with 76,977 participants, 19 LLMs argued 707 British political positions in dialogue. Persuasiveness was driven mainly by information density, while tailoring to user traits added little, and post-training for persuasion raised effectiveness by up to 50%. The most persuasive models also produced the most inaccurate claims. Caveat: the persuasion effects are measured in survey experiments, and accuracy was tested on a subset of claims.

Technology: 19 open and closed LLMs (including GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, Grok-3)

5 Nov 2025Edison Scientific

Kosmos: an AI scientist for autonomous discovery

Each run reads about 1,500 papers and executes 42,000 lines of analysis code; outside scientists judged 79.4% of its conclusions accurate. Of seven discoveries, three reproduce results already obtained by humans and four are new. Limitations include spurious correlations, a cost of about $200 per run, and a six-month work gain estimated by beta users.

Technology: Kosmos (agents with a structured world model)

2 Jul 2025Helmholtz Munich; Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics; University of Tübingen

Centaur: a foundation model that predicts human behaviour across psychology experiments

Fine-tuning Llama 3.1 70B on Psych-101, a dataset of about 10 million human choices from 160 psychology experiments, produced a model that predicts and simulates human behaviour in experiments expressed in natural language, including tasks it had not seen. The authors report that its internal representations align better with human neural activity after fine-tuning. Caveat: a 2025 critique argues that its language comprehension is limited and that it fails to follow altered instructions.

Technology: Centaur (Llama 3.1 70B fine-tuned on Psych-101)

15 Nov 2024Stanford University; Google DeepMind; Northwestern University; University of Washington

Generative agents built from interview transcripts simulate 1,000 people

LLM agents built from participants' full interview transcripts reproduced their General Social Survey answers at about 85% of the rate at which participants reproduced their own answers two weeks later, a normalized score. They also matched personality and economic-game results (normalized correlations of about 0.80 and 0.66) and replicated four of five behavioural experiments. Caveat: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed, and the results are normalized rather than raw accuracy.

Technology: Generative agent architecture with LLM and full interview transcripts

13 Sep 2024MIT Sloan School of Management; Cornell University; American University

Conversations with an AI chatbot durably reduce conspiracy beliefs

Across experiments with more than 2,000 participants, three dialogues with GPT-4 reduced belief in the targeted conspiracy theory by about 20%, and the effect persisted for two months and spilled over to unrelated conspiracies. A professional fact-checker rated 99.2% of 128 sampled chatbot claims as true. Caveats: lab setting, the chatbot did not reduce belief in true conspiracies, and most participants still held the hoax belief.

Technology: GPT-4 (DebunkBot dialogue system)
