11 Jun 2026Princeton University

Machine-learning screening identifies bacterial-selective membrane-active compounds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The June 2026 bioRxiv version describes an initial machine-learning-guided screen of ZINC20 compounds balancing predicted antibacterial activity, mammalian-cell toxicity, and mechanism-of-action novelty. Among 61 purchased candidates, six showed partial growth inhibition; 49 were non-toxic to HepG2 cells at 50 μM. Flow cytometry indicated membrane disruption for four of the six initial active hits and membrane depolarization for two. A September version reports additional screening and revised models; its expanded results are not attributed to the June version here. The study does not report a novel protein target or clinical antibiotic.

Technology: Graph-convolutional neural networks for antibacterial activity/toxicity with XGBoost mechanism-of-action classification and novelty filtering

A machine-learning-assisted TESS search confirms a previously unknown hot Jupiter

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The T16 pipeline processed 83.7 million TESS Cycle 1 full-frame-image light curves and reported 10,091 new transiting-planet candidates, alongside previously known candidates. Radial-velocity follow-up confirmed TIC 183374187 b as a newly identified hot Jupiter, demonstrating that the semi-automated search yielded at least one bona fide new planet. Most entries in the catalog remain candidates rather than confirmed planets.

14 Jan 2026Princeton University; University of Alberta; University of British Columbia; University of Toronto; Baylor College of Medicine; collaborating institutions

DeepMet anticipates and helps identify dozens of previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: DeepMet, an LSTM chemical language model trained on known human metabolites, generated a ranked space of metabolite-like structures and was integrated with mass spectrometry data. Researchers report identifying several dozen previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites across standards, human biofluids, mouse tissues and repository data. Some standard-supported assignments still have isomer ambiguity; the study also found known metabolites missing or misannotated in HMDB.

Technology: DeepMet chemical language model (three-layer LSTM over SMILES), integrated with MS/MS-spectrum prediction and meta-learning

Editorial relevance 60 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned metabolite-like chemical structures and prioritized structures likely to exist; combined with accurate mass/MS/MS and reference standards to support identification of previously unrecognized metabolites.
Test and result
Researchers report identifying several dozen previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites across standards, human biofluids, mouse tissues and repository data.
Limitation / still unknown
A subset of assigned structures retain regioisomer/stereoisomer ambiguity; human oversight helped prioritize structures for synthesis. The model explores chemical space similar to its training set and may miss divergent biosynthetic chemistry. The paper distinguishes novel metabolites from known compounds missing in HMDB.

24 May 2025Princeton University; and NOAA Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory

Samudra: an AI emulator of the global ocean for climate simulations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Samudra, a UNet-style emulator trained on the ocean component of a state-of-the-art climate model (OM4 within CM4), reproduces sea surface height, velocities, temperature and salinity across the full ocean depth. It runs about 150 times faster than the original ocean model and remains stable for centuries. The authors note it struggles to reproduce the correct magnitude of forcing trends while staying stable, so it needs further work.

Technology: Samudra UNet-style ocean emulator

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
Samudra was compared with the original climate model's ocean component: no drift relative to the truth, reproduction of depth structure and interannual variability, stability for centuries, and about 150 times faster.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors state the emulator struggles to reproduce the correct magnitude of forcing trends while staying stable, and this needs further work.

6 Dec 2020Princeton University; DeepMind; New York University; Flatiron Institute; Carnegie Mellon University

AI-derived symbolic formula improves dark-matter halo overdensity estimates in a cosmological simulation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A graph neural network trained on a detailed N-body cosmological dark-matter simulation was distilled by symbolic regression into an analytic estimator for a halo’s overdensity from its mass and the masses, positions and velocities of nearby halos. On the analyzed simulation snapshot, the new expression reduced mean absolute error to 0.0882, compared with 0.121 for the authors’ existing hand-designed estimator. The formula’s mass-weighted neighborhood term decreases with separation and includes dependence on the central halo’s mass.

Technology: Sparse-representation Graph Neural Network followed by Eureqa genetic-programming symbolic regression

Editorial relevance 40 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The team trained a graph neural network to predict dark-matter halo overdensity using a cosmological N-body simulation. L1 regularization encouraged a compact learned message representation; Eureqa symbolic regression then fitted explicit equations to the learned graph-network components and replaced them with a closed-form model. This process yielded a previously unreported analytic halo-environment estimator and enabled comparison against the existing hand-designed formula.
Test and result
A graph neural network trained on a detailed N-body cosmological dark-matter simulation was distilled by symbolic regression into an analytic estimator for a halo’s overdensity from its mass and the masses, positions and velocities of nearby halos.
Limitation / still unknown
The formula was derived and evaluated on a single final-time-step dataset from an N-body simulation (215,854 halos), not validated against observations or independent cosmological simulations. The authors note that some terms’ physical interpretation was unclear and reserved detailed interpretation for future astrophysics work. It is a new useful estimator for the studied simulated halo population, not a confirmed universal law of dark-matter structure.
