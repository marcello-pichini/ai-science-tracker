1 May 2026Princeton University and collaborating institutions

A machine-learning-assisted TESS search confirms a previously unknown hot Jupiter

The T16 pipeline processed 83.7 million TESS Cycle 1 full-frame-image light curves and reported 10,091 new transiting-planet candidates, alongside previously known candidates. Radial-velocity follow-up confirmed TIC 183374187 b as a newly identified hot Jupiter, demonstrating that the semi-automated search yielded at least one bona fide new planet. Most entries in the catalog remain candidates rather than confirmed planets.

Technology: T16 semi-automated transit search with random-forest machine-learning vetting

14 Jan 2026Princeton University; University of Alberta; University of British Columbia; University of Toronto; Baylor College of Medicine; collaborating institutions

DeepMet anticipates and helps identify dozens of previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites

DeepMet, an LSTM chemical language model trained on known human metabolites, generated a ranked space of metabolite-like structures and was integrated with mass spectrometry data. Researchers report identifying several dozen previously unrecognized mammalian metabolites across standards, human biofluids, mouse tissues and repository data. Some standard-supported assignments still have isomer ambiguity; the study also found known metabolites missing or misannotated in HMDB.

Technology: DeepMet chemical language model (three-layer LSTM over SMILES), integrated with MS/MS-spectrum prediction and meta-learning

24 May 2025Princeton University and NOAA Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory

Samudra: an AI emulator of the global ocean for climate simulations

Samudra, a UNet-style emulator trained on the ocean component of a state-of-the-art climate model (OM4 within CM4), reproduces sea surface height, velocities, temperature and salinity across the full ocean depth. It runs about 150 times faster than the original ocean model and remains stable for centuries. The authors note it struggles to reproduce the correct magnitude of forcing trends while staying stable, so it needs further work.

Technology: Samudra UNet-style ocean emulator

9 Apr 2025Princeton University, Allen Institute, and Baylor College of Medicine

MICrONS: a map of one cubic millimeter of mouse visual cortex

A map with over half a billion connections and about 75,000 reconstructed neurons from roughly 28,000 sections, produced over nine years by more than 150 researchers. AI traced every neuron outline, and the result requires human checking, much of which is already done. This is about one-thousandth of a mouse connectome, and cell counts vary within the text.

Technology: AI neuron segmentation

6 Dec 2020Princeton University; DeepMind; New York University; Flatiron Institute; Carnegie Mellon University

AI-derived symbolic formula improves dark-matter halo overdensity estimates in a cosmological simulation

A graph neural network trained on a detailed N-body cosmological dark-matter simulation was distilled by symbolic regression into an analytic estimator for a halo’s overdensity from its mass and the masses, positions and velocities of nearby halos. On the analyzed simulation snapshot, the new expression reduced mean absolute error to 0.0882, compared with 0.121 for the authors’ existing hand-designed estimator. The formula’s mass-weighted neighborhood term decreases with separation and includes dependence on the central halo’s mass.

Technology: Sparse-representation Graph Neural Network followed by Eureqa genetic-programming symbolic regression

  • AI role: The team trained a graph neural network to predict dark-matter halo overdensity using a cosmological N-body simulation. This process yielded a previously unreported analytic halo-environment estimator and enabled comparison against the existing hand-designed formula.
