27 Oct 2026KTH Royal Institute of Technology; Stanford University

Rolling ridge forecasts failed to beat zero in an ETF return backtest

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a 2005–2025 ETF study, a rolling ridge model using past return features produced negative mean out-of-sample R² at every tested regularization level against a zero-return forecast. Scores approached zero as stronger regularization shrank the predictions.

Technology: Ridge regression with rolling retraining

Editorial relevance 40 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The fitted regression generated return forecasts from past observations, enabling a retrospective comparison of predictive signal against the zero baseline.
Test and result
The model was repeatedly fitted on trailing observations and scored on the following period. The paper compared multiple regularization levels with a zero forecast.
Limitation / still unknown
The ETFs were selected from ones still trading at study time, producing survivorship bias. The result concerns the chosen predictors, horizons and historical universe.

27 Oct 2026Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur; Indian Institute of Technology Delhi; Tata Consultancy Services Research

AlphaEarth crop embeddings transferred less well between the United States and India

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a satellite crop-classification study, a classifier trained on US data scored 0.326 macro F1 in Northern India using AlphaEarth embeddings, versus 0.493 using seasonal harmonic features. The ranking also favored harmonic features in the reverse direction, even though embeddings performed better in held-out US county comparisons.

Technology: AlphaEarth Foundations geospatial embeddings; supervised crop classifiers

Editorial relevance 40 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned AlphaEarth embeddings encoded satellite imagery for the classifiers. Comparing those embeddings with engineered seasonal features quantified the regional transfer deficit.
Test and result
The study compared satellite representations on crops represented in US field-scale data from 2017–2023 and Northern India data from 2021, with spatial validation and cross-region transfer.
Limitation / still unknown
The geographies differ in seasons, sample selection and field structure. The results do not establish transferability to other regions or to all crops.

26 Oct 2026Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; University of Chinese Academy of Sciences

Tactile guidance improved immediate racket-skill ratings in a randomized study

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: After ten minutes of practice, 12 learners given contact and motion guidance improved by 0.72 levels on a four-level expert rating scale. Twelve learners who received only proficiency-level feedback gained 0.05 levels. The difference was 0.67 levels (95% confidence interval 0.39 to 0.98).

Technology: GNN–LSTM pose estimator; graph-informed drive–grip encoder and decoder

Editorial relevance 67 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A learned pose estimator reconstructed contact geometry from tactile input. Learned control representations and regional analysis informed feedback on how each learner’s contact and timing differed from an expert reference.
Test and result
A separate cohort of 24 was randomly assigned to equal-duration practice. Three experts rated shuffled before-and-after clips without labels identifying the group or assessment time.
Limitation / still unknown
This small study evaluated immediate changes on five racket skills. Durability and transfer to match play were unestablished.

26 Oct 2026University of Waterloo; Cameco

A mine-cavity study found that geometric prediction accuracy varied by ground history

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: On held-out cavity scans from one mine, a 3D U-Net achieved a mean overlap score of 0.601 versus 0.491 for the planning baseline. The ranking reversed in previously unexcavated ground: 0.525 for the learned model versus 0.592 for the baseline.

Technology: Ensemble of fitted three-dimensional U-Nets

Editorial relevance 40 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The U-Net ensemble used recorded field inputs to estimate cavity geometry. Its predictions were compared with recorded cavity scans.
Test and result
The study randomly held out historical cavities and compared volume overlap against surveyed boundaries and the site’s geometric baseline.
Limitation / still unknown
The data came from one mine and roughly 90% of training-domain examples involved neighboring filled excavations. The subgroup scores do not establish prospective or cross-site reliability.

23 Oct 2026Queen’s University Belfast; Sapphire Capital Partners

A paired venture screening study recorded lower analyst time for AI-assisted reports

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a paired study of 20 applications at one investment firm, mean analyst time was 44.7 minutes for manual screening and 18.9 minutes for screening with GPT-6 Luna-assisted reports. Assisted time included verifying sources and making corrections. Seventy percent of reports needed at least one correction.

Technology: GPT-6 Luna; document interpretation and evidence-assisted scoring

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
DealsFlow used GPT-6 Luna to extract and score supporting information from application documents and retrieved sources. Analysts then verified and corrected the resulting reports.
Test and result
Each application was independently processed by both workflows, with a different analyst in each and roles counterbalanced. The analysts also adjudicated the factual support of 240 cited claims.
Limitation / still unknown
Two analysts affiliated with the participating fund completed the unblinded comparison. Each application was assessed by a different analyst in each workflow, so counterbalancing leaves analyst-by-workflow effects unresolved. The assisted time excludes unattended machine time. Decision quality and returns were outside this comparison.

22 Oct 2026Texas A&M University; Brigham Young University; University of Brasília

The lightest and heaviest jiu-jitsu classes showed distinct match progression

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fitted model of 78 men’s no-gi Brazilian jiu-jitsu matches found that the 66 kg and over-99 kg groups differed from the middle weight classes in how recorded match states led to eventual outcomes. The matches came from one 2019 championship.

Technology: Hierarchical absorbing Markov model with Bayesian random-partition clustering

Editorial relevance 40 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fitted hierarchical absorbing Markov model and an Ewens–Pitman attraction random-partition model infer transition patterns by weight class from recorded matches.
Test and result
The model pooled recorded transitions across classes and compared posterior probabilities of eventual match outcomes separately by weight class. The analysis used 244 sequences after excluding the absolute division from the original 93-match dataset.
Limitation / still unknown
The findings concern this tournament and the model’s grouping and state definitions. They do not establish a causal effect of body weight or generalize to other competitions.

27 Jan 2026Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin; and University of Edinburgh

DinoTracker: AI classifies dinosaur footprints

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An unsupervised model trained on nearly 2,000 fossil footprints and millions of simulated variations identifies eight features of variation and agrees with experts about 90% of the time. Some tracks over 200 million years old resemble bird feet, which could suggest bird origins tens of millions of years earlier. The interpretation is open: they could be early dinosaurs whose feet happened to look similar.

Technology: DinoTracker (unsupervised model)

Editorial relevance 60 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An unsupervised disentangled variational autoencoder found eight shape-variation features without prior labels, and predicted which dinosaur made each track.
Test and result
An unsupervised variational autoencoder was trained on 1,974 fossil footprints; after labeling, its groupings agreed with published expert identifications in 80-93% of cases (the press release says about 90%).
Limitation / still unknown
The interpretation is open: the tracks could be early dinosaurs whose feet resembled birds' feet; the study states no limitations section.
