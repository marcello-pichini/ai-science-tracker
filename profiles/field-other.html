Artificial neural network analysis links immersion with self-efficacy in VR upper-limb training

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Artificial neural networks analyzed self-reports from 280 healthy adults who used a VR upper-limb training system. With ten-fold cross-validation, the ANN analysis ranked immersion above learning motivation as a predictor of task-specific self-efficacy, while linear structural-equation analysis ranked learning motivation higher. This preprint reports associations in young adults from one city, without causal evidence or rehabilitation outcomes.

Technology: Artificial neural networks with 10-fold cross-validation

Test and result
ANN models used immersion, learning motivation, interactive experience, and perceived incentives to predict affective trust and task-specific self-efficacy; the study reports 10-fold cross-validation and RMSE. The underlying data are self-reports from 280 users.
Limitation / still unknown

20 Nov 2026University of Electronic Science and Technology of China; City University of Hong Kong; The University of Tokyo

Learned wing sensing supports damage detection and lift recovery in a flapping robot

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A prototype flapping-wing robot used integrated wing sensors with a learned Kriging surrogate to reconstruct wing motion and a linear SVM to classify damaged states. The SVM achieved 94.6% accuracy on 168 held-out samples, and a separate damaged-wing bench demonstration increased flapping frequency within 0.1 seconds to restore average lift near the intact baseline. These are device-specific physical sensing and control results, with calibration and comparison against binocular-vision measurements. They do not establish autonomous free-flight performance, generalization across robot designs or sustained field operation.

Technology: Physics-informed Kriging surrogate model and linear support vector machine (SVM)

Editorial relevance 53 other Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The physics-informed Kriging surrogate maps measured voltage/current amplitudes and flapping frequency to wing-motion angles; a linear SVM classifies faults from the derived signal features. These learned operations support the experimentally demonstrated intrinsic motion sensing, fault classification and lift-compensation control.
Test and result
The Kriging model was calibrated using two signal cycles and evaluated on other cycles at each frequency against binocular-vision measurements; reported mean unit-pitching-angle error was below 10% and maximum flapping-angle error 1.1%. For fault diagnosis, 504 samples trained a linear SVM and 168 test samples yielded 94.6% accuracy across normal and three failure states. The authors also report a damaged-wing feedback demonstration with frequency rising from 12.9 to 19.3 Hz in 0.1 seconds and average lift restored near the intact baseline.
Limitation / still unknown
Generalization to other wing structures, sensor builds, operating conditions, and untethered flight; evidence for field deployment or sustained operational use.
