Human-in-the-loop AI copilot improves topology-optimized structure design

Source check: Needs source check. Not independent scientific confirmation.

27 Jan 2026Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin; and University of Edinburgh

DinoTracker: AI classifies dinosaur footprints

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An unsupervised model trained on nearly 2,000 fossil footprints and millions of simulated variations identifies eight features of variation and agrees with experts about 90% of the time. Some tracks over 200 million years old resemble bird feet, which could suggest bird origins tens of millions of years earlier. The interpretation is open: they could be early dinosaurs whose feet happened to look similar.

Technology: DinoTracker (unsupervised model)

Editorial relevance 60 other Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An unsupervised disentangled variational autoencoder found eight shape-variation features without prior labels, and predicted which dinosaur made each track.
Test and result
An unsupervised variational autoencoder was trained on 1,974 fossil footprints; after labeling, its groupings agreed with published expert identifications in 80-93% of cases (the press release says about 90%).
Limitation / still unknown
The interpretation is open: the tracks could be early dinosaurs whose feet resembled birds' feet; the study states no limitations section.
