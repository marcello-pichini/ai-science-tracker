30 Dec 2024ATLAS Collaboration; CERN Large Hadron Collider

OmniFold enables an unbinned 24-dimensional Z+jets cross-section measurement by ATLAS

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 139 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, ATLAS simultaneously unfolded 24 correlated Z+jets kinematic observables with the machine-learning method OmniFold and released the particle-level result as an unbinned event dataset. The measured total fiducial cross section is 1,808 ± 42 fb; most illustrative differential bins have 3–5% total uncertainty, rising to about 15% in tails. The measurement is more precise than the compared predictions for many distributions, especially Sherpa. The event-level release also supports derived observables, including an infrared-and-collinear-safe τ₂₁ measurement in a specified fiducial region.

Technology: OmniFold iterative machine-learning unfolding

Editorial relevance 60 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
OmniFold used neural-network classifiers to iteratively reweight simulated events so the detector-level simulation matched collision data, then propagated the learned correction to particle-level events. That multivariate unfolding handled 24 correlated observables simultaneously and produced event weights with uncertainty variations, enabling a high-dimensional unbinned measurement and event-level construction of additional derived observables. The paper validates closure on pseudo-data and reports its uncertainty components.
Test and result
Using 139 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, ATLAS simultaneously unfolded 24 correlated Z+jets kinematic observables with the machine-learning method OmniFold and released the particle-level result as an unbinned event dataset.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a new high-dimensional precision measurement of known Z+jets production, not a new particle or beyond-Standard-Model discovery. The released measurement is unbinned in 24 measured variables; displayed plots may be binned for presentation. The authors report mostly 3–5% uncertainties in illustrative bins, up to about 15% in tails, with dominant unfolding-prior/hidden-variable uncertainties for some observables. Pseudo-data closure is generally good, with exceptions including a p-value of 0.038 for leading-jet transverse momentum; users are given phase-space and covariance-use guidance.
