5 Nov 2026University of Bern

Closed-loop machine learning maps multimetallic glucose-oxidation catalysts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Gaussian process regression surrogates trained on measured data guided which Au-Ir-Pt-Pd-Rh-Ru nanoparticle compositions to electrodeposit and test next. Highly active compositions lay near the Au-Pt edge, while the most poisoning-resistant were central with Rh and Pd up to 20 at%, revealing an activity versus poisoning-resistance trade-off. The loop's own experiments are the validation, without separate holdout, and only alkaline glucose oxidation was studied.

Technology: Gaussian process regression surrogates in closed-loop selection, with SHAP interpretation

23 Oct 2026University of Bern; Bern University Hospital; Diabetes Center Berne; Technical University of Denmark; University of Tübingen; University of Lucerne

Learned image groups reveal a low-sensitivity slice in cardiomegaly detection

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fitted subgroup model assigned chest X-rays in a 22,281-image CheXpert-Plus test set to image groups. Across discovered groups, cardiomegaly sensitivity ranged from 0.10 to 0.98. One group associated with higher BMI and male sex had sensitivity of 0.10 and specificity of 0.95. The group was defined by learned image features and classifier scores, and its demographic profile was assessed afterward.

Technology: DOMINO Gaussian mixture; CLIP; BiomedCLIP; ResNet-50

Editorial relevance 47 medicine PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A modified DOMINO Gaussian mixture learned image subgroups using pretrained CLIP or BiomedCLIP features and the predictions of a trained ResNet-50 cardiomegaly classifier. The mixture was fitted on the validation set and assigned memberships in the test set.
Test and result
The authors fitted subgroup memberships on 22,263 validation images and then assigned an existing separate test partition. Real-world subgroup analyses were distinct from synthetic-artifact checks.
Limitation / still unknown
The subgroup is defined by the fitted model, with demographic enrichment assessed afterward. The low sensitivity is imprecise (0.103 ± 0.097, reported 95% Wilson uncertainty). Methods describes an image split and does not establish patient-disjointness. The study does not establish causal effects of BMI or sex or externally reproduced clinical benefit.

24 Nov 2022University of Bern; University Hospital of Bern

Machine-learning model reduces misdiagnosis of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A prospective cohort of 1,393 patients with suspected HIT from 10 centers (119 confirmed by the HIPA reference assay) was used to train models on six predictors, with a 25% validation set. AUROC was 0.99 (95% CI 0.97-1.00) for all models, and false negatives fell by 50.0%, 66.7% and 64.3% versus the recommended algorithm for ELISA, PaGIA and CLIA. Validation was a hold-out within one cohort, so wider validation is still needed.

Technology: Support vector machine and gradient boosting machine on clinical and immunoassay data

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution

Test and result
Models were compared against the 4Ts score plus immunoassay in a 25% hold-out set of a prospective cohort, with HIPA as reference.
Limitation / still unknown
Performance in other settings is unknown. Abstract-based; full text not reviewed.

27 Aug 2022University of Bern; Lausanne University Hospital; University of Lausanne; University Hospital Basel; Hôpital du Valais

An EEG model retrospectively estimates outcomes across critical-illness etiologies

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A one-dimensional convolutional network analyzed EEGs from 358 adults with acute consciousness impairment. Patient-held-out cross-validation yielded AUCs of .721 for six-month survival and .703 for favorable functional outcome. Higher-confidence subsets had better performance at reduced coverage; this was retrospective modeling, not a randomized test of AI-guided care.

Technology: t-VGG GAP one-dimensional convolutional neural network

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The CNN learned EEG features directly to classify clinical outcomes and etiologies; Grad-CAM and visual review explored model behavior without proving a new causal mechanism.
Test and result
This post-hoc analysis reused the four-hospital CERTA EEG trial. A fixed previously published CNN architecture used overlapping epochs, while stratified five-fold splitting kept all epochs from a patient together; patient predictions averaged epoch outputs. Prognosis used survival at six months and the best Cerebral Performance Category measured at four weeks or six months. All 358 patients contributed prognostic evaluation; diagnostic etiology classification used 277 eligible patients and reached 54.5% accuracy.
Limitation / still unknown
Original trial randomization concerned EEG recording strategy, not this learned model. The best-of-two-timepoints functional endpoint is not a fixed six-month recovery measure. Model-output confidence selected progressively smaller subgroups and does not establish calibrated clinical certainty; higher-confidence performance must not be reported as whole-cohort accuracy. Earlier random-forest work used the same cohort, so this is not an independent replication of its findings. Treatment decisions and withdrawal of life support may affect observed prognosis; authors argue that using the first EEG reduced but did not remove that concern. There was no held-out new hospital, prospective model-guided treatment or demonstrated patient benefit. The represented date is the documented August 2022 online journal version, without an earliest-priority assertion.

5 Aug 2022University of Bern

Machine-learning-guided peptide design yielded non-hemolytic anticancer candidates

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A recurrent neural network generated peptide sequences and classified predicted activity and hemolysis; a genetic-algorithm route also generated analogues. After sequence and physicochemical filters, researchers synthesized and tested 33 peptides. The paper reports 11 active anticancer peptides in its cell assays, four non-hemolytic under its red-cell assay. These in-vitro results do not establish cancer-cell specificity, clinical safety or therapeutic efficacy. A related 2020 modeling paper is separate from this experimental study; no claim of absolute sequence novelty is made.

Technology: Generative recurrent neural network, genetic algorithm, and recurrent-neural-network activity and hemolysis classifiers

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
RNN and genetic-algorithm generation with RNN activity and hemolysis classifiers guided peptide selection for synthesis and testing.
Test and result
The abstract reports that 33 peptides were experimentally evaluated, 11 were active in anticancer cell assays, and four of the active peptides were non-hemolytic in the reported assays.
Limitation / still unknown
In-vitro activity and red-cell hemolysis do not establish cancer-cell specificity, clinical safety or treatment efficacy. Detailed potency measurements and absolute sequence novelty were not reassessed in this bounded review.
