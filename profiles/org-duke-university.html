10 Sep 2026Duke University; University of California, Irvine; independent research collaboration

AI-discovered proof resolves the Komlós vector-balancing conjecture

The paper proves that for every finite family of Euclidean-norm-at-most-one vectors, signs can be chosen so the signed sum has infinity norm below 3√(2π), independent of vector count and dimension. It derives a corresponding square-root-in-sets bound for Beck–Fiala discrepancy. The authors explicitly credit the Odin Automatic AI Research Agent with discovering the proof; a separate elementary proof was posted independently. The algorithmic construction of such signings is a separate follow-up result and is not claimed here.

Technology: Odin Automatic AI Research Agent

18 Jul 2026Duke University

Deep-learning splice prediction links a PARP2 variant to altered protein isoforms and influenza susceptibility

By combining single-cell splicing-QTL analysis during influenza A infection with SpliceAI/Pangolin annotation and statistical fine-mapping, researchers prioritized causal splice variants. They experimentally characterized rs2297616 in PARP2: its A allele creates an alternative 5′ splice donor, adding 39 nucleotides and producing a protein isoform 13 amino acids longer. CRISPR-edited A549 cells showed the predicted splice and protein changes; the A-allele edited cells also had higher influenza A infection than G-allele cells. The variant was associated with higher viral burden in a panel of 96 lymphoblastoid cell lines.

Technology: SpliceAI and Pangolin deep-learning splice-effect predictors, integrated with single-cell scHi-HOST sQTL mapping and SuSiE statistical fine-mapping

7 Mar 2026Duke University; Duke Microbiome Center

Machine learning prioritizes microbiome metabolites that stimulate interleukin-8 secretion

Researchers trained ML models on public drug-development data to prioritize biological properties of microbiome metabolites, then tested predictions prospectively. In vitro experiments identified previously unreported IL-8 secretion stimulation by spermine and spermidine, metabolites previously regarded as anti-inflammatory. The paper reports average accuracy of 92.86% in prospective experiments, while cautioning that sample sizes were small.

Technology: Machine-learning property prediction models implemented in KNIME and trained on public drug-development data
