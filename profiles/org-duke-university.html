10 Sep 2026Duke University; University of California, Irvine; independent research collaboration

AI-discovered proof resolves the Komlós vector-balancing conjecture

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The paper proves that for every finite family of Euclidean-norm-at-most-one vectors, signs can be chosen so the signed sum has infinity norm below 3√(2π), independent of vector count and dimension. It derives a corresponding square-root-in-sets bound for Beck–Fiala discrepancy. The authors explicitly credit the Odin Automatic AI Research Agent with discovering the proof; a separate elementary proof was posted independently. The algorithmic construction of such signings is a separate follow-up result and is not claimed here.

Technology: Odin Automatic AI Research Agent

18 Jul 2026Duke University

Deep-learning splice prediction links a PARP2 variant to altered protein isoforms and influenza susceptibility

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: By combining single-cell splicing-QTL analysis during influenza A infection with SpliceAI/Pangolin annotation and statistical fine-mapping, researchers prioritized causal splice variants. They experimentally characterized rs2297616 in PARP2: its A allele creates an alternative 5′ splice donor, adding 39 nucleotides and producing a protein isoform 13 amino acids longer. CRISPR-edited A549 cells showed the predicted splice and protein changes; the A-allele edited cells also had higher influenza A infection than G-allele cells. The variant was associated with higher viral burden in a panel of 96 lymphoblastoid cell lines.

Technology: SpliceAI and Pangolin deep-learning splice-effect predictors, integrated with single-cell scHi-HOST sQTL mapping and SuSiE statistical fine-mapping

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep-learning splice models prioritized variants predicted to alter donor/acceptor sites; statistical fine-mapping and sQTL association narrowed causal candidates. The AI-supported analysis nominated rs2297616; the authors then tested its splice and protein-isoform effects using reporter assays, CRISPR-edited A549 cells, sequencing and western blotting, and assessed influenza infection in edited cells.
Test and result
They experimentally characterized rs2297616 in PARP2: its A allele creates an alternative 5′ splice donor, adding 39 nucleotides and producing a protein isoform 13 amino acids longer.
Limitation / still unknown
The 96-donor LCL association is observational and modest (the paper reports p=0.029 for the genotype association). Infection experiments used edited A549 lung epithelial cells, not patients or an in-vivo infection model. The same locus had been linked to alternative splicing previously, but the paper provides new evidence connecting genotype to PARP2 protein isoform expression and to influenza susceptibility. Broader viral-susceptibility implications remain unproven.

7 Mar 2026Duke University; Duke Microbiome Center

Machine learning prioritizes microbiome metabolites that stimulate interleukin-8 secretion

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers trained ML models on public drug-development data to prioritize biological properties of microbiome metabolites, then tested predictions prospectively. In vitro experiments identified previously unreported IL-8 secretion stimulation by spermine and spermidine, metabolites previously regarded as anti-inflammatory. The paper reports average accuracy of 92.86% in prospective experiments, while cautioning that sample sizes were small.

Technology: Machine-learning property prediction models implemented in KNIME and trained on public drug-development data

Editorial relevance 53 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Predicted and triaged microbiome metabolites for target assays, including IL-8 activation; prospective laboratory assays then established the unexpected cytokine-stimulatory activity of spermine and spermidine.
Test and result
Researchers trained ML models on public drug-development data to prioritize biological properties of microbiome metabolites, then tested predictions prospectively. The selected metabolites were tested in new in-house assays; this is direct experimental testing within the discovery study.
Limitation / still unknown
The unexpected activity is demonstrated in vitro and does not establish in-vivo host effects. The article explicitly cautions that prospective validation sample sizes were small; reported model metrics vary by task.
