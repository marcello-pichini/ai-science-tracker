11 Oct 2024Gladstone-UCSF Institute of Genomic Immunology; University of California, San Francisco; Johns Hopkins University; Stanford University; University of California, Berkeley

CRISPR perturbation and Bayesian network inference map immune-disease regulatory cascades in primary CD4+ T cells

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The study used CRISPR knockouts of 84 genes in primary human CD4+ T cells and a new linear latent causal Bayes (LLCB) network-inference method to estimate 211 regulatory connections. Integrating the inferred network with immune GWAS analyses identified an interconnected module in which KMT2A was inferred upstream of JAK-STAT and IL-2 signaling regulators including IRF4, STAT5A/B and IL2RA, linking an epigenetic regulator to immune-disease-associated pathways.

Technology: Linear latent causal Bayes (LLCB), a Bayesian extension of linear latent causal network inference, applied to CRISPR knockout and RNA-sequencing data

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
LLCB inferred direct and indirect regulatory relationships from perturbation-associated expression effects across 84 targeted genes. The model-derived network and gene-program analysis, combined with immune GWAS statistics, supported the specific KMT2A-to-JAK-STAT/IL-2 regulatory finding. CRISPR perturbation supplied the experimental perturbation data; the network algorithm converted those measurements into the reported regulatory map.
Test and result
The study used CRISPR knockouts of 84 genes in primary human CD4+ T cells and a new linear latent causal Bayes (LLCB) network-inference method to estimate 211 regulatory connections.
Limitation / still unknown
The study perturbed 84 selected genes in primary CD4+ T cells from three donors, so this is not a complete T-cell regulatory map. LLCB “direct effects” are statistical effects adjusted for other perturbed genes and are not evidence of physical binding; effects from unperturbed genes can be attributed to modeled edges. The GWAS integration identifies pathway links, not proof that the inferred cascade causes clinical disease. A prior bioRxiv version (10.1101/2023.09.17.557749) is the same study and is not counted separately.
