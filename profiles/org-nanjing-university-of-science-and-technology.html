Reinforcement-learning-guided experiments produce a Zr-based bulk metallic glass with 1.7 GPa yield strength and 10.2% elongation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A knowledge-augmented reinforcement-learning search over composition space, guided by learned property models and then validated by synthesis, identified a Zr-based bulk metallic glass, Zr63Cu15Al10Ni10Fe2. Single-step suction-cast specimens reached 1.7 GPa yield strength and 10.2% compressive plastic strain at room temperature. Across tested Fe-alloyed compositions, measured strength trends tracked model predictions; tungsten substitutions did not show the same ductility response.

Technology: AIMATDESIGN knowledge-augmented reinforcement-learning framework, using machine-learning property predictors and large-language-model-assisted model refinement and domain-knowledge rewards

AI contribution
An RL agent explored high-dimensional alloy compositions using ML classification/regression predictions as rewards and feasibility guidance. LLM-assisted feedback corrected unreliable model predictions and expert materials rules informed late-stage rewards. The authors used this workflow to choose Zr-based bulk-glass compositions for synthesis and mechanical testing; the experimentally tested Zr63Cu15Al10Ni10Fe2 composition achieved the reported strength and ductility result. This AI-guided composition search is the discovery contribution; the LLM was one component of a larger RL/ML workflow.
Test and result
A knowledge-augmented reinforcement-learning search over composition space, guided by learned property models and then validated by synthesis, identified a Zr-based bulk metallic glass, Zr63Cu15Al10Ni10Fe2.
Limitation / still unknown
The reported measurements are room-temperature compression tests on single-step suction-cast samples; the paper notes that plastic strain was systematically lower than predicted for most tested specimens, partly because surface defects and residual stresses were not represented in the model. The 10.2% value is for the specific Fe2 composition and test protocol, not a general alloy-family property. This is distinct from a separate study of an ML-designed bulk metallic glass optimized for hardness (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-73008-0): the present result concerns Zr63Cu15Al10Ni10Fe2 and its strength/ductility balance.
