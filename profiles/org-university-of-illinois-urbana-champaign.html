A closed-loop platform engineers enzymes with minimal human input

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The July 2025 Nature Communications paper reports AI-guided biofoundry engineering of two enzymes in four rounds over four weeks, testing fewer than 500 variants per enzyme. Different AtHMT variants achieved about 90-fold greater ethyl-iodide substrate preference and 16-fold ethyltransferase activity; YmPhytase activity improved 26-fold at neutral pH. The system combines ML variant selection, language-model access and automated experiments; the authors describe conditional-to-high automation.

28 Aug 2024University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; University of Toronto; Vector Institute; University of Chicago; University of California, Berkeley

Closed-loop learning links donor–acceptor molecules’ photostability to higher triplet states

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A closed-loop synthesis and learning campaign found that solution photostability across a donor–bridge–acceptor molecular series correlated with the high-energy portion of the triplet excited-state manifold, beyond the commonly considered lowest triplet state. Researchers tested the model-derived hypothesis on an experimental set and changed solvent triplet energy to test the proposed Dexter triplet-energy-transfer degradation pathway. The study used Bayesian optimization and interpretable supervised learning to extract the chemical hypothesis from measured photostability data.

Technology: Closed-loop transfer (CLT), combining Gryffin Bayesian optimization with physics-feature supervised learning

Editorial relevance 60 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Bayesian optimization recommended molecules for automated modular synthesis and photostability assays; an interpretable supervised model using physics-based features identified a triplet-state hypothesis, which researchers tested with experimental test sets and solvent-energy perturbation. AI guided experimental selection and surfaced a specific chemical relationship; the follow-up measurements tested it.
Test and result
Researchers tested the model-derived hypothesis on an experimental set and changed solvent triplet energy to test the proposed Dexter triplet-energy-transfer degradation pathway.
Limitation / still unknown
The finding concerns solution photostability in the studied donor–bridge–acceptor chemical space; it does not by itself establish a universal law for all organic molecules or performance in processed films/devices. The work used human expertise in the loop and experimentally tested the model-generated hypothesis. Device deployment was not demonstrated.

LAISS-assisted review reclassifies anomalous ZTF transients

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Expert review of 455 previously observed transients changed 16 normal labels to anomalous; the random-forest model flagged 11 of these. 4 May 2023University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; University of Michigan

BacterAI experimentally maps amino-acid growth requirements of two oral Streptococcus species

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: BacterAI autonomously designed and ran growth experiments with robotic liquid handling to learn amino-acid requirements for the oral commensals Streptococcus gordonii and S. sanguinis. It learned separate growth rules for each organism and showed that the closely related species have different amino-acid auxotrophies. For S. gordonii, its neural-network growth predictions exceeded 90% accuracy after nine days and 3,024 experiments; the article also reports transfer-learning tests in larger media.

Technology: BacterAI, a reinforcement-learning agent coupled to iterative neural-network growth models and automated laboratory robots

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The agent selected successive combinations of amino acids to test based on prior growth results, executed those tests through an automated pipeline, and distilled results into interpretable growth rules. It enabled an unbiased experimental mapping of nutrient requirements from a blank slate for both species.
Test and result
BacterAI autonomously designed and ran growth experiments with robotic liquid handling to learn amino-acid requirements for the oral commensals Streptococcus gordonii and S. sanguinis.
Limitation / still unknown
The measured outcomes are in defined growth media under the tested laboratory conditions, not direct evidence about nutrient exchange or health effects in a human oral microbiome. The article establishes that the two species have different amino-acid requirements; transfer learning and high growth-prediction accuracy describe the method’s performance, not separate biological discoveries. Replicate growth assays showed high agreement, but inferred rules may not cover every environmental condition.

31 Mar 2023University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; Cornell University; Georgia Institute of Technology

CLEAN experimentally identifies SAM hydrolase activity in previously uncharacterized proteins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign developed CLEAN, a contrastive-learning enzyme-function predictor, and used it to identify the SAM hydrolase activity of the previously uncharacterized protein TTHA0338. In vitro HPLC–MS and enzyme-kinetic experiments also corrected the annotation of MJ1651 and confirmed the multiple activities of SsFIA.

Technology: CLEAN (contrastive learning-based enzyme annotation model)

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CLEAN predicted enzyme commission functions from protein sequences; researchers then validated the predicted activity experimentally. It was the model that nominated TTHA0338 as a SAM hydrolase, not an autonomous discovery or validation system.
Test and result
In vitro HPLC–MS and enzyme-kinetic experiments also corrected the annotation of MJ1651 and confirmed the multiple activities of SsFIA.
Limitation / still unknown
The study validated a small, selected set of proteins, and also showed CLEAN can assign functions to non-enzymes; the demonstrated result is specific to the tested proteins. TTHA0338 is one of multiple results reported by this paper; record describes the previously unknown function rather than treating every model benchmark as a separate discovery.
