6 Nov 2024University of Liverpool

Mobile autonomous robots for exploratory synthetic chemistry

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Mobile robots move samples between a synthesis platform, a UPLC-MS spectrometer, and a benchtop NMR, while a heuristic decision-maker evaluates data and chooses reactions. Demonstrated on structural diversification, supramolecular chemistry, and photochemical synthesis. The AI is heuristic and there is no speed or accuracy metric; the announcement says robots will not have a ‘Eureka’ moment.

Technology: Heuristic decision-making with mobile robots

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A heuristic decision-maker processes analytical data and autonomously decides whether a reaction proceeds to the next step.
Test and result
Mobile robots were demonstrated on three exploratory chemistry problems (structural diversification, supramolecular chemistry, photochemical synthesis); the page reports no reaction counts or accuracy metrics, only that the AI made the same or similar decisions as a human researcher.
Limitation / still unknown
The page reports no quantitative accuracy metrics, only two robots were used, and the system lacks a 'Eureka' moment.

22 Jul 2023University of Liverpool; University of Essex

Machine learning guides proofs of braid and flat-braid invariants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A one-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron classified examples of three-strand braids and suggested algebraic criteria. Authors converted the observed patterns into proofs. The paper proves a complete criterion for triviality of flat braids on three strands; its result does not establish a complete invariant for arbitrary braids or the neural network itself as a proof system.

Technology: Supervised multilayer perceptron

21 Sep 2021University of Liverpool

Unsupervised machine learning guides discovery of a lithium solid electrolyte

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A variational autoencoder ranked unexplored element combinations for lithium solid electrolytes. Researchers combined those rankings with crystal-structure prediction and synthesis to discover Li₃.₃SnS₃.₃Cl₀.₇, then characterized its structure and lithium-ion transport. Impedance, NMR and lithium-cell measurements supported a new transport pathway and compatibility with lithium metal.

Technology: Unsupervised machine learning for element selection with crystal structure prediction

Editorial relevance 73 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An unsupervised variational autoencoder ranked chemical element combinations; human selection and crystal-structure calculations directed synthesis.
Test and result
Synthesis, diffraction, impedance and NMR characterization of the selected lithium solid electrolyte, with lithium plating/stripping measurements.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a laboratory material study; full-battery performance and independent replication were not assessed.

8 Jul 2020University of Liverpool

A mobile robotic chemist

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A mobile robot ran 688 experiments in eight days in a ten-variable space, guided by a batch Bayesian algorithm, to search for photocatalysts that produce hydrogen from water; the best mixtures were six times more active than the initial formulations. This optimizes a formulation, not a new structure; only the abstract was reviewed.

Technology: Batch Bayesian optimization and mobile robot

Editorial relevance 80 chemistry Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A batch Bayesian algorithm chose which experiments the mobile robot ran next, automating the researcher rather than the instruments.
Test and result
688 autonomous experiments over eight days in a ten-variable space; the best photocatalyst mixtures were about six times more active than the initial formulations.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract lists no limitations of this study; it notes that atomistic simulations cannot yet capture multi-scale complexity and that robots are hard to adopt widely across diverse samples and instruments.
