13 Nov 2026University of Liverpool; University of Cambridge

Mapping the Phase Space of Cubic Chiral Semiconductors: High-Throughput Search,Stability, and Prototype Families

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A high-throughput computational search of 24,227 cubic chiral structures identified 368 candidates on the computed convex hull and 3,048 within 50 meV/atom. Selected candidates were refined with density-functional theory, and several prototype families showed computed intermediate-band features. This is a computational candidate set, not experimental synthesis.

Technology: M3GNet relaxed randomly generated structures and ALIGNN predicted convex-hull distances to screen candidates before DFT refinement.

Test and result

Limitation / still unknown
Predicted stability and electronic structures have not been confirmed by synthesis; results depend on computational approximations.

14 Sep 2026University of Liverpool; University of Manchester

Quantitative control and recording of materials-synthesis processes using an automated experimentation platform

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An automated experimentation platform produced ZIF-8 under three solution-dispensing speeds; DLS measurements showed a repeatable dependence of particle-size distribution on speed, and XRD confirmed ZIF-8 as the principal crystalline phase. A Gemini agent generated device-control code that was reviewed before execution. Limited conditions and repeats do not establish the mechanism.

Technology: A Gemini 3.5 Flash agent generates robot and device control code for a quantitatively controlled materials-synthesis experiment; authors review/validate generated code before execution.

28 Aug 2025University of Liverpool; University of Helsinki

ColabFold-supported binding tests identify CDKL5 interactions with synaptic scaffolds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint first posted 28 August 2025 used ColabFold/AlphaFold modeling to support a proposed interaction between the neuronal CDKL5 isoform and a SHANK1 PDZ domain. Fluorescence-polarization and NMR experiments supported direct binding, while disrupting the terminal binding motif abolished it; the preprint reported an affinity of 8.5 micromolar. The November 2026 journal version reports 7.6 micromolar for the same interaction and further cellular/neuronal context. These are versions of one result. AI supported a structural interaction hypothesis; binding assays provide the direct evidence. The distinct talin-binding result is not attributed to AI here, and the work does not establish clinical effects or treatment for CDKL5 deficiency disorder.

Technology: ColabFold/AlphaFold protein-complex modeling of the CDKL5 C-terminal peptide and SHANK1 PDZ domain

Family AlphaFold: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 biology PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
ColabFold generated a complex model supporting a sequence/motif-based CDKL5–SHANK1 interaction hypothesis, which was tested with binding and perturbation assays. AI is not credited with the separate talin finding or clinical effects.
Test and result
ColabFold-supported PDZ-binding hypothesis tested by fluorescence polarization, NMR and disruption of the terminal binding motif, with tests of other synaptic PDZ domains. The 2025 v1 reported SHANK1 affinity 8.5 micromolar; the 2026 journal version reports 7.6 micromolar. Both support the same specific direct-binding finding.
Limitation / still unknown
No atomic-resolution experimental structure of the predicted complex or patient-derived/in vivo disease validation is reported. Neuronal localization and spine effects use transfected/overexpression systems and do not establish treatment or clinical outcomes. The paper also reports talin binding; that result is not attributed to AlphaFold here.

6 Nov 2024University of Liverpool

Mobile autonomous robots for exploratory synthetic chemistry

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Mobile robots move samples between a synthesis platform, a UPLC-MS spectrometer, and a benchtop NMR, while a heuristic decision-maker evaluates data and chooses reactions. Demonstrated on structural diversification, supramolecular chemistry, and photochemical synthesis. The AI is heuristic and there is no speed or accuracy metric; the announcement says robots will not have a ‘Eureka’ moment.

Technology: Heuristic decision-making with mobile robots

Machine learning guides proofs of braid and flat-braid invariants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A one-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron classified examples of three-strand braids and suggested algebraic criteria. Authors converted the observed patterns into proofs. The paper proves a complete criterion for triviality of flat braids on three strands; its result does not establish a complete invariant for arbitrary braids or the neural network itself as a proof system.

30 Jan 2022University of Liverpool

Mixed-effects deep learning diagnoses COVID-19 from CT, externally validated in China

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A deep learning model with a mixed-effects layer modeling slice relationships was trained on CT volumes from 1,110 patients in Moscow and externally validated on 796 patients from a Chinese consortium (CC-CCII). External AUROC was 0.936 (95% CI 0.910-0.961), with sensitivity 0.753 and specificity 0.909 at a 0.5 threshold; calibration, decision-curve analysis and saliency maps were also assessed. Data were collected retrospectively in early 2020. Preprint of 2022-01-30.

Technology: Deep learning model with a mixed-effects layer on CT volumes to diagnose COVID-19

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Preprint Suggest a correction

Test and result
External geographic validation on 796 patients from China (AUROC 0.936), with calibration and decision-curve analysis.

21 Sep 2021University of Liverpool

Unsupervised machine learning guides discovery of a lithium solid electrolyte

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A variational autoencoder ranked unexplored element combinations for lithium solid electrolytes. Researchers combined those rankings with crystal-structure prediction and synthesis to discover Li₃.₃SnS₃.₃Cl₀.₇, then characterized its structure and lithium-ion transport. Impedance, NMR and lithium-cell measurements supported a new transport pathway and compatibility with lithium metal.

Technology: Unsupervised machine learning for element selection with crystal structure prediction

AI contribution

Test and result
Synthesis, diffraction, impedance and NMR characterization of the selected lithium solid electrolyte, with lithium plating/stripping measurements.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a laboratory material study; full-battery performance and independent replication were not assessed.

8 Jul 2020University of Liverpool

A mobile robotic chemist

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A mobile robot ran 688 experiments in eight days in a ten-variable space, guided by a batch Bayesian algorithm, to search for photocatalysts that produce hydrogen from water; the best mixtures were six times more active than the initial formulations. This optimizes a formulation, not a new structure; only the abstract was reviewed.

Technology: Batch Bayesian optimization and mobile robot

Editorial relevance 80 chemistry Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A batch Bayesian algorithm chose which experiments the mobile robot ran next, automating the researcher rather than the instruments.
Test and result
688 autonomous experiments over eight days in a ten-variable space; the best photocatalyst mixtures were about six times more active than the initial formulations.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract lists no limitations of this study; it notes that atomistic simulations cannot yet capture multi-scale complexity and that robots are hard to adopt widely across diverse samples and instruments.
