12 Nov 2026MIT; Imperial College London

Unrealistic emissions scenarios help AI climate models prepare for unfamiliar futures

MIT researchers with Imperial College London designed a small synthetic set of deliberately unrealistic emissions scenarios to train climate emulators, which normally need six to eight full Earth system model runs that each take months. The method needs only one model run, and the emulator predicted unseen scenarios and local pollutant-control effects where traditional emulators failed. It was validated with a simple climate model rather than a full Earth system model, and the article reports no quantitative accuracy metrics.

Technology: Climate emulator trained on optimized synthetic scenarios

22 Sep 2026Stanford University; Boston University; University of Auckland; Colorado State University

Machine learning attributes post-2015 emissions to intensification of Europe’s recent heat extremes

A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology. It found >95% probability that anthropogenic emissions since the 2015 Paris Agreement intensified Europe’s summer temperature extremes in 2021–2025; for the June 2025 heatwave, regional mean temperature is estimated to have been 0.34 °C lower under 2015 cumulative emissions, with >99% probability.

Technology: Generative diffusion model for spatially explicit temperature fields

7 Aug 2026University of Oxford; UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology; UK Met Office

Data-driven equations reveal rainfall-linked North Atlantic decadal variability

Using SINDy equation discovery on monthly observational indices from 1950–2022, researchers derived three linked low-order equations for Atlantic Multidecadal Variability, the North Atlantic Oscillation and North Atlantic precipitation. The model recovered a distinct roughly 20-year component and highlighted precipitation-linked terms as robust components of the learned dynamics; tests against the independent 1900–1939 record found skill for statistics of the variability indices and rainfall. The equations generate hypotheses about ocean-atmosphere feedbacks; they do not establish a single causal mechanism or guarantee forecasts.

Technology: Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) equation-discovery algorithm

Neural-network satellite soil-moisture data expose global hotspots and pathways of rainfall coupling

An information-flow analysis of daily observations found statistically significant local surface-soil-moisture effects on precipitation over about 15.7% of global land (60°S–60°N) in the boreal warm season, with eight regional hotspots. Sensible-heat mediation dominated most hotspots, while evapotranspiration mediation dominated two African hotspots; remote moisture availability and boundary-layer-height variability helped explain regional differences. Only four of 16 assessed CMIP6 models reproduced the ERA5-derived relationship between soil-moisture variability and causal coupling.

Technology: Neural-network-inversion satellite soil-moisture product (NNSm), analyzed with Liang–Kleeman information-flow causal inference
