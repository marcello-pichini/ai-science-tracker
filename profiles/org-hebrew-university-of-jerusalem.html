29 Aug 2024Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Learned call analysis identifies receiver-specific marmoset vocal patterns

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Classifiers identified receiver-related structure in calls from ten captive marmosets. Playback experiments also found stronger responses to calls originally directed at the listener. The authors interpret these patterns as vocal labels; a later commentary disputes whether the evidence establishes name-like reference, and the authors defend that interpretation. The bounded finding is receiver-specific acoustic and behavioral differentiation, not decoded human-like names.

Technology: Random-forest acoustic classifiers

28 Jul 2024Hebrew University of Jerusalem; University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; collaborators

Machine learning uncovers four new extracellular contractile injection-system toxins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An XGBoost classifier trained on genomic and biochemical features prioritized eCIS-associated toxins from bacterial and archaeal genomes. Four candidates, EAT14–17, were experimentally shown to be toxic in bacterial and/or yeast cells; EAT14 also inhibited Rac1/Cdc42-mediated mitogenic signaling in human cells.

Technology: XGBoost classifier for extracellular contractile injection-system-associated toxins (EATs)

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The classifier used genomic and protein biochemical features to score candidate EAT genes across eCIS-positive microbial genomes. The model narrowed candidates for functional tests; heterologous expression assays validated four previously uncharacterized toxins, with follow-up active-site mutations and a human-cell signaling assay.
Test and result
Four candidates, EAT14–17, were experimentally shown to be toxic in bacterial and/or yeast cells; EAT14 also inhibited Rac1/Cdc42-mediated mitogenic signaling in human cells.
Limitation / still unknown
Four of eight selected candidates were toxic under the tested heterologous-expression assays; the authors report an empirical positive predictive value of 50% for this sample and discuss possible host/compartment mismatch. Toxin delivery by native eCIS and physiological target organisms remain incompletely established. EAT14’s signaling effect was shown in human cells, not as a therapeutic effect.
