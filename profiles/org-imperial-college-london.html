13 Nov 2026Imperial College London; University of Antwerp and Antwerp University Hospital; Maastricht University Medical Center; Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center; Zhongshan Hospital of Fudan University

AI-enhanced electrocardiography for detection and prediction of dilated cardiomyopathy

In the journal abstract, an AI-ECG model trained on more than 330,000 ECG–echocardiogram pairs detected the dilated-cardiomyopathy phenotype (AUROC 0.87–0.97) and predicted later disease (C-index 0.80–0.87). In familial cohorts, five-year negative predictive values were 95.5% in Antwerp and 91.5% in Maastricht; positive predictive values were 42.4% and 39.4%. These are diagnostic and risk-prediction findings, not demonstrated clinical benefit.

Technology: Explainable deep learning model applied to electrocardiography

31 Oct 2023Imperial College London; University of Nottingham

Machine learning exposed a structure in terminal-singularity classification

A neural-network classifier trained on eight-dimensional, Picard-rank-two toric varieties achieved 95% accuracy on a five-million-example test set and enabled rapid generation of a much larger predicted landscape of Q-Fano varieties. The authors report that the model’s classification pattern led them to formulate a new combinatorial criterion for terminal singularities, which they prove mathematically. The learned model suggested the route to the theorem; it did not supply the proof.

Technology: Feed-forward neural-network classifier trained on toric-variety weight matrices

Relevance 60 mathematics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The authors report a 95% classification accuracy on a balanced test split of five million eight-dimensional toric-variety examples and use the model to explore a larger Q-Fano landscape. They formulate and prove a new combinatorial criterion for terminal singularities, motivated by the ML analysis.
  • Still unknown: The 95% is internal held-out classification performance, not independent external validation; the authors state the neural network does not itself perform the theorem’s divisibility-based test or proof.
  • AI role: The trained neural-network classifier revealed a tractable pattern in the classification landscape and directly motivated the authors’ new combinatorial criterion; the authors separately establish its correctness by proof.

8 Sep 2023Imperial College London; University of Nottingham

Machine learning helps recover dimension from quantum periods for a class of Fano varieties

A feed-forward neural network classified the dimension of Fano varieties from quantum-period coefficients with 98% accuracy on the authors’ dataset. The authors report that machine learning helped formulate rigorous asymptotic results, which prove dimension recovery for specified low-Picard-rank toric Fano classes. The broader conjecture that the quantum period uniquely determines every Fano variety remains open; the empirical classification accuracy is not itself a proof. The journal article appeared 2023-09-08, before the same work’s 2023-09-11 arXiv posting.

Technology: Feed-forward neural network

15 Mar 2023Imperial College London; Osa Conservation

Acoustic learning maps spider-monkey associations with forest disturbance

A learned call detector, manual confirmation and occupancy modeling analyzed recordings from 341 Costa Rican sites. In this sample, Geoffroy's spider monkey was detected only above 80% forest cover and away from nearby primary paved roads. These are regional observational associations, not causal or universal habitat thresholds; imperfect detection and the focus on one call type limit inference.

Technology: Attention-equipped residual convolutional call detector
