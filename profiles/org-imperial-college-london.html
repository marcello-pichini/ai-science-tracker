13 Nov 2026Imperial College London; University of Antwerp and Antwerp University Hospital; Maastricht University Medical Center; Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center; Zhongshan Hospital of Fudan University

AI-enhanced electrocardiography for detection and prediction of dilated cardiomyopathy

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In the journal abstract, an AI-ECG model trained on more than 330,000 ECG–echocardiogram pairs detected the dilated-cardiomyopathy phenotype (AUROC 0.87–0.97) and predicted later disease (C-index 0.80–0.87). In familial cohorts, five-year negative predictive values were 95.5% in Antwerp and 91.5% in Maastricht; positive predictive values were 42.4% and 39.4%. These are diagnostic and risk-prediction findings, not demonstrated clinical benefit.

Technology: Explainable deep learning model applied to electrocardiography

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
Validated in UK Biobank, Zhongshan Hospital and Antwerp cohorts, and in two familial cohorts (Antwerp n=166, Maastricht n=177), compared with traditional ECG features and echocardiographic traits.
Limitation / still unknown

22 Oct 2026Imperial College London

More specific wording narrowed semantic representations in four language models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers compared sentences that made a meaning more specific with sentences that merely added detail. Across four language models, the more specific versions formed tighter neighborhoods in model activation space. The pattern persisted after removing five dominant directions of variation.

Technology: Gemma 4, Llama 3.2, Qwen2.5, Pythia

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Pretrained activation geometry is measured as controlled texts move from broader meanings to more specific descriptions, with blind human checks on the contrasts.
Test and result
Controlled refinement and elaboration chains were checked using blind human judgments. Across the four models, the contraction contrast persisted in 86–100% of layers after the five dominant directions were removed.
Limitation / still unknown
The result depends on the constructed text contrasts and representation measures. It does not demonstrate causal use of the contraction in other language tasks.

31 Oct 2023Imperial College London; University of Nottingham

Machine learning exposed a structure in terminal-singularity classification

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural-network classifier trained on eight-dimensional, Picard-rank-two toric varieties achieved 95% accuracy on a five-million-example test set and enabled rapid generation of a much larger predicted landscape of Q-Fano varieties. The authors report that the model’s classification pattern led them to formulate a new combinatorial criterion for terminal singularities, which they prove mathematically. The learned model suggested the route to the theorem; it did not supply the proof.

Technology: Feed-forward neural-network classifier trained on toric-variety weight matrices

Editorial relevance 60 mathematics PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The trained neural-network classifier revealed a tractable pattern in the classification landscape and directly motivated the authors’ new combinatorial criterion; the authors separately establish its correctness by proof.
Test and result
The authors report a 95% classification accuracy on a balanced test split of five million eight-dimensional toric-variety examples and use the model to explore a larger Q-Fano landscape. They formulate and prove a new combinatorial criterion for terminal singularities, motivated by the ML analysis.
Limitation / still unknown
The 95% is internal held-out classification performance, not independent external validation; the authors state the neural network does not itself perform the theorem’s divisibility-based test or proof.

8 Sep 2023Imperial College London; University of Nottingham

Machine learning helps recover dimension from quantum periods for a class of Fano varieties

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A feed-forward neural network classified the dimension of Fano varieties from quantum-period coefficients with 98% accuracy on the authors’ dataset. The authors report that machine learning helped formulate rigorous asymptotic results, which prove dimension recovery for specified low-Picard-rank toric Fano classes. The broader conjecture that the quantum period uniquely determines every Fano variety remains open; the empirical classification accuracy is not itself a proof. The journal article appeared 2023-09-08, before the same work’s 2023-09-11 arXiv posting.

Technology: Feed-forward neural network

Editorial relevance 60 mathematics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A neural network analyzed quantum-period coefficients and helped identify dimension-related patterns used to formulate asymptotic results, which the authors prove for restricted classes.
Test and result
The article combines a dataset classification result with rigorous asymptotic proofs for specified classes.
Limitation / still unknown
98% is empirical accuracy on the paper’s dataset; the broad uniqueness conjecture remains open. Journal publication preceded arXiv submission.

28 Apr 2023Imperial College London

Automated segmentation and radiomics stratify risk in serous ovarian cancer

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An end-to-end pipeline combined U-Net-based automated segmentation of primary ovarian cancer lesions on contrast-enhanced CT with radiomics and machine learning survival models, in 607 retrospectively collected scans. Segmentation Dice scores were 0.90, 0.88 and 0.80 across validation and two test sets; the best radiomics model had C-index 0.66 (validation), 0.72 (TCIA) and 0.60 (KEM). It improved risk stratification over CA-125, residual disease and FIGO stage in the authors' comparison. Retrospective; preprint of 2023-04-28.

Technology: U-Net-based automated segmentation plus radiomics with 13 feature reduction methods and 12 machine learning algorithms for survival prediction

15 Mar 2023Imperial College London; Osa Conservation

Acoustic learning maps spider-monkey associations with forest disturbance

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A learned call detector, manual confirmation and occupancy modeling analyzed recordings from 341 Costa Rican sites. In this sample, Geoffroy's spider monkey was detected only above 80% forest cover and away from nearby primary paved roads. These are regional observational associations, not causal or universal habitat thresholds; imperfect detection and the focus on one call type limit inference.

Technology: Attention-equipped residual convolutional call detector

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Limitation / still unknown

29 Oct 2020Imperial College London; Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust

Early cardiac MRI images identify pleural effusions in retrospective testing

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An adapted HRNet segmented early anatomical cardiac MRI images and quantified pleural fluid. In a 200-scan test set containing 42 report-positive effusions, fluid quantification distinguished report-positive scans with an AUC of 0.906. Adaptive scanning and patient benefits were proposed rather than tested.

Technology: HRNet convolutional neural network

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An adapted HRNet learned pixel labels from clinician annotations; segmented slices were reconstructed into a 3D fluid volume used to distinguish report-positive pleural effusions.
Test and result
Retrospective 575-scan study across two hospitals and two scanner manufacturers: 375 scans for training/validation and 200 for testing. Clinicians labelled 1,500 development slices. HRNet segmentation and 3D fluid quantification yielded pleural-effusion AUC 0.906 against final clinical reports; 42 of 200 test scans had reported effusions.
Limitation / still unknown
Report-based reference rather than an independently adjudicated effusion standard; no prospective workflow or patient-benefit test. Generalization beyond these centers remains uncertain. Separate chamber-size claims require manufacturer-specific regression calibration whose fitting partition is unclear, and are not represented here. Threshold-specific accuracy is omitted because optimal cut-offs were evaluated in the study dataset.
