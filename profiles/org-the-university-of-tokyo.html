6 Nov 2026The University of Tokyo; National Astronomical Observatory of Japan; Tohoku University; Subaru Telescope

PCFNet-guided spectroscopy confirms four protoclusters at redshift 3.3–3.4

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution. Twenty-three spectroscopically identified members are largely on the star-forming main sequence. Model-weighted estimates put star-formation-rate density above cosmic averages, while metallicity differences are marginal in two systems and consistent with the field in the others.

Technology: PCFNet point-cloud deep-learning protocluster finder

Editorial relevance 60 astronomy PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
PCFNet used broad-band optical photometry and galaxy positions to assign protocluster-membership probabilities and identify candidate regions. The model outputs determined which regions received Subaru/MOIRCS follow-up and prioritized slit targets; their probabilities also informed member selection and the authors’ volume/SFR-density estimator. Spectroscopy then tested those predictions, finding a 40% member detection rate among high-probability galaxies and confirming three of four observed regions.
Test and result
Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a small follow-up sample: four of 121 PCFNet candidates were targeted, and the parent catalogue is trained on simulated galaxies. Four protoclusters are confirmed across three of four observed regions; a fourth observed candidate region had no significant redshift peak. One of the confirmed regions had previously been reported as a photometric candidate, so this is a spectroscopic confirmation/characterization result rather than four wholly unprecedented structures. The halo masses are inferred from member stellar masses, not direct lensing measurements. The elevated SFR densities depend on membership-probability weighting and are sensitive to assumed geometry, volume and methodology. Metallicity excesses are only 1.4–2 sigma in two systems; two others are statistically consistent with the field. The authors’ proposed gas-inflow/recycling explanation is interpretive.

21 Oct 2026The University of Tokyo; TIER IV; Huawei

A hidden-state shift can cause a language model to repeat one token

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers isolated a shift shared across responses during language-model preference training. Inserting the learned shift into an untreated model reproduced a failure in which one token dominated generation. The failure appeared in 14 of 17 tested model lineages under the study’s per-token ranking objective.

Technology: Preference-optimized language models, including Llama-2 and Llama-3.1

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The researchers isolated a common direction learned during preference training and tested its contribution by transplanting it into an untreated language model.
Test and result
The authors isolated the shared offset, compared transplantation with magnitude-matched random directions, and tested masking of the component in the training loss.
Limitation / still unknown
The failure depends on model and objective. Related hidden shifts were also observed without generation collapse in tested standard preference-training runs.

5 Oct 2026The University of Tokyo; Los Alamos National Laboratory

Recurrent neural network reveals near-critical seeding of topological defects in Kibble–Zurek simulations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In numerical simulations of a one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg phase transition, a recurrent neural network predicted the final locations of topological defects from short sequences of order-parameter fluctuations during the Kibble–Zurek impulse regime, before freeze-out and before the defects were macroscopically visible. Prediction error across quench rates followed power-law scaling consistent with the Kibble–Zurek freeze-out time, indicating that information about the eventual defect pattern is encoded in near-critical fluctuations in this model.

Technology: Recurrent neural network (RNN); LSTM and Transformer architectures were also checked

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The RNN analyzed short time sequences from numerical order-parameter dynamics and inferred the final kink count and positions from small fluctuations during the impulse regime. This allowed the authors to identify when information about future defect positions becomes predictable, a quantity not directly provided by conventional Kibble–Zurek defect-density scaling. They report similar prediction behavior with LSTM and Transformer models, suggesting the result is not specific to one architecture.
Test and result
In numerical simulations of a one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg phase transition, a recurrent neural network predicted the final locations of topological defects from short sequences of order-parameter fluctuations during the Kibble–Zurek impulse regime, before freeze-out and before the defects were macroscopically visible.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a computational result from a noisy one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg model, not an experimental observation of defects in a material or cosmological system. The paper uses overdamped dynamics (η=1), low noise temperature θ=10⁻⁸, a finite periodic grid and simulated training/test samples; similar behavior at another noise setting is reported in supplementary material. The finding is consistent with the Kibble–Zurek mechanism and an earlier deconstruction of its dynamics; it does not establish that this prediction method applies unchanged to all phase transitions. The PRL article was published 2026-10-05; the original arXiv preprint was submitted 2025-08-28 and revised as v2 on 2026-09-10.

23 May 2020The University of Tokyo; The University of Tokyo Hospital

Hierarchical Clustering Analysis for Predicting 1-Year Mortality After Starting Hemodialysis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding / tested result: Prospective single-centre cohort of 101 patients newly starting hemodialysis: hierarchical clustering over 46 clinical variables identified a subgroup associated with the highest 1-year mortality (age/sex-adjusted HR 10.2, 95% CI 2.94–46.8). This is a source-reported observational association, not a validated biological subtype, causal explanation, or treatment recommendation.

Technology: Ward agglomerative hierarchical clustering with K-means sensitivity analysis

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Scientific article Suggest a correction

Publication: Publication status unspecified in annotation · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Ward hierarchical clustering grouped 101 new hemodialysis patients on standardized clinical variables; the clusters were associated with one-year mortality, with K-means used as a sensitivity analysis.
