6 Nov 2026The University of Tokyo; National Astronomical Observatory of Japan; Tohoku University; Subaru Telescope

PCFNet-guided spectroscopy confirms four protoclusters at redshift 3.3–3.4

Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution. Twenty-three spectroscopically identified members are largely on the star-forming main sequence. Model-weighted estimates put star-formation-rate density above cosmic averages, while metallicity differences are marginal in two systems and consistent with the field in the others.

Technology: PCFNet point-cloud deep-learning protocluster finder

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution.
  • Still unknown: This is a small follow-up sample: four of 121 PCFNet candidates were targeted, and the parent catalogue is trained on simulated galaxies. Four protoclusters are confirmed across three of four observed regions; a fourth observed candidate region had no significant redshift peak. One of the confirmed regions had previously been reported as a photometric candidate, so this is a spectroscopic confirmation/characterization result rather than four wholly unprecedented structures. The halo masses are inferred from member stellar masses, not direct lensing measurements. The elevated SFR densities depend on membership-probability weighting and are sensitive to assumed geometry, volume and methodology. Metallicity excesses are only 1.4–2 sigma in two systems; two others are statistically consistent with the field. The authors’ proposed gas-inflow/recycling explanation is interpretive.
  • AI role: PCFNet used broad-band optical photometry and galaxy positions to assign protocluster-membership probabilities and identify candidate regions. The model outputs determined which regions received Subaru/MOIRCS follow-up and prioritized slit targets; their probabilities also informed member selection and the authors’ volume/SFR-density estimator. Spectroscopy then tested those predictions, finding a 40% member detection rate among high-probability galaxies and confirming three of four observed regions.

5 Oct 2026The University of Tokyo; Los Alamos National Laboratory

Recurrent neural network reveals near-critical seeding of topological defects in Kibble–Zurek simulations

In numerical simulations of a one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg phase transition, a recurrent neural network predicted the final locations of topological defects from short sequences of order-parameter fluctuations during the Kibble–Zurek impulse regime, before freeze-out and before the defects were macroscopically visible. Prediction error across quench rates followed power-law scaling consistent with the Kibble–Zurek freeze-out time, indicating that information about the eventual defect pattern is encoded in near-critical fluctuations in this model.

Technology: Recurrent neural network (RNN); LSTM and Transformer architectures were also checked

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: In numerical simulations of a one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg phase transition, a recurrent neural network predicted the final locations of topological defects from short sequences of order-parameter fluctuations during the Kibble–Zurek impulse regime, before freeze-out and before the defects were macroscopically visible.
  • Still unknown: This is a computational result from a noisy one-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg model, not an experimental observation of defects in a material or cosmological system. The paper uses overdamped dynamics (η=1), low noise temperature θ=10⁻⁸, a finite periodic grid and simulated training/test samples; similar behavior at another noise setting is reported in supplementary material. The finding is consistent with the Kibble–Zurek mechanism and an earlier deconstruction of its dynamics; it does not establish that this prediction method applies unchanged to all phase transitions. The PRL article was published 2026-10-05; the original arXiv preprint was submitted 2025-08-28 and revised as v2 on 2026-09-10.
  • AI role: The RNN analyzed short time sequences from numerical order-parameter dynamics and inferred the final kink count and positions from small fluctuations during the impulse regime. This allowed the authors to identify when information about future defect positions becomes predictable, a quantity not directly provided by conventional Kibble–Zurek defect-density scaling. They report similar prediction behavior with LSTM and Transformer models, suggesting the result is not specific to one architecture.

Evidence sources: https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/fstv-lsvh
