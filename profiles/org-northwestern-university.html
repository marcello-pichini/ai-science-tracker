23 Nov 2026Northwestern University; Shirley Ryan AbilityLab; Karolinska Institutet; University of California, San Diego; McGill University

Cross-species classifier identifies a human dopaminergic subtype depleted in Parkinson’s disease

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A hierarchical mixture-of-experts classifier transferred mouse dopaminergic-neuron labels to human single-nucleus datasets, identifying an Anxa1-associated population. The preprint reports this population was more depleted in Parkinson’s disease than comparator subtypes in a postmortem analysis of eight control and seven Parkinson’s donors. The model uses logistic-regression experts trained on a mouse reference; donor-aware abundance models support an association rather than a causal mechanism. Cross-species label transfer, small donor cohorts and retrospective tissue limit generalization; inferred connectivity and candidate drug targets are not direct human tracing or demonstrated therapy.

Technology: Hierarchical mixture-of-experts classifier (logistic-regression experts)

Editorial relevance 53 biology Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A tree of trained logistic-regression experts predicts subtype labels from gene expression and transfers mouse taxonomy to human nuclei, enabling the subtype-specific disease comparison.
Test and result
Existing mouse/human transcriptomic data; 24,419 mouse reference cells, five-fold cell-stratified validation and a held-out test; human depletion comparison uses eight control and seven Parkinson’s donors with donor-aware mixed models.
Limitation / still unknown
Causal susceptibility, in-vivo human projections and therapeutic benefit are not established. Cells are not independent donor replicates; cross-species annotation may constrain inferred subtype identity.

3 Nov 2026Northwestern University; Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center of Northwestern University; Technical University of Munich

ExPert infers likely causal perturbations from cell states

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: ExPert learns one prototype per perturbation in a latent space shared by 12 cellular contexts, trained on 8,964 perturbations and 19.5 million cells, and ranks 19,895 candidate genes per query. It more than doubled held-out-context recall over the best existing method with 1,000-fold less compute, and in alcohol-associated liver disease it implicated mRNA export, with nuclear poly(A) retention and isoform switching experimentally confirmed. Experiment sizes and held-out numbers were not read.

Technology: ExPert latent-prototype model ranking gene perturbations from observed cell states

24 Oct 2026Northwestern University

Matching global force left local interface laws unresolved in a learned model

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a simulated three-fiber composite, matching the global force–displacement curve did not reliably recover the local interface laws, even when the reference and inverse fits used the same fixed learned constitutive prior. Adding local strain observations improved recovery of the known simulated reference. The study tests a conditional computational inference limit, without validation on physical specimens.

Technology: Physics-constrained neural constitutive model; fitted latent coordinates

Editorial relevance 33 materials PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A pretrained physics-constrained constitutive network represented local adhesion laws. Fitted latent variables enabled comparing different observation regimes under a consistent learned family.
Test and result
The authors held the learned constitutive network fixed and compared force-only, strain-only and combined inverse fits across 144 interface latent variables.
Limitation / still unknown
General force-based nonidentifiability and the value of local strain observations were known; novelty is limited to this prior-restricted conditional example. Synthetic reference data were generated within the same learned family, so robustness to an incorrect prior, physical specimens or untested geometries is not established. Numerical failure of a fit is not a formal impossibility proof.

29 Jul 2025Northwestern University

Machine learning predicts next-day extubation from ICU electronic health records

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 37 electronic health record features aggregated overnight for each ICU day, XGBoost, LightGBM, logistic regression, LSTM and RNN models were built on a single-center prospective cohort (448 patients, 3,095 ICU days). The best model, an LSTM, predicted next-day extubation with AUROC 0.870 internally and 0.870 on an external community-hospital cohort (333 patients, 2,835 ICU days). Plateau pressure and Richmond Agitation Sedation Scale score were among the most informative features, and 63.8% of predicted extubations occurred within three days of true extubation. The model has not been tested in clinical deployment, and both ICUs are in one health system.

Technology: LSTM and gradient-boosting models on electronic health record features to predict next-day extubation

29 May 2025Northwestern University; California Institute of Technology; Stockholm University; Liverpool John Moores University; NSF-Simons AI Institute for the Sky; Zwicky Transient Facility collaboration

Autonomous BTSbot-nearby follow-up captures an infant supernova’s flash spectrum and constrains progenitor mass loss

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light. The early spectra showed short-lived Hα, He II and C IV flash-ionization features; comparison with two radiative-hydrodynamics model grids constrained the red-supergiant progenitor’s enhanced pre-explosion mass-loss rate to roughly 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ solar masses per year.

Technology: BTSbot machine-learning transient classifier integrated with the BTSbot-nearby autonomous follow-up program

Editorial relevance 53 astronomy Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
BTSbot identified the transient in ZTF alert data; host-distance and youth filters in BTSbot-nearby automatically triggered target-of-opportunity spectroscopy. This shortened latency to approximately seven minutes from alert-filter pass to first spectrum (spectroscopy was obtained about 0.7 days after first light), enabling observation of ephemeral flash features. Human and instrument teams obtained/interpreted observations; radiation-hydrodynamics models supported the mass-loss inference.
Test and result
The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light.
Limitation / still unknown
The AI contribution is event identification and automated prioritization, not inference of the stellar mass-loss physics. The rate is model-dependent and derived from two radiative-hydrodynamics code grids under adopted wind assumptions. The paper reports an individual nearby Type IIP event; this is not a population-level claim. The transient was originally reported to the Transient Name Server by a human observer, while the BTSbot-nearby program supplied rapid automated follow-up.

20 Jan 2025Northwestern University; Stanford University

Machine learning-guided cell-free engineering creates specialized amide synthetases for pharmaceutical molecules

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions. Ridge-regression ML guided enzyme variants that produced nine small-molecule pharmaceuticals with activity 1.6- to 42-fold above the parent; initial substrate-promiscuity assays found McbA could make 11 pharmaceutical compounds plus hybrid molecules. This is experimental enzyme engineering, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy of the products.

Technology: Augmented ridge-regression ML models within a cell-free sequence-function mapping and enzyme-engineering workflow

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Used measured variant fitness data to predict and select higher-activity McbA variants for distinct amide-forming reactions; selected variants were synthesized and assayed.
Test and result
A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions.
Limitation / still unknown
The platform optimizes in-vitro enzyme production of molecules and does not establish drug efficacy, manufacturing readiness, or broad transferability beyond tested reactions. The nine-product gains are relative to the parent enzyme under the reported assay conditions.

Learned feature selection identifies exploratory lymphedema-dose associations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In76 treated head-and-neck cancer patients, an ensemble feature-selection analysis linked selected lymphatic-region dose features with recorded lymphedema. Subsequent competing-risk models described associations with external and internal swelling. These retrospective results do not establish a deployable forecast or causal dose effect.

Technology: Support vector machine recursive feature elimination; random forest; XGBoost; LASSO

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Test and result

Selection and cutoff derivation reused the small cohort. Follow-up duration appeared among classification predictors, precluding an admission-time prediction interpretation. External/internal describe edema location, not independent validation cohorts. Prior cohort reporting, incomplete nesting and unmeasured confounding limit novelty and inference.

22 Jun 2021Northwestern University; The University of Texas at San Antonio Health Science Center; Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center of Northwestern University

Machine learning on serum mass spectra and AFP detects hepatocellular carcinoma

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Serum from three cohorts was split into development, internal validation and independent validation sets; Deep MALDI mass spectra were fed to the VeriStrat classification algorithm together with AFP, and machine learning classified samples as cancer or no cancer. Sensitivity and specificity were both above 80% for HCC, with improved detection over AFP for all tumor sizes, especially small lesions. Specificity did not depend on cause or severity of underlying disease. The independent validation set was small, and the authors call for a prospective study.

Technology: VeriStrat classification algorithm with machine learning on Deep MALDI mass spectra and AFP

AI contribution

Test and result

Limitation / still unknown
Performance in a larger prospective study; full text not reviewed.
