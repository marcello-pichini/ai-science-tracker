Autonomous BTSbot-nearby follow-up captures an infant supernova’s flash spectrum and constrains progenitor mass loss

The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light. The early spectra showed short-lived Hα, He II and C IV flash-ionization features; comparison with two radiative-hydrodynamics model grids constrained the red-supergiant progenitor’s enhanced pre-explosion mass-loss rate to roughly 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ solar masses per year.

Technology: BTSbot machine-learning transient classifier integrated with the BTSbot-nearby autonomous follow-up program

20 Jan 2025Northwestern University; Stanford University

Machine learning-guided cell-free engineering creates specialized amide synthetases for pharmaceutical molecules

A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions. Ridge-regression ML guided enzyme variants that produced nine small-molecule pharmaceuticals with activity 1.6- to 42-fold above the parent; initial substrate-promiscuity assays found McbA could make 11 pharmaceutical compounds plus hybrid molecules. This is experimental enzyme engineering, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy of the products.

Technology: Augmented ridge-regression ML models within a cell-free sequence-function mapping and enzyme-engineering workflow
