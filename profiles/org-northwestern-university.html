Physics-based differentiable imaging detects 51 Eridani b in a JWST band where standard processing did not

A physics-based coronagraph model optimized with automatic differentiation and gradient descent recovered 51 Eridani b in JWST/NIRCam F430M images at 4.3 μm, where the published standard-processing result was a non-detection. The paper also reports two- to fifteen-fold sensitivity gains across its test observations.

Technology: FLIP (Physics-based Learning for Imaging Planets), a differentiable instrument model optimized with automatic differentiation

29 May 2025Northwestern University; California Institute of Technology; Stockholm University; Liverpool John Moores University; NSF-Simons AI Institute for the Sky; Zwicky Transient Facility collaboration

Autonomous BTSbot-nearby follow-up captures an infant supernova’s flash spectrum and constrains progenitor mass loss

The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light. The early spectra showed short-lived Hα, He II and C IV flash-ionization features; comparison with two radiative-hydrodynamics model grids constrained the red-supergiant progenitor’s enhanced pre-explosion mass-loss rate to roughly 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ solar masses per year.

Technology: BTSbot machine-learning transient classifier integrated with the BTSbot-nearby autonomous follow-up program

  • Still unknown: The AI contribution is event identification and automated prioritization, not inference of the stellar mass-loss physics. The rate is model-dependent and derived from two radiative-hydrodynamics code grids under adopted wind assumptions. The paper reports an individual nearby Type IIP event; this is not a population-level claim. The transient was originally reported to the Transient Name Server by a human observer, while the BTSbot-nearby program supplied rapid automated follow-up.
  • AI role: BTSbot identified the transient in ZTF alert data; host-distance and youth filters in BTSbot-nearby automatically triggered target-of-opportunity spectroscopy. This shortened latency to approximately seven minutes from alert-filter pass to first spectrum (spectroscopy was obtained about 0.7 days after first light), enabling observation of ephemeral flash features. Human and instrument teams obtained/interpreted observations; radiation-hydrodynamics models supported the mass-loss inference.

20 Jan 2025Northwestern University; Stanford University

Machine learning-guided cell-free engineering creates specialized amide synthetases for pharmaceutical molecules

A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions. Ridge-regression ML guided enzyme variants that produced nine small-molecule pharmaceuticals with activity 1.6- to 42-fold above the parent; initial substrate-promiscuity assays found McbA could make 11 pharmaceutical compounds plus hybrid molecules. This is experimental enzyme engineering, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy of the products.

Technology: Augmented ridge-regression ML models within a cell-free sequence-function mapping and enzyme-engineering workflow
