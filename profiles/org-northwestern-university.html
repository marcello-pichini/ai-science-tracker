29 May 2025Northwestern University; California Institute of Technology; Stockholm University; Liverpool John Moores University; NSF-Simons AI Institute for the Sky; Zwicky Transient Facility collaboration

Autonomous BTSbot-nearby follow-up captures an infant supernova’s flash spectrum and constrains progenitor mass loss

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light. The early spectra showed short-lived Hα, He II and C IV flash-ionization features; comparison with two radiative-hydrodynamics model grids constrained the red-supergiant progenitor’s enhanced pre-explosion mass-loss rate to roughly 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ solar masses per year.

Technology: BTSbot machine-learning transient classifier integrated with the BTSbot-nearby autonomous follow-up program

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
BTSbot identified the transient in ZTF alert data; host-distance and youth filters in BTSbot-nearby automatically triggered target-of-opportunity spectroscopy. This shortened latency to approximately seven minutes from alert-filter pass to first spectrum (spectroscopy was obtained about 0.7 days after first light), enabling observation of ephemeral flash features. Human and instrument teams obtained/interpreted observations; radiation-hydrodynamics models supported the mass-loss inference.
Test and result
The BTSbot-nearby system used the BTSbot machine-learning classifier to identify SN 2024jlf in ZTF data and autonomously trigger a spectroscopic follow-up request, enabling spectroscopy about 0.7 days after first light.
Limitation / still unknown
The AI contribution is event identification and automated prioritization, not inference of the stellar mass-loss physics. The rate is model-dependent and derived from two radiative-hydrodynamics code grids under adopted wind assumptions. The paper reports an individual nearby Type IIP event; this is not a population-level claim. The transient was originally reported to the Transient Name Server by a human observer, while the BTSbot-nearby program supplied rapid automated follow-up.

20 Jan 2025Northwestern University; Stanford University

Machine learning-guided cell-free engineering creates specialized amide synthetases for pharmaceutical molecules

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions. Ridge-regression ML guided enzyme variants that produced nine small-molecule pharmaceuticals with activity 1.6- to 42-fold above the parent; initial substrate-promiscuity assays found McbA could make 11 pharmaceutical compounds plus hybrid molecules. This is experimental enzyme engineering, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy of the products.

Technology: Augmented ridge-regression ML models within a cell-free sequence-function mapping and enzyme-engineering workflow

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Used measured variant fitness data to predict and select higher-activity McbA variants for distinct amide-forming reactions; selected variants were synthesized and assayed.
Test and result
A cell-free design-build-test-learn platform generated sequence-function data from 1,217 McbA variants across 10,953 reactions.
Limitation / still unknown
The platform optimizes in-vitro enzyme production of molecules and does not establish drug efficacy, manufacturing readiness, or broad transferability beyond tested reactions. The nine-product gains are relative to the parent enzyme under the reported assay conditions.

11 Oct 2023Northwestern University; California Institute of Technology

BTSbot workflow automatically confirms and classifies SN 2023tyk

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: BTSbot selected SN 2023tyk from ZTF alerts and requested robotic spectroscopy; SNIascore supplied its Type Ia classification. The integrated workflow publicly reported the result without human intervention in those steps. This is one transient result, not a new supernova class or a claim that BTSbot alone performed the observation. It preceded the expanded 2024 study.
