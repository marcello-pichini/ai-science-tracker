28 Jun 2023University of Toronto; King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST)

Machine-learning-guided experiments identify a high-power-factor PbSe:SnSb thermoelectric family

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A closed-loop error-correction learning workflow guided synthesis of PbSe alloyed with SnSb and documented a previously unexplored low-temperature thermoelectric family. At 300 K, the best tested composition, 2 wt% SnSb-doped PbSe, had a measured power factor above 850 µW mK⁻² and more than twice that of PbSe. Density-functional calculations attribute the modeled transport improvement to Sn/Sb interactions that reduce hole effective mass.

Technology: Closed-loop error-correction learning with a neural-network update to a prior materials-property model; composition representations compared included Magpie, Roost and CrabNet

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A model trained on published experimental thermoelectric data ranked candidate compositions, and a dense neural-network error-correction model incorporated local experimental comparisons to adapt predictions to the authors’ synthesis and measurement conditions. Iterative model-guided experiments selected the PbSe:SnSb family and 2 wt% SnSb-doped PbSe for characterization. AI supplied composition down-selection and adaptive experiment guidance; the authors synthesized and measured the samples.
Test and result
A closed-loop error-correction learning workflow guided synthesis of PbSe alloyed with SnSb and documented a previously unexplored low-temperature thermoelectric family.
Limitation / still unknown
The result is specific to the tested powder-processing, composition and measurement protocol, including synthesis below 300 °C; it is not evidence of device-level or commercial performance. The reported mechanism for the increased power factor is supported by DFT calculations, not a direct measurement of carrier effective mass. The paper compares the 2 wt% composition with PbSe and reports more than 2× power factor at room temperature.

THESEUS derives resource-efficient designs for high-dimensional photonic GHZ and heralded Bell states

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Technology: THESEUS

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The authors’ AI-enabled automated design algorithm used an interpretable weighted, colored-graph representation of photonic experiments, gradient-based optimization, and topology reduction to search open experiment-design problems and expose generalizable physical concepts. The authors identify the work as explainable AI; the contribution is the algorithmically found design/concept, not merely faster benchmarking.
Test and result
The interpretable THESEUS design algorithm produced a three-dimensional six-photon GHZ-state blueprint that suppresses unwanted state terms toward arbitrarily high fidelity, with lower count rates, and a heralded three-dimensional Bell-state blueprint using destructive interference to cancel vacuum contributions.
Limitation / still unknown
These are theoretically calculated experiment blueprints, not experimentally demonstrated states in this paper. For the six-photon GHZ design, unwanted amplitudes can be reduced arbitrarily by adjusting graph weights, at the cost of lower count rates; feasibility is argued for contemporary photonics. The heralded Bell design’s estimated count rate and fidelity are modeled, not measured.
