Machine learning prioritizes a protective Arabidopsis leaf fungus that suppresses oomycete infection

Four classifiers (random forest, support-vector machine, logistic regression and multilayer perceptron) used six years of natural Arabidopsis leaf-microbiome data to prioritize taxa associated with Albugo infection status. Follow-up plant assays found that selected health-associated microbes reduced infection; Cystofilobasidium macerans OTU126 gave the strongest individual protection, reducing Albugo infection by an average of 73%. In a 15-member synthetic leaf community, adding this fungus increased the reported infection reduction to 40.25%, compared with 19.36% for the community alone.

Technology: Random forest, support-vector machine, logistic regression and multilayer perceptron classifiers with cross-validated feature selection

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Follow-up plant assays found that selected health-associated microbes reduced infection; Cystofilobasidium macerans OTU126 gave the strongest individual protection, reducing Albugo infection by an average of 73%.
  • Still unknown: This is a controlled Arabidopsis–Albugo plant-pathogen model, not a field crop efficacy trial. ML features are associations with infection status; they do not establish the microbes caused naturally observed health status. Isolates were matched to predicted OTUs using marker sequences, which the authors note do not fully establish strain-level functional identity.   • AI role: Models classified infected versus uninfected plants from microbial community profiles and prioritized disease-associated and health-associated taxa for follow-up. Researchers then tested selected cultured isolates in planta and tested Cystofilobasidium in a synthetic community.

19 Feb 2026University of Tübingen; Max Planck Institute for the Science of Light; University of Toronto; University of California, Berkeley; Stanford University; xAI

Language model discovers general construction rules for two previously unknown families of quantum experiments

A sequence-to-sequence transformer generated readable Python meta-programs that construct photonic experimental blueprints for entire families of quantum states, rather than only individual target states. In the final paper, two successful generalizations were previously unknown: a photonic construction for general spin-1/2 states with no adjacent spin-ups, and one for states of the Majumdar–Ghosh model. The generated programs matched states beyond the three examples used as input, providing computational evidence of the inferred rules.

Technology: Transformer-based sequence-to-sequence language model trained on synthetic quantum-state and experimental-blueprint pairs

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A sequence-to-sequence transformer generated readable Python meta-programs that construct photonic experimental blueprints for entire families of quantum states, rather than only individual target states.
  • Still unknown: This is a computational design result, not a laboratory realization of the proposed photonic experiments. Generalization was tested by checking generated states at finite larger sizes; the authors state that arbitrary code equivalence for all system sizes is not straightforward to prove. The two constructions are counted as one paper-level discovery.
  • AI role: Generated human-readable Python meta-programs from short sequences of target quantum states; the model’s programs proposed reusable experimental construction rules for whole state families, which the authors checked by simulating output states at larger system sizes.
