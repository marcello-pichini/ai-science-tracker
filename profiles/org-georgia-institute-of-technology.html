29 Oct 2026Georgia Institute of Technology; Emory University; The Wallace H. Coulter Department of Biomedical Engineering

Deep learning of lymphocyte architecture on H&E slides predicts colon cancer outcome

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: CoATIL, a computational pathology framework, quantifies tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte spatial organization from routine H&E whole-slide images in 461 patients with microsatellite-stable or MSI-low colon adenocarcinoma. Trained on 140 patients and validated internally (n = 52) and externally in TCGA (n = 130) and a Guangdong hospital (n = 139), it was associated with progression-free survival (HR 1.32 per standard deviation, 95% CI 1.13-1.55). It was compared with a clinical model and eight foundation-model approaches; the study is retrospective and the authors state that prospective validation is warranted.

20 Oct 2026Georgia Institute of Technology

Learned bubble reconstruction shows focus-dependent distribution agreement

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A convolutional multitask model trained on simulated optical images was applied without experimental fine-tuning to bubble shadowgraphs at four radial planes. Reconstructed size distributions overlapped in-focus experimental references by approximately 68–92%, with poorer agreement farther from the focal reference. This is a configuration-specific population measurement validation; experimental three-dimensional coordinates were not independently known for each bubble.

Technology: Focus-conditioned convolutional multitask neural network

Editorial relevance 33 physics PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A learned convolutional appearance representation, conditioned on focal-plane position, jointly infers size and depth from optical signatures and enables the scoped distribution-level reference comparison.
Test and result
The model jointly predicts depth relative to the known focal plane and physical-equivalent diameter. Experimental populations contained 142, 971, 859 and 204 reconstructed bubbles; distributions were compared with plane-specific in-focus references. A separate 11,679-bubble center-plane reference comparison is reported.
Limitation / still unknown
The 97.6% normalized depth accuracy belongs to synthetic data, not experimental depth truth. Model training describes a randomized crop development split alongside a timestamp-separated test; these partitions should not be conflated. Experimental references are distribution-level and share the imaging facility. Center-reference comparisons use differing summaries (approximately 92% versus 86% overlap); no single pooled value is inferred. Optical configuration, segmentation, overlap and defocus limit transfer; no general three-dimensional accuracy or improved flow process was established.

29 Jul 2024Georgia Institute of Technology; University of Pittsburgh; University of California, Santa Barbara

MODIFY-guided library screening identifies a cytochrome c variant active in both carbon–boron and carbon–silicon bond formation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers applied MODIFY, an ensemble model combining protein language models and sequence-density models, to design a diverse high-fitness library for Rhodothermus marinus cytochrome c. Experimental screening identified variant MLYPPT, which catalyzed both non-natural borylation and silylation: reported total turnover numbers were 2,880 and 2,740, with enantiomeric ratios of 94:6 and 97:3, respectively. The same library yielded distinct high-performing variants for each reaction.

Technology: MODIFY (ML-optimized library design with improved fitness and diversity)

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MODIFY used evolutionary sequence models and protein language models for zero-shot fitness estimates, then co-optimized predicted fitness and residue-level diversity to propose a 1,000-variant library. Researchers synthesized and assayed sampled library members; the model-guided library contained experimentally measured new cytochrome c variants, including MLYPPT with activity in both reactions.
Test and result
Experimental screening identified variant MLYPPT, which catalyzed both non-natural borylation and silylation: reported total turnover numbers were 2,880 and 2,740, with enantiomeric ratios of 94:6 and 97:3, respectively.
Limitation / still unknown
The C–B and C–Si reactions had previously been demonstrated with separately evolved cytochrome c variants; this finding is the discovery of additional, structurally distinct variants and a generalist MLYPPT catalyst. The reported yields and enantiomeric ratios are from the study's assays; industrial scale-up was not shown.

19 Jul 2024Georgia Institute of Technology; and University of Connecticut

AI-guided polyVERSE discovery yields a high-temperature dielectric polymer with 8.3 J cm⁻³ energy density

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The polyVERSE workflow used reaction-aware polymer generation and machine-learning property screens to identify PONB-2Me5Cl, which the researchers synthesized and cast into films. Experimental characterization measured a discharged energy density of 8.3 J cm⁻³ at 200 °C and a breakdown field of 750 MV m⁻¹; the paper compares this energy density with commercial polymer dielectrics at the same temperature. This is a laboratory polymer-film result, not a packaged capacitor or aerospace-ready device.

Technology: polyVERSE with four polymer graph neural-network property predictors

29 Mar 2024Georgia Institute of Technology; University of Science and Technology of China; North Carolina State University; Chinese Academy of Sciences

Deep-learning-enhanced Sparta earthquake catalog resolves a shallow multifault rupture

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: For the 2020 Mw 5.1 Sparta, North Carolina sequence, an EQTransformer-based catalog combined with matched-filter detections and differential-time relocations contains 1,761 high-precision events, about 5.8 times the original NEIC count. The denser locations resolve several shallow clusters and support the authors’ interpretation that the mainshock nucleated near the intersection of a blind strike-slip fault and a reverse fault associated with the surface rupture; moment-tensor inversion places its centroid at about 1.3 km depth.

Technology: Earthquake Transformer (EQTransformer) deep-learning phase/event detector, followed by matched-filter detection and differential-time relocation

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
EQTransformer detections expanded the template catalog, which seeded matched-filter detection; the resulting event set was relocated to improve temporal and spatial resolution used to infer the sequence’s shallow fault geometry. The fault interpretation is author analysis from the denser catalog plus waveform and moment-tensor methods, not a model-generated classification alone.
Test and result
For the 2020 Mw 5.1 Sparta, North Carolina sequence, an EQTransformer-based catalog combined with matched-filter detections and differential-time relocations contains 1,761 high-precision events, about 5.8 times the original NEIC count.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a retrospective study of one 2020 earthquake sequence, using a local temporary-network deployment. The authors describe the inferred fault intersection/flower-structure interpretation as possible; the fit for the smallest cluster is poor and its geometry uncertain. The result is not a forecast or general fault model. The AI contribution was event detection within a hybrid workflow, not independent geological validation.

22 Jan 2021Georgia Institute of Technology

Transformer-classified reviews map regional patterns in EV availability discussions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: BERT and XLNet classified topics in an archive of 127,257 U.S. charging-station reviews. Spatial analysis placed relatively frequent availability discussions in smaller communities in parts of the West and Midwest, with different patterns in the South and Northeast. The result concerns review-topic frequency, not independently measured charging shortages.

Technology: BERT and XLNet supervised topic classification

Editorial relevance 33 humanities Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned language classifiers scaled manual topic labels across the review corpus, enabling the source-specific spatial analysis. Conventional mapping summarized those learned labels; predicted discussion is kept distinct from observed infrastructure conditions.
Test and result
The 2011–2015 archive covered 12,720 charging locations and 29,532 users. Human-designed topics were learned from expert-annotated reviews; ten cross-validation runs gave overall accuracy 91.6% and F1 .83/.84 for BERT/XLNet. Predicted topic frequencies were mapped using 2013 metropolitan/micropolitan boundaries and standardized separately into regional quantiles. The source describes smaller-community availability discussion patterns in parts of the West/Midwest and more major-metropolitan patterns in the South/Northeast.
Limitation / still unknown
Availability is a broad discussion topic encompassing whether stations are available; frequent discussion is not a direct estimate of shortage, station failure or unmet demand. Regional quantile normalization prevents treating the map as a common absolute availability scale. Reviews appear only when users post, do not contain charging transactions and cannot establish population-wide access or causal infrastructure effects. The data were reanalyzed from an existing archive, and topics were manually specified rather than discovered unsupervised. Annotator experts were trained students, not industry professionals; the 100-review audited comparison does not establish universal superhuman annotation. Full supplemental training partitions and spatial counts were not reviewed, so geographic unit-count claims are not reproduced. This represents the January 2021 journal version, despite the 2020 DOI token.
