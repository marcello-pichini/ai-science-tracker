26 Oct 2026The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Macao Polytechnic University; The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Language models missed more requirements with little wording in common with the final request

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 405 CL-bench Life tasks, direct answers from four language models passed 67.4% of rubric criteria with low wording overlap with the final request, compared with 81.1% of higher-overlap criteria. The analysis uses word overlap as a proxy and does not establish that wording alone caused the difference.

Technology: GPT-6 Luna; Kimi K3; Grok 4.7; GLM-5

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Four frozen language models generated corpus-task answers. A rubric judge scored outputs from the same benchmark before testing the proposed harness.
Test and result
The study grouped 5,333 classifiable rubric criteria by overlap with the final request. The evaluators assessed individual outputs under the benchmark rubric.
Limitation / still unknown
Groups can differ in underlying difficulty and scoring criteria. Rubrics are nested within tasks, and the result does not establish effects in live workflows.

21 Oct 2026The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications

Matching final answers masked different forgetting patterns during fine-tuning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers recovered 100% final performance on an old generation task across several replay budgets for a Qwen model. The same training runs nevertheless followed different trajectories in the probability assigned to correct old answers. The finding came from evaluating checkpoints throughout training as well as the final model.

Technology: Qwen and Mistral language-model fine-tuning

Family Qwen: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Fine-tuned Qwen and Mistral models enabled direct measurement of task outputs and loss at multiple checkpoints under matched training protocols.
Test and result
The study varied old-task replay budgets at matched training settings and separately measured old-task generation at the endpoint and negative log-likelihood across training checkpoints.
Limitation / still unknown
The result is specific to these models, old-task response tests and replay schedules. Checkpoint behavior cannot establish whether knowledge was removed or merely harder to elicit. The qualified original Qwen retained-response bank contained only 17 examples; its 100% endpoint is not broad preservation of old-task knowledge.

26 Jan 2026The Chinese University of Hong Kong

Machine-learning seismic catalogue reveals a long-period swarm before Sierra Negra eruption onset

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning phase-picking and template-matching workflow expanded the catalogue for the 2018 Sierra Negra eruption onset, identifying a northwestern-caldera swarm of long-period microearthquakes missing from earlier manually picked records. The authors report the swarm began about two hours after a Mw 5.4 intra-caldera earthquake and preceded edifice failure and magma intrusion. The proposed fluid-triggering sequence is an interpretation of retrospective seismic and geodetic observations, not a prospective eruption forecast.

Technology: Volpick EQTransformer-based seismic phase picker combined with template matching and geodetic analysis

Editorial relevance 53 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The learned EQTransformer-based phase picker supported a denser event catalogue; combined with template matching, this exposed a previously unreported long-period swarm in the archived seismic record.

The authors report 1,224 volcano-tectonic and 711 long-period events and identify a long-period swarm missing from the earlier manually picked catalogue, beginning about two hours after the Mw 5.4 earthquake.

8 Apr 2024The Chinese University of Hong Kong; WSL Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research; Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Sichuan Earthquake Administration; Géoazur

AI-assisted seismic catalog links the 2018 Baige landslide-dammed lakes to increased local earthquakes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An earthquake catalog built with the PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector and cross-correlation/template matching revealed a statistically significant (over 99%) increase in small earthquakes as the second Baige landslide-dammed lake approached peak water level; rates returned toward background after breach. Coulomb-stress modeling found about 90% of the earthquakes in regions of positive modeled stress change, supporting likely triggering through direct water loading and pore-pressure diffusion. The paper presents this as evidence that earthquake–surface-hazard triggering can be bidirectional.

Technology: PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector, combined with PyOcto association and cross-correlation/template-matching methods

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
PhaseNet detected P- and S-wave arrivals in continuous seismic records; these detections supported a new 2014–2023 earthquake catalog used to identify and statistically test the local seismicity increase. Coulomb-stress modeling then assessed a possible physical mechanism.
Test and result
An earthquake catalog built with the PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector and cross-correlation/template matching revealed a statistically significant (over 99%) increase in small earthquakes as the second Baige landslide-dammed lake approached peak water level; rates returned toward background after breach.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a case study of one pair of 2018 landslide-dammed lakes and nearby faults. Event-location uncertainties are several kilometers; fault geometry and fluid-diffusion parameters require assumptions. The authors conclude the lakes likely triggered the sequence, rather than directly observing causality, and the global implication is modeled potential.

6 Jun 2022The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Chinese Academy of Sciences; Qingdao National Laboratory for Marine Science and Technology; Southern University of Science and Technology; Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution

EQTransformer-based catalog reveals a 50-km-deep outer-rise fault at the Southern Mariana trench

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Applying EQTransformer phase detection to near-field ocean-bottom seismometer recordings collected near the Challenger Deep yielded more than 1,975 earthquakes. Relocation and waveform-similarity clustering outlined a southern Mariana outer-rise fault extending approximately 20–50 km below the seafloor (about 50 km maximum depth), inferred to be a reactivated normal fault; the depth suggests more incoming-plate water input than prior estimates.

Technology: EQTransformer machine-learning seismic phase detector

Editorial relevance 53 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Detected and picked P- and S-wave phases in near-field ocean-bottom seismometer data, producing a denser earthquake catalog. Researchers manually reviewed selected phases, relocated the events and used waveform-similarity clustering to delineate the fault.
Test and result
Applying EQTransformer phase detection to near-field ocean-bottom seismometer recordings collected near the Challenger Deep yielded more than 1,975 earthquakes.
Limitation / still unknown
The fault depth, fault type and hydration implications are inferred from a localized seismic cluster, waveform similarity, and regional tomography; focal mechanisms could not be robustly resolved, and the authors state that limited station coverage does not establish whether such deep faults are widespread.

1 Nov 2021The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Sichuan Earthquake Agency

High-resolution machine-learning catalog outlines a potential unmapped fault in the Weiyuan shale-gas region

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning event detector combined with waveform cross-correlation produced a high-resolution catalog for injection-induced seismicity in the Weiyuan area, identifying one-third more earthquakes than the routine catalog and improving magnitude completeness. Spatial migration patterns show a pore-pressure diffusion front; a cluster outlines a potential unmapped fault that may have hosted the region’s largest recorded event, the September 2019 Mw 5.2 earthquake.

Technology: Machine-learning earthquake detector combined with waveform cross-correlation (the abstract does not name a particular detector architecture)

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Detected additional earthquakes that enabled higher-resolution relocation and spatiotemporal mapping; authors use the denser catalog to infer a migrating pore-pressure front and identify a cluster outlining a potential fault trace.
Test and result
A machine-learning event detector combined with waveform cross-correlation produced a high-resolution catalog for injection-induced seismicity in the Weiyuan area, identifying one-third more earthquakes than the routine catalog and improving magnitude completeness.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors describe the fault as potential and state that it may have led to the September 2019 Mw 5.2 event; this is an interpretation, not a directly imaged fault or established causal proof. Results are from one field area and are limited by the station coverage.
