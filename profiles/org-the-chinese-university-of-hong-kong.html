8 Apr 2024The Chinese University of Hong Kong; WSL Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research; Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Sichuan Earthquake Administration; Géoazur

AI-assisted seismic catalog links the 2018 Baige landslide-dammed lakes to increased local earthquakes

An earthquake catalog built with the PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector and cross-correlation/template matching revealed a statistically significant (over 99%) increase in small earthquakes as the second Baige landslide-dammed lake approached peak water level; rates returned toward background after breach. Coulomb-stress modeling found about 90% of the earthquakes in regions of positive modeled stress change, supporting likely triggering through direct water loading and pore-pressure diffusion. The paper presents this as evidence that earthquake–surface-hazard triggering can be bidirectional.

Technology: PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector, combined with PyOcto association and cross-correlation/template-matching methods

6 Jun 2022The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Chinese Academy of Sciences; Qingdao National Laboratory for Marine Science and Technology; Southern University of Science and Technology; Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution

EQTransformer-based catalog reveals a 50-km-deep outer-rise fault at the Southern Mariana trench

Applying EQTransformer phase detection to near-field ocean-bottom seismometer recordings collected near the Challenger Deep yielded more than 1,975 earthquakes. Relocation and waveform-similarity clustering outlined a southern Mariana outer-rise fault extending approximately 20–50 km below the seafloor (about 50 km maximum depth), inferred to be a reactivated normal fault; the depth suggests more incoming-plate water input than prior estimates.

Technology: EQTransformer machine-learning seismic phase detector

1 Nov 2021The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Sichuan Earthquake Agency

High-resolution machine-learning catalog outlines a potential unmapped fault in the Weiyuan shale-gas region

A machine-learning event detector combined with waveform cross-correlation produced a high-resolution catalog for injection-induced seismicity in the Weiyuan area, identifying one-third more earthquakes than the routine catalog and improving magnitude completeness. Spatial migration patterns show a pore-pressure diffusion front; a cluster outlines a potential unmapped fault that may have hosted the region’s largest recorded event, the September 2019 Mw 5.2 earthquake.

Technology: Machine-learning earthquake detector combined with waveform cross-correlation (the abstract does not name a particular detector architecture)
