12 May 2026Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine; Sichuan University; Naval Medical University; Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College

MISPOP machine learning screening identifies the oncolytic peptide Dermaseptin-S9

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The MISPOP ensemble screened a natural peptide library of 1,033 sequences, prioritized 16 oncolytic-peptide candidates and selected five for synthesis and testing. Dermaseptin-S9 showed membrane disruption and immunogenic-cell-death readouts in vitro and over 92% tumor-growth inhibition in a B16F10 melanoma mouse model.

Technology: MISPOP mechanism-informed ensemble combining XGBoost, deep neural networks and transfer learning with physicochemical priors

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The machine-learning ensemble integrated three predictors and mechanism-related physicochemical features to prioritize candidate oncolytic peptides from a natural sequence library. Five shortlisted peptides were synthesized and evaluated; Dermaseptin-S9 was tested in cell-based membrane-disruption/ICD assays and a mouse melanoma model.
Test and result

Limitation / still unknown

Only five of 16 model-prioritized candidates were synthesized and evaluated.

29 Nov 2024Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine; MedMind Technology; Tsinghua University

Learned synthetic CT enables physically tested lung-radiotherapy dose planning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A UNet++ model converted cone-beam scans into synthetic CT images for lung-cancer radiotherapy planning. Plans based on 17 patients’ images were compared with planning-CT references and checked through actual phantom irradiation. Measured dose deviations were 2–7%, comparable to the reference-plan deviations in that experiment. This supports a bounded medical-physics feasibility result, not formal dosimetric equivalence, improved patient outcomes or demonstrated adaptive treatment delivery.

Technology: UNet++ with ResNet50 backbone for synthetic CT generation

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
UNet++ learns the image-to-image conversion needed to reduce cone-beam CT intensity errors before conventional dose calculation. The generated-image plans, rather than only image-quality scores, are tested against physical dose measurements.
Test and result
Model development used 70 patients and image evaluation 15;17 additional patients supplied paired cone-beam/planning CT scans for offline planning comparisons. Generated-image plans underwent physical ArcCHECK and thermoluminescent-dose measurements on phantoms, with 2–7% dose deviations reported.
Limitation / still unknown
The clinical images and plan comparison were retrospective; phantom irradiation does not establish actual adaptive patient-treatment benefit. Nonsignificant dose differences do not prove equivalence. The tumor-volume gradient index differed, and some tumor-dose metrics had weaker agreement. The source’s percentage image-MAE improvement is inconsistent with its displayed means and is not reproduced here. Validation is limited to the studied scanner/workflow and geometry.
