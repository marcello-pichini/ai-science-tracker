23 Aug 2026Chinese Academy of Sciences; University of Chinese Academy of Sciences

Deep learning maps six high-potential deep-Earth scattering zones

Researchers used deep learning with iterative manual optimization to identify 174,929 PKP precursors in more than two million earthquake waveforms recorded from 1990 to 2024. The expanded global map connected previously isolated sampling regions and highlighted six high-potential scattering zones near the core–mantle boundary that the authors report had not previously been documented. These are model-inferred targets for later seismic imaging, not fully characterized structures.

Technology: Deep-learning PKP precursor detector with human iterative optimization

18 Jun 2026Chinese Academy of Sciences

Deep learning guides higher-activity SpuFz1 nuclease variants

A DeepSequence-based predictor ranked SpuFz1 mutations without experimental activity labels; experimentally confirmed substitutions were combined into SpuFz1-v3. At the B2M locus in HEK293T cells, the article reports 35.52% indel formation versus 3.07% for wild type. Comparative sequence analysis, not this AI model, guided the separate ultrashort RNA design. The reported activity is assay- and locus-specific; off-target and clinical safety are not established.

Technology: DeepSequence-based Fanzor-Fitness Predictor

3 Apr 2025Chinese Academy of Sciences; Stem Cell and Brain Research Institute, Inserm; BGI-Research; Tencent AI for Life Sciences Lab

AI-assisted macaque claustrum atlas links cell types with projection zones

Learned segmentation and Spatial-ID annotation helped connect macaque claustral cell-type distributions with four projection zones identified through tracer experiments. Different glutamatergic compositions preferentially connected to different brain regions. This is the April 3, 2025 journal version, preceded by a related 2024 conference abstract; comparisons reuse published macaque-cortex data, and the findings do not establish a causal account of consciousness.

Technology: Mask R-CNN; Spatial-ID; nnUNet

Machine learning-guided amidase engineering improves enantioselectivity

Random-forest models using chemistry and geometry descriptors were trained on 240 prior amidase datasets and used to guide substrate and variant selection. The primary article reports that variant I198F/W328F achieved an enantioselectivity E-value of 303.5, compared with 5.7 for wild type, for the reported substrate assay. Most experimental substrate predictions agreed, with identified exceptions. This bounded laboratory result does not establish broad performance across substrates or industrial application.

Technology: Random-forest classification models and descriptor-guided variant design

8 Jul 2024Chinese Academy of Sciences; University of Chinese Academy of Sciences; Macau University of Science and Technology; University of California, Davis; Queen’s University

Machine learning identifies sediment-derived granites in the Hadean zircon record, supporting early subduction

A machine-learning classifier trained on trace-element fingerprints identified detrital zircon signatures from sediment-derived S-type granites as old as 4.24 billion years in the Jack Hills record. Across global detrital zircon data, the authors report long-term cyclic changes in S-type granite proportions and infer that exposed, weathered continental crust and subduction operated by about 4.2 Ga. The result bears on early Earth geodynamics, not a direct fossil or biological discovery.

Technology: Trace-element Support Vector Machine (TSVM) classifier

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A machine-learning classifier trained on trace-element fingerprints identified detrital zircon signatures from sediment-derived S-type granites as old as 4.24 billion years in the Jack Hills record.
  • Still unknown: The early-subduction interpretation is inferred from zircon trace elements and remains one interpretation of sparse Hadean geological evidence. The article reports that only the combination of early subaerial weathering and subduction explains the prevalence of the inferred S-type granites; it does not directly observe tectonics at 4.2 Ga.
  • AI role: Classified zircon as S-type or non-S-type from multielement geochemical fingerprints, allowing the authors to identify the oldest sediment-derived granite signal and quantify its temporal distribution across detrital-zircon records.

18 Jan 2024Chinese Academy of Sciences; Zhejiang Ocean University; collaborators

Deep learning reveals a new seismicity distribution along the Kabaw Fault in central Myanmar

A deep-learning-based catalog pipeline processed 18 months of seismic-array data and located 1,891 shallow earthquakes, more than twice the 849 events in the prior manual catalog. The denser catalog shows that shallow Kabaw Fault seismicity disappears south of about 22.8°N while deeper events occur west of the fault, and it fills part of a previously under-sampled seismicity gap; the authors interpret this contrast as potentially reflecting along-strike variation in deformation associated with oblique Indian-plate subduction.

Technology: DEPALORE pipeline using Earthquake Transformer, transfer-learned PickNet, REAL association, NonLinLoc location and HypoDD relocation

AI-assisted macaque cortex atlas reveals regional cell-type organization

AI-assisted segmentation and Spatial-ID annotation helped map 264 transcriptome-defined cell types across the macaque cortex. Regional cell-type composition tracked cortical hierarchy, and cross-species comparisons identified primate-specific types enriched in layer 4. This is the July 12, 2023 journal version; the atlas is observational, uses male monkeys, and has limited transcript capture per spatial cell.

Technology: AI-assisted cell segmentation; Spatial-ID (deep neural network and graph convolutional network)

27 Jun 2023Chinese Academy of Sciences; Qi Biodesign; BGI-Qingdao; Aarhus University

AlphaFold-assisted structural clustering discovers new deaminase functions and compact base-editing enzymes

The team used AlphaFold2 structure predictions to cluster the deaminase family and found that most proteins in a DddA-like clade were not double-stranded DNA deaminases. The work characterized smaller single-strand-specific deaminases, developed a compact cytidine deaminase suitable for packaging a base editor into one AAV, and demonstrated robust editing by a clade member in soybean.

Technology: AlphaFold2-assisted structure-based protein-family clustering
