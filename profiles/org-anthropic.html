4 Nov 2026Anthropic

First 3D structure of a prokaryotic vault particle, with Claude

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Claude searched protein databases, designed DNA constructs, reduced purification from about three days to about 90 minutes, and processed cryo-EM data into an initial model in 34 hours. The result is a particle made of 96 copies of a bacteriophage protein, with about 25% more internal volume than the mammalian version. Its biological function is unknown; this is a preprint, and Claude made errors that humans corrected.

20 Oct 2026Anthropic

Claude designs binders against five scorpion neurotoxins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a mostly autonomous 64-hour campaign, Claude designed minibinders against five neurotoxins. External labs tested 211 designs, 17 of which met the criteria; the best (Cn2-B1 and Cn2-B2) had affinities in the low nanomolar range. Only recombinant toxins were tested, with no neutralization tests in cells or animals; Anthropic does not claim to have produced an antivenom.

Technology: Claude

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Claude selected toxin structures and hotspots, generated 318,484 designs, filtered and co-folded them, ran about 24 hours autonomously, then produced 180 selected designs after human follow-ups.
Test and result
External labs tested 211 designs by surface plasmon resonance: Adaptyv Bio in cell-free expression and Twist Bioscience in human kidney cells; a binder needed KD of 1 µM or tighter in every replicate at both labs and no CLEC12A signal, giving 17 binders. The two collaborating external laboratories tested candidates from the discovery project; this establishes direct testing rather than a separately established independent follow-up replication.
Limitation / still unknown
Only recombinant toxins were tested, with no neutralization in cells or animals; the authors do not know whether the binders neutralize venom and do not claim an antivenom; multitoxin designs failed.

16 Oct 2026Anthropic

Claude designs more efficient quantum circuits

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Claude designed circuits for eight basic operations (chemistry, optimization, physical simulation, and number theory), assessed by an automated verifier. In several cases they outperform the best published circuits; for example, a point addition used 17 times fewer gates at the cost of 12% more qubits. Key identities were checked with simulation and Lean; a memory shortcut in the decoder fails about once in 10,000 times.

Technology: Claude

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Claude wrote circuits as Python code and revised them in a closed loop with evaluator feedback, with no human input between rounds, and reconstructed branch information to reduce memory.
Test and result
An automated evaluator checked each circuit for correctness and scored qubits times gates; results were compared with published designs, divsteps was checked by simulation and Lean, and decoder memory was measured over 403 million simulated decodings.
Limitation / still unknown
The decoder memory shortcut fails about once in 10,000 runs; gains apply to individual primitives, not a full fault-tolerant machine; Claude was conservative and needed prompts to broaden its search.

5 Oct 2026Anthropic; Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Claude discovers algorithms that refute the 3SUM and APSP time-complexity hypotheses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents. The single underlying technique computes selected entries of thin matrix products more efficiently, and known reductions yield the main algorithmic consequences. The paper says an internal Anthropic Claude research model discovered the algorithm autonomously; the human authors then understood, simplified, strengthened and extended it. One paper-level theoretical-computer-science result is counted.

Technology: Internal Anthropic Claude research model (specific model version undisclosed)

Publication: Preprint · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The paper states that Claude was tasked with checking and improving cryptographic constructions but instead developed the new thin-matrix-product algorithm, first for average-case and then worst-case problems, in a 16-million-output-token session with no human input. The authors subsequently worked to understand, simplify, strengthen and extend the algorithm. Anthropic later used an internal model to Lean-certify specified main results.
Test and result
Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents.
Limitation / still unknown

Lean certification covers the explicitly enumerated theorems, not every statement or presentation detail in the 76-page paper. The authors take responsibility for the paper and developed important extensions/consequences after the initial AI-discovered algorithm.

4 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude formalizes Fermat's theorem in Lean

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A proof of Fermat's Last Theorem fully machine-verified in Lean: Claude produced most of it independently over 11 days, with about 13 million lines and 29,500 intermediate theorems, and a comparator confirms that its statement matches Mathlib's. It follows a simplified version of Wiles's proof, uses Mathlib and parts of an Imperial College project, and is probably longer than necessary.

Technology: Claude, Lean

Test and result
The proof was checked by Lean using only standard axioms; a comparator tool confirmed the theorem statement matches Mathlib's, and mathematician Kevin Buzzard reviewed it.

23 Mar 2026Anthropic

Theoretical physics with Claude: the Sudakov shoulder in the C parameter

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Physicist Matthew Schwartz (Harvard) guided Claude Opus 4.5 through a resummation calculation for e+e− collisions, producing a paper with a new factorization theorem and testable predictions against data in about two weeks. Schwartz checked every step; the model claimed checks it had not done and made errors.

Technology: Claude Opus 4.5

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Claude Opus 4.5, run through Claude Code, performed the calculations including the factorization derivation, numerical analysis, simulations and manuscript preparation, under physicist supervision.
Test and result
Results are validated against EVENT2 Monte Carlo simulations, with analytic curves matching the histograms; the paper does not describe a comparison with experimental data, and the author checked each step, with GPT and Gemini cross-checking the written calculations.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper does not describe comparison with experimental data (the author mentions a follow-up project for this); the author states that LLMs cannot yet do original theoretical physics autonomously and that the work needed expert evaluation.
