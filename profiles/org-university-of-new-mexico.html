Simulated training data locates archaeological tar-kiln-like sites in Louisiana, field check finds WWII features

Machine learning models trained on simulated landscape data flagged 9 of 12 known targets (model 1) and all 12 (model 2, with 686 false positives among 709 remaining predictions) in Kisatchie National Forest, Louisiana; a model trained on real South Carolina tar kilns found 11 of 12. Field visits to 11 targets and augering of 2 found none of the expected tar-kiln features, and the authors concluded the structures more likely relate to a WWII military training site. Evidence caveat: only 12 known targets, and field validation is qualitative.

Technology: Machine learning classifiers trained on simulated archaeological sites (with a real tar-kiln-trained comparison model)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Models trained on simulated objects flagged known tar-kiln-like targets, and 11 targets were visited in the field with 2 augered; no expected tar-kiln features were found, and the authors concluded the features are most likely associated with military training exercises.
  • Still unknown: Access to all targets was limited by dense understory, and further historical research is needed to confirm the function of the objects.
  • AI role: Mask R-CNN models trained on simulated lidar-embedded objects produced predictions, a rule-based script removed likely natural mounds, and human archaeologists reviewed and chose field targets.
