2 Nov 2026Northwestern Polytechnical University; International Joint Institute of Intelligent Fluid Mechanics; National Key Laboratory of Aircraft Configuration Design

Symbolic regression derives aircraft aerodynamic scaling across sharp-cone and blunt-body geometries

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR) analysis of hypersonic simulation data derived an interpretable non-power-product scaling relation for axial-force coefficient across sharp-cone and blunt-body configurations. It combines angle of attack, Mach number and Reynolds number through a dominant term and a viscous correction; the relation has a shared functional form but geometry-specific fitted constants. The authors interpret the constants as reflecting differences in shock/base drag, friction drag and pressure drag. This is a new model-derived scaling result from simulations, not a wind-tunnel or flight measurement.

Technology: Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR), a progressive symbolic-regression framework for dimensionless physical relations

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CoSR progressively searched for interpretable dimensionless groups and combined their relationships instead of fitting one end-to-end expression. Applied to hypersonic simulation data, it identified a dominant angle-of-attack/Mach term and a Reynolds-number-dependent viscous correction, then fitted geometry-specific constants for two body shapes. Earlier canonical-law and fluid examples in the same paper are framework validations and are not counted separately here.
Test and result
A Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR) analysis of hypersonic simulation data derived an interpretable non-power-product scaling relation for axial-force coefficient across sharp-cone and blunt-body configurations.
Limitation / still unknown
The aerodynamic relation was inferred from hypersonic simulation data for two configurations (sharp cone and blunt body), not independently measured flight or wind-tunnel data. It is a parameterized model over the analyzed configuration/data range; external validation and broader geometry coverage are not established in the source. The simpler dominant component is linked to known hypersonic invariance, while the learned viscous correction and geometry-specific scaling provide the paper’s new modeled relationship. Do not treat the authors’ drag-component interpretations as direct measurements.

26 Oct 2026Northwestern Polytechnical University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University

A vision-assisted drone tracked moving pipes in a physical test

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a physical moving-pipe test, a small drone using learned visual label detection and a model-predictive controller recorded 6.73% average tracking error, compared with 15.13% under a standard PID controller. The study compared control methods in a bounded experimental rig.

Technology: YOLOv5; learned visual label detection; model predictive control

10 Aug 2025Northwestern Polytechnical University; Pennsylvania State University

Symbolic-regression turbulence analysis separates transferable flow physics from dataset-specific closure terms

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using high-fidelity data for plane channels, zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layers and adverse-pressure-gradient boundary layers, the authors used symbolic regression to decompose learned turbulence-closure corrections into inner-layer, outer-layer and pressure-gradient components. They report that retaining the generalisable inner-layer term and adding flow-specific corrections improved predictions on held-out boundary-layer and aerofoil configurations, including unseen geometries and Reynolds numbers, outperforming baseline RANS closures in a priori and a posteriori tests. The authors identify conflation of transferable and training-specific flow behavior as a source of poor model generalization in the tested setting.

Technology: Symbolic regression applied to high-fidelity turbulence simulation data

Editorial relevance 47 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Symbolic regression generated interpretable closure expressions from progressively expanded flow datasets. Decomposition of the learned terms let the researchers distinguish inner-layer components that transferred across flow configurations from outer-layer and pressure-gradient corrections that were specific to flow classes, enabling improved out-of-training-flow predictions.
Test and result
Using high-fidelity data for plane channels, zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layers and adverse-pressure-gradient boundary layers, the authors used symbolic regression to decompose learned turbulence-closure corrections into inner-layer, outer-layer and pressure-gradient components.
Limitation / still unknown
The finding concerns model-derived closure components and validation on selected numerical flow configurations, not a new direct physical measurement. The claimed root cause of poor generalization is the authors' conclusion about data-driven turbulence closures in the studied context, not a universal causal theorem about all turbulence models. Out-of-sample tests and comparative RANS simulations support the stated transfer result.

Unsupervised learning guides experimental superalloy design

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A gradient-boosted regressor and unsupervised sampling workflow guided fabrication and differential-scanning-calorimetry measurements of nine superalloys selected outside the initial training compositions. Three alloys had gamma-prime solvus temperatures about 52 °C above the relevant comparators. This is a phase-solvus result, not a blade service-temperature or engine-performance result.
