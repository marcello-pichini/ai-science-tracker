Symbolic regression derives aircraft aerodynamic scaling across sharp-cone and blunt-body geometries

A Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR) analysis of hypersonic simulation data derived an interpretable non-power-product scaling relation for axial-force coefficient across sharp-cone and blunt-body configurations. It combines angle of attack, Mach number and Reynolds number through a dominant term and a viscous correction; the relation has a shared functional form but geometry-specific fitted constants. The authors interpret the constants as reflecting differences in shock/base drag, friction drag and pressure drag. This is a new model-derived scaling result from simulations, not a wind-tunnel or flight measurement.

Technology: Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR), a progressive symbolic-regression framework for dimensionless physical relations

10 Aug 2025Northwestern Polytechnical University; Pennsylvania State University

Symbolic-regression turbulence analysis separates transferable flow physics from dataset-specific closure terms

Using high-fidelity data for plane channels, zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layers and adverse-pressure-gradient boundary layers, the authors used symbolic regression to decompose learned turbulence-closure corrections into inner-layer, outer-layer and pressure-gradient components. They report that retaining the generalisable inner-layer term and adding flow-specific corrections improved predictions on held-out boundary-layer and aerofoil configurations, including unseen geometries and Reynolds numbers, outperforming baseline RANS closures in a priori and a posteriori tests. The authors identify conflation of transferable and training-specific flow behavior as a source of poor model generalization in the tested setting.

Technology: Symbolic regression applied to high-fidelity turbulence simulation data

The claimed root cause of poor generalization is the authors' conclusion about data-driven turbulence closures in the studied context, not a universal causal theorem about all turbulence models. Out-of-sample tests and comparative RANS simulations support the stated transfer result.
  • AI role: Symbolic regression generated interpretable closure expressions from progressively expanded flow datasets. Decomposition of the learned terms let the researchers distinguish inner-layer components that transferred across flow configurations from outer-layer and pressure-gradient corrections that were specific to flow classes, enabling improved out-of-training-flow predictions.
