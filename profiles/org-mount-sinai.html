19 May 2020Mount Sinai

AI and clinical data to identify COVID-19 from CT scans

In a test on 279 patients, a system combining CT scans and clinical information reached 84% sensitivity, compared with 75% for radiologists in the reported comparison. Retrospective study on data collected in China, without evidence of effectiveness in prospective clinical use.

Technology: Multimodal deep learning

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Trained on 626 of 905 patients and tested on the remaining 279 against radiologists: sensitivity 84% versus 75%, AUC 0.92, and 68% of RT-PCR-positive cases with normal CT correctly identified.
  • Still unknown: Intended for use when CT scans and clinical history are both available; CT alone may have limited negative predictive value; prospective clinical use not evaluated; specificity not reported.
  • AI role: Combined chest CT findings with clinical symptoms, exposure history and lab results to identify COVID-19-positive patients.
