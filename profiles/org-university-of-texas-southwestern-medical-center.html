6 Sep 2024University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center; Texas Heart Institute; Houston Methodist DeBakey Heart & Vascular Center; Duke University; National Heart Centre Singapore; University of Toronto; University of Colorado; Massachusetts General Hospital

Machine learning identifies a high-risk diabetic cardiomyopathy phenotype linked to future heart failure

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Unsupervised clustering of 25 echocardiographic and biomarker variables in 1,199 ARIC participants with diabetes identified three phenogroups; the group with the greatest cardiac remodelling and neurohormonal-stress abnormalities had 12.1% five-year heart-failure incidence, versus 4.6% and 3.1% in the other groups. A deep-neural-network classifier trained to recognize this phenotype identified corresponding high-risk groups in the external CHS cohort (n=802) and UT Southwestern EHR cohort (n=5,071); phenotype classification was associated with incident heart failure in both.

Technology: Unsupervised hierarchical clustering and a deep neural network (DeepNN) classifier, with random forest variable-importance analysis

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The authors used unsupervised hierarchical clustering of echocardiography and cardiac biomarkers to define outcome-associated diabetic-cardiomyopathy phenogroups, then trained and externally validated a DeepNN classifier to recognize the high-risk phenotype. Random forest permutation importance prioritized NT-proBNP, left-ventricular mass, Doppler measurements, left-atrial dimension and LV end-diastolic volume.
Test and result

Limitation / still unknown
The cohorts were observational; association with future heart failure does not establish that model-guided intervention improves outcomes. The phenotype is an operational study definition, not a universally accepted diagnostic threshold. The authors describe potential preventive use, but it was not tested. The cohorts were external validations rather than prospective deployment trials.
