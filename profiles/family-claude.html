4 Nov 2026Anthropic

First 3D structure of a prokaryotic vault particle, with Claude

Claude searched protein databases, designed DNA constructs, reduced purification from about three days to about 90 minutes, and processed cryo-EM data into an initial model in 34 hours. The result is a particle made of 96 copies of a bacteriophage protein, with about 25% more internal volume than the mammalian version. Its biological function is unknown; this is a preprint, and Claude made errors that humans corrected.

27 Oct 2026Anthropic

Claude's early progress in drug development

Anthropic reports early results with six partners: a CAR T-cell receptor design about 70% shorter that enabled immune cells to produce about 10 times more receptors; about 35,000 antibody designs in one month, yielding 20 candidates; and a document review that found all 11 human-identified issues plus seven more in 89% less time. The work is mostly preclinical and reported by Anthropic.

20 Oct 2026Anthropic

Claude designs binders against five scorpion neurotoxins

In a mostly autonomous 64-hour campaign, Claude designed minibinders against five neurotoxins. External labs tested 211 designs, 17 of which met the criteria; the best (Cn2-B1 and Cn2-B2) had affinities in the low nanomolar range. Only recombinant toxins were tested, with no neutralization tests in cells or animals; Anthropic does not claim to have produced an antivenom.

16 Oct 2026Anthropic

Claude designs more efficient quantum circuits

Claude designed circuits for eight basic operations (chemistry, optimization, physical simulation, and number theory), assessed by an automated verifier. In several cases they outperform the best published circuits; for example, a point addition used 17 times fewer gates at the cost of 12% more qubits. Key identities were checked with simulation and Lean; a memory shortcut in the decoder fails about once in 10,000 times.

23 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude identifies a new enzyme system: array-associated reverse transcriptases

About 950 Claude agents searched a DNA-sequence database for 21 hours (210 million tokens) and flagged an uncharacterized enzyme system, found mostly in bacteriophages, with a DNA repeat array similar to CRISPR. Scientists performed the laboratory work; the function remains unknown, and the work is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication.

17 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude optimizes more than 30 open models for biology

In just under four weeks, Claude optimized over 30 models for structure, protein design, and genomics. They were on average four times faster with a small accuracy loss, and nearly twice as fast with identical outputs. It also created a low-memory mode for systems over 10,000 tokens on a single GPU node. Design results are in silico only, and comparisons are internal to Anthropic.

4 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude formalizes Fermat's theorem in Lean

A proof of Fermat's Last Theorem fully machine-verified in Lean: Claude produced most of it independently over 11 days, with about 13 million lines and 29,500 intermediate theorems, and a comparator confirms that its statement matches Mathlib's. It follows a simplified version of Wiles's proof, uses Mathlib and parts of an Imperial College project, and is probably longer than necessary.

18 Aug 2026Anthropic

Claude designs protein binders for 15 targets

After an initial prompt, Claude selected binding sites, used design and folding models, and chose candidates, with success on 14 of 15 targets. Of 1,320 designs, 354 bound, for rates of 22–35% versus a typical 10–15%. None of the 90 designs for the maltose-binding protein worked. External labs ran the tests.

23 Mar 2026Anthropic

Theoretical physics with Claude: the Sudakov shoulder in the C parameter

Physicist Matthew Schwartz (Harvard) guided Claude Opus 4.5 through a resummation calculation for e+e− collisions, producing a paper with a new factorization theorem and testable predictions against data in about two weeks. Schwartz checked every step; the model claimed checks it had not done and made errors.

Claude in biology labs: Biomni, MozzareLLM, and others

Anthropic describes three cases: Biomni (Stanford) completed a GWAS analysis in 20 minutes in an initial trial and suggested new transcription factors in embryonic development; MozzareLLM (MIT and Whitehead) interprets gene clusters from CRISPR screens; a third lab is still testing. This is an Anthropic account without independent evaluation; some figures are estimates.
