7 Mar 2024Toyota Research Institute; Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory; University of California, Berkeley; North Carolina State University; Lucideon

AI- and DFT-guided synthesis identifies a previously unreported calcium-ruthenate phase

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An autonomous materials-discovery workflow used AI-feedback density-functional-theory simulations to select six previously well-explored ternary oxide systems for synthesis. In the Ca–Ru–O campaign, experiments yielded two previously unreported phases; room-temperature diffraction and follow-up characterization most clearly support an isolated low-temperature calcium-rich phase, approximately Ca1+xRuO3+y, distinct from conventional CaRuO3. The authors state the realized phases were not the specific crystal structures predicted by the simulation.

Technology: CAMD (computer-aided materials discovery), an AI-feedback density-functional-theory phase-search workflow

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CAMD mapped candidate phases across chemical systems and informed expert down-selection. Human researchers designed and ran synthesis and characterization. The successful calcium-ruthenate phases emerged experimentally in the AI-prioritized system but did not match the exact predicted crystal structures; authors describe one success among six systems and stress current AI-guided synthesis limitations.
Test and result
An autonomous materials-discovery workflow used AI-feedback density-functional-theory simulations to select six previously well-explored ternary oxide systems for synthesis.
Limitation / still unknown
One low-temperature phase was isolated and extensively characterized; the second high-temperature phase remained multiphase/metastable and structurally unresolved, so this record rests on the better-supported low-temperature result. Exact composition and location of excess Ca remain under investigation. AI/DFT guided chemical-system selection but did not correctly predict the realized phase structure; authors report only one successful system among six and do not claim AI accelerated the overall process.
