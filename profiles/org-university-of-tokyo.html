Random-forest analysis of continuous seismic waveforms identifies five previously unreported slow-slip events in western Shikoku

A random-forest regression model trained on seismic waveform features predicted GNSS-derived displacement-rate changes over roughly six years in western Shikoku, Japan. Comparing detections with earlier catalogs, the authors report five previously unreported short-term slow-slip events among 23 detected episodes; GNSS deformation patterns and fitted fault models support interpreting the transients as slow slip. The paper-level result is a retrospective detection method and event set, not prospective earthquake prediction.

Technology: Random-forest regression model
