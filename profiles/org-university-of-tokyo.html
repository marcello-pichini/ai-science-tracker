8 Jan 2026University of Tokyo; Tohoku University; National Research Institute for Earth Science and Disaster Resilience, Japan

Random-forest analysis of continuous seismic waveforms identifies five previously unreported slow-slip events in western Shikoku

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random-forest regression model trained on seismic waveform features predicted GNSS-derived displacement-rate changes over roughly six years in western Shikoku, Japan. Comparing detections with earlier catalogs, the authors report five previously unreported short-term slow-slip events among 23 detected episodes; GNSS deformation patterns and fitted fault models support interpreting the transients as slow slip. The paper-level result is a retrospective detection method and event set, not prospective earthquake prediction.

Technology: Random-forest regression model

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Mapped statistical features from continuous seismic waveforms to GNSS-derived displacement rates and flagged sharp predicted increases as possible short-term slow-slip events; authors then fitted fault models and compared deformation with existing event catalogs.
Test and result
A random-forest regression model trained on seismic waveform features predicted GNSS-derived displacement-rate changes over roughly six years in western Shikoku, Japan.
Limitation / still unknown
The study is retrospective and geographically limited to western Shikoku. Model-predicted rates can differ from observations; smoothing can make events appear longer, a practical threshold affects counts (raising it leaves two newly identified events), and the authors state that events without associated tremor may be missed. It does not establish a prospective earthquake-warning capability or a causal trigger for the event that followed the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake.

25 Sep 2023University of Tokyo

Graph neural network screen of 1.18 million CYTOP functional groups yields three polymer electrets with improved charge stability

Source check: Needs source check. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Mao et al. identified ionization potential as a descriptor of charging performance, used high-throughput calculations and graph neural networks to screen 1,176,591 functional groups on CYTOP, and synthesized three candidates as 15 micrometre films that held surface potentials above 3.1 kV in magnitude for over 1,500 h (extrapolated longevity 146 years at 80 C). Abstract-level review; baselines and measurement protocol not assessed, and the longevity figure is an estimate.

Technology: Graph neural network models with high-throughput computation

Test and result
