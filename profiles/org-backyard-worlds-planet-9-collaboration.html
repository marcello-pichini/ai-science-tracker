17 Oct 2024Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 collaboration; NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; California Institute of Technology/IPAC; Northern Arizona University

SMDET-selected ultracool-dwarf candidates include two objects with spectroscopically confirmed T-dwarf types

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The SMDET image-level neural network identified 118 previously uncatalogued high-proper-motion ultracool-dwarf candidates in WISE time-series images. Follow-up near-infrared spectra for two selected objects yielded T5 and T3 classifications, confirming their estimated spectral types within one subtype.

Technology: SMDET, a convolutional neural network for detecting faint moving sources in WISE time-series coadds

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Scanned WISE image sequences and prioritized faint, fast-moving source candidates; researchers cross-checked catalogs, estimated photometric types and distances, and acquired spectra for two objects to verify their T-dwarf classifications.
Test and result
The SMDET image-level neural network identified 118 previously uncatalogued high-proper-motion ultracool-dwarf candidates in WISE time-series images.
Limitation / still unknown
Most of the 118 objects remain candidates, and the study spectroscopically checks only two. Some candidates were also identified by citizen scientists, so the AI was part of a human/algorithm discovery process. The counted finding is limited to the two spectra and the candidate search result as one paper-level report.
