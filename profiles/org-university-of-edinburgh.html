20 Oct 2026University of Edinburgh; National Taiwan University

Audio and text share neurons that influence recognition in three models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In three audio-language models, researchers found neurons associated with comparable audio and text concepts. Adjusting their activity using text-derived information changed audio recognition more than matched random neurons in 42 of 72 model–attribute combinations. Disabling the identified neurons affected the target task before the other tested tasks in 10 of 12 model–task combinations. Effects differed across models and covered a limited set of acoustic attributes.

Technology: Qwen2.5-Omni; DeSTA2.5-Audio; Audio Flamingo 3

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The researchers located overlapping units in learned feed-forward activations, then altered or removed those activations to test effects on audio recognition.
Test and result
The authors evaluated four categories of acoustic recognition across three models, selected units on validation data and tested deactivation on separate recognition examples. They compared with random sets, unrelated recognition tasks and sets responsive to audio alone.
Limitation / still unknown
This result is scoped to the chosen models, labels and intervention definitions. Steering was weaker in DeSTA2.5-Audio, and the result does not establish a universal audio–text or human perceptual mechanism.

13 May 2026University of Edinburgh; and Cancer Research UK Scotland Centre

Machine learning prioritizes compounds active against heterogeneous glioblastoma stem-cell models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers trained cell-survival predictors on image-based screening data from six patient-derived glioblastoma stem-cell lines and screened more than 12,000 compounds. Experimental follow-up identified three compounds with activity across the GBM phenotype; HSP90 inhibitor XL-888 eliminated all six tested lines at nanomolar IC50 values, with analyses consistent with HSP90 inhibition and downstream client-protein disruption.

Technology: Machine-learning cell-survival predictors trained on high-throughput phenotypic screening

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
ML models trained on in-house imaging/compound-response data predicted cell survival and prioritized compounds from a >12,000-compound screen; experimental assays across patient-derived GBM stem-cell models validated three leads and tested mechanisms. Researchers performed the wet-lab work and mechanism analyses.
Test and result
Experimental follow-up identified three compounds with activity across the GBM phenotype; HSP90 inhibitor XL-888 eliminated all six tested lines at nanomolar IC50 values, with analyses consistent with HSP90 inhibition and downstream client-protein disruption.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an in-vitro lead-discovery study across six patient-derived cell lines, not clinical efficacy. XL-888 is an existing HSP90 inhibitor and its proposed development as a GBM lead needs medicinal-chemistry optimization, including improved blood–brain-barrier penetration; no patient outcomes are reported.

25 Nov 2025University of Edinburgh; British Geological Survey and University of Padua

Deep learning forecasts aftershock rates in seconds, comparable to ETAS

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Neural networks trained on earthquake catalogues from California, New Zealand, Italy, Japan and Greece forecast the number of aftershocks in the following 24 hours after magnitude 4+ earthquakes. Their quality is reported as comparable to the operational ETAS model, while producing results in seconds rather than hours or days. The abstract reports no numeric skill scores, so the comparison rests on the full paper.

Technology: Deep neural networks for spatiotemporal seismicity rate forecasting, benchmarked against ETAS

Editorial relevance 53 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep neural networks learned short-term spatiotemporal seismicity rates from high-resolution catalogues.
Test and result
Deep-learning seismicity forecasts were compared with an epidemic-type aftershock sequence benchmark and a persistence null hypothesis on Southern California and Italy test datasets; they matched ETAS at much lower processing time.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract says performance depends on training data that represent the properties of earthquake sequences.

2 Jan 2025University of Edinburgh; University of Lille; Heriot-Watt University; University of Southern Denmark; University of Dundee; University of Southern Denmark and collaborating UK Biobank institutions

Deep learning enables multi-site bone marrow adiposity genetics in UK Biobank

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Deep learning measured bone marrow fat fraction at four skeletal sites in UK Biobank MRI data; after quality control, 48,608 participants contributed to genome-wide analyses. In the white-participant meta-GWAS, the study reported 67, 147, 134 and 174 independent significant variants for femoral head, total hip, femoral diaphysis and spine, mapping to 54, 90, 43 and 100 genes, respectively. Analyses also identified site- and sex-specific genetic associations and added signals in multi-ancestry analyses.

Technology: Previously validated deep-learning models for MRI segmentation and bone marrow fat-fraction measurement

Editorial relevance 53 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The study applied deep-learning models to segment four skeletal regions and derive bone marrow fat-fraction phenotypes at UK Biobank scale. Those AI-derived measurements were the phenotypes used in the study's subsequent genetic association analyses; the paper's genetic findings are not themselves outputs of a generative model.
Test and result
Deep learning measured bone marrow fat fraction at four skeletal sites in UK Biobank MRI data; after quality control, 48,608 participants contributed to genome-wide analyses.
Limitation / still unknown
The GWAS, transcriptome-wide and colocalization results are statistical associations and candidate gene mappings, not proof of causal mechanisms. Ancestry subgroup sizes differ, and results do not establish clinical utility or disease prevention. The deep-learning measurement method was established in an earlier study; this record concerns the distinct population-scale phenotype and genetic findings. A 2025 author correction fixed a misspelling of AKNA as ANKA; it did not alter the reported analysis or findings.
