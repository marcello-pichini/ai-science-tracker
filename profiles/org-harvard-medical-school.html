24 Nov 2025Harvard Medical School

popEVE: diagnosing rare developmental disorders

Applied to about 30,000 patients with severe undiagnosed developmental disorders, it led to a diagnosis in about one-third of cases and identified variants in 123 previously unknown genes, 25 of which were independently confirmed by other labs. This is preliminary clinical use; further checks are needed before adoption, and the page gives no detailed metrics.

Technology: popEVE (variant-effect prediction model)

25 Sep 2024Harvard Medical School

TxGNN: repurposing existing drugs for rare diseases

The model covers about 8,000 drugs across 17,080 diseases and is nearly 50% better than competitors at identifying candidates and 35% more accurate on contraindications. It was tested on 1.2 million medical records and on three rare diseases excluded from training. Computational predictions only, with no laboratory or clinical confirmation.

Technology: TxGNN (graph neural network)

4 Sep 2024Harvard Medical School

CHIEF: detecting cancer and predicting survival from slides

Trained on 15 million patches and 60,000 slides and tested on more than 19,400 slides from 32 datasets and 24 hospitals: it detects cancer with about 94% accuracy across 15 datasets, predicts mutations in 54 genes with over 70% accuracy, and improves survival prediction by 8% across 17 institutions. Retrospective validation; not trained on rare diseases or premalignant tissue.

Technology: CHIEF (pathology foundation model)

11 Oct 2023Harvard Medical School and University of Oxford

EVEscape: predicting viral variants before they emerge

Starting from January 2020, the tool predicted SARS-CoV-2 mutations with accuracy similar to experimental antibody-binding assays and ranked their prevalence better; it generalizes to HIV and influenza. Retrospective validation; prospective performance is not reported.

Technology: EVEscape
