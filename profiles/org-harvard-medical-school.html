9 Nov 2026Harvard Medical School; Harvard College; New York Genome Center; Rush Alzheimer’s Disease Center, Rush University

TriSCOPE links cell-type-specific Alzheimer’s expression programs to inherited risk loci

TriSCOPE combined cell-level predictive modeling, donor-aware differential expression and pseudotime analysis across six single-nucleus RNA-sequencing datasets (about 4.2 million nuclei from 627 donors). It identified cell-type-specific predictively expressed genes that were often missed by univariate differential-expression analysis; conditional GWAS–eQTL colocalization supported 27 gene–cell-type pairs at Alzheimer’s risk loci, including pairs without significant differential expression. Selected gene signals and analytical branches were compared across independent cohorts.

Technology: TriSCOPE, using LightGBM-based multivariate predictive modeling integrated with differential expression and pseudotime analysis

24 Nov 2025Harvard Medical School

popEVE: diagnosing rare developmental disorders

Applied to about 30,000 patients with severe undiagnosed developmental disorders, it led to a diagnosis in about one-third of cases and identified variants in 123 previously unknown genes, 25 of which were independently confirmed by other labs. This is preliminary clinical use; further checks are needed before adoption, and the page gives no detailed metrics.

Technology: popEVE (variant-effect prediction model)

25 Sep 2024Harvard Medical School

TxGNN: repurposing existing drugs for rare diseases

The model covers about 8,000 drugs across 17,080 diseases and is nearly 50% better than competitors at identifying candidates and 35% more accurate on contraindications. It was tested on 1.2 million medical records and on three rare diseases excluded from training. Computational predictions only, with no laboratory or clinical confirmation.

Technology: TxGNN (graph neural network)

4 Sep 2024Harvard Medical School

CHIEF: detecting cancer and predicting survival from slides

Trained on 15 million patches and 60,000 slides and tested on more than 19,400 slides from 32 datasets and 24 hospitals: it detects cancer with about 94% accuracy across 15 datasets, predicts mutations in 54 genes with over 70% accuracy, and improves survival prediction by 8% across 17 institutions. Retrospective validation; not trained on rare diseases or premalignant tissue.

Technology: CHIEF (pathology foundation model)

28 Aug 2024Harvard Medical School; and Imagine Institute, University of Paris

Machine-learning Treg framework identifies SMARCC1 as a previously unrecognized regulator

A framework combining single-cell ATAC-seq, motif analysis, topic modeling, machine learning and genetic variation identified SMARCC1 as a previously unrecognized controller of regulatory T-cell chromatin programs. CRISPR-Cas9 deletion of Smarcc1 in Tregs produced the predicted loss of accessibility in topic-3 regulatory regions, supporting the computationally inferred connection.

Technology: Integrated machine-learning framework using topic modeling and high-density genetic variation over single-cell ATAC-seq profiles

14 May 2024Harvard Medical School; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; collaborating institutions

Machine learning links mycobacterial protein disorder to targeted degradation potential

Researchers combined a genetic chemical-proximity platform and experimental screening of 72 Mycobacterium smegmatis protein candidates with machine learning. A Lasso model trained on measured degradation data identified N-terminal disorder propensity as associated with degradation potential and predicted candidate targets among 348 essential proteins conserved between M. smegmatis and M. tuberculosis. Experimentally induced degradation of selected essential M. smegmatis proteins inhibited bacterial growth and, for some targets, potentiated antimicrobial compounds.

Technology: Lasso regression model trained on sequence and structural protein descriptors, including AlphaFold2 confidence and predicted disorder propensity

EVEscape: predicting viral variants before they emerge

Starting from January 2020, the tool predicted SARS-CoV-2 mutations with accuracy similar to experimental antibody-binding assays and ranked their prevalence better; it generalizes to HIV and influenza. Retrospective validation; prospective performance is not reported.

21 Mar 2022Harvard Medical School; Ege University; Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard; University of California San Diego

Deep learning assesses heart allograft rejection from biopsy whole-slide images

A deep learning system analysing gigapixel endomyocardial biopsy slides detected allograft rejection with an AUC of 0.962, distinguished cellular and antibody-mediated rejection (AUCs 0.958 and 0.874) and graded rejection with an AUC of 0.833. In a human reader study it was non-inferior to conventional assessment and reduced interobserver variability and assessment time. Caveat: test cohorts from the US, Turkey and Switzerland are retrospective and the authors call for clinical trials.

Technology: Deep learning on whole-slide images for endomyocardial biopsies
