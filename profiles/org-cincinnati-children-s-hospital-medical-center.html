Elastic-net analysis identifies an exploratory seven-protein anti-TNF response signature in Crohn’s disease

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Elastic-net logistic models helped derive a seven-protein candidate signature associated with anti-TNF treatment outcomes in a cohort of 80 children and young adults with Crohn’s disease. A separate analysis fitted models to expression of the corresponding genes in 324 PANTS participants, reporting mean AUPRCs of 0.704 at baseline and 0.76 at week 14. This is exploratory, retrospective model evaluation on prospectively collected observational cohorts, not a locked-model prospective trial. The second analysis refitted transcriptomic models and does not independently validate plasma protein measurements. Modest cohort size, missing follow-up, non-standardized treatment and 90% White enrollment in the discovery cohort limit interpretation.

Technology: Elastic-net-regularized logistic regression

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Elastic-net logistic regression evaluated candidate protein/outcome relationships and selected a seven-protein panel; corresponding gene-expression models were refitted and tested in PANTS.
Test and result
Discovery cohort: 80 participants; candidate proteins selected using outcome associations and regularized predictive models. Independent PANTS cohort analysis used 814 RNA-seq samples from 324 participants and separately trained/tested gene-expression models.
Limitation / still unknown
No orthogonal protein-level validation; refitted cross-omic models are not an external frozen protein-panel validation. Feature/outcome exploration and observational treatment variation limit generalization; no causal or treatment-selection benefit established.

30 Aug 2025Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center; University of Cincinnati; Connecticut Children’s Medical Center; Nationwide Children’s Hospital; Children’s Hospital of Wisconsin

Learned correction improves retrospective infliximab concentration estimates in young patients

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: XGBoost corrected errors from a population-pharmacokinetic Bayesian model using measured infliximab concentrations in young patients with Crohn’s disease. Internal cross-validation reduced concentration prediction error, but some cases worsened and no external or prospective treatment-benefit evaluation was shown. The result concerns measured drug concentrations, without established improvements in dosing safety or disease outcomes.

Technology: XGBoost error correction integrated with population-pharmacokinetic Bayesian estimation

Editorial relevance 33 biology Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
XGBoost learned residual prediction patterns and adjusted the prior pharmacokinetic estimates; comparison with measured subsequent concentrations supplies the specific clinical validation result. The pre-existing Bayesian model/dashboard alone is not treated as a new discovery.
Test and result
The source analyzed 292 successive-concentration pairs from 93 patients aged 2–22 years. XGBoost learned prediction errors using clinical features and prior drug measurements. Five-fold evaluation reported RMSE 3.78 ± 0.85 micrograms/mL versus 4.8 for Bayesian estimation alone. Absolute error improved modestly on average; the source also reports worsened predictions in a subset.
Limitation / still unknown
Multiple observations per patient were present, but explicit patient-grouped folds were not established in the reviewed Methods. Correlation-based feature selection and algorithm comparisons may affect internal performance; independent final evaluation was absent. Low concentrations below quantification were excluded. This is retrospective evaluation, without demonstrated clinical remission, safety improvement or validated continuous-learning deployment.

21 Jul 2025Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center; University of Cincinnati College of Medicine; University of Wisconsin–Madison

EHR deep learning and sequencing identify two previously undiagnosed Noonan syndrome cases

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Investigators applied a deep-learning model to diagnosis text in the electronic health records of 92,428 pediatric patients. It flagged 171 patients above a prespecified Noonan syndrome risk threshold for chart review; genetic sequencing and specialist assessment identified two previously undiagnosed Noonan syndrome cases among evaluated patients, in addition to three patients diagnosed during the study period. The confirmed cases had pathogenic variants in Noonan-associated genes, including PTPN11 and LZTR1. This supplies real-world evidence that EHR-based model screening can surface rare-disease cases for clinical genetic evaluation.

Technology: Deep convolutional neural network trained on structured electronic-health-record diagnosis text to estimate Noonan syndrome risk

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The previously developed deep-learning model scored 92,428 patient records and ranked 171 individuals above a prespecified risk threshold. The study team reviewed high-risk records, then used clinical genetic assessment and sequencing to evaluate patients without a prior diagnosis; these follow-up tests confirmed two previously undiagnosed cases. Model risk trajectories were also reconstructed from cumulative EHR diagnoses to assess whether the signal appeared before final diagnosis.
Test and result
The confirmed cases had pathogenic variants in Noonan-associated genes, including PTPN11 and LZTR1.
Limitation / still unknown
This was a real-world retrospective screening evaluation at one pediatric biobank, not a prospective screening trial. Sequencing identified two cases among 83 patients evaluated, and the overall precision among the 171 high-risk patients was 2.92%; the model therefore required substantial chart review and genetic confirmation. Retrospective risk trajectories suggest possible earlier recognition but do not establish earlier care or improved outcomes.

27 May 2023Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center

Machine-learning alerts increase referrals for epilepsy surgery evaluation in a randomized trial

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: At one specialized pediatric epilepsy center, an NLP support system using an SVM and n-gram features screened neurology notes and randomized providers of 284 identified potential candidates to receive an alert or usual care. During a median 24-month follow-up, presurgical evaluation referrals were 9.8% with alerts and 3.1% in controls (adjusted HR 3.21; 95% CI 0.95–10.8; one-sided p=.03); nine alert-group children and no controls underwent surgery. The trial was prospective and randomized, but involved one center, had a wide confidence interval, and used one-sided testing; it does not establish benefit across other settings.

Technology: Natural-language processing with a supervised support vector machine and n-gram features

Editorial relevance 73 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An NLP/SVM system screened free-text EHR neurology notes for potential surgical candidates and generated provider alerts.
Test and result
A prospective randomized trial compared provider alerts with standard care among 300 potential candidates at 14 outpatient clinic sites of one pediatric epilepsy center; presurgical referrals and subsequent surgery were recorded.
Limitation / still unknown
Generalizability beyond the specialized center and the effect in other care settings were not established. The referral estimate was imprecise, and the paper reports one-sided p values.
