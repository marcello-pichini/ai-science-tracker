2 Nov 2026University College London

Machine learning guides synthesis of low-ruthenium perovskite oxygen evolution catalysts

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A library of thirty high-to-medium entropy La(Ru,Ni,Mn,Fe)O3 catalysts was synthesised and screened electrochemically; the best low-Ru composition gave 325 mV overpotential at 10 mA cm-2 with 4% decay over 70 h. The data informed machine-learning-guided synthesis of twelve new compositions, and La1.02Ru0.16Ni0.36Mn0.12Fe0.34O3 reached 269 mV, a Tafel slope of 62 mV dec-1 and over 90% voltage retention after 85 h. The abstract does not name the machine-learning algorithm; the library is small.

Technology: Machine-learning model trained on electrochemical screening data to propose new compositions

18 May 2026University College London; The Francis Crick Institute; Royal Marsden NHS Foundation Trust; University College London Hospitals; Royal Holloway, University of London

A graph neural network nominates OSM–OSMR for melanoma immunotherapy, with ex-vivo explant evidence

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: MIDAS, a multimodal graph-neural-network model, ranked candidate immuno-oncology targets from gene-interaction, patient multi-omic and immune-biology data. In eight stage III/IV melanoma patient-derived explants, researchers blocked OSM–OSMR signalling with an anti-OSM antibody. The paper reports reduced dysfunctional CD8+ T-cell populations and lower CCL4 levels; PTPN22 inhibition did not produce comparable changes in this assay.

Technology: MIDAS, a multimodal graph neural network for immuno-oncology target ranking

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MIDAS integrated gene-interaction networks with patient multi-omics, immune-cell biology and genetic-perturbation evidence to rank candidate immunotherapy targets. Researchers selected OSM–OSMR for functional follow-up and tested pathway blockade in patient-derived tumour explants; the model nominated the target, while the ex-vivo experiments measured the T-cell and cytokine outcomes.
Test and result
The paper reports reduced dysfunctional CD8+ T-cell populations and lower CCL4 levels; PTPN22 inhibition did not produce comparable changes in this assay.
Limitation / still unknown
Functional follow-up used only eight stage III/IV melanoma patient-derived explants. This is an ex-vivo preclinical result, not treatment of patients or evidence of clinical benefit. OSM–OSMR biology has prior literature in other tumour settings; the new paper-specific contribution is MIDAS target nomination and measured perturbation effects in melanoma explants. Do not generalize to other cancers or claim a validated therapy.

24 Dec 2022University College London

Machine learning separates genetic salt-losing tubulopathies from other hypokalemia

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 419 hypokalemic patients (291 with genetically confirmed Gitelman or Bartter syndrome) from London, Taipei and Kobe, algorithms trained on London and Taipei data were tested on the Kobe set. A decision table using serum bicarbonate and urinary sodium excretion achieved 74% accuracy, above any single biochemical variable. The authors conclude accuracy is insufficient to replace genotyping.

Technology: Decision table machine-learning algorithm on routine biochemistry

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
ML algorithms identified biochemical variable combinations that distinguish salt-losing tubulopathies.
Test and result
Algorithms trained on two cohorts were tested on a third, with genotyping as reference.
Limitation / still unknown
Accuracy is modest; sample is from specialist clinics. Abstract-based; full text not reviewed.

25 May 2022University College London

Learned tracking and agent models examine rapid mouse route choice

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Neural tracking supported evidence that mice favor shorter routes and smaller shelter angles before escape experience. Model-based learning agents reproduced rapid route choice from recorded exploration, whereas the tested model-free agent failed under that limited experience.

Technology: DeepLabCut neural pose estimation; model-based reinforcement learning

Editorial relevance 40 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A learned pose network extracts movement; reinforcement-learning agents test algorithmic explanations of recorded behavior.
Test and result
Custom-trained pose estimation measured trajectories. Q-learning, DYNA-Q and Influence Zones agents were compared using42 retained exploration sessions.
Limitation / still unknown
Agent agreement does not identify the biological learning algorithm. Recorded sessions and repeated trials are not independent neural-mechanism tests; alternative prior-knowledge explanations remain. No causal brain mechanism is claimed.

26 Jan 2022University College London; King's College London

Deep learning DR grading tested on handheld non-mydriatic images in India

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A commercial algorithm (Zeiss VISUHEALTH-AI DR) developed on mydriatic images was evaluated prospectively on 20,489 eyes of 11,199 people in house-to-house screening across 20 regions of India. Referable DR detection had sensitivity 72.08% and specificity 85.65%, with referable precision only 29.60; gradability sensitivity was 91.22% and specificity 65.06%. Accuracy depends on image quality, and performance was limited for this setting.

Technology: Existing deep learning DR grading algorithm (Zeiss VISUHEALTH-AI DR) applied to handheld camera images

28 May 2021University College London; University College London Hospitals; The Netherlands Cancer Institute

Longitudinal MRI learning classifies treated high-grade glioma changes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A support-vector machine combined structural and perfusion MRI measurements to classify progression, pseudoprogression and stable disease after high-grade glioma treatment. In a retrospective 64-patient study, longitudinal combined features reached reported accuracy of 94.7% in the 19-patient subset with serial perfusion scans. Repeated internal cross-validation, a mostly clinical imaging reference and feature-selection uncertainty limit generalization; no prospective treatment benefit was demonstrated.

Technology: Radial-basis-function support-vector machine; LASSO feature selection

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
LASSO selects image-derived measurements and an RBF support-vector classifier combines structural/perfusion changes to distinguish recorded post-treatment categories.
Test and result
Repeated internal two-fold cross-validation with histology in 13 patients and longitudinal clinical/radiological consensus in the others; serial perfusion available in 19 patients.
Limitation / still unknown
External frozen-model performance and clinical benefit remain unestablished; the small serial-scan subset, reference incorporation and feature-selection timing can inflate apparent accuracy.

30 Apr 2020University College London

DeepMCNN neural network diagnoses malaria on thick blood films from routine labels

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A convolutional neural network, DeepMCNN, was trained on routine clinical-microscopy labels from quality-controlled clinics in Ibadan, Nigeria, and also reports parasite and white blood cell counts for parasitemia estimation. In prospective validation against expert-level diagnosis it reached sensitivity 0.92 and specificity 0.90, with positive and negative predictive values of 0.92 and 0.90. Evaluation was in a single holoendemic urban setting.

Technology: Deep Malaria Convolutional Neural Network (DeepMCNN) classifier on thick blood film images
