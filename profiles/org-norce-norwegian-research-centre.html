Machine learning identifies a projected shift from DIC- to alkalinity-dominated ocean CO₂-flux variability

Kernel ridge regression reconstructed present and future air–sea CO₂-flux variability in five Earth-system models. The analysis found dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) and alkalinity to be the main drivers, while DIC’s relative importance generally declines in warming scenarios—especially in the equatorial Pacific, Southern Ocean and North Atlantic—as anthropogenic carbon uptake flattens vertical DIC gradients. This result is conditional on the models and scenarios analyzed.

Technology: Kernel ridge regression and machine-learning clustering/classification

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Kernel ridge regression reconstructed present and future air–sea CO₂-flux variability in five Earth-system models.
  • Still unknown: This is an analysis of outputs from five Earth-system models and four historical/future scenarios, not a measurement of future ocean conditions.   • AI role: Kernel ridge regression reconstructed CO₂-flux variability from Earth-system-model variables; machine-learning analysis compared predictor importance among spatial clusters and climate scenarios to identify the changing roles of DIC and alkalinity.
