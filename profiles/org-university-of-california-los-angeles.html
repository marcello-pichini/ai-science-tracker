20 Nov 2026University of California, Los Angeles; National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases

AlphaFold3-aided cryo-EM resolves native Anopheles salivary protein complexes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Biochemical and mass-spectrometry analyses identified native interactions among Anopheles mosquito salivary proteins, including Saglin and SGS4/SGS5. AlphaFold predictions helped identify subunits, and AlphaFold3 models supplied initial structures for fitting into cryo-electron-microscopy density, followed by manual model building and refinement. The resulting Saglin–SGS4 structure and related complexes reveal native protein organization and partner-dependent conformations. AI assisted structural assignment; experimental maps and biochemical observations provide the direct evidence. The proposed relevance to malaria transmission remains a hypothesis: the study does not demonstrate parasite-invasion, transmission-blocking or clinical outcomes.

Technology: AlphaFold and AlphaFold 3 protein-structure prediction, used to guide subunit identification and cryo-EM model building

AI contribution
AlphaFold predictions, including AlphaFold3 models, were used to help identify Saglin and build protein models into cryo-EM density, contributing to structural assignment of the newly characterized native protein complexes.
Test and result
Mass spectrometry and biochemical analyses of native mosquito salivary-gland extracts/saliva established SGS4/SGS5 association with Saglin and other SG1 proteins; cryo-EM structures were fit and manually adjusted against experimental density. AlphaFold3 predictions supplied initial structural models and supported protein identification/model construction.

4 Nov 2026University of California, Los Angeles; Lehigh University

A machine-learning connectome separates SuperAgers from Alzheimer's disease and tracks cognitive vulnerability

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A sparse SVM on resting-state fMRI connectivity defined the Alzheimer's Resilience-Vulnerability Connectome, which distinguished SuperAgers from AD (AUC 0.85, N=290) and replicated in N=143. In cognitively unimpaired adults, AD-like individuals showed faster cognitive decline, greater amyloid-beta accumulation and higher conversion risk. The AUC comes from cross-validation; observational imaging, and two GWAS loci (CLYBL, FRMD6) are exploratory.

Technology: Sparse support vector machine on regional functional connectivity

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The SVM classifier weights defined the connectome.
Test and result
10-fold cross-validation in the discovery cohort (N=290), independent replication (N=143) and longitudinal outcome analyses.
Limitation / still unknown
Causal relevance of the connectome and the exploratory GWAS loci.

26 Oct 2026University of California, Los Angeles

A retrospective study evaluated MedJev for in-hospital mortality prediction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using records available 24 hours after admission, MedJev ranked eventual in-hospital deaths with an AUROC of 0.926 (95% confidence interval 0.881 to 0.960). The supervised XGBoost comparison scored 0.902, with a paired interval that included no difference. MedJev’s unadjusted risk estimates were substantially miscalibrated.

Technology: MedJev, based on Qwen3.5-9B; Platt scaling

Editorial relevance 40 medicine PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MedJev converted patient-history events available at the prediction time into outcome-token probabilities, which were evaluated against recorded deaths.
Test and result
Adult patients still hospitalized at 24 hours were eligible. Evaluation examples and supervised-model/recalibration fitting used the source-described patient-disjoint MIMIC-IV MEDS partitions. Recalibration used separately partitioned labeled records and improved calibration without changing AUROC.
Limitation / still unknown
The retrospective single-center data do not establish external performance or patient benefit. MedJev was first adapted on MIMIC-IV Note, but the primary Methods do not explicitly establish that this adaptation excluded the evaluated patients or their later notes. The described MEDS evaluation split therefore does not authenticate checkpoint-level patient independence or absence of note-derived outcome exposure. The discrimination comparison was against supervised models at the tested local-label budget, not unlimited supervised training.

25 Oct 2026University of California, Los Angeles; California NanoSystems Institute

A trained optical processor reproduced functions with more harmonics than its input encoding

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A visible-light processor refined through reinforcement learning approximated a nine-harmonic function using an input encoding with four harmonics. The measured output reproduced regions of rapid oscillation as the scalar input changed. The result concerns the bandwidth of the processor’s input–output function.

Technology: Proximal policy optimization; learned diffractive optical decoder

23 Oct 2026University of California, Los Angeles; National Taiwan University

Chemistry-infused bandit drives autonomous discovery of hydrogen evolution catalysts

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An interpretable decision-making bandit, guided by a human chemistry-informed hypothesis of a locally quadratic relation between synthesis conditions and activity, was coupled to a fully automated electrochemical platform exploring a 12-dimensional space for the hydrogen evolution reaction. The campaign discovered previously unreported earth-abundant electrocatalysts that the authors describe as at least comparable to Pt-based ones, and the bandit produced model-derived hypotheses on how synthesis conditions affect performance. The abstract reports no numbers, and the Pt comparison is the authors' statement.

9 Oct 2025University of California, Los Angeles; University of Alabama at Birmingham

Multimodal language models extract seizure semiology and support patient-held-out seizure classification

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An October 2025 preprint analyzed 90 video-EEG-confirmed events from 29 patients at one epilepsy-monitoring unit using vision-language and audio-language models to extract clinical features. With leave-one-patient-out testing, KNN using automated features achieved F1 and AUC of 0.76, below the classifier using expert features. Automated feature extraction itself had mean F1 of 0.51 against expert annotations, and the small retrospective cohort does not establish clinical utility or external generalization. A later journal version was dated November 2026; the detailed results here describe the accessible preprint.

Technology: InternVL vision-language models and Qwen audio/vision-language models with downstream KNN and other classifiers

29 Sep 2025University of California, Los Angeles

LASSO DNA methylation predictor identifies C9orf72 repeat expansion carriers

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Genome-wide methylation arrays on 27 carriers and 250 non-carriers of the pathogenic C9orf72 expansion found eight hypermethylated CpG sites at the locus, and a LASSO predictor of carrier status reached 98.6% average accuracy. In an independent cohort of 1,589 bipolar disorder cases, 580 relatives and 289 controls, it flagged four carriers that repeat-primed PCR confirmed. Training included only 27 carriers and the validation found four, so precision at low prevalence is not established.

Deep-learning-guided electrochemistry identifies a mechanistic outlier in cobalt–organohalide reactions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

3 Jul 2023University of California, Los Angeles; Stanford University; EarlyDiagnostics; University of Southern California; University of California, San Diego

Deep learning detects elevated tissue-derived cfDNA across cancer and cirrhosis cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A study built a fragment-level methylation atlas from 521 noncancer tissue samples spanning 29 human tissue types and used it with cfSort, a supervised deep neural network, to estimate tissue composition in plasma cell-free DNA. Applying cfSort to patient data, the investigators found higher cfDNA fractions from the affected tissue in liver, lung, colorectal and stomach cancer and cirrhosis cohorts than in healthy controls; the estimated fractions also increased with cancer stage. The tissue-origin signal was reproduced on an independent whole-genome-bisulfite-sequencing dataset for liver cancer. In serial samples from four non-small-cell lung cancer patients receiving anti-PD-1 therapy, estimated liver or kidney tissue fractions tracked measured biochemical markers of organ injury.

Technology: cfSort, an ensemble of two dense deep neural networks trained on simulated mixtures using a fragment-level tissue methylation atlas

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The team used the supervised cfSort model to deconvolve methylation profiles in plasma cfDNA and estimate contributions from 29 tissue types. This enabled the reported comparison of affected-tissue cfDNA fractions in cancer and cirrhosis patient cohorts versus controls and longitudinal tracking of tissue-injury signals during immunotherapy. The atlas and model were also evaluated against simulated mixtures and independent methylation data.
Test and result
Applying cfSort to patient data, the investigators found higher cfDNA fractions from the affected tissue in liver, lung, colorectal and stomach cancer and cirrhosis cohorts than in healthy controls; the estimated fractions also increased with cancer stage.
Limitation / still unknown
The disease cohorts were retrospective and the clinical analyses demonstrate association and measurement feasibility, not prospective diagnostic benefit or improved outcomes. The clinical groups were 21 cirrhosis patients and 201 cancer patients, with 100 healthy controls; cross-platform liver-cancer validation used 24 cases and 32 controls. Anti-PD-1 injury monitoring involved only four NSCLC patients at three timepoints. The authors note that disease-specific methylation markers could improve cancer detection beyond tissue-of-origin fractions.

27 Apr 2022University of California, Los Angeles; Leiden University Medical Center; Cedars-Sinai Medical Center

Claims-based models estimate later inflammatory-bowel-disease care events

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random forests and LASSO models used one year of insurance claims to estimate subsequent-year hospitalizations and surgery in patients with inflammatory bowel disease. In a temporally shifted cohort of 69,165 patients, reported AUCs were 0.73 for hospitalization and 0.71 for surgery. The training cohort contained 72,178 patients. This retrospective utilization prediction does not establish prevention benefit; possible patient overlap between annual cohorts, claims-based outcomes and validation-set threshold selection limit interpretation.

Technology: Random forest; LASSO logistic regression

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Forests and penalized logistic models learn associations between prior-year utilization features and next-year claims-defined care events, enabling measured retrospective outcome-prediction comparisons.
Test and result
2015 baseline claims/2016 events supplied training data, while 2016 baseline/2017 events supplied temporal evaluation. Outcomes were claims-defined IBD hospitalization and surgery; the model compared fitted predictions with later recorded events.
Limitation / still unknown
Independent-patient generalization, biological disease mechanisms and improved treatment decisions remain unestablished. The source does not explicitly establish nonoverlapping patients across annual cohorts. Thresholds were selected on validation predictions; calibration was imperfect. A linked editorial was authenticated as an Editorial/Comment from primary metadata, but its subscription-only body was not reviewed.
