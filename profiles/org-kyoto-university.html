26 Jun 2026Kyoto University; Kobe City Medical Center General Hospital

Automated CT muscle measurements reveal exploratory shoulder-motion associations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 13 patients contributing 14 shoulders, automated preoperative CT muscle measurements were associated with motion after reverse shoulder replacement. Larger deltoid volume correlated with anterior elevation (rho 0.640), while several other muscle measures showed exploratory associations. The small unadjusted study does not establish a clinical prediction rule or causal effects.

Technology: TotalSegmentator deep-learning anatomical segmentation

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned anatomical segmentation supplied muscle regions; fixed attenuation thresholds and ordinary Spearman statistics then quantified composition and associations.
Test and result
A retrospective single-center cohort had at least one year of follow-up. TotalSegmentator segmented six muscles; conventional CT attenuation thresholds quantified composition. Scapular-volume-normalized deltoid volume correlated with anterior elevation at rho 0.640 (reported P=.014). No significant external-rotation associations were established.
Limitation / still unknown
One patient contributed both shoulders, with within-person dependence not explicitly handled. Many exploratory correlations were tested without a stated multiplicity adjustment; no adjustment for age, sex, indication or baseline motion was performed. Some reported bootstrap confidence intervals cross zero despite nominal P<.05. Methods describe cuff-tear arthropathy, but Results also include osteonecrosis and rheumatoid arthritis. Repeating the same automated pipeline is not independent segmentation validation.

30 Oct 2025Kyoto University; Disaster Prevention Research Institute, Kyoto University

Machine learning predicts timing and fault-state evolution in meter-scale laboratory earthquakes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Random Forest using a network representation of acoustic-emission event catalogs predicted the timing of meter-scale laboratory mainshocks from tens of seconds to milliseconds ahead (test-set log R²=0.84). Feature analysis found systematic changes in event intervals and seismic moments as failure approached; comparison with dynamic simulations led the authors to infer that acoustic emissions indirectly encode stress evolution on creeping fault patches. This is a laboratory-scale result, not a validated method for forecasting natural earthquakes.

Technology: Random Forest machine-learning model; deep-learning comparison model

Editorial relevance 53 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The Random Forest model made the time-to-failure predictions from the event catalog; the authors analyzed predictive features and compared the results to dynamic simulations to infer that catalog signals encode evolving fault state. The machine-learning analysis is the core result, not merely automated event detection.
Test and result
A Random Forest using a network representation of acoustic-emission event catalogs predicted the timing of meter-scale laboratory mainshocks from tens of seconds to milliseconds ahead (test-set log R²=0.84).
Limitation / still unknown
This is one meter-scale rock-friction experiment (experiment LB12-011) and a corresponding numerical model; forecast skill varies with foreshock density and cannot be generalized to natural earthquakes. The article was corrected on 2026-02-02 to replace an uncorrected proof PDF; the correction says it did not describe a scientific-result change.

30 May 2025Kyoto University

Machine-learning eplet risk score links HLA mismatches to donor-specific antibodies

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across six liver, lung and kidney transplant cohorts from two centers (978 donor-recipient pairs), certain mismatched HLA eplets were strongly linked to donor-specific antibody development while others were weakly linked, with a consistent trend across organs. Machine learning used these hierarchical associations to build an eplet risk score that outperformed traditional eplet mismatch assessments. T cell proliferation in mixed lymphocyte reactions correlated with the score and was reduced by antibody inhibition of a mismatched DSA-associated eplet. The abstract gives no numeric performance values or validation split.

Technology: Machine learning built an eplet risk score from mismatched HLA eplet associations

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine learning derived the eplet risk score from eplet-level associations.
Test and result
Retrospective association and machine-learning scoring in six transplant cohorts, with an in vitro mixed lymphocyte reaction correlation.
Limitation / still unknown
Validation split and numeric performance; prospective clinical use; full text not reviewed.
