GPT-6 Astra constructs mutually orthogonal Latin squares that improve lower bounds at 33 orders

GPT-6 Astra produced explicit mutually orthogonal Latin-square constructions that improve known lower bounds for N(n) at 33 values of n from 22 through 494. The same paper reports resulting bounds at other orders and derives a strongly regular graph with parameters (484,126,40,30) from the order-22 construction; these connected consequences are counted once as one paper-level result.

29 Oct 2026University of Cambridge; California Institute of Technology

GPT-6 Astra resolves the spectral isometry conjecture for arbitrary unital C*-algebras

The paper proves that every unital surjective complex-linear spectral isometry between unital C*-algebras is a Jordan isomorphism; without unitality, such a map is a central-unitary multiple of a Jordan isomorphism. The proof establishes that entire one-parameter groups of unital spectral isometries are algebra automorphisms, then reduces the general case to the commutative case. The authors report that the result was formally verified in Lean.

Technology: OpenAI GPT-6 Astra

9 Oct 2026Yu Chen; and Yiting Wang (affiliations not specified in arXiv record)

GPT-6 Pro proves a quadratic-size threshold suffices for random permutations to contain every length-n pattern

The paper proves that a uniformly random permutation of length 32n² contains every permutation of length n as a pattern with probability at least 1−exp(−c n/log n), for an absolute c>0 and sufficiently large n. This removes the prior log-log factor from the sufficient-length bound, resolves Alon’s open 1000n² problem, and establishes the correct Θ(n²) order.

Technology: OpenAI GPT-6 Pro

4 Oct 2026University of British Columbia; University of Graz

GPT-6 Astra proves a linear coloring bound for graphs excluding clique minors

The authors present a proof of the Linear Hadwiger conjecture: there is a constant C such that every graph with no K_t minor is colorable with at most Ct colors. GPT-6 Astra developed the proof after the authors directed it to the dense case and then asked it to bridge existing bounds; the resulting proof combines several known frameworks with a new bootstrap argument. The result is an asymptotic linear bound, not the stronger Hadwiger conjecture χ(G)≤h(G).

GPT-6 Astra finds a counting proof of the Erdős–Sós tree-embedding conjecture

A counting argument proves that every finite graph with average degree greater than t−2 contains every tree on t vertices, resolving the Erdős–Sós conjecture. The proof counts adjacency-marked vertex orderings and uses an induction on the tree to bound those not supporting a rooted copy. The exposition states the sharp classical theorem and records its proof was discovered by GPT-6 Astra; a Lean repository checks the benchmark formalization of the result. Related expository papers of the same proof are aliases, not separate discoveries.
