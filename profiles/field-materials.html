MatterSim screening identifies low-conductivity lithium-metal candidates

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint used the MatterSim learned interatomic potential to calculate phonons for 42,347 stable, previously observed compounds and flag 11,176 with predicted phonon gaps larger than 1 THz. Screening and existing-database thermal descriptors prioritized lithium-metal binaries; targeted first-principles Boltzmann calculations then predicted room-temperature lattice thermal conductivities of 2.47, 0.63 and 0.33 W m−1 K−1 for LiPt, LiTl and LiBi, respectively. These are computational properties of known compounds, not new synthesis or experimentally measured performance. The large candidate list is model-derived; a separate DFT benchmark does not independently validate every screened material.

Technology: MatterSim v1.0.0-5M machine-learning interatomic potential

20 Nov 2026Amirkabir University of Technology

Bayesian modeling estimates configuration-dependent uncertainty in printed PLA bending

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Research Square preprint trained a Bayesian physics-informed neural network on bending data from 60 groups of printed PLA specimens and combined it with finite-element simulation. In ten within-study validation configurations, predicted uncertainty varied by raster angle and infill, with the 60-degree and reduced-infill configurations exceeding twice the estimated variation of the 0-degree configuration. All ten experimental validation values lay within the reported 95% intervals, but the sample is too small to establish precise uncertainty calibration. The result is limited to the studied printer, material and bending conditions, without separate-team replication or demonstrated service reliability.

Technology: Bayesian physics-informed neural network with Monte Carlo dropout embedded in finite-element analysis

Editorial relevance 40 materials PreprintScientific article Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A physics-constrained neural network with Monte Carlo dropout estimates flexural-modulus distributions; sampled moduli are propagated through finite-element simulations to estimate configuration-dependent prediction uncertainty.
Test and result
The PINN was developed from experimental data for 60 FDM polylactic-acid specimen groups, with ten independent configurations evaluated. Reported R² was 0.9935, MAPE 1.85%, and 95% confidence intervals contained all ten experimental validation values. Reliability analysis estimated coefficient-of-variation uncertainty of 3.8–9.0% across configurations and corresponding safety factors of 1.11–1.30.
