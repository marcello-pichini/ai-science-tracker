30 Oct 2026LHCb Collaboration; CERN

LHCb reports first single-experiment evidence for B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ decay

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 18 fb⁻¹ of Run 3 data and earlier LHCb data, the collaboration reports first single-experiment evidence for the rare B⁰→μ⁺μ⁻ decay, with a combined significance of 3.2σ and measured branching fraction (1.14 +0.42/−0.39 stat. +0.06/−0.05 syst.)×10⁻¹⁰. A boosted decision tree classified candidates and suppressed combinatorial background in the fit. This is evidence below the 5σ discovery threshold; results agree with Standard Model expectations. The date is the original preprint submission; CERN announced the result on 3 November 2026.

Technology: LHCb boosted decision tree (BDT) candidate classifier and multivariate trigger classifiers

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The primary analysis uses a BDT to suppress combinatorial background, scores candidates and divides the dimuon mass fit into five response bins to improve signal sensitivity; it also uses multivariate classifiers in the software trigger. CERN states that machine-learning background suppression, together with detector upgrades, enabled the analysis to isolate these rare signals. The measured evidence comes from the collaboration's collision data and statistical fit, not an AI prediction.

Limitation / still unknown
This is a preprint submitted to Physical Review X and reports 3.2σ evidence, not a 5σ observation. Earlier joint LHCb–CMS evidence was not confirmed by later Run 1–2 analyses; this result is the first single-experiment evidence, not the first ever claim. The paper reports all measurements as consistent with Standard Model expectations. Machine learning contributes to candidate selection/background control along with substantial detector upgrades.

18 Sep 2026LHCb Collaboration; CERN

LHCb observes the doubly charmed Ωcc⁺ baryon with an 8.7σ global significance

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 6.3 fb⁻¹ of 13.6 TeV proton-proton collisions recorded in 2024, LHCb observed an Ωc⁰π⁺ mass peak with 8.7σ global significance and identified it as the weakly decaying doubly charmed Ωcc⁺ baryon. The reported mass is 3725.9 ± 1.0 (stat) ± 0.2 (syst) ± 0.4 (lifetime) ± 0.6 (external) MeV/c². A boosted decision tree suppressed random-track combinatorial background; the result is a preprint submitted to Physical Review Letters.

Technology: Boosted decision tree classifier (TMVA toolkit)

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The BDT was trained on simulated Ωcc⁺ signal decays and wrong-charge Ωc⁰π⁻ combinations from data and used to suppress the dominant random-track combinatorial background in the offline candidate selection. The background rejection supported the mass-spectrum analysis yielding the first observation.
Test and result
Using 6.3 fb⁻¹ of 13.6 TeV proton-proton collisions recorded in 2024, LHCb observed an Ωc⁰π⁺ mass peak with 8.7σ global significance and identified it as the weakly decaying doubly charmed Ωcc⁺ baryon.
Limitation / still unknown
The source is an LHCb collaboration preprint submitted to PRL, not yet a peer-reviewed journal publication as of its arXiv version. The observed peak has 8.7σ global significance; the mass uncertainty includes an explicit lifetime-dependent selection-bias term because the Ωcc⁺ lifetime is unknown. BDT use is an analysis component, not the sole basis of the observation.

29 Jul 2025LHCb Collaboration; CERN

LHCb makes the first high-significance observation of the rare Σ⁺ → pμ⁺μ⁻ decay

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 5.4 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton collision data collected in 2016–2018, LHCb observed 237 ± 16 Σ⁺ → pμ⁺μ⁻ decays and measured a branching fraction of (1.08 ± 0.17) × 10⁻⁸. The dimuon spectrum showed no evidence of resonant structures, and the results are compatible with Standard Model expectations. This is the first high-significance observation; earlier HyperCP and LHCb Run 1 results were evidence only.

Technology: LHCb boosted decision tree (BDT) classifier

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The BDT combined kinematic and geometric variables, trained on simulated signal and data sidebands representing combinatorial background, and was used in the final candidate selection. Its output threshold was optimized together with particle-identification and Λ-veto criteria to maximize expected significance. This classifier contributed to background suppression; it was one component of the analysis, alongside upgraded trigger selections, detector reconstruction, and the statistical fit.
Test and result
Using 5.4 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton collision data collected in 2016–2018, LHCb observed 237 ± 16 Σ⁺ → pμ⁺μ⁻ decays and measured a branching fraction of (1.08 ± 0.17) × 10⁻⁸.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper reports an observation of the nonresonant rare decay, not evidence for the hypothetical intermediate X⁰ suggested by the earlier HyperCP candidates; no resonant dimuon structure is found. The measured branching fraction is compatible with Standard Model expectations. AI supported event selection and background reduction but is not the sole basis of the result.

16 Jul 2025LHCb Collaboration; CERN

LHCb observes CP violation for the first time in baryon decays

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 9 fb⁻¹ of LHCb Run 1 and Run 2 data, the collaboration measured a phase-space-averaged CP asymmetry of (2.45 ± 0.46 ± 0.10)% in Λb⁰ → pK⁻π⁺π⁻ and its conjugate, a 5.2σ observation. In the resonance-dominated R(pπ⁺π⁻)K⁻ region, the asymmetry was (5.4 ± 0.9 ± 0.1)%, 6.0σ from zero. Machine-learning BDT selection suppressed combinatorial background; neural-network PID variables mitigated particle misidentification.

Technology: Boosted decision tree event classifier; neural-network particle-identification variables

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The BDT used decay topology, displacement and kinematics to suppress random combinations in the four-track signal selection. Neural-network PID variables helped mitigate misidentified final-state particle backgrounds. These tools supported signal purity for the baryon–antibaryon asymmetry measurement.
Test and result
Using 9 fb⁻¹ of LHCb Run 1 and Run 2 data, the collaboration measured a phase-space-averaged CP asymmetry of (2.45 ± 0.46 ± 0.10)% in Λb⁰ → pK⁻π⁺π⁻ and its conjugate, a 5.2σ observation.
Limitation / still unknown
This is the first reported baryon-decay CP-violation observation, with significance 5.2σ for the phase-space-integrated result and 6.0σ in the specified resonance region. Reported asymmetries average over phase space; the paper says a full amplitude-structure investigation remains future work. Small asymmetries may cancel across angular-momentum amplitudes. The measured CP asymmetry is distinct from raw yield asymmetry; production/detection nuisance asymmetries are subtracted using a control channel.
