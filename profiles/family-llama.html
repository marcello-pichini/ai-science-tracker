19 Nov 2026Stanford University

Calibrated AI detectors estimate rising AI-assisted drafting in four state courts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Stanford researchers applied the learned EditLens detector with ScoreMix calibration to 127,518 complaints from four U.S. county court systems. Their selected settings estimated rising AI-like drafting, reaching about 38% in Santa Clara and 36% in San Francisco in 2026 for non-debt-collection complaints. Calibration used documented hallucination filings and assumed their detector-score distribution represented other AI-assisted writing. Detector settings, imperfect controls, case mix and selection of conspicuous AI mistakes limit the estimates. These are population estimates, not proof that any particular filing used AI, fabricated citations or affected a judicial outcome. November 19 is the institutional announcement date; the working paper posting day is unestablished.

Technology: EditLens (Llama-3.2-3B); ScoreMix calibration

27 Oct 2026Boston Children’s Hospital; Harvard Medical School; New Jersey Institute of Technology

Related model checkpoints separate two forms of sycophancy

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 13 checkpoints from five open model lineages, base models already responded differently to sources named in a question. In a separate conversational test on MMLU, the three measurable base models rarely abandoned their firmest correct answers after a bare objection, whereas the released models did so on 6% to 52% of those items.

Technology: OLMo-2; Llama 3.1; Tülu 3; Qwen2.5; Mistral 7B

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned base and stage-matched post-trained checkpoints generated and scored answers under paired source, answer-firmness and conversational challenges.
Test and result
Across 13 checkpoints and four multiple-choice datasets, the single-turn ordering by source credibility was already visible in base models. On main MMLU runs the three measurable base models scarcely abandoned their firmest correct answers after a bare objection, whereas released models abandoned 6–52%. OLMo-2 base did not pass the conversational readout check.
Limitation / still unknown
The tests used multiple-choice letter answers. One base checkpoint could not be scored reliably in conversation. Checkpoint changes do not isolate which post-training ingredients caused the effects.

26 Oct 2026Independent researcher Marios Tsatsos

Language-model safety probes transferred despite shifts in learned directions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across four language-model families, the directions fitted to separate benign and harmful requests shifted by about 17° to 35° after distillation or instruction tuning. Applying the base probes unchanged shifted AUC by −0.011 to +0.027 on XSTest and −0.015 to +0.053 on an independent prompt set.

Technology: Fitted linear activation probes; Qwen2.5, Llama 3.1 and distilled DeepSeek-R1 variants

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Fitted activation classifiers estimated directions and classification performance on base and successor models. Their transfer separated change in direction from change in discrimination.
Test and result
Linear classifiers trained on the four base models were evaluated unchanged on two matched successors per family. Transfer was compared with the same probe’s performance on the base model.
Limitation / still unknown
Similar observed accuracy does not establish equivalence, and XSTest fold intervals are too narrow. On OR-Bench the probes flagged 29% to 42% of benign prompts that sounded harmful.

24 Oct 2026Arizona State University

Language-model credence updates moved outside the starting opinion range

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a controlled study of eight instruction-tuned language models, 22% to 68% of updates fell outside the interval between the two stated starting credences. Depending on model, 4% to 38% did so opposite the drift measured without peer input. Small model populations also sometimes spread farther apart under schedules that never widened opinions in a classical averaging comparison.

Technology: Qwen2.5; Gemma; Llama 3.1; Mistral 7B; token-probability credence readout

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Eight instruction-tuned Qwen, Gemma, Llama and Mistral models responded to controlled scalar credence exchanges. Output token probabilities gave observable estimates of the updated credence, with matched no-peer controls.
Test and result
Across 73,080 operator measurements, 22.3%–68.3% of model updates left the initial opinion interval, and 3.7%–38.0% did so opposite the no-peer drift. Small model populations widened their opinion range in 5.9%–25.6% of rounds, versus zero for a DeGroot averaging comparison using the same schedules.
Limitation / still unknown
The readout concerns single-claim scores in a fixed prompt design, not general belief or human behavior. The study used models no larger than 9 billion parameters, some with quantization. The 10,000-agent analysis was a simulation of fitted functions.

22 Oct 2026Imperial College London

More specific wording narrowed semantic representations in four language models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers compared sentences that made a meaning more specific with sentences that merely added detail. Across four language models, the more specific versions formed tighter neighborhoods in model activation space. The pattern persisted after removing five dominant directions of variation.

Technology: Gemma 4, Llama 3.2, Qwen2.5, Pythia

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Pretrained activation geometry is measured as controlled texts move from broader meanings to more specific descriptions, with blind human checks on the contrasts.
Test and result
Controlled refinement and elaboration chains were checked using blind human judgments. Across the four models, the contraction contrast persisted in 86–100% of layers after the five dominant directions were removed.
Limitation / still unknown
The result depends on the constructed text contrasts and representation measures. It does not demonstrate causal use of the contraction in other language tasks.

22 Oct 2026University of Copenhagen; Aalborg University Copenhagen; Bocconi University

Country names and query language shifted AI answers in different ways

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In tests with three language models, naming a country in the user’s message moved open-ended responses 30–44% closer to the same model’s answers when assigned that country as a persona. Changing the query language instead shifted multiple-choice responses away from the associated persona in all three models.

Technology: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Ministral-3-14B-Instruct-2512, Gemma-3-27B-IT

21 Oct 2026The University of Tokyo; TIER IV; Huawei

A hidden-state shift can cause a language model to repeat one token

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers isolated a shift shared across responses during language-model preference training. Inserting the learned shift into an untreated model reproduced a failure in which one token dominated generation. The failure appeared in 14 of 17 tested model lineages under the study’s per-token ranking objective.

Technology: Preference-optimized language models, including Llama-2 and Llama-3.1

Family Llama: profile · models page

AI contribution
The researchers isolated a common direction learned during preference training and tested its contribution by transplanting it into an untreated language model.
Test and result
The authors isolated the shared offset, compared transplantation with magnitude-matched random directions, and tested masking of the component in the training loss.
Limitation / still unknown
The failure depends on model and objective. Related hidden shifts were also observed without generation collapse in tested standard preference-training runs.

21 Oct 2026University of Copenhagen; University of Cambridge

Training AI models on one value shifted their responses on another

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers fine-tuned four language models using examples framed around restraint. On held-out scenarios, all four shifted toward responses labeled collectivist, although individualism was not the training target. The finding describes changes in model responses under a synthetic evaluation.

Technology: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-12B-Instruct, Ministral-3-8B-Instruct-2512

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The study separately fine-tuned four pretrained language models on synthetic examples of individual cultural-value dimensions and then tested labeled response preferences in unrelated dimensions.
Test and result
Adapters and their original models scored the same held-out scenarios, with 1,500 examples per value pole. The shift in relative likelihood was significant in each of the four models under the reported multiple-comparison correction. For restraint-trained adapters, the signed table entries correspond to decreases in the raw high-individualism likelihood margin.
Limitation / still unknown
The categories and response examples are synthetic operationalizations. The result does not establish model beliefs, human preferences or behavior in deployment.

20 Oct 2026Harvard University; Stanford University

Weights linked to AI sycophancy also support factual correction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers pruned 1,000 weights selected for their role in agreeing with incorrect user claims in Llama 3.1 and Qwen2.5. The intervention reduced this kind of agreement, but it also weakened the models’ tendency to correct their own wrong answers after seeing accurate claims. The result suggests the two behaviors partly share mechanisms in the models and tasks studied.

Technology: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Researchers used gradient responses of two pretrained language models to select weights associated with agreeing with incorrect user claims, then intervened on those weights.
Test and result
Pruning was compared with unpruned models and magnitude-matched random pruning. Tests distinguished accurate and inaccurate claims, source cues, independent factual recall, and weight-set overlap.
Limitation / still unknown
The experiment establishes a contribution of the selected weights on two checkpoints and controlled tasks. It does not establish a single mechanism for all forms of sycophancy.

19 Oct 2026University of Michigan; Jinesis Lab, University of Toronto & Vector Institute; University of Oxford; EuroSafeAI; Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems

Consistency training weakens answer cues while a cue-following circuit stays active

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers applied four consistency-training objectives to Gemma-2-2B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The trained models followed the training cue less often but also tended to discount correct suggestions, especially when cues appeared before the answer choices. Replacing activations in the known cue circuit with their uncued values corrected 59.8% to 91.0% of remaining cue-induced errors, compared with at most 12.1% for random attention heads in the same layers. This arXiv v1 preprint tests particular multiple-choice prompts in two models, with weaker transfer to unfamiliar cues and some circuit results based on one seed.

Technology: Gemma-2-2B-it; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; consistency training

Family Llama: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
In two pretrained language models, learned attention-head activations were intervened on to localize the cue-related errors that remained after consistency training.
Test and result
Four adapter-based training objectives used the same training prompts. Behavioral tests compared trained and original responses to correct and wrong suggestions. The mechanistic test replaced activations in a previously identified circuit and compared corrected errors with those from patching random heads in the same layers.
Limitation / still unknown
Transfer to other model families, document formats and cue types was not assessed by this comparison. Some mechanistic estimates use one seed, and the matched-question selectivity analysis was exploratory.

2 Jul 2025Helmholtz Munich; Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics; University of Tübingen

Centaur: a foundation model that predicts human behaviour across psychology experiments

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Fine-tuning Llama 3.1 70B on Psych-101, a dataset of about 10 million human choices from 160 psychology experiments, produced a model that predicts and simulates human behaviour in experiments expressed in natural language, including tasks it had not seen. The authors report that its internal representations align better with human neural activity after fine-tuning. Caveat: a 2025 critique argues that its language comprehension is limited and that it fails to follow altered instructions.

Technology: Centaur (Llama 3.1 70B fine-tuned on Psych-101)

Family Llama: profile · models page

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A large language model was the base, fine-tuned on about 10 million human choices from 160 experiments.
Test and result
Centaur, a language model fine-tuned on Psych-101, was evaluated on held-out participants and on generalization to unseen cover stories, modified task structures and new domains, and its representations were checked against human neural activity.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract states no limitations; the authors present the case study as a demonstration.
