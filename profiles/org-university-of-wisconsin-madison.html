26 Oct 2026University of Wisconsin-Madison; Chalmers University of Technology

Pose errors became easier to read after two vision models fused their views

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: On 903 valid difficult image pairs, linear probes distinguished VGGT pose errors with an AUC of 0.935 from its late features and about 0.65 from its per-image encoder. A second reconstruction model showed a similar layer pattern. The signal emerged after the networks began combining information across views.

Technology: VGGT; π³; fitted linear activation probes

Editorial relevance 47 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Linear predictors fitted to features at successive layers measured where information about pose failure became available.
Test and result
The probes used frozen activation features and category-held-out evaluation, with shuffled-label and image-size controls. The authors also compared predictions with confidence outputs and tested transfer to other datasets.
Limitation / still unknown
The pairs were deliberately hard and the failure signal varied with dataset and rotation versus translation errors. Layer profiles show decodable information; they do not establish the causal computation or improvements in multi-view reconstruction.

22 Oct 2026University of Wisconsin-Madison; University of Oxford

Unacknowledged suggestions influenced AI reasoning through question and filler tokens

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers found that activation changes at questions and filler tokens carried part of an unacknowledged suggestion’s influence in two language models. Swapping those native activations partially shifted the models’ next-token predictions toward the predictions made without the suggestion.

Technology: Gemma-4-E4B-it, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B

Family DeepSeek: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fitted residual stream editor identified candidate locations where hidden representations preserve a suggestion’s influence, which researchers tested with independent native activation patching.
Test and result
The two-target patch recovered 16.2% of the counterfactual distribution shift in Gemma and 19.3% in the DeepSeek model, measured by the study’s KL-divergence recovery metric. Equal-size random token controls recovered less.
Limitation / still unknown
The experiment concerns held-fixed responses under the tested suggestions. It establishes partial mediation at the selected locations, with effects that differ by model.

8 May 2025University of Wisconsin-Madison

Experimental feedback guides ESM-2 to brighter CreiLOV variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Reinforcement Learning from eXperimental Feedback (RLXF) used a learned fluorescence reward model to align ESM-2 with measured CreiLOV sequence-function data. In the peer-reviewed study, the authors expressed 60 generated variants in E. coli and measured cellular fluorescence; six of ten aligned ESM-2 five-mutant designs exceeded CreiLOV fluorescence, and three aligned designs exceeded CreiLOV-T7S. The first bioRxiv version (posted 2025-05-08) already described experimentally enhanced fluorescence from RLXF-aligned designs; detailed measurements here are from the 2026 journal version. The earlier ICLR workshop comparison was computational and did not report new wet-lab fluorescence assays.

Technology: 650-million-parameter ESM-2 protein language model aligned by reinforcement learning from experimental feedback using a learned sequence-fluorescence reward model

Family ESM / ESMFold: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A learned sequence-fluorescence reward model trained on measured CreiLOV variant data guides supervised fine-tuning and PPO alignment of ESM-2. The aligned model generates the candidate sequences that were expressed and assayed.
Test and result
The journal article reports 60 CreiLOV designs expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) and measured for total cellular fluorescence. Six of ten aligned ESM-2 five-mutant designs exceeded CreiLOV fluorescence, and three aligned designs exceeded the prior CreiLOV-T7S control; the brightest reported design was 1.2-fold above T7S.
Limitation / still unknown
The measurements use total cellular fluorescence and are specific to tested designs/conditions; broader cellular contexts, protein function beyond fluorescence, and independent reproduction are not established. The qualitative enhanced-fluorescence result is present in the 2025 preprint abstract; detailed design-set metrics are verified from the later journal version.

4 Oct 2024University of Wisconsin-Madison

Iterative Gaussian process screening discovers selective aurein-derived antifungal peptides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression workflow iteratively selected 23 synthetic α/β-peptide analogues for synthesis and measurements of fungal inhibition and human-red-blood-cell hemolysis. One lead, peptide 6-4, was a measured variant of an existing aurein-derived template and achieved a selectivity index of 57.1 (60.9 using the study’s finer MIC interval) versus 1.2 for aurein 1.2. Its high selectivity was driven by low hemolysis; the result is a specific peptide-design and assay contribution, not a new peptide scaffold or evidence of therapeutic efficacy. The ChemRxiv v1 result was posted on 2024-10-04; the peer-reviewed journal article followed in 2025.

Technology: Iterative uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression using molecular descriptors and measured antifungal/hemolysis labels

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression model selected peptide variants for synthesis and assays, and measured outcomes were fed back into subsequent selection rounds.
Test and result
Across six iterative rounds, uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression selected 23 peptide variants for synthesis and antifungal/hemolysis assays. Lead 6-4 had a selectivity index of 57.1 (60.9 using the study’s finer MIC interval), versus 1.2 for aurein 1.2; the authors attribute the selectivity to reduced hemolysis.
Limitation / still unknown
Peptide 6-4 modifies a known aurein-derived template. The result is a measured sequence and assay outcome, not evidence of a new scaffold or therapeutic efficacy. The version-specific first public result is the 2024 ChemRxiv preprint; the journal article followed in 2025.

17 Mar 2024University of Wisconsin-Madison

A biophysics-informed protein language model designs functional GFP variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Mutational Effect Transfer Learning (METL) combines transformer pretraining on simulated protein biophysics with fine-tuning on experimental sequence–function data. Using only 64 experimental GFP examples, the authors designed 20 previously untested variants and measured their fluorescence; 16 showed measurable fluorescence, although none was brighter than wild-type GFP. The result demonstrates functional design under a small-data setup, not improved brightness over wild type. The result was present in the bioRxiv version posted 2024-03-17; the GEM@ICLR 2024 workshop paper provides the detailed 20-variant assay report, and the later journal version is the same study.

Technology: Mutational Effect Transfer Learning (METL), a transformer protein language model pretrained on simulated biophysical features and fine-tuned on experimental data

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
METL-Local was pretrained on simulated GFP sequence/biophysical data, fine-tuned using 64 experimental examples, and used with sequence optimization to select variants for wet-lab testing.
Test and result
Twenty METL-designed GFP variants were synthesized and experimentally measured; 16 showed measurable fluorescence, but none exceeded wild-type brightness.
Limitation / still unknown
The panel contains 20 designed variants in a specific GFP task; the result does not show improvement over wild-type brightness or establish general performance across protein families.

Across ten rounds, the study reports over twice the production of the starting natural sequences under tested conditions; the top sequence also had enhanced catalytic rate on palmitoyl-ACP. 24 Aug 2021University of Wisconsin-Madison

Low-field MRI-derived synthetic CT is tested in liver treatment replanning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 37 liver cases, U-Net-generated synthetic CT images from low-field MRI were evaluated in retrospective treatment replanning. Both loss-function variants met the reported planning objectives in this source cohort. The study does not establish clinical equivalence, independent deployment safety or patient benefit.

Technology: U-Net with MSE and VGG16-based perceptual loss

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned MRI-to-CT synthesis supplied images for a defined dosimetric reference comparison; conventional treatment-planning software computed the plans.
Test and result
The 37 patients supplied 2,561 liver-region image slices. Ten-fold evaluation grouped patients, leaving 3–4 people out per fold. Synthetic images generated for held-out cases were compared with each patient’s original CT in target/organ-at-risk planning. Perceptual and MSE variants had mean absolute image errors of 35.64 and 35.67 HU, and all synthetic-CT plans met the source planning objectives.
Limitation / still unknown
The same small single-system cohort informed preliminary training choices, so this is not an external independent validation. Missing fine structures such as ribs and registration between separately acquired MRI/CT remain limitations. Reported nonsignificant comparisons are not equivalence tests, and visual image quality is not delivered-dose safety. Manual target contours were transferred from original CT; an entirely CT-free clinical workflow was not tested. No actual treatment-outcome comparison or demonstrated clinical benefit is reported. The August 2021 online version is represented; its issue date is 2022.

19 Apr 2020University of Wisconsin-Madison

Development and evaluation of machine learning models for voxel dose predictions in online adaptive magnetic resonance guided radiation therapy

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Artificial neural networks predicted patient-specific three-dimensional dose distributions from target and organ-at-risk structures for online adaptive MR-guided radiotherapy. Cross-validation on plans from 53 patients showed agreement with selected dose metrics and flagged 25 plans as potentially inferior. The retrospective planning analysis did not test effects on treatment decisions or patient outcomes.

Technology: Artificial neural networks predict patient-specific three-dimensional dose distributions from anatomical structures

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Artificial neural networks predict patient-specific three-dimensional dose distributions from anatomical structures
Test and result
Cross-validation on more than 300 abdominal-cancer treatment plans reported R² values of 0.97, 0.95 and 0.95 for V95, V100 and D95; prediction intervals identified about 10% (25) of plans as lower quality.
Limitation / still unknown
Retrospective plan analysis; clinical integration and improved patient outcomes were not established.
