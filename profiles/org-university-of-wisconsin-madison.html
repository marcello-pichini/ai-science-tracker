8 May 2025University of Wisconsin-Madison

Experimental feedback guides ESM-2 to brighter CreiLOV variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Reinforcement Learning from eXperimental Feedback (RLXF) used a learned fluorescence reward model to align ESM-2 with measured CreiLOV sequence-function data. In the peer-reviewed study, the authors expressed 60 generated variants in E. coli and measured cellular fluorescence; six of ten aligned ESM-2 five-mutant designs exceeded CreiLOV fluorescence, and three aligned designs exceeded CreiLOV-T7S. The first bioRxiv version (posted 2025-05-08) already described experimentally enhanced fluorescence from RLXF-aligned designs; detailed measurements here are from the 2026 journal version. The earlier ICLR workshop comparison was computational and did not report new wet-lab fluorescence assays.

Technology: 650-million-parameter ESM-2 protein language model aligned by reinforcement learning from experimental feedback using a learned sequence-fluorescence reward model

AI contribution
A learned sequence-fluorescence reward model trained on measured CreiLOV variant data guides supervised fine-tuning and PPO alignment of ESM-2. The aligned model generates the candidate sequences that were expressed and assayed.
Test and result
The journal article reports 60 CreiLOV designs expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) and measured for total cellular fluorescence. Six of ten aligned ESM-2 five-mutant designs exceeded CreiLOV fluorescence, and three aligned designs exceeded the prior CreiLOV-T7S control; the brightest reported design was 1.2-fold above T7S.
Limitation / still unknown

The qualitative enhanced-fluorescence result is present in the 2025 preprint abstract; detailed design-set metrics are verified from the later journal version.

4 Oct 2024University of Wisconsin-Madison

Iterative Gaussian process screening discovers selective aurein-derived antifungal peptides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression workflow iteratively selected 23 synthetic α/β-peptide analogues for synthesis and measurements of fungal inhibition and human-red-blood-cell hemolysis. One lead, peptide 6-4, was a measured variant of an existing aurein-derived template and achieved a selectivity index of 57.1 (60.9 using the study’s finer MIC interval) versus 1.2 for aurein 1.2. Its high selectivity was driven by low hemolysis; the result is a specific peptide-design and assay contribution, not a new peptide scaffold or evidence of therapeutic efficacy. The ChemRxiv v1 result was posted on 2024-10-04; the peer-reviewed journal article followed in 2025.

Technology: Iterative uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression using molecular descriptors and measured antifungal/hemolysis labels

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression model selected peptide variants for synthesis and assays, and measured outcomes were fed back into subsequent selection rounds.
Test and result
Across six iterative rounds, uncertainty-aware Gaussian process regression selected 23 peptide variants for synthesis and antifungal/hemolysis assays. Lead 6-4 had a selectivity index of 57.1 (60.9 using the study’s finer MIC interval), versus 1.2 for aurein 1.2; the authors attribute the selectivity to reduced hemolysis.
Limitation / still unknown
Peptide 6-4 modifies a known aurein-derived template. The result is a measured sequence and assay outcome, not evidence of a new scaffold or therapeutic efficacy. The version-specific first public result is the 2024 ChemRxiv preprint; the journal article followed in 2025.

17 Mar 2024University of Wisconsin-Madison

A biophysics-informed protein language model designs functional GFP variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Mutational Effect Transfer Learning (METL) combines transformer pretraining on simulated protein biophysics with fine-tuning on experimental sequence–function data. Using only 64 experimental GFP examples, the authors designed 20 previously untested variants and measured their fluorescence; 16 showed measurable fluorescence, although none was brighter than wild-type GFP. The result demonstrates functional design under a small-data setup, not improved brightness over wild type. The result was present in the bioRxiv version posted 2024-03-17; the GEM@ICLR 2024 workshop paper provides the detailed 20-variant assay report, and the later journal version is the same study.

Technology: Mutational Effect Transfer Learning (METL), a transformer protein language model pretrained on simulated biophysical features and fine-tuned on experimental data

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
METL-Local was pretrained on simulated GFP sequence/biophysical data, fine-tuned using 64 experimental examples, and used with sequence optimization to select variants for wet-lab testing.
Test and result
Twenty METL-designed GFP variants were synthesized and experimentally measured; 16 showed measurable fluorescence, but none exceeded wild-type brightness.
Limitation / still unknown
The panel contains 20 designed variants in a specific GFP task; the result does not show improvement over wild-type brightness or establish general performance across protein families.

5 Oct 2021University of Wisconsin-Madison

Machine learning guides fatty acyl-ACP reductase engineering

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning-guided iterative design–test–learn workflow engineered fatty acyl-ACP reductase variants for microbial fatty alcohol production. Across ten rounds, the study reports over twice the production of the starting natural sequences under tested conditions; the top sequence also had enhanced catalytic rate on palmitoyl-ACP. These are laboratory results, not industrial-scale production. The journal article date is used here; the earlier preprint’s exact posting and result-version differences were not checked.

Technology: Machine-learning-guided protein engineering; Gaussian-process optimization

Test and result
The paper reports ten design–test–learn rounds, in-vivo microbial fatty-alcohol production and catalytic-rate characterization on palmitoyl-ACP.
