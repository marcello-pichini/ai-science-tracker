10 Sep 2026Cornell University; California Institute of Technology; Liquid Sunlight Alliance

AI-guided autonomous experiments map phase domains in bismuth–titanium oxide films

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A robotic active-learning campaign using SARA-H mapped processing regions that stabilize δ-Bi₂O₃ and Bi₂Ti₂O₇ in thin films and examined dwell-dependent phase behavior in the Bi–Ti–O system. The study also experimentally supported the prediction that bismuth substitution in TiO₂ inhibits the anatase-to-rutile transformation. This is a human-in-the-loop experimental materials result; the compounds/phases were not all newly discovered, while the mapped processing domains and comparative evidence are the study-specific contribution.

Technology: SARA-H (Scientific Autonomous Reasoning Agent with human input) and automated probabilistic phase labeling

9 Mar 2026Cornell University; TU Wien

AI-assisted work proves a Latin-square imbalance bound, with optimal examples through order 52

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A collaborative LLM-plus-symbolic-computation study proved that every n×n Latin square with n ≡ 1 (mod 3) has imbalance at least 4n(n−1)/9. It introduced near-perfect permutations that attain the bound and computationally found independently verified matching examples for every such order from 4 through 52. The universal lower-bound theorem was formally checked in Lean 4.

Technology: Claude Opus 4.5 LLM agent with SageMath, Rust exhaustive solver, simulated annealing and Lean 4 verification tools

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 67 mathematics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The agent identified an all-even row-distance parity pattern in numerical search data, formulated and helped prove the lower bound, and used model review to catch proof errors. SageMath and a Rust solver supported symbolic analysis and enumeration; simulated annealing found matching constructions through n=52. Human strategic direction supplied the decisive pivot to the minimum-positive-imbalance question. Lean 4 formally verifies the lower-bound theorem itself.
Test and result
A collaborative LLM-plus-symbolic-computation study proved that every n×n Latin square with n ≡ 1 (mod 3) has imbalance at least 4n(n−1)/9.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an arXiv preprint (v2 dated 2026-08-13), not yet peer-reviewed in the sources checked. The universal proven theorem is a lower bound for n ≡ 1 mod 3. Matching constructions, and hence demonstrated tightness, are computationally established only for n=4,7,…,52; the paper does not prove that the bound is attained for every larger order. Lean 4 formalizes the lower-bound inequality, not the finite construction search or universal tightness.

13 Aug 2025Cornell University; and collaborating institutions

PepMLM designs experimentally binding target-conditioned peptide ligands, including peptides used for targeted protein degradation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: PepMLM fine-tunes ESM-2 using masked-language modeling conditioned on a target protein sequence to generate de novo linear peptide binders without structural input. In the reported experiments, selected peptides showed sequence-specific binding to targets including NCAM1 and AMHR2; incorporation into ubiquitin-based antibody constructs enabled targeted degradation of disease-relevant proteins, including Huntington’s disease drivers and viral phosphoproteins.

Technology: PepMLM, ESM-2 fine-tuned with target-sequence-conditioned masked language modeling

14 Feb 2024Cornell University; The Ohio State University; Google Research; Harvard University; Ewha Womans University

Interpretable machine learning finds a perpendicular-spin signature consistent with a spinon Fermi surface

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A correlator convolutional neural network trained on projective snapshots from numerical simulations of the Kitaev–Heisenberg model identified a characteristic correlation pattern in the intermediate gapless phase when analyzing spin snapshots perpendicular to the applied field. The authors interpret the learned pattern as Friedel oscillations of gapless spinons forming a Fermi surface, supporting earlier theoretical proposals for this phase and suggesting measurable correlations for future experiments.

Technology: Quantum-classical QuCl approach using a correlator convolutional neural network and regularization-path analysis

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Analyzed simulated projective-measurement snapshots and surfaced the phase signature: spin correlations in the direction perpendicular to the magnetic field, interpreted as the spatial pattern of Friedel oscillations associated with gapless spinons.
Test and result
A correlator convolutional neural network trained on projective snapshots from numerical simulations of the Kitaev–Heisenberg model identified a characteristic correlation pattern in the intermediate gapless phase when analyzing spin snapshots perpendicular to the applied field.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a numerical/theoretical result based on DMRG wavefunctions and simulated snapshots, not an experimental observation of a quantum spin liquid. The authors interpret the learned motif as evidence consistent with a spinon Fermi surface and offer it as a prediction to guide experiments; they do not claim it is already experimentally confirmed.

9 Feb 2024Cornell University; Stanford University; Argonne National Laboratory; University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Machine-learning X-ray analysis provides bulk evidence for a Bragg-glass phase in PdₓErTe₃

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers combined extensive X-ray diffraction with X-ray Temperature Clustering (X-TEC) to identify Bragg-glass signatures in the charge-density-wave material PdₓErTe₃. Their analysis extracted diffraction features across about 20,000 Brillouin zones and found a diverging correlation length and a phase diagram consistent with a Bragg-glass transition. The result provides bulk diffraction evidence for a phase that had been difficult to establish experimentally.

Technology: X-ray Temperature Clustering (X-TEC), a machine-learning analysis of X-ray diffraction peaks

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Automatically extracted and clustered charge-density-wave diffraction peaks across temperature and momentum, enabling high-throughput estimation of peak spread/correlation length from a large set of Brillouin zones; researchers interpreted these features as evidence for Bragg-glass order.
Test and result
Researchers combined extensive X-ray diffraction with X-ray Temperature Clustering (X-TEC) to identify Bragg-glass signatures in the charge-density-wave material PdₓErTe₃.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors frame the result as evidence for the Bragg-glass phase based on characteristic diffraction behavior, rather than direct imaging of all underlying defects. Machine learning enabled the scale of peak extraction; physical interpretation and measurements were done by the team.

9 Jun 2022Cornell University; Argonne National Laboratory; University of Tennessee, Knoxville; University of Maryland; National Institute of Standards and Technology

X-ray machine-learning analysis reveals opposing Cd/Re displacements and Re 5d involvement in Cd₂Re₂O₇ structural order

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Unsupervised X-ray diffraction temperature clustering (X-TEC) analyzed high-volume temperature-dependent diffraction data from Cd₂Re₂O₇. The extracted selection rules indicate that Cd and Re displacements are approximately equal in amplitude and opposite in phase, revealing a previously unrecognized involvement of Re 5d electrons in the structural order. The analysis also resolves X-ray diffraction evidence for fluctuations associated with the structural transitions. This is a data-driven interpretation of measured diffraction, not a new direct imaging of atom trajectories.

Technology: X-ray diffraction temperature clustering (X-TEC), an interpretable unsupervised machine-learning method using Gaussian-mixture clustering and reciprocal-space label smoothing

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
X-TEC clustered temperature trajectories of diffraction intensities across a high-volume reciprocal-space dataset to identify structural order parameters and weaker diffuse-scattering fluctuations. Its data-driven selection rules exposed the reciprocal-space pattern from which researchers inferred approximately equal, out-of-phase Cd and Re displacements and the Re 5d contribution; the manual crystallographic analysis would have required selecting only a small number of peaks.
Test and result
Unsupervised X-ray diffraction temperature clustering (X-TEC) analyzed high-volume temperature-dependent diffraction data from Cd₂Re₂O₇.
Limitation / still unknown
The displacement relation and Re 5d involvement are physical interpretations inferred from XRD selection rules and X-TEC clustering, not direct atom-by-atom imaging. The work reports XRD evidence for a structural Goldstone mode; it does not claim that all Goldstone-mode physics in Cd₂Re₂O₇ was previously unknown. The findings concern the analyzed crystal and temperature-dependent synchrotron diffraction data.
