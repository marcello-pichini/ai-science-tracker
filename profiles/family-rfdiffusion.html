16 Jan 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Computer-designed proteins neutralize snake toxins

Small, stable proteins designed with AI bind and neutralize three-finger toxins from cobras and mambas in the lab; in mice, they yield 80–100% survival against lethal neurotoxin doses, depending on the toxin. Laboratory and animal studies only; traditional antivenoms remain central.

Technology: AI protein design (RFdiffusion)

10 Apr 2024University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Antibodies designed from scratch with RFdiffusion

Baker’s group showed RFdiffusion can be adapted to generate antibodies: in laboratory tests, designs bound influenza virus and other targets as intended. Proof-of-concept study, first circulated as a preprint in March 2024; experts quoted by Nature call it promising but an early step; no affinity values reported.

19 Dec 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Designed proteins bind peptides with the highest affinity reported

Using RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, researchers designed proteins that bind helical peptides such as glucagon, neuropeptide Y and parathyroid hormone, with what the authors consider the highest reported affinity for a computer-generated biomolecule. They detect low concentrations of peptides in human serum and withstand heat; a parathyroid-hormone biosensor produced a 21-fold increase in bioluminescence. The press release gives no affinity values.

Technology: RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN

12 Jul 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

RFdiffusion: designing new proteins with a diffusion model

A guided diffusion model generates tailored protein structures, such as proteins that bind tightly to another molecule, and outperforms previous methods across a wide range of problems. The post says only that ‘a useful fraction’ of generated designs work as intended, without figures; the software was released in March 2023.
