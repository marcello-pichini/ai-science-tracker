10 Jun 2026Weizmann Institute of Science; École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL)

AI-designed bacterial anti-defense proteins enable phage infection and plasmid retention

RFdiffusion-based de novo binder design and experimental screening produced synthetic proteins that inhibited three bacterial defense systems: Thoeris and Avs1 in Bacillus subtilis assays, and DdmDE in Vibrio cholerae. Five designed proteins inhibited Thoeris and nine inhibited Avs1 in the reported screens. An engineered SBSphiJ phage carrying both anti-Thoeris and anti-Avs1 proteins replicated in B. subtilis cells expressing both defenses. In native V. cholerae O1 El Tor, two of four tested DdmDE binders improved plasmid resistance to clearance.

Technology: RFdiffusion de novo binder design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold2/3 structure-guided candidate scoring

21 Dec 2025Weill Cornell Medicine; Macao Polytechnic University; University of Gdańsk

CycleRFdiffusion-designed TP4 is a reproducible cyclic peptide binder to TREM2

A structure-conditioned workflow combining CycleRFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and HighFold generated and screened 1,500 cyclic peptide–TREM2 complexes and prioritized four candidates. The lead TP4 showed weak but reproducible TREM2 binding in spectral-shift, microscale-thermophoresis and surface-plasmon-resonance assays. The authors report favorable plasma stability and moderate metabolic stability, presenting the work as an initial proof of concept for TREM2-targeted cyclic binders.

Technology: CycleRFdiffusion backbone design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and HighFold structural prediction/screening

3 Dec 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design (Baker lab)

RFdiffusion2 scaffolds enzyme active sites at atomic level

RFdiffusion2 scaffolded all 41 active sites in a diverse benchmark, compared with 16 for previous methods. Designs were tested experimentally, with fewer than 96 sequences needed per case to find active enzymes across three catalytic mechanisms. The journal abstract's three-mechanism count conflicts with a five-reaction claim in the Institute for Protein Design announcement, so the abstract should be treated as authoritative.

3 Dec 2025University of Washington Institute for Protein Design (Baker lab)

Computational design of zinc metallohydrolases with RFdiffusion2

RFdiffusion2 designed zinc metallohydrolases from quantum-chemistry-derived active-site geometries. The first round of 96 designs produced an enzyme with catalytic efficiency of 16,000 M-1 s-1, and a second round of 96 yielded three more highly active enzymes, with k/K up to 53,000 M-1 s-1. Crystal structures (PDB 9PYJ, 9PYL) were determined, but the evidence covers one designed enzyme family.

Technology: RFdiffusion2 with quantum-chemistry active-site models

Riff-Diff: computational enzyme design by catalytic motif scaffolding

The December 2025 Nature version reports Riff-Diff designs for retro-aldol and Morita-Baylis-Hillman reactions, with activities comparable to enzymes optimized by experimental evolution. Six high-resolution structures support near-atomic active-site placement. The abstract describes a hybrid of machine learning and atomistic modelling. The work also has a bioRxiv preprint first indexed in August 2024; the January 2026 journal issue is later than online publication.

Technology: Riff-Diff (RFdiffusion with rotamer-based motif placement)

18 Apr 2025University of Washington; University of California, Los Angeles; Howard Hughes Medical Institute

Serine hydrolases designed from scratch

The April 2025 Science version describes RFdiffusion enzyme backbones and PLACER assessment of catalytic-state ensembles. Laboratory characterization found catalytic efficiency up to 2.2 × 10^5 M^-1 s^-1, crystal structures with C-alpha RMSD below 1 angstrom, and catalysts in five folds different from natural serine hydrolases. This summary follows the abstract and figure descriptions; an earlier bioRxiv version was posted in August 2024.

Technology: RFdiffusion and PLACER

8 Nov 2024National Cheng Kung University; and collaborating institutions

GRACE designs two carbonic-anhydrase-like enzymes with measured catalytic activity

The GRACE workflow combined RFdiffusion structure generation, ProteinMPNN sequence design, CLEAN enzyme classification and solubility/structure filtering to select two carbonic-anhydrase-like sequences from 10,000 candidates. The authors report experimental expression and carbonic anhydrase activity: dCA12_2 at 400 WAU/mL and dCA23_1 at 260 WAU/mL in the reported assay.

Technology: GRACE (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and CLEAN, with computational solubility/structural filters)

19 Dec 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Designed proteins bind peptides with the highest affinity reported

Using RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, researchers designed proteins that bind helical peptides such as glucagon, neuropeptide Y and parathyroid hormone, with what the authors consider the highest reported affinity for a computer-generated biomolecule. They detect low concentrations of peptides in human serum and withstand heat; a parathyroid-hormone biosensor produced a 21-fold increase in bioluminescence. The press release gives no affinity values.

Technology: RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN
