27 Aug 2021INRAE; Sorbonne Université; CNRS; IRD; Université de Paris; Université Côte d’Azur; Daegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology

Machine learning identifies moth-receptor agonists that attract larvae

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random-forest and k-nearest-neighbors QSAR models trained on known odorant responses prioritized natural-product candidates for two Spodoptera littoralis odorant receptors. The paper reports receptor responses for selected compounds and Y-maze preference for seven strong agonists; all seven were preferred at 10⁻² dilution, while three retained preference at 10⁻³. The percentages of receptor-active candidates apply to selected test sets and are not field trap accuracy. The fly-neuron assay is heterologous, and larval choice is laboratory evidence, not crop-protection efficacy or ecological safety. This extends the same group’s 2020 receptor-ligand study; count the 2021 behavior result as one study, not each molecule or a separate model discovery.

Technology: Random-forest QSAR and k-nearest-neighbors classifier

AI contribution
The models ranked candidate natural-product ligands for SlitOR24/SlitOR25; researchers selected the targets and candidates and performed the receptor and larval behavioral experiments.
Test and result
Selected ML-prioritized receptor ligands were tested electrophysiologically and in larval Y-maze assays; the paper reports the stated dose-dependent preference outcomes.
Limitation / still unknown
No field efficacy, human health benefit, broad ecological selectivity or global priority is established. The study extends earlier same-group work; its compounds and behavior result are bounded to the reported assays.
