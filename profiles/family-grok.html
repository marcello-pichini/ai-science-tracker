Paper trading agents echoed peer wording without a detectable immediate trade shift

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a seven-day paper trading study with 200 agents across four model families, later-starting board agents reused peers' phrases 6.8 percentage points more often when they could see a fresh post. A timing-based comparison found no detectable same-morning shift in matching trades, with an estimate of 2.4 points and a 95% interval of −4.1 to 9.3.

Technology: Opus 5.5; GPT-6.1 Sol; Grok 4.6; DeepSeek V4 Pro

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Four learned model families ran separate paper trading accounts and produced peer messages while retaining independent decisions. Frozen views and time-stamped paper orders made phrase and order comparisons possible.
Test and result
Timing-based comparisons found 6.8 percentage points more same-morning eight-word phrase reuse for pairs with an opportunity to read a recent peer post (95% interval 1.7–11.8, 68 board group mornings). In a separate comparison subtracting timing differences in isolated groups, same-direction orders in the same stock differed by 2.4 points (95% interval −4.1 to 9.3).
Limitation / still unknown
The study used one group per communication regime and model family, with only seven trading days and simulated fills at live prices. Timing was not randomized. The interval leaves room for some short-run effects and says nothing about market impact.

Language models missed more requirements with little wording in common with the final request

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 405 CL-bench Life tasks, direct answers from four language models passed 67.4% of rubric criteria with low wording overlap with the final request, compared with 81.1% of higher-overlap criteria. The analysis uses word overlap as a proxy and does not establish that wording alone caused the difference.

Technology: GPT-6 Luna; Kimi K3; Grok 4.7; GLM-5

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Four frozen language models generated corpus-task answers. A rubric judge scored outputs from the same benchmark before testing the proposed harness.
Test and result
The study grouped 5,333 classifiable rubric criteria by overlap with the final request. The evaluators assessed individual outputs under the benchmark rubric.
Limitation / still unknown
Groups can differ in underlying difficulty and scoring criteria. Rubrics are nested within tasks, and the result does not establish effects in live workflows.

4 Dec 2025University of Oxford; UK AI Security Institute; London School of Economics; Stanford University; MIT

The levers of political persuasion with conversational AI

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In three large experiments with 76,977 participants, 19 LLMs argued 707 British political positions in dialogue. Persuasiveness was driven mainly by information density, while tailoring to user traits added little, and post-training for persuasion raised effectiveness by up to 50%. The most persuasive models also produced the most inaccurate claims. Caveat: the persuasion effects are measured in survey experiments, and accuracy was tested on a subset of claims.

Technology: 19 open and closed LLMs (including GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, Grok-3)
