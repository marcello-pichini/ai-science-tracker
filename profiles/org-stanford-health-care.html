Random-forest metabolite profiling reduces false-positive newborn screens for four inherited disorders

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A 121-analyte LC–MS/MS panel combined established markers, untargeted-metabolomics features and chromatographically resolved isobars with random-forest classification of California newborn-screen dried blood spots. In 883 archived infant samples, the study reports that the second-tier approach maintained 100% sensitivity while reducing false positives by 83% for glutaric acidemia type I, 84% for methylmalonic acidemia, 100% for ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency and 51% for very-long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency. Several long-chain acylcarnitine and hydroxy-acylcarnitine isobar features contributed to classification, although their chemical identities were unresolved.

Technology: Random Forest classifier trained on targeted LC–MS/MS metabolite profiles

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The classifier modeled multianalyte profiles from true-positive and false-positive screen-positive samples to distinguish the groups for four inherited metabolic disorders. Feature-importance analysis showed that complex profiles, including unresolved long-chain acylcarnitine/hydroxy-acylcarnitine isobars, contributed to classification; the model's second-tier performance was assessed against known case status.
Test and result
In 883 archived infant samples, the study reports that the second-tier approach maintained 100% sensitivity while reducing false positives by 83% for glutaric acidemia type I, 84% for methylmalonic acidemia, 100% for ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency and 51% for very-long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency.
Limitation / still unknown
This was a proof-of-principle second-tier simulation on residual archived California newborn-screen specimens, not a prospective implementation study. The validation sample was not a fully independent external set, and archived-sample degradation and processing batch effects limited biomarker discovery; some discriminating isobar identities and biological significance remain unknown. The reported sensitivity and false-positive reductions are cohort-specific and do not establish improved health outcomes or routine clinical utility.
