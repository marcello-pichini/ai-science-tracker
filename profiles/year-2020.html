24 Dec 2020Brigham and Women's Hospital; Massachusetts General Hospital; Harvard Medical School

Topic models reveal differing clinical-note trajectories around vasculitis treatment

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An unsupervised analysis of 113,048 notes from 660 vasculitis patients found differing documentation trajectories around treatment initiation. Kidney-inflammation mentions peaked near treatment start, whereas other manifestations were more persistent; psychiatric symptoms also appeared before treatment. These are patterns in documentation, not measured disease incidence or causal treatment effects.

Technology: Latent Dirichlet allocation with cross-run topic stabilization

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Unsupervised learned word-topic distributions expose documentation themes and temporal contrasts that would be missed by restricting analysis to structured codes.
Test and result
Three learned 100-topic models yielded 90 stable topics, which two vasculitis experts independently labeled and reconciled. Notes were aligned from two years before to five years after treatment in three-month windows. Topics for glomerulonephritis peaked near treatment initiation while pulmonary and other symptom mentions persisted. Source-reported correlations with structured-data trends were .80 for treatment and .92 for glomerulonephritis.
Limitation / still unknown
This is one retrospective healthcare-system cohort of prevalent PR3/MPO-ANCA-positive cases, with no non-vasculitis comparator or independent clinical validation. Before-treatment notes were fewer and involved fewer patients. Topic scores are fractions of assigned words within pooled documentation windows, not patient risks or incidence; copy-and-paste, templates, note density and outside-system care can affect trends. Psychiatric symptoms and diagnostic delays were already known in vasculitis; this analysis does not establish their first discovery. No causal treatment effect or prospective diagnostic performance is shown.

6 Dec 2020Princeton University; DeepMind; New York University; Flatiron Institute; Carnegie Mellon University

AI-derived symbolic formula improves dark-matter halo overdensity estimates in a cosmological simulation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A graph neural network trained on a detailed N-body cosmological dark-matter simulation was distilled by symbolic regression into an analytic estimator for a halo’s overdensity from its mass and the masses, positions and velocities of nearby halos. On the analyzed simulation snapshot, the new expression reduced mean absolute error to 0.0882, compared with 0.121 for the authors’ existing hand-designed estimator. The formula’s mass-weighted neighborhood term decreases with separation and includes dependence on the central halo’s mass.

Technology: Sparse-representation Graph Neural Network followed by Eureqa genetic-programming symbolic regression

Editorial relevance 40 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The team trained a graph neural network to predict dark-matter halo overdensity using a cosmological N-body simulation. L1 regularization encouraged a compact learned message representation; Eureqa symbolic regression then fitted explicit equations to the learned graph-network components and replaced them with a closed-form model. This process yielded a previously unreported analytic halo-environment estimator and enabled comparison against the existing hand-designed formula.
Test and result
A graph neural network trained on a detailed N-body cosmological dark-matter simulation was distilled by symbolic regression into an analytic estimator for a halo’s overdensity from its mass and the masses, positions and velocities of nearby halos.
Limitation / still unknown
The formula was derived and evaluated on a single final-time-step dataset from an N-body simulation (215,854 halos), not validated against observations or independent cosmological simulations. The authors note that some terms’ physical interpretation was unclear and reserved detailed interpretation for future astrophysics work. It is a new useful estimator for the studied simulated halo population, not a confirmed universal law of dark-matter structure.
