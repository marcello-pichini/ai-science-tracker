24 Dec 2020Brigham and Women's Hospital; Massachusetts General Hospital; Harvard Medical School

Topic models reveal differing clinical-note trajectories around vasculitis treatment

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An unsupervised analysis of 113,048 notes from 660 vasculitis patients found differing documentation trajectories around treatment initiation. Kidney-inflammation mentions peaked near treatment start, whereas other manifestations were more persistent; psychiatric symptoms also appeared before treatment. These are patterns in documentation, not measured disease incidence or causal treatment effects.

Technology: Latent Dirichlet allocation with cross-run topic stabilization

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Unsupervised learned word-topic distributions expose documentation themes and temporal contrasts that would be missed by restricting analysis to structured codes.
Test and result
Three learned 100-topic models yielded 90 stable topics, which two vasculitis experts independently labeled and reconciled. Notes were aligned from two years before to five years after treatment in three-month windows. Topics for glomerulonephritis peaked near treatment initiation while pulmonary and other symptom mentions persisted. Source-reported correlations with structured-data trends were .80 for treatment and .92 for glomerulonephritis.
Limitation / still unknown
This is one retrospective healthcare-system cohort of prevalent PR3/MPO-ANCA-positive cases, with no non-vasculitis comparator or independent clinical validation. Before-treatment notes were fewer and involved fewer patients. Topic scores are fractions of assigned words within pooled documentation windows, not patient risks or incidence; copy-and-paste, templates, note density and outside-system care can affect trends. Psychiatric symptoms and diagnostic delays were already known in vasculitis; this analysis does not establish their first discovery. No causal treatment effect or prospective diagnostic performance is shown.

Autonomous machine-learning experiments identify a dual-anion aqueous sodium electrolyte with a wider stability window

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A robotic electrochemistry stand coupled to Bayesian optimization tested 140 aqueous electrolyte formulas over 40 hours and selected a non-intuitive sodium mixture of NaClO4 and NaNO3.
