4 May 2026University of California, San Francisco (Fraser and DeGrado groups)

A designed generalist binding protein evolves into a highly active designed enzyme

Crystallographic fragment screening found weak binders of a designed protein, which were then evolved into a catalyst that the authors describe as the most active designed enzyme to date. AI models were used in the redesign step, while the crystal structures and enzyme assays were experimental. Kinetic values and design counts were not checked in the primary paper, so the record claim rests on press statements.

Technology: AI-guided protein redesign (specific models not verified)

23 Feb 2026University of California, Berkeley (Yaghi group)

LFAST: an LLM agent drives robotic synthesis of the new COF-2000 framework

A GPT-4o-based agent (LFAST) screened reaction conditions over a 96-condition matrix and refined them after each round, raising the crystallinity index of a reference covalent organic framework by 350% and yielding COF-2000, a previously unreported material with higher structural order. The reported validation is crystallinity measurement of the synthesized products; the authors do not pursue applications. Publication date is taken from secondary reports of the JACS paper and should be checked against the journal page.

Technology: LFAST (LLM For Accelerated Synthesis Technique), GPT-4o agent with robotic experimentation

14 May 2021University of California, San Francisco; Harvard Medical School

Neural network ensemble for prenatal detection of complex congenital heart disease

An ensemble of neural networks trained on 107,823 fetal ultrasound images reached an AUC of 0.99, 95% sensitivity and 96% specificity in an internal test set of 4,108 fetal surveys with 0.9% CHD. Its sensitivity was comparable to that of clinicians and it stayed robust on outside-hospital and lower-quality images. Caveat: retrospective study with a low-prevalence internal test set.

Technology: Ensemble of convolutional neural networks with segmentation models
