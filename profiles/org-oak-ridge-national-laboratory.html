7 Nov 2022Oak Ridge National Laboratory; University of Tennessee, Knoxville

Autonomous microscopy discovers an edge plasmon in layered MnPS₃

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Deep-kernel-learning-guided STEM-EELS measurements identified a low-energy edge-localized plasmon response in the layered antiferromagnet MnPS₃, distinct from the higher-energy bulk plasmon response. The system used local structure to predict spectra and uncertainty, then autonomously selected where to measure based on a physics-defined objective. After 10–25 spectra, about 1% of the image-space points, it localized the edge mode to the flake boundary.

Technology: Deep kernel learning (feed-forward neural-network feature embedding with a Gaussian-process kernel and physics-based acquisition/scalarizer)

Editorial relevance 53 physics Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model learned a structure-to-electron-energy-loss-spectrum relationship from sequential measurements and used its predicted response and uncertainty to choose subsequent STEM probe locations. A user-specified spectral scalarizer prioritized a low-energy feature relative to the bulk plasmon; the closed-loop instrument then discovered and mapped an edge-localized response without pretraining on MnPS₃ sample data.
Test and result
Deep-kernel-learning-guided STEM-EELS measurements identified a low-energy edge-localized plasmon response in the layered antiferromagnet MnPS₃, distinct from the higher-energy bulk plasmon response.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper demonstrates the mode in the reported exfoliated MnPS₃ flake and STEM-EELS setup. The physical search objective was supplied by researchers (a spectral feature below the bulk-plasmon peak), so the AI did not independently formulate the scientific question. The authors report that the edge response varied with local boundary shape and present this as a proof-of-concept discovery workflow, not a complete theory of MnPS₃ plasmon behavior.

4 Nov 2022Oak Ridge National Laboratory; Argonne National Laboratory; Instituto de Física de Líquidos y Sistemas Biológicos, UNLP-CONICET

Machine-learning-assisted neutron scattering shows hydrostatic pressure tunes interactions in spin ice

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Machine-learning-assisted analysis of diffuse neutron scattering from Dy₂Ti₂O₇ at ambient pressure and about 1.3 GPa extracted pressure-dependent Hamiltonian parameters and found that hydrostatic pressure modifies further-neighbor magnetic interactions and the material’s nanoscale magnetic-glass ordering. The analysis supports using pressure to tune the spin-ice glass state. A transition at substantially higher pressure is a model-based prediction, not an observed transition in this experiment.

Technology: Nonlinear autoencoder, radial-basis generative surrogate and hierarchical clustering for neutron-scattering analysis

Editorial relevance 53 materials Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The nonlinear autoencoder compressed measured and simulated diffuse neutron-scattering volumes; a trained generative surrogate enabled fast forward prediction of scattering from spin-Hamiltonian parameters, while clustering and inverse mapping constrained parameters against the experiment. Those ML components made it practical to analyze pressure-dependent measurements and infer the change in further-neighbor couplings during the neutron experiment workflow.
Test and result
Machine-learning-assisted analysis of diffuse neutron scattering from Dy₂Ti₂O₇ at ambient pressure and about 1.3 GPa extracted pressure-dependent Hamiltonian parameters and found that hydrostatic pressure modifies further-neighbor magnetic interactions and the material’s nanoscale magnetic-glass ordering.
Limitation / still unknown
The reported pressure comparison is between ambient pressure and an estimated hydrostatic pressure of about 1.3 GPa, using separate measurement setups. The change in magnetic couplings and glass morphology is inferred by fitting a spin model to scattering with ML-assisted analysis, not measured as a direct microscopic coupling. The authors predict that substantially higher pressure may induce a phase transition; no such transition was observed here. Some analysis and experimental steering remained manual.

4 Apr 2022Oak Ridge National Laboratory; Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago; Nagoya Institute of Technology and collaborators

Active-learning experiments reveal distinct on-field and off-field polarization-switching behavior in ferroelectrics

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An active-learning workflow paired with automated piezoresponse-force microscopy discovered relationships between local ferroelectric domain structure and polarization-switching hysteresis. The experimentally selected sampling paths and points differed substantially between on-field and off-field hysteresis loops, indicating that the two measurement modes are dominated by different mechanisms. This is a bounded result from the reported ferroelectric sample and spectroscopy workflow, not a universal mechanism established for all ferroelectrics.

Technology: Machine-learning active-learning framework for automated piezoresponse force microscopy and spectroscopy

Editorial relevance 53 materials PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model learned correlations between high-dimensional local domain structure and hysteresis-loop switching characteristics, and used loop descriptors and uncertainty to guide the automated microscopy and spectroscopy measurements. Human physics insight was encoded in the acquisition function.
Test and result
An active-learning workflow paired with automated piezoresponse-force microscopy discovered relationships between local ferroelectric domain structure and polarization-switching hysteresis.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors report experimental correlation and different on-field/off-field discovery paths, interpreting this as evidence that the measurement modes are dominated by different mechanisms. The study does not establish a universal causal account across materials; it reports a specific automated PFM/spectroscopy investigation.
