9 Jun 2026Carnegie Mellon University

Machine learning shows hepatocyte-like cell lines poorly reproduce primary liver enhancer activity

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The authors compared four immortalized hepatocyte-like cell lines with primary liver tissue using chromatin-accessibility assays, motif analysis and a previously trained multi-species liver-enhancer classifier. Classifier sensitivity was below 60% on the cell-line enhancers; only HepG2 exceeded 50%, supporting the bounded finding that these lines incompletely reproduce native liver regulatory landscapes. Modifying AML12 culture conditions did not substantially improve similarity. The first preprint version already reports this result on 9 June 2026; a later version was posted 22 November. The result concerns selected cell lines and enhancer datasets, not every liver experiment, and classifier output is an indirect readout of regulatory similarity rather than direct proof of enhancer function.

Technology: Published multi-species liver-enhancer activity classifier trained on primary-liver sequences

Editorial relevance 47 biology Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A learned enhancer classifier trained on primary liver data was applied as a quantitative readout of liver-like enhancer activity across cell lines, helping identify their divergence from primary liver and HepG2 as closest among those tested.
Test and result
The previously trained classifier was evaluated on held-out enhancer chromosomes; it had <60% sensitivity on HLC enhancers and >50% only on HepG2. Wet-lab RT-qPCR, flow cytometry and ATAC-seq characterized cell lines; four cell lines were compared with primary tissue.
Limitation / still unknown
Preprint, selected cell lines and accessibility datasets; classifier sensitivity is an indirect sequence-pattern readout, not a direct functional assay of every enhancer. Architecture is not specified in the reviewed current paper. No universal suitability or clinical benefit is established.

26 May 2025Carnegie Mellon University; University of Toronto Structural Genomics Consortium; University of British Columbia; University of Ottawa and collaborators

Active learning discovers eight experimental binders for the LRRK2 WDR domain

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A free-energy molecular-dynamics and machine-learning active-learning workflow optimized compounds against the understudied LRRK2 WDR domain. Of 35 analogs experimentally tested, eight showed measurable binding confirmed by surface plasmon resonance and, for fluorinated compounds, an orthogonal 19F-NMR assay; measured dissociation constants ranged from 18 to 230 μM.

Technology: Active-learning-guided relative-binding-free-energy (AL-RBFE) workflow using molecular-dynamics thermodynamic-integration calculations and an ML affinity-prediction model

Editorial relevance 47 chemistry Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An iterative ML model selected ligand analogs for expensive free-energy simulations, using resulting calculations to choose compounds for further rounds and experimental validation. Of 35 procured/tested compounds, eight were confirmed as binding to LRRK2 WDR by SPR, with 19F-NMR used for orthogonal confirmation of fluorinated molecules.
Test and result
Of 35 analogs experimentally tested, eight showed measurable binding confirmed by surface plasmon resonance and, for fluorinated compounds, an orthogonal 19F-NMR assay; measured dissociation constants ranged from 18 to 230 μM.
Limitation / still unknown
The experiments establish ligand binding, not functional inhibition or a therapeutic effect. Confirmed Kd values (18–230 μM) indicate modest affinity, and the work is an optimization round around two previously confirmed challenge hits rather than an initial target hit discovery. This is not evidence of Parkinson’s disease efficacy.

27 Sep 2022Carnegie Mellon University

Robot-guided optimization identifies six non-aqueous electrolytes with improved fast-charge performance

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A closed-loop platform paired Clio, an automated electrolyte-formulation and conductivity-testing robot, with Dragonfly Bayesian optimization. In 42 experiments over two workdays, it selected six non-aqueous electrolyte formulations from a LiPF₆ and EC/EMC/DMC design space. The team tested these formulations in 220 mAh graphite‖NMC pouch cells; all showed improved fast-charging capability relative to a baseline electrolyte selected in advance from the same design space. The search reached its reported target about six times faster than random search using the same automated setup.

Technology: Dragonfly Bayesian optimization coupled to the Clio robotic electrolyte experimentation platform

Editorial relevance 60 materials Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Dragonfly selected electrolyte formulations for experiments and used measurement feedback from Clio to adapt subsequent selections. The closed-loop system optimized measured ionic conductivity over a non-aqueous single-salt/ternary-solvent search space; selected formulations were then assembled and evaluated by the researchers in pouch cells.
Test and result
A closed-loop platform paired Clio, an automated electrolyte-formulation and conductivity-testing robot, with Dragonfly Bayesian optimization.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a search within a defined design space of LiPF₆ in EC/EMC/DMC solvents, not a claim that the constituent chemicals or all six electrolyte recipes were wholly unprecedented. The optimization target was bulk ionic conductivity, with fast-charge cell performance as a subsequent validation. Pouch-cell tests used nominal 220 mAh graphite‖LiNi0.5Mn0.3Co0.2O2 cells and a baseline chosen from the same design space; results do not establish commercial-scale manufacturing or long-term fleet performance. The paper compares optimization time with random search in the same automated experiment.

A Bag of Tricks for Automated De Novo Design of Molecules with the Desired Properties: Application to EGFR Inhibitor Discovery

Source check: Needs source check. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A deep recurrent generator with reinforcement learning nominated EGFR compounds; enzyme assays confirmed 4 of 15 predicted actives, including two with nanomolar potency. Five predicted-inactive controls were inactive. This is a small preclinical proof of concept.

Technology: Generative recurrent neural network with reinforcement learning

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A deep recurrent neural network generated SMILES molecules; reinforcement learning used an EGFR random-forest activity predictor with transfer learning, experience replay and reward shaping to guide generation and selection.
Test and result
The v1 preprint reports four confirmed inhibitors among 15 assayed predicted actives and five inactive predicted-inactive controls. Two predicted-active compounds were insoluble and not assayed. Two hits had nanomolar inhibition comparable to staurosporine; the selected-batch hit fraction does not establish a general success rate.
Limitation / still unknown
The result is a proof-of-concept assay on 15 predicted actives and five predicted-inactive controls. The preprint does not establish therapeutic efficacy or generalize its hit rate beyond this target and protocol.

23 Dec 2020Carnegie Mellon University

Autonomous machine-learning experiments identify a dual-anion aqueous sodium electrolyte with a wider stability window

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A robotic electrochemistry stand coupled to Bayesian optimization tested 140 aqueous electrolyte formulas over 40 hours and selected a non-intuitive sodium mixture of NaClO4 and NaNO3. On platinum, the blend had a wider electrochemical stability window than the NaClO4 baseline despite lower total salt concentration; follow-up tests reported a 24 mV increase in the OER potential at the stated leakage-current criterion and 58% lower current density at 2 V. The result is a measured electrolyte formulation under the paper’s electrochemical protocols, not evidence of a commercial battery or field-ready cell.

Technology: Dragonfly Bayesian optimization software coupled to the Otto robotic electrochemistry test stand

Editorial relevance 47 materials Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Dragonfly selected electrolyte formulations sequentially from real-time measurements and guided autonomous experiment requests to Otto; the paper reports the mixed-anion sodium formulation as the optimization result.
Test and result
A robotic electrochemistry stand coupled to Bayesian optimization tested 140 aqueous electrolyte formulas over 40 hours and selected a non-intuitive sodium mixture of NaClO4 and NaNO3.
Limitation / still unknown
The study measured electrochemical behavior primarily on platinum electrodes and reports a wider stability window for the selected aqueous blend under its stated protocol. This does not establish long-term performance, commercial readiness, or general improvement across battery chemistries.
