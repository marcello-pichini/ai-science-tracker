2 Nov 2026University of California, Berkeley

Machine learning guided design of strong transcriptional activation domains

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The preprint abstract reports computational generation of 500,000 candidate transcriptional activation domains and selection of 18,750 high-scoring sequences for yeast experiments using sequence-to-function models including three neural networks. More than 90% of 16,770 well-measured sequences functioned as activation domains. Some designed sequences retained activity in fungi, plants and human cells. Model agreement motivated the MADRIGAL consensus predictor. Review is limited to the abstract and original-server metadata.

Technology: Neural-network sequence-to-function models (three networks); consensus predictor MADRIGAL

Neural networks scored sequences generated from known activation-domain-like sequences; high-scoring sequences were selected for experimental validation and the consensus model MADRIGAL was developed.
Test and result
18,750 selected sequences were tested experimentally in yeast; over 90% of 16,770 well-measured sequences functioned as activation domains, and some retained function in fungi, plants and human cells.

19 Oct 2026University of California, Berkeley; University of Michigan

AI trace analysis finds shared computer workflows among blind and sighted users

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Claude Sonnet 5 helped label the procedures behind recorded computer actions in a reanalysis of A11y-CUA. Across 30 tasks performed by eight blind or low-vision users and eight sighted users, the groups were more similar at the level of methods such as searching and editing than at the level of input actions in 23 tasks. Similarity rose by an average of 42.3 points for those 23 tasks. The result comes from a small desktop-task sample and depends on the authors' semantic categories and similarity measures.

Technology: Claude Sonnet 5; Multi-level Action Parsing

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Claude Sonnet 5 semantically labeled and segmented actions into comparable methods and goals, permitting comparisons that go beyond the recorded input events.
Test and result
The team compared sequence, pattern and effort across layers of computer interaction on existing traces. A separate annotation evaluation compared the pipeline with manual labels on 30 traces from three datasets. Many method-label mismatches reflected differences in where action segments started or ended.
Limitation / still unknown
The reported convergence depends on the taxonomy, desktop task mix and small participant sample. Transfer to mobile or other workflows was not established.

24 Jun 2026University of California, Berkeley; University of Chicago; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Halmstad University; Region Halland; University of Gothenburg; Sahlgrenska University Hospital

Deep learning finds an ECG biomarker for sudden cardiac death

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The Nature paper, published online on 2026-06-24, reports a deep-learning ECG biomarker evaluated retrospectively in Swedish data and external US and Taiwanese datasets. Its high-risk group comprised 2.2% of the sample and had a 7.0% annual sudden-cardiac-death rate, versus 4.6% for reduced LVEF; 86.1% were not flagged by LVEF. Among high-risk patients with defibrillators, sudden cardiac death was 54.4% lower than model-expected, an observational association subject to confounding and not proof of benefit.

Technology: Deep learning ECG model with a generative ECG waveform model for visualization

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep learning model discovered the predictive ECG waveform pattern; generative model used to visualize it.
Test and result
Existing Swedish ECG/death-certificate data, a held-out lockbox created before analysis, external US ventricular-arrhythmia and Taiwanese arrhythmic-arrest evaluations; observational defibrillator associations.
Limitation / still unknown
Main paper reviewed; supplementary attachments not read. The authors explicitly identify residual confounding and the need for randomized testing of defibrillator benefit in model-high-risk patients.

25 Nov 2025University of California, Berkeley; Monash University; VinUniversity; University of Haifa; Humboldt University of Berlin

AI-guided experiments uncover new zeolitic imidazolate framework crystals

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks. The campaign crystallized new structures using 10 of 48 previously untested linkers, doubling the discovery rate of random exploration (700 versus 1,400 experiments) and increasing the single-linker Zn-ZIF library by about one-third. The paper reports structure-resolved products, including eleven previously unreported crystals across its discovery campaign.

Technology: Algorithmic Iterative Reticular Synthesis (AIRES)

Editorial relevance 60 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AIRES used probabilistic random-forest and Gaussian-process crystallization models to guide experiment selection across linker chemistries and reaction conditions. Its greedy and batch decision rules prioritized experiments predicted to yield distinct new crystals; automated synthesis, computer-vision crystal screening and subsequent single-crystal X-ray diffraction supplied experimental outcomes for model updates and structure determination. The AI-guided campaign found structures for 10 of 48 new linkers, with twice the distinct-crystal discovery rate of random exploration.
Test and result
AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks.
Limitation / still unknown
This is one paper-level experimental discovery campaign. The reported efficiency comparison is against a random-exploration baseline within the study, not an independent replication. Structures were identified by single-crystal X-ray diffraction; the paper describes discovery of ZIF crystals but does not establish application performance. The campaign covered a defined single-linker Zn-ZIF chemical and reaction space, not all reticular materials.

21 May 2020University of California, Berkeley; Monash University; University of Melbourne

AcRanker-guided discovery of AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 anti-CRISPR proteins

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: AcRanker, an XGBoost ranking model using amino-acid sequence features, ranked proteins from prophage regions in self-targeting Streptococcus genomes. Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.

Technology: AcRanker, an XGBoost pairwise ranking model trained on amino-acid composition and grouped di- and tri-mer features

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model ranked candidate proteins from predicted prophage regions for anti-CRISPR likelihood; the researchers selected candidates from those rankings and biochemically tested them. It directly prioritized the proteins later named AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21, whose Cas9 inhibition was measured in vitro.
Test and result
Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors selected 10 candidates for follow-up, successfully purified nine and reported three validated candidates across their experiments, including ML3/AcrIIA12, which had also been identified separately as an LmoCas9 inhibitor during the study. AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 were roughly tenfold less potent against SpyCas9 than the established AcrIIA4 control; tested potency and host range are assay-specific. The 2025 Evo paper reports distinct generated proteins EvoAcr1–5 and separate assays; shared anti-CRISPR function does not make these identical discoveries.
