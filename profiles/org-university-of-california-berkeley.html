Machine learning guided design of strong transcriptional activation domains

The preprint abstract reports computational generation of 500,000 candidate transcriptional activation domains and selection of 18,750 high-scoring sequences for yeast experiments using sequence-to-function models including three neural networks. More than 90% of 16,770 well-measured sequences functioned as activation domains. Some designed sequences retained activity in fungi, plants and human cells. Model agreement motivated the MADRIGAL consensus predictor. Review is limited to the abstract and original-server metadata.

Technology: Neural-network sequence-to-function models (three networks); consensus predictor MADRIGAL

24 Jun 2026University of California, Berkeley; University of Chicago; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Halmstad University; Region Halland; University of Gothenburg; Sahlgrenska University Hospital

Deep learning finds an ECG biomarker for sudden cardiac death

The Nature paper, published online on 2026-06-24, reports a deep-learning ECG biomarker evaluated retrospectively in Swedish data and external US and Taiwanese datasets. Its high-risk group comprised 2.2% of the sample and had a 7.0% annual sudden-cardiac-death rate, versus 4.6% for reduced LVEF; 86.1% were not flagged by LVEF. Among high-risk patients with defibrillators, sudden cardiac death was 54.4% lower than model-expected, an observational association subject to confounding and not proof of benefit.

Technology: Deep learning ECG model with a generative ECG waveform model for visualization

  • AI role: Deep learning model discovered the predictive ECG waveform pattern; generative model used to visualize it.

25 Nov 2025University of California, Berkeley; Monash University; VinUniversity; University of Haifa; Humboldt University of Berlin

AI-guided experiments uncover new zeolitic imidazolate framework crystals

AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks. The campaign crystallized new structures using 10 of 48 previously untested linkers, doubling the discovery rate of random exploration (700 versus 1,400 experiments) and increasing the single-linker Zn-ZIF library by about one-third. The paper reports structure-resolved products, including eleven previously unreported crystals across its discovery campaign.

Technology: Algorithmic Iterative Reticular Synthesis (AIRES)

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks.
  • Still unknown: This is one paper-level experimental discovery campaign. Its greedy and batch decision rules prioritized experiments predicted to yield distinct new crystals; automated synthesis, computer-vision crystal screening and subsequent single-crystal X-ray diffraction supplied experimental outcomes for model updates and structure determination. The AI-guided campaign found structures for 10 of 48 new linkers, with twice the distinct-crystal discovery rate of random exploration.

21 May 2020University of California, Berkeley; Monash University; University of Melbourne

AcRanker-guided discovery of AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 anti-CRISPR proteins

AcRanker, an XGBoost ranking model using amino-acid sequence features, ranked proteins from prophage regions in self-targeting Streptococcus genomes. Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.

Technology: AcRanker, an XGBoost pairwise ranking model trained on amino-acid composition and grouped di- and tri-mer features

Relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.
  • Still unknown: The authors selected 10 candidates for follow-up, successfully purified nine and reported three validated candidates across their experiments, including ML3/AcrIIA12, which had also been identified separately as an LmoCas9 inhibitor during the study. AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 were roughly tenfold less potent against SpyCas9 than the established AcrIIA4 control; tested potency and host range are assay-specific. The 2025 Evo paper reports distinct generated proteins EvoAcr1–5 and separate assays; shared anti-CRISPR function does not make these identical discoveries.
  • AI role: The model ranked candidate proteins from predicted prophage regions for anti-CRISPR likelihood; the researchers selected candidates from those rankings and biochemically tested them. It directly prioritized the proteins later named AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21, whose Cas9 inhibition was measured in vitro.
