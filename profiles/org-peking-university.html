20 Nov 2026Peking University; Soochow University

Hybrid NMR structure maps H-NS contacts with AT-rich DNA

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A solution NMR structure of an E. coli H-NS domain bound to AT-rich DNA revealed auxiliary linker contacts beside its established AT-hook-like recognition motif. Mutational and NMR analyses supported their cooperative contribution to DNA binding. TALOS-N neural-network predictions supplied protein dihedral restraints alongside experimental NMR/NOE measurements and structural refinement. This is a hybrid experimental/computational result; the study did not isolate the learned component’s contribution or establish independent replication.

Technology: TALOS-N neural-network-based protein torsion-angle prediction

Editorial relevance 53 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
TALOS-N, an artificial-neural-network-based hybrid predictor, supplied protein dihedral-angle restraints used in the H-NS85–137/AT-rich-DNA solution structure calculation. The structural interpretation also depends on NMR/NOE measurements, mutational/NMR analyses and other structure-refinement steps.
Test and result
The solution structure calculation used experimentally measured NOE restraints and TALOS-N-derived protein dihedral restraints; 20 lowest-energy conformers were selected. Mutational and NMR titration analyses tested the proposed H-NS/DNA contact contributions.

19 Nov 2026Peking University; Peking University People's Hospital

Transformer model stratifies axial elongation risk in orthokeratology lens wearers

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A transformer-based Hierarchical Attention Model (HAM) was trained on 1,639 myopia patients (3,271 eyes) from Peking University People's Hospital using routine eye examinations and questionnaires. On the training set precision was 0.852 and recall 0.853, with an AUC of 0.98 for high-risk patients; in temporal validation on 216 patients (356 eyes) collected at different times, precision was 85.33%, recall 75.12% and F1 79.90%. The abstract says it outperformed nine other models; validation is from the same hospital.

Technology: Transformer-based Hierarchical Attention Model with SHAP and permutation importance

31 Aug 2026Peking University; Peking University Third Hospital

Multimodal diffusion model generates future knee radiographs to forecast osteoarthritis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: KOA-Diff, a latent diffusion model fusing baseline X-rays, MRI and clinical data, was trained on the Osteoarthritis Initiative and tested externally on a private cohort of 80 knees. External binary Kellgren-Lawrence grade accuracy was 90.0% with joint space width error 2.06%, and in the Osteoarthritis Initiative accuracy was 84.0-91.8% over 96 months. Experts rated synthesized features about 3.9-4.2 out of 5, and model assistance raised clinicians' accuracy in identifying high-risk patients from 58.3% to 77.5%; the external cohort is small.

Technology: KOA-Diff latent diffusion model with multimodal fusion generating future knee radiographs

4 Aug 2026Peking University; Center for Life Sciences; Tsinghua University

AMPainterV2 designs membrane-lytic antimicrobial peptides by imitation learning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: AMPainterV2 uses generative adversarial imitation learning with a Mixture-of-Experts policy supporting insertion, deletion and mutation. In vitro, all ten top-ranked peptides evolved from random sequences were active membrane-lytic AMPs, the best with a mean MIC of 1.5 uM and membrane-lytic activity superior to polymyxin B. A deletion-only version yielded six miniaturized peptides with comparable or improved activity and an average 36% length reduction. Preprint; in vitro data only.

26 Jan 2023Peking University; Peking University Third Hospital

Bayesian network predicts low or failed fertilization in IVF and ICSI cycles

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Bayesian network learned from 106,640 fresh IVF/ICSI cycles at one Chinese centre (4,339 low fertilization rate and 5,199 total fertilization failure cases) used 18 of 24 predictors; infertility type, ART method and number of retrieved oocytes directly influenced the probabilities. With tenfold cross-validation, accuracy was 91.7%, and AUC was 0.779 (95% CI 0.766 to 0.791) for total failure versus controls and 0.807 (0.790 to 0.824) for total failure versus low rate. The authors note single-centre data and the need for external verification.

Technology: Bayesian network machine learning model on 24 clinical predictors
