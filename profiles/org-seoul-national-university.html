24 Nov 2026Seoul National University

Deep learning EEG metric linked to mortality in obstructive sleep apnea

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A deep-learning model trained on 22,044 polysomnography recordings estimated the apnea-hypopnea index from a single EEG channel, correlating r=0.88 with the scored index in internal testing and r=0.50 to 0.62 in three external test sets. Two derived metrics, the discrepancy between the EEG-based and scored index and an estimate from epochs without scored respiratory events, were associated with all-cause and non-cardiovascular mortality among participants free of cardiovascular disease after adjustment for the apnea-hypopnea index and clinical risk factors; the event-free estimate was also associated with cardiovascular mortality in the highest versus lowest quartile. Data came from the KISS, MESA, MrOS and SHHS cohorts. The findings are retrospective associations in a preprint, external agreement is weaker, hazard ratios and per-cohort sizes were not reviewed, and the authors suggest the discrepancy is not solely model error.

Technology: Deep-learning model estimates the apnea-hypopnea index from a single EEG channel; derived discrepancy metrics are tested against mortality.

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The deep-learning model produces the EEG-based index whose discrepancy from the scored index is the tested metric.
Test and result
Internal and three external test sets for index estimation, then retrospective cohort association of the derived metrics with mortality after adjustment for the apnea-hypopnea index and clinical risk factors.
Limitation / still unknown
Weaker external agreement; no causal or clinical-utility evidence; hazard ratios, covariate list, follow-up and per-cohort sizes not reviewed; not peer reviewed; one author holds related patents and leads a company.

18 Nov 2026Seoul National University; Korea Institute for Advanced Study

XCAPE proposes nine previously unmatched crystal structure assignments

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In an arXiv preprint, researchers used XCAPE and the SevenNet-Omni machine learning interatomic potential to revisit existing powder X-ray diffraction patterns. In the subset without matching reference structures, they reported nine plausible atomic arrangements, eight halides and one sulfide, that met diffraction and energy criteria. These are inferred structural assignments from existing measurements and computed energies. The preprint does not establish that the assignments are unique or independently confirmed.

Technology: SevenNet-Omni machine learning interatomic potential (XCAPE)

Editorial relevance 47 materials Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
Existing powder diffraction patterns were compared with predictions, using an energy criterion to assess plausibility. The nine assignments are the subset reported in Section 2.5.
Limitation / still unknown
Unique assignment and independent experimental confirmation were not established in the reviewed preprint.

15 Jun 2026Seoul National University; Korea University; Yeungnam University

Machine learning identifies a low-tin lead-free relaxor composition window

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A multimodal literature-mining workflow and a physics-guided deep ensemble screened more than 150 million hypothetical compositions. The study reports that the x=0.01 and x=0.02 members of (Sr0.48Na0.26Bi0.26)(Ti1−xSnx)O3 met the authors’ predefined dielectric-stability targets while retaining room-temperature permittivity above 3300. The parent composition family and Sn substitution were already known; the result is a constrained low-Sn composition window, not a new parent chemistry. The reported upper-temperature stability criteria do not establish device certification or deployment.

Technology: Multimodal literature mining; deep neural-network ensemble

31 Mar 2026Seoul National University; Yonsei University

Later perioperative records improve persistent kidney-injury classification

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An ensemble evaluated on 595 later-year cardiac-surgery patients reported ROC areas increasing from 0.807 with preoperative inputs to 0.878 with information through 48 hours after surgery. The target was postoperative kidney injury failing to recover within three days. This is retrospective temporal evaluation, not proof of live prevention or benefit from changing model-ranked factors.

Technology: Random forest; XGBoost; extra-trees soft-voting ensemble

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Three learned tree ensembles combine progressively updated perioperative records; SHAP characterizes predictive contributions rather than causal intervention effects.
Test and result
The cohort contained 2285 patients, including 168 persistent-injury cases. Earlier 2018-2022 patients trained/tuned models with patient-separated fourfold evaluation; 2023 patients formed the 595-person temporal test. Imputation was fitted inside development folds.
Limitation / still unknown
Patients dying within two days or missing key biosignals were excluded, limiting applicability to the full preoperative population. The 48-hour model uses later information closer to the outcome, not real-time streaming. The comparator score targets dialysis-requiring failure rather than the same persistence endpoint, so broad clinical superiority is not claimed. Single-centre Korean data and retrospective development do not establish external transfer, a prospectively frozen workflow or modifiable causal targets.

27 Nov 2025Seoul National University; Ewha Womans University; Keimyung University

Longitudinal data revealed distinct epilepsy outcome trajectories

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 2,586 retrospectively followed epilepsy patients, the study identified five longitudinal trajectory clusters, including three associated with better and two with poorer outcomes. One poorer-outcome cluster had persistent high seizure burden and hippocampal sclerosis. Single-center retrospective data and internal evaluation do not establish prospective clinical benefit or external generalization.

7 Jan 2025Seoul National University; and Galux Inc.

A protein-language-model workflow discovers high-activity kynureninase enzymes for tumor models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A workflow combining homology search, protein-language-model embeddings and regression prioritized kynureninases by predicted catalytic activity. Researchers experimentally tested four top-ranked candidates; the most active had nearly twice the turnover number of the prior best, and enzyme treatment was associated with a 3.4-fold reduction in tumor weight in a mouse comparison.

Technology: Protein language model embeddings coupled with regression in the SeekRank enzyme-prioritization workflow

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model represented homologous kynureninase sequences with protein-language-model embeddings and regressed kinetic activity to rank enzyme candidates. Four high-ranked candidates were tested in vitro and in vivo; the abstract reports one with nearly two-fold improved turnover number and reduced tumor weight in a mouse model.
Test and result
Researchers experimentally tested four top-ranked candidates; the most active had nearly twice the turnover number of the prior best, and enzyme treatment was associated with a 3.4-fold reduction in tumor weight in a mouse comparison.
Limitation / still unknown
The evidence is a published paper abstract; it describes an enzyme/treatment experiment in a mouse tumor model, not clinical efficacy. The reported tumor-weight reduction is model-specific; no claim of human therapeutic benefit is made.

1 Aug 2022Seoul National University; Kyungpook National University; Kavli Institute for Astronomy and Astrophysics, Peking University

Deep learning and spectroscopy confirm four faint quasars at redshift about 5

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A deep-learning and Bayesian-information-criterion search of Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam photometry selected four faint high-redshift quasar candidates. Palomar spectroscopy found broad Lyman-alpha emission and breaks in all four, confirming redshifts between 4.7 and 5.6; one object also received a black-hole mass estimate.

Technology: Deep-learning photometric classifier combined with Bayesian information criterion

Editorial relevance 53 astronomy Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The classifier shortlisted high-redshift quasar candidates from survey photometry; human researchers selected four for observation and used telescope spectra to confirm all four as quasars.
Test and result
A deep-learning and Bayesian-information-criterion search of Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam photometry selected four faint high-redshift quasar candidates.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper spectroscopically confirms four targeted candidates, not the full 35-object catalog. The selection method combines deep learning with a Bayesian information criterion and conventional candidate filtering; AI was one component rather than the sole discovery mechanism.
