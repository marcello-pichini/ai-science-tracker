4 Jul 2024Boston University; Stanford University; Peking Union Medical College Hospital; Columbia University Irving Medical Center; University of Nebraska Medical Center; additional clinical and research centers contributing cohorts

Multimodal AI distinguishes ten dementia etiologies across nine clinical datasets

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A multimodal AI model analyzed demographics, personal and family history, medications, cognitive and functional testing, and neuroimaging across nine datasets totaling 51,269 participants. It assigned probabilities to ten primary or contributing dementia etiologies and addressed mixed cases. The paper reports microaveraged AUROC 0.96 for dementia-etiology differentiation; model probabilities aligned with etiology-specific biomarkers and postmortem pathology. In a review of 100 cases, AI-augmented neurologist assessment outperformed neurologist-only ratings.

Technology: Multimodal machine-learning model combining clinical, neuropsychological, functional and neuroimaging data

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model integrated routinely collected clinical variables and multimodal neuroimaging to estimate cognitive status and probabilities for ten dementia etiologies, including mixed etiologies and cases with incomplete features. It was evaluated across independent cohorts and compared with neurologist assessments; its output is the etiology-specific classification and probability result.
Test and result
A multimodal AI model analyzed demographics, personal and family history, medications, cognitive and functional testing, and neuroimaging across nine datasets totaling 51,269 participants.
Limitation / still unknown
The nine-dataset analysis is retrospective and includes in-house as well as external cohorts. The 100-case clinician comparison is small, and the study did not test prospective changes in care or patient outcomes. The authors call for prospective studies before claiming improved patient care. Model probabilities aligned with available biomarkers and postmortem evidence but are not histopathologic confirmation for every individual prediction.

21 May 2024Boston University; U.S. Army Combat Capabilities Development Command Soldier Center

A self-driving lab finds a polymer shell with record energy-absorbing efficiency

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A human-supervised self-driving laboratory selected and physically tested more than 25,000 additively manufactured polymer shell designs across an 11-dimensional parameter space. The campaign found a structure with 75.2% energy-absorbing efficiency, exceeding the paper’s previously reported maximum of 71.8% for balsa wood, and the authors derived transferable design heuristics from the resulting dataset. The result is a structural design for specified compression thresholds, not a universal material efficiency ranking.

Technology: MAMA BEAR self-driving laboratory with Bayesian optimization and automated additive manufacturing/testing

Editorial relevance 60 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Bayesian optimization selected designs from over a trillion possible parameter combinations; the laboratory manufactured and mechanically tested candidates, while human researchers monitored and periodically modified the campaign.
Test and result
A human-supervised self-driving laboratory selected and physically tested more than 25,000 additively manufactured polymer shell designs across an 11-dimensional parameter space.
Limitation / still unknown
The result concerns generalized cylindrical shells made from a limited set of polymers and a defined nondimensional metric, whose value depends on threshold stress. The optimization was human-in-the-loop, and transfer to other geometries, materials or operational settings is not established.

31 Oct 2022Boston University

Deep-learning control links single-cell expression to growth responses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A learned predictive controller enabled high-throughput measurements linking gene-expression patterns with functional outcomes in individual engineered bacterial cells. The study exposed heterogeneous growth responses under antibiotic challenge. The original result was public in the October 2022 preprint and later appeared in a 2024 journal article. This is a laboratory model-system study, not a clinical resistance result or validated intervention.
