6 Jun 2024Michigan State University; Stanford University; University of California, Berkeley

PhaseNet-TF expands Tonga earthquake catalog and resolves the intermediate-depth double seismic zone

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A semi-supervised PhaseNet-TF workflow detected and relocated 13,616 events from a one-year Tonga OBS and land-station record, about 11 times the manually picked reference catalog. The denser catalog delineates the intermediate-depth double seismic zone to about 300 km and indicates that its lower plane is limited to roughly 19°–21°S, rather than continuous along the studied section. The study did not recover a deeper 350–460 km double zone; sparse events and location uncertainties limit inference at those depths.

Technology: PhaseNet-TF deep-learning phase picker with semi-supervised iterative retraining

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The time-frequency neural picker detected P/S arrivals in noisy ocean-bottom seismometer data; iterative semi-supervised retraining expanded picks, while phase association and double-difference relocation filtered unreliable events. This expanded catalog enabled the localized double-seismic-zone inference.
Test and result
A semi-supervised PhaseNet-TF workflow detected and relocated 13,616 events from a one-year Tonga OBS and land-station record, about 11 times the manually picked reference catalog.
Limitation / still unknown
The catalog is based on one year of OBS and land data. About half of events are outside the array and have larger hypocenter uncertainty from azimuthal gaps; velocity-model choice adds absolute-location uncertainty. The authors say the absent 350–460 km double zone may reflect insufficient events, not definitive absence. Stress and hydration explanations are interpretations, and some rare events above the slab may be false.

10 Mar 2022Michigan State University; Freie Universität Berlin; Université d’Orléans–CNRS; University of California, Los Angeles; Yale University

Unsupervised clustering links deep-earthquake scaling to hydration differences among northwest Pacific slabs

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: K-means clustering of Japan Meteorological Agency deep-earthquake catalogs separated the northwest Pacific slab segments; Gutenberg–Richter analysis found a magnitude 3.7–3.8 kink in Honshu and Izu seismicity, with b values falling from about 1.4–1.7 to 0.6–0.7. The authors infer that small-event excess and an approximately kilometre-scale metastable-olivine-wedge rim may reflect deep hydration and enhanced transformational faulting.

Technology: Unsupervised machine-learning clustering (K-means, checked against spectral clustering and Gaussian mixture models)

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Clustered deep-earthquake distributions into four slab segments, enabling region-specific b-value analyses that exposed the Honshu/Izu magnitude kink and its proposed relation to slab hydration.
Test and result
K-means clustering of Japan Meteorological Agency deep-earthquake catalogs separated the northwest Pacific slab segments; Gutenberg–Richter analysis found a magnitude 3.7–3.8 kink in Honshu and Izu seismicity, with b values falling from about 1.4–1.7 to 0.6–0.7.
Limitation / still unknown
The observed b-value kink is a statistical result. Hydrous defects and an approximately 1 km unstable wedge rim are the authors’ geodynamic interpretation, not direct water-content measurements; the authors discuss magnitude-detection and model assumptions.
