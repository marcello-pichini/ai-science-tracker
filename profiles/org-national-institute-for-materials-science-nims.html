19 Nov 2025National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS); University of Tokyo

Bayesian optimization experimentally identifies a high-AHE Fe–Co–Ni–Ta–Ir amorphous film

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An autonomous closed-loop search combining composition-spread experiments with Bayesian optimization reached a maximum anomalous Hall resistivity of 10.9 μΩ·cm in an amorphous Fe44.9Co27.9Ni12.1Ta3.3Ir11.7 thin film deposited at room temperature. This measured value exceeded the study’s target of 10 μΩ·cm and the values found in the initial random-exploration cycles.

Technology: Bayesian optimization for composition-spread experiments

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The method selects which five-element alloy composition-spread film to make next and which pair of elements to grade across it, using prior AHE measurements. The closed loop then executes deposition, multi-device fabrication and AHE measurement, feeding results into the next composition proposal; the Bayesian optimization was central to reaching the high-AHE Fe–Co–Ni–Ta–Ir composition.
Test and result
An autonomous closed-loop search combining composition-spread experiments with Bayesian optimization reached a maximum anomalous Hall resistivity of 10.9 μΩ·cm in an amorphous Fe44.9Co27.9Ni12.1Ta3.3Ir11.7 thin film deposited at room temperature.
Limitation / still unknown
The 10.9 μΩ·cm maximum is the best among 234 compositions explored over 18 cycles in this search, not a global proof of the maximum over all compositions. The demonstrated film is amorphous and was measured at room temperature on thermally oxidized silicon; the study does not report a completed sensor device or independent reproduction. Although the experimental decision loop was automated, human sample transfers between instruments remained.

3 Sep 2025National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS)

Machine learning guided discovery of enhanced anomalous Hall resistivity in Fe–Ir–Pt films

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning screen trained on experimental Fe–heavy-element binary alloys pointed to a ternary Fe–Ir–Pt composition range with enhanced anomalous Hall effect. High-throughput composition-spread measurements experimentally found a peak anomalous Hall resistivity of 6.5 μΩ·cm, above the prior 5.25 μΩ·cm maximum reported for Fe–X binary systems. Scaling analysis attributed the enhancement to an extrinsic contribution.

Technology: Gaussian process regression using Magpie composition descriptors

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model was trained on measured Fe–X binary anomalous Hall data and screened 13,260 possible Fe-based ternary compositions for a larger effect. Its predicted Fe–Ir–Pt region guided experimental testing in composition-spread films; the machine-learning prediction and high-throughput measurement together identified the enhanced-effect composition range.
Test and result
A machine-learning screen trained on experimental Fe–heavy-element binary alloys pointed to a ternary Fe–Ir–Pt composition range with enhanced anomalous Hall effect.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a thin-film anomalous Hall resistivity result; the paper does not demonstrate a finished magnetic sensor or device. The machine-learning model overpredicted absolute values for the measured Fe–Ir–Pt compositions, although it qualitatively identified the enhancement and composition trend. The reported extrinsic origin comes from the paper’s scaling analysis. The result is limited to the measured composition-spread films and does not establish performance across bulk material forms or devices.

15 Jun 2022National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS); South China Normal University

Machine-learning-guided Fe₂P-type alloys reach cryogenic magnetocaloric temperatures without rare earths

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An artificial neural network trained on reported experimental compositions guided optimization of Fe₂P-type Mn–Fe–P–Si alloys for sub-77 K magnetocaloric use. Experiments measured a 97 K transition for Mn₁.₇₀Fe₀.₃₀P₀.₆₃Si₀.₃₇ at 1 T; replacing a small fraction of Fe with Co lowered the transition to 73 K. The Co-containing composition showed a 7.5 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ magnetic entropy change and a 1.4 K reversible adiabatic temperature change under a 0–5 T field.

Technology: Artificial neural network trained on a curated, literature-mined dataset of experimental Fe₂P-type magnetocaloric compounds

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The team mined published experimental composition/property results to train an artificial neural network for composition optimization toward lower transition temperatures. Predictions guided selection of Mn₁.₇₀Fe₀.₃₀P₀.₆₃Si₀.₃₇, then a minor Co-for-Fe substitution was tested experimentally to reduce its transition temperature below 77 K. AI guided composition selection; the reported magnetic and caloric properties came from measurements on synthesized samples.
Test and result
An artificial neural network trained on reported experimental compositions guided optimization of Fe₂P-type Mn–Fe–P–Si alloys for sub-77 K magnetocaloric use.
Limitation / still unknown
The reported compositions are rare-earth-free but still contain transition metals and phosphorus/silicon; results characterize laboratory alloys and do not establish a working hydrogen-liquefaction refrigerator. The 73 K transition requires Co substitution and is reported with a modest reduction in magnetization. The 1.4 K reversible adiabatic change is measured over 0–5 T; stated performance and operating window should not be generalized beyond the investigated alloys.
