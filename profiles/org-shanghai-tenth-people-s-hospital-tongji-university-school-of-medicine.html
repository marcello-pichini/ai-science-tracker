6 Oct 2024Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine; Yangpu Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine

Machine learning and Mendelian randomization prioritize CA2 as a diabetic nephropathy biomarker

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across six kidney-expression datasets, researchers identified 314 differentially expressed genes in diabetic nephropathy and used LASSO, SVM-RFE and random forest to prioritize seven candidate genes. Mendelian-randomization integration further prioritized carbonic anhydrase II (CA2); its ROC AUC exceeded 0.878 across the analyzed datasets. The authors also reported qPCR validation of CA2 in clinical patient and control samples and an association with inferred immune-cell infiltration.

Technology: LASSO, support-vector-machine recursive feature elimination and random-forest feature selection

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Three machine-learning approaches integrated candidate-gene selection from kidney-expression datasets; Mendelian randomization with expression-QTL data then prioritized CA2, which was evaluated as a diagnostic biomarker and measured by qPCR in clinical samples. The models supported gene prioritization and discrimination, while qPCR supplied the wet-lab follow-up.
Test and result
The authors also reported qPCR validation of CA2 in clinical patient and control samples and an association with inferred immune-cell infiltration.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a candidate biomarker study, not evidence of a clinically deployed test or patient benefit. Reported AUC values are retrospective dataset performance; qPCR validation supports differential expression but does not establish CA2 as causal or disease-specific. The accessible abstract does not report the clinical validation sample size, so none is inferred.
