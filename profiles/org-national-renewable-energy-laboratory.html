3 Sep 2025National Renewable Energy Laboratory; BOTTLE Consortium; Agile BioFoundry; SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory; Harvard Medical School

Machine-learning-guided mining identifies 91 previously unknown PET hydrolases, including acid-active enzymes

Researchers iteratively combined sequence mining, machine-learning activity/property predictors and high-throughput assays to screen natural enzyme homologs. They experimentally identified 91 previously unknown PET hydrolases among the 212 candidates assayed; 35 had activity on crystalline PET at pH 4.5, and four matched or exceeded a benchmark enzyme under that screening condition.

Technology: Three-round iterative machine-learning pipeline (including VAE/rank model, supervised activity predictors, ESM embeddings and property predictors)

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: Researchers iteratively combined sequence mining, machine-learning activity/property predictors and high-throughput assays to screen natural enzyme homologs.
  • Still unknown: The article defines PET hydrolase by demonstrated PET-depolymerizing activity and notes that activity may be promiscuous relative to the enzyme’s native role. Assays are small-scale and 48 hours; the authors say further enzyme engineering is needed for meaningful bioreactor studies. This is distinct from the catalogued AI-engineered FAST-PETase: it discovers natural homologs and diverse acid/temperature activity profiles rather than optimizing one known PETase scaffold.
  • AI role: Machine-learning activity, thermal-stability and acid-tolerance predictions prioritized natural homologs across successive mining rounds; new assay results updated later predictors. Researchers expressed/purified over 200 candidates and measured PET hydrolysis to verify activity.
