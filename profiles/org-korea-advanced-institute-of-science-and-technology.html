Active learning discovers three high-entropy thermoelectrics with zT above 2

A Gaussian-process model and Bayesian active-learning loop used an average thermoelectric quality factor as a physics-informed descriptor to search a nine-element chalcogenide composition space of 16,206 candidates. Across six iterations, the team synthesized and measured 80 samples; three high-entropy chalcogenides reached peak zT values of about 2.1 at 573 K, 2.3 at 473 K and 2.1 at 723 K. Atomic-scale analysis of one composition also found Cu-rich interstitial clusters and local lattice strain, proposed to contribute to low lattice thermal conductivity.

Technology: Gaussian-process regression with Bayesian active learning and a physics-informed average thermoelectric quality-factor descriptor

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A Gaussian-process model and Bayesian active-learning loop used an average thermoelectric quality factor as a physics-informed descriptor to search a nine-element chalcogenide composition space of 16,206 candidates.
  • Still unknown: The zT values are experimental material-property measurements, not efficiencies demonstrated in a fabricated commercial thermoelectric device. The model used the average quality factor as a surrogate descriptor and trained on only 80 samples from the large composition space; it did not experimentally test all 16,206 candidates. Proposed high-entropy segmented-element efficiencies assume idealized interfaces. The Cu interstitial-cluster mechanism is supported by microscopy/spectroscopy for the characterized sample but is the authors’ interpretation of why performance improves.
  • AI role: The model used uncertainty-aware exploration and exploitation to select the next formulations for synthesis from a 16,206-composition design space. Experimental feedback from 80 samples across six active-learning iterations updated the Gaussian-process surrogate; model-guided selection yielded three compositions with measured zT above 2. The authors then characterized the materials and interpreted atomic arrangements and transport properties.
