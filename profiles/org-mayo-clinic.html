29 Apr 2026Mayo Clinic

REDMOD: pancreatic cancer signals in CT scans taken before diagnosis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Among nearly 2,000 abdominal CT scans originally considered normal, an AI radiomics model identified 73% of prediagnostic pancreatic cancers, with a median lead time of about 16 months. Validation included images from multiple institutions and protocols. This analysis used scans taken before diagnosis; clinical benefit and false positives in practice still need prospective evaluation.

Technology: REDMOD (Radiomics-based Early Detection Model)

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fully automated radiomics framework with AI segmentation and an ensemble classifier on a 40-feature signature from standard-of-care CT scans.
Test and result
REDMOD was trained on 969 patients from a multi-institutional cohort and tested on an independent set of 493 patients (63 pre-diagnostic), with external specificity checks on further datasets and a comparison with radiologists; AUC 0.82 and 73.0% sensitivity, versus 38.9% for radiologists.
Limitation / still unknown
The article states that prospective validation in high-risk cohorts is the necessary next step; no explicit limitations section.

22 Aug 2022Mayo Clinic; University of Rochester Medical Center; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

AI detects Parkinson's disease from nocturnal breathing signals

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An attention-based AI model extracting breathing from radio waves during sleep detected Parkinson's disease with an AUC of 0.90 on held-out data and 0.85 on external sets, across 7,671 individuals. Its severity estimates correlated with MDS-UPDRS scores (R = 0.94), and it worked for at-home assessment. Caveat: largely retrospective data and no prospective diagnostic trial reported.

Technology: Attention-based deep learning on contactless radio-wave breathing signals

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Attention-based AI model extracting breathing from radio waves during sleep to detect and grade Parkinson's disease.
Test and result
Parkinson's detection was tested on held-out data (AUC 0.90) and external test sets (AUC 0.85) across 7,671 individuals, and severity estimates were compared with MDS-UPDRS scores (R = 0.94).
Limitation / still unknown
The authors describe the findings as initial evidence that the model may be useful for risk assessment before clinical diagnosis.

6 May 2021Mayo Clinic

EAGLE: AI electrocardiograms to detect reduced heart function

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A randomized study across 120 primary-care groups at 45 sites evaluated neural-network alerts on electrocardiograms from 22,641 adults. The intervention produced five additional diagnoses of low ejection fraction per thousand patients, without a global increase in echocardiography use. The result concerns identification of dysfunction, not improved clinical outcomes.

Technology: Convolutional neural network for electrocardiograms

Editorial relevance 73 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Neural network analysed electrocardiograms and alerted clinicians to possible low ejection fraction.
Test and result
A cluster-randomised trial across 120 primary-care teams (22,641 adults) compared AI-ECG alerts with usual care on new low-EF diagnoses within 90 days.
Limitation / still unknown
Not reported in the abstract read; the trial measured diagnosis rather than clinical outcomes.
