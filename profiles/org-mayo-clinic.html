20 Nov 2026Mayo Clinic; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Cornell University

A retrospective prostate-cancer messaging study finds different strengths in GPT-4 and clinician replies

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A retrospective study compared clinical-team replies with a GPT-4-based bot for 158 prostate-cancer patient inquiries. Clinicians rated team replies higher on completeness, correctness and clarity, while bot replies scored higher on empathy and more often required major editing. Four LLM graders favored bot replies and showed lower inter-grader agreement than clinician graders. The reviewed publisher abstract describes an evolving rubric and response-quality evaluation, without establishing patient outcomes, prospective workflow utility or clinical safety effects; full Methods were inaccessible.

Technology: GPT-4-based patient-message drafting bot and LLM-based graders

Family GPT-4: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A GPT-4-based bot drafts responses in a specific oncology workflow and LLM graders evaluate the same response set; the study measures concrete strengths and limitations of this AI-assisted communication task.
Test and result
Retrospective comparative assessment of replies to 158 prostate-cancer patient inquiries, using three human evaluation rounds and one LLM-grader round. Clinicians rated team replies higher in completeness, correctness and clarity; the bot higher in empathy; bot replies required major edits more often. LLM graders favored bot replies and showed low agreement (alpha 0.07–0.18) versus clinician graders (0.53–0.81).
Limitation / still unknown
The article abstract does not establish that patients received the generated replies, any effect on patient outcomes, or prospective clinical utility. Full Methods were not accessible from the Nature article route during this check; the rubric changed across the clinician rounds.

29 Apr 2026Mayo Clinic

REDMOD: pancreatic cancer signals in CT scans taken before diagnosis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Among nearly 2,000 abdominal CT scans originally considered normal, an AI radiomics model identified 73% of prediagnostic pancreatic cancers, with a median lead time of about 16 months. Validation included images from multiple institutions and protocols. This analysis used scans taken before diagnosis; clinical benefit and false positives in practice still need prospective evaluation.

Technology: REDMOD (Radiomics-based Early Detection Model)

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Press releasePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fully automated radiomics framework with AI segmentation and an ensemble classifier on a 40-feature signature from standard-of-care CT scans.
Test and result
REDMOD was trained on 969 patients from a multi-institutional cohort and tested on an independent set of 493 patients (63 pre-diagnostic), with external specificity checks on further datasets and a comparison with radiologists; AUC 0.82 and 73.0% sensitivity, versus 38.9% for radiologists.
Limitation / still unknown
The article states that prospective validation in high-risk cohorts is the necessary next step; no explicit limitations section.

28 Sep 2023Mayo Clinic

A seven-feature model estimates complications after congenital heart reoperation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers trained a retrospective seven-feature risk model in 1,011 adults undergoing repeat congenital heart surgery. The selected gradient-boosted model reported test AUC 0.763 and validation AUC 0.782 for a composite of death and major complications. External validation and evidence of clinical benefit remain absent.

Technology: XGBoost gradient boosting and SHAP feature analysis

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Gradient boosting fitted clinical variables to a composite postoperative outcome; SHAP ranked model contributions and supported a reduced seven-variable model.
Test and result
The 2010–2022 single-center cohort was randomly split 70%/20%/10% for training/testing/validation. Eighteen preselected clinical variables were reduced to seven using SHAP contribution. Systemic ventricular ejection fraction was most influential in the fitted models. Reduced-model test/validation AUCs were .763/.782, with reported validation CI .568–.804 and five-fold range .631–.764.
Limitation / still unknown
Authors selected a model at the 90th percentile AUC from bootstrapped model distributions; this is not an unbiased locked-model estimate. Rare composite events, single-center surgical selection and feature/model selection constrain generalization. Logistic-regression AUC .727 had overlapping confidence intervals, so superiority is not established. Conventional survival analysis of heart failure is separate from the machine-learning result, and SHAP influence is not causation.

26 Apr 2023Mayo Clinic

AI-assisted whole-pancreas MRI measurements correlate with CT attenuation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 158 paired MRI/CT examinations, whole-pancreas fat fraction and CT attenuation showed a Spearman correlation of −.755. A locally trained U-Net assisted part of the MRI annotation, with manual correction of every assisted segmentation. The retrospective study also found substantial slice-to-slice variability; it did not independently validate automated segmentation or disease prediction.

Technology: U-Net pancreas annotation assistance and CNN-based liver segmentation

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A U-Net trained during the annotation process assisted later MRI masks before manual correction; a separate CNN estimated liver fat. Learned segmentation aided measurement collection, while conventional correlations provided the scientific result.
Test and result
Of 543 potentially paired examinations, selection/exclusion yielded 158 subjects with MRI and non-contrast CT within six months, plus 59 MRI-only examinations for 217 total MRI measurements. Mean pancreatic MRI fat fraction correlated inversely with CT attenuation (Spearman −.755); slice-to-slice MRI variation increased with mean whole-pancreas fat fraction (Spearman .51). The prior CT-only study is acknowledged separately.
Limitation / still unknown
All pancreas masks were manually drawn or manually corrected after CNN assistance. The source does not quantify how many final subjects required learned assistance, and this is not a model-accuracy validation. The locally trained MRI U-Net requires independent testing according to the authors. Referral-based inclusion, exclusion of inflammatory/neoplastic disease, image-quality selection, imperfect MRI resolution and up-to-six-month modality separation constrain interpretation. No pancreatic histological ground truth or equivalence of fat measures is established by correlation, and demographics/diabetes associations are observational. The source extends earlier CT-only measurements; it is not a first discovery that fat and CT attenuation are related.

22 Aug 2022Mayo Clinic; University of Rochester Medical Center; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

AI detects Parkinson's disease from nocturnal breathing signals

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An attention-based AI model extracting breathing from radio waves during sleep detected Parkinson's disease with an AUC of 0.90 on held-out data and 0.85 on external sets, across 7,671 individuals. Its severity estimates correlated with MDS-UPDRS scores (R = 0.94), and it worked for at-home assessment. Caveat: largely retrospective data and no prospective diagnostic trial reported.

Technology: Attention-based deep learning on contactless radio-wave breathing signals

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Attention-based AI model extracting breathing from radio waves during sleep to detect and grade Parkinson's disease.
Test and result
Parkinson's detection was tested on held-out data (AUC 0.90) and external test sets (AUC 0.85) across 7,671 individuals, and severity estimates were compared with MDS-UPDRS scores (R = 0.94).
Limitation / still unknown
The authors describe the findings as initial evidence that the model may be useful for risk assessment before clinical diagnosis.

6 May 2021Mayo Clinic

EAGLE: AI electrocardiograms to detect reduced heart function

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A randomized study across 120 primary-care groups at 45 sites evaluated neural-network alerts on electrocardiograms from 22,641 adults. The intervention produced five additional diagnoses of low ejection fraction per thousand patients, without a global increase in echocardiography use. The result concerns identification of dysfunction, not improved clinical outcomes.

Technology: Convolutional neural network for electrocardiograms

Editorial relevance 73 medicine Press releasePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Neural network analysed electrocardiograms and alerted clinicians to possible low ejection fraction.
Test and result
A cluster-randomised trial across 120 primary-care teams (22,641 adults) compared AI-ECG alerts with usual care on new low-EF diagnoses within 90 days.
Limitation / still unknown
Not reported in the abstract read; the trial measured diagnosis rather than clinical outcomes.
