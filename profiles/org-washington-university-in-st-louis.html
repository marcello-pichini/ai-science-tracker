16 Nov 2026Washington University in St. Louis

QORA designs quantum control sequences that extend spin coherence in diamond

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In an arXiv preprint, Washington University in St. Louis researchers report that QORA designed AID-ROD-1, a sequence that nearly doubled measured coherence time relative to DROID-C3PO in a dense ensemble of spin-1 nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond. The agent system used simulation and laboratory feedback under human oversight. The research team subsequently analyzed the mechanisms behind the improvement. The comparison is specific to the tested ensemble, and independent replication was not established in the reviewed preprint.

Technology: QORA (GPT-6 Astra and Sorol)

8 Feb 2023Washington University in St. Louis; collaborating institutions

CellOracle-guided zebrafish perturbations identify axial-mesoderm effects of noto and lhx1a loss

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The CellOracle machine-learning workflow used single-cell gene-regulatory networks to prioritize transcription factors for perturbation in zebrafish development. CRISPR/Cas9 tests confirmed a model-predicted, previously unreported increase in prechordal-plate differentiation after loss of noto, alongside the known loss of notochord. Model-prioritized lhx1a loss also inhibited axial-mesoderm differentiation: single-cell RNA-seq found expansion of early notochord cells and reductions in later notochord and prechordal-plate populations; orthogonal RNA assays supported reduced notochord/prechordal markers.

Technology: CellOracle, a machine-learning gene-regulatory-network model with in-silico transcription-factor perturbation

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model built context-specific networks from single-cell data, simulated transcription-factor loss, and prioritized noto and lhx1a for experimental follow-up. Researchers used CRISPR/Cas9 crispants, single-cell RNA-seq and marker assays to test the predicted zebrafish cell-fate effects; the prechordal-plate increase after noto loss was reported as previously unrecognized.
Test and result
CRISPR/Cas9 tests confirmed a model-predicted, previously unreported increase in prechordal-plate differentiation after loss of noto, alongside the known loss of notochord.
Limitation / still unknown
Evidence is from zebrafish crispants during embryonic development, not stable mutants or mammalian/human systems; the authors report no gross morphological change despite transcriptomic/cell-population effects. The CellOracle model depends on inferred networks and makes cell-state-shift predictions, not direct causal proof of all inferred edges. A later unreviewed 2024 preprint raises implementation and benchmarking concerns, particularly about its use of ATAC-seq for distal regulatory interactions and comparisons with other GRN tools; this record relies only on the specific perturbation predictions tested experimentally and does not adopt the paper's broader benchmarking claims.
