CHANCE uses tumor genomes to prioritize non-cancer drugs with activity in patient-derived cancer cells

CHANCE, a supervised machine-learning model integrating patient-specific coding and non-coding mutations with drug and protein-interaction information, screened 5,197 TCGA/ICGC tumors against 923 FDA-approved non-oncology drugs. For ex-vivo validation, the investigators sequenced tumor cells from seven patients with pancreatic or esophageal cancer and tested CHANCE-prioritized drugs; at least one predicted non-oncology drug showed anticancer activity in tumor cells from five of the seven patients. This is patient-specific cell-assay validation, not a treatment-response study in patients.

Technology: CHANCE, a supervised gradient-boosted-tree model integrating coding/non-coding variants, protein–protein interaction networks, drug targets and drug-response data
