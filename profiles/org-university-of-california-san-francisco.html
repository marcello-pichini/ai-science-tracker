CellTransformer maps previously uncatalogued regions of the mouse brain

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A transformer model applied to spatial transcriptomics mapped fine-grained mouse-brain tissue domains and revealed hundreds of regions absent from existing anatomical atlases. The study evaluated the data-driven domains against the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework and reported strong alignment alongside the novel subdivisions.

21 Feb 2024University of California, San Francisco; Stanford University; University of California health-system collaborators; University of Oxford

EHR machine learning and genetic colocalization link osteoporosis to female Alzheimer’s risk near MS4A6A

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random-forest models trained on UCSF records (749 expert-diagnosed Alzheimer’s disease cases and 250,545 controls) identified clinical features predictive up to seven years before the recorded onset; the seven-year model’s AUROC was 0.72 using clinical features alone. Matched-cohort follow-up in external University of California health-system records found faster Alzheimer’s diagnosis after osteoporosis in women. Genetic colocalization analyses supported a female-specific shared signal between Alzheimer’s disease, low heel bone mineral density and MS4A6A expression near the MS4A gene cluster. The paper also reports shared APOE-locus support for hyperlipidemia and Alzheimer’s disease.

Technology: Random-forest models on longitudinal electronic health records, interpreted with the SPOKE biomedical knowledge network

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Random-forest models ranked EHR-derived clinical features associated with future Alzheimer’s diagnosis. The team used matched models to prioritize hypotheses, then used SPOKE to connect clinical predictors to biological networks and independent health-system data plus GWAS/eQTL colocalization to examine the osteoporosis and hyperlipidemia associations. The model-generated prioritization led to follow-up of the female-specific osteoporosis signal near MS4A6A.
Test and result
Matched-cohort follow-up in external University of California health-system records found faster Alzheimer’s diagnosis after osteoporosis in women.
Limitation / still unknown
This is hypothesis-generating observational work. EHR features may reflect healthcare access, clinician behavior, coding and biological processes; the authors explicitly state that further validation across omics data types is needed. Genetic colocalization and matched cohort associations do not establish that osteoporosis causes Alzheimer’s disease or that treating osteoporosis prevents it. The prediction model is not a prospective clinical-impact evaluation, and early risk discrimination is modest with the low-prevalence outcome.

ExoGRU-guided experiments support RBM24 involvement in small-RNA secretion

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A recurrent model of RNA-sequence features helped nominate RNA-binding proteins involved in secretion. This mechanism study was public in an April 2023 preprint before the 2024 journal version. It does not establish a universal secretion mechanism, in-vivo benefit or a therapeutic result.

8 Jun 2021University of California, San Francisco; Oregon Health & Science University

Cross-tissue machine learning prioritizes a 13-gene rheumatoid-arthritis activity score

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning feature-selection pipeline integrated public whole-blood (1,885 samples) and synovial-tissue (284 samples) transcriptomic datasets and selected a 13-gene rheumatoid-arthritis score. Across five independent transcriptomic datasets, the score tracked DAS28 disease activity, distinguished rheumatoid arthritis from osteoarthritis, separated polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis from healthy controls, and was associated with treatment effects. In an independent immunoblot cohort, two of six tested proteins, TNFAIP6/TSG-6 and HSP90AB1/HSP90, were confirmed.

Technology: Cross-tissue machine-learning feature-selection pipeline for transcriptomic data

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The pipeline selected a compact 13-gene signature from public blood and synovial expression datasets; the study evaluated its association with disease activity and diagnostic distinctions across five independent transcriptomic datasets, then tested six selected proteins by immunoblot in a separate patient cohort.
Test and result
In an independent immunoblot cohort, two of six tested proteins, TNFAIP6/TSG-6 and HSP90AB1/HSP90, were confirmed.
Limitation / still unknown
The transcriptomic inputs were previously published datasets and the reported clinical associations are observational; the study does not establish a deployable diagnostic test or causal disease mechanism. The independent protein follow-up confirmed two of six proteins tested, not the entire 13-gene panel, and validation in larger prospective cohorts is needed. Treatment-effect association should not be interpreted as prospective prediction of individual response.

14 May 2021University of California, San Francisco; Harvard Medical School

Neural network ensemble for prenatal detection of complex congenital heart disease

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An ensemble of neural networks trained on 107,823 fetal ultrasound images reached an AUC of 0.99, 95% sensitivity and 96% specificity in an internal test set of 4,108 fetal surveys with 0.9% CHD. Its sensitivity was comparable to that of clinicians and it stayed robust on outside-hospital and lower-quality images. Caveat: retrospective study with a low-prevalence internal test set.

Technology: Ensemble of convolutional neural networks with segmentation models

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An ensemble of neural networks identifies cardiac views and classifies normal versus complex congenital heart disease.
Test and result
Trained on 107,823 images from 1,326 retrospective studies; on 4,108 fetal surveys (0.9% CHD), AUC 0.99, 95% sensitivity, 96% specificity, 100% negative predictive value; comparable to clinicians and robust on outside-hospital images.
Limitation / still unknown
Prospective screening performance is not reported in the abstract.
