6 Aug 2026University of Chicago

Machine-learning-guided CsrA homolog screens reveal divergent bacterial regulatory activities

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Swarm-seq assay tested more than 500 bacterial CsrA homologs for regulation of Bacillus subtilis swarming and found two functional classes: several homologs strongly inhibited swarming while others failed despite conserved sequence and canonical GGA motifs in the hag transcript. A random-forest model trained on these assays predicted homolog activity across thousands of sequences; experimental testing supported predicted Class I regulatory activity for Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus CsrA and Pseudomonas putida RsmA. The findings indicate sequence conservation and the canonical RNA motif do not alone determine these tested regulatory effects.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning classifier trained on Swarm-seq functional assay data

High-throughput swarming assays generated a functional dataset; machine learning was then trained to classify homolog functional activity across the bacterial domain and nominate homologs for validation. Selected CsrABb and RsmAPp predictions were experimentally validated.

25 Sep 2025University of Chicago

Active learning identifies high-performing electrolyte solvents for anode-free lithium metal cells

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers used sequential Bayesian experimental design with Bayesian model averaging to select electrolyte solvents for testing in Cu||LiFePO4 cells, feeding cycle-capacity results back into the model over seven campaigns. The study reports seven top-performing solvent electrolytes and says four distinct solvents rival state-of-the-art performance; three were previously unreported as electrolytes for anode-free cells. Five of nine compounds tested in the final batch exceeded 100 mAh/g discharge capacity at cycle 20. The result is limited to the tested cell configuration and cycling conditions and does not establish commercial battery performance.

Technology: Active learning with sequential Bayesian experimental design, Bayesian model averaging and Gaussian-process surrogate models

Editorial relevance 60 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A sequential active-learning framework selected each batch of candidate solvents using Bayesian experimental design and Bayesian model averaging, then incorporated measured electrochemical outcomes to update its next selections.
Test and result
Electrolytes were tested iteratively in Cu||LiFePO4 cells. The study reports seven top-performing solvents; three had not previously been reported as anode-free battery electrolytes, and four distinct solvents rivaled state-of-the-art performance. Five of nine batch-7 electrolytes exceeded 100 mAh/g discharge capacity at cycle 20.
Limitation / still unknown
Performance is limited to the reported cell chemistry, 1 M LiFSA configuration and cycling protocol; it does not establish full-cell energy density, lifetime in commercial formats or manufacturing readiness.

3 Sep 2024University of Chicago; Old Dominion University; Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1; ENS de Lyon; CNRS

Neural operators reveal nonreciprocal interactions behind microfluidic nonlinear waves

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Interpreting a microfluidic droplet experiment movie with a Fourier neural operator and sparse regression yielded a KdV–Burgers-type continuum model containing a dispersive term absent from the prior Burgers description. The authors’ simulations attribute the nonlinear-wave propagation and stabilization to nonreciprocal hydrodynamic interactions. The work analyzes existing experimental data and does not report a new experiment or a general proof for all nonequilibrium crystals.

Technology: Fourier neural operator, interpretable neural-network pipeline and sparse regression

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A Fourier neural operator learned latent experimental dynamics from the microfluidic movie; sparse regression extracted the governing equation, and the learned term guided the interpretation of nonreciprocal hydrodynamic interactions.
Test and result
The authors trained/interpreted a neural-operator network on a microfluidic droplet experiment movie and used sparse regression to derive a KdV–Burgers-type model. The new dispersive term improved the shock-front description relative to Burgers’ equation; a microscopic model attributes nonlinear-wave stabilization/propagation to nonreciprocal hydrodynamic interactions. The source says the model was trained on an existing experimental movie.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a model-derived interpretation of a specific microfluidic experiment and supporting simulations, not a new experiment or a general proof for all nonequilibrium crystals.
