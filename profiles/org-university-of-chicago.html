Machine-learning-guided CsrA homolog screens reveal divergent bacterial regulatory activities

A Swarm-seq assay tested more than 500 bacterial CsrA homologs for regulation of Bacillus subtilis swarming and found two functional classes: several homologs strongly inhibited swarming while others failed despite conserved sequence and canonical GGA motifs in the hag transcript. A random-forest model trained on these assays predicted homolog activity across thousands of sequences; experimental testing supported predicted Class I regulatory activity for Bdellovibrio bacteriovorus CsrA and Pseudomonas putida RsmA. The findings indicate sequence conservation and the canonical RNA motif do not alone determine these tested regulatory effects.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning classifier trained on Swarm-seq functional assay data

DeepScribe: reading cuneiform signs on Persepolis tablets

A machine-learning model recognizes signs on tablets from the Persepolis Fortification Archive with about 80% accuracy on tablets excluded from training (over 6,000 annotated images and over 100,000 signs in the database). About 20% of signs are wrong; testing is internal only, and the source is a university announcement.

Technology: DeepScribe (machine learning on images)
