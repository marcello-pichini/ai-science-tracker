SCimilarity links fibrosis-associated macrophage states across diseases and validates a matching in-vitro cell state

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: SCimilarity searched a 23.4-million-cell reference atlas spanning 412 single-cell RNA-sequencing studies using query profiles from interstitial-lung-disease macrophages and fibroblasts. It identified fibrosis-associated macrophage-like states in other fibrotic diseases, COVID-19 and selected tumour datasets, and a 3D hydrogel culture as a high-ranking in-vitro match; the authors report experimentally demonstrating that this system reproduces the queried macrophage state.

Technology: SCimilarity, a deep metric-learning model for querying cell-state similarity across single-cell atlases

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model ranked cell profiles in a 23.4-million-cell atlas by similarity to ILD-derived macrophage/fibroblast query profiles, linking these states to disease/tissue contexts and nominating the 3D hydrogel culture for experimental follow-up.
Test and result
It identified fibrosis-associated macrophage-like states in other fibrotic diseases, COVID-19 and selected tumour datasets, and a 3D hydrogel culture as a high-ranking in-vitro match; the authors report experimentally demonstrating that this system reproduces the queried macrophage state.
Limitation / still unknown
Most cross-disease and tumour-context links are computational similarity observations in previously published single-cell datasets; they do not establish causal roles in those diseases. Some rare tissue/cancer matches are from individual datasets or samples. The in-vitro 3D hydrogel assay is the experimentally tested part; broad disease associations need further biological validation.
