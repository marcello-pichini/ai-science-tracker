26 Oct 2026East China Normal University; Brunel University of London

Layers important to LoRA adaptation varied across tasks

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In an intervention study on fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B, the seven layers most important to adaptation had a mean pairwise overlap of 39.5% by the Jaccard measure across four tasks. Only the first three layers appeared in every top-seven list.

Technology: Qwen2.5-0.5B; low-rank adaptation; activation path patching

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Path patching intervened on learned LoRA activations to measure each layer’s contribution on four datasets.
Test and result
The intervention compares layer-level learned contributions, then ranks the layers separately for the four task datasets.
Limitation / still unknown
The overlap depends on selected datasets, model, rankings and layer cutoff. It does not establish identical sparsity across architectures.

20 Oct 2026East China Normal University; London School of Economics and Political Science

Graph learning found a fairness–cooperation link in a British survey sample

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A learned graph fitted to 1,805 Great Britain responses in the European Social Survey consistently linked perceived police procedural fairness with willingness to cooperate. The link appeared in all 20 near-best graph classes, whereas the link between moral alignment and cooperation was uncertain. Directions in the reported ordered graph came from the researchers' theory-based ordering, so the findings describe relationships in survey responses.

Technology: DAGGLE; latent Gaussian graph structure learning

Editorial relevance 40 humanities PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
DAGGLE learned latent-construct adjacency structure jointly with measurement and structural parameters. It used likelihood-based edge screening and comparison across graph equivalence classes; theory supplied any reported link orientation.
Test and result
The study examined 15 survey measures and five latent constructs. It compared candidates with the published structural equation model and exhaustively assessed the small graph-class space in addition to the fitted search. The fairness–cooperation association was retained with the same sign in reported subgroup fits under the specified ordering.
Limitation / still unknown
The associations are retrospective and model-dependent. The data do not identify the direction of the fairness–cooperation relationship, or show that changing policing would cause a change in willingness to cooperate.

3 Mar 2026East China Normal University; Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology; Pacific Climate Impacts Consortium; University of Victoria

Machine learning uses historical warming patterns to constrain future climate projections

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A CatBoost model trained on CMIP6 simulations used the observed 1971–2020 grid-cell warming pattern to identify tropical and polar regions that best constrain later global warming. Applying these spatial constraints reduced projection error variance by about 70%, compared with about 48% using global-mean warming alone; the results depend on model-based emergent relationships and are projections rather than direct observations of future climate.

Technology: CatBoost machine-learning emergent-constraint model

Editorial relevance 53 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned grid-cell-scale relationships between historical warming fields and future global mean warming across CMIP6 simulations, then ranked grid cells by their influence on constrained projections.
Test and result
A CatBoost model trained on CMIP6 simulations used the observed 1971–2020 grid-cell warming pattern to identify tropical and polar regions that best constrain later global warming.
Limitation / still unknown
The study is a climate-model emergent-constraint analysis; uncertainty reduction was assessed with leave-one-model-out cross-validation, not by validating future outcomes. The reported probabilities are scenario-conditional projections.

5 Nov 2024East China Normal University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Westlake University; Microsoft Research Asia; collaborators

XRD-guided learning identifies Li₆AsSe₅I as a room-temperature superionic conductor

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: XRDMatch, a semi-supervised model using X-ray diffraction patterns as its descriptor, screened unlabeled crystal data and identified 38 lithium superionic-conductor predictions. The paper says 32 were supported by prior literature, six were new candidates assessed with ab initio molecular dynamics, and Li₆AsSe₅I was synthesized and experimentally confirmed as a superionic conductor. The source abstract does not establish that all 38 are new or experimentally validated; this record covers the single reported synthesized material.

Technology: XRDMatch semi-supervised learning framework

Editorial relevance 53 materials Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling let the model learn from limited labeled conductivity data and unlabeled ICSD X-ray diffraction patterns. It nominated Li₆AsSe₅I for synthesis, where the researchers experimentally confirmed superionic conductivity.
Test and result
XRDMatch, a semi-supervised model using X-ray diffraction patterns as its descriptor, screened unlabeled crystal data and identified 38 lithium superionic-conductor predictions.
Limitation / still unknown
The publisher abstract says six newly predicted candidates were quantified with ab initio molecular dynamics, but names only Li₆AsSe₅I as synthesized and experimentally confirmed. It reports 32 additional predictions matched recent literature. The full publisher article was inaccessible during the source review (HTTP 403); the annotation is limited to the primary publisher abstract and its article metadata.
