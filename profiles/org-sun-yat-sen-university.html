24 Nov 2026Sun Yat-sen University; Guangdong Pharmaceutical University; East China Normal University

Learned brain-activity profiles guide tested drug leads in zebrafish models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A November 24, 2026 preprint combined large-scale zebrafish brain imaging, learned clustering and random-forest ranking to select drug conditions for follow-up. Several candidates improved movement in larval toxin models and preserved dopamine-neuron reporter signals under some conditions. These are preliminary, compound- and model-dependent findings rather than evidence of Parkinson’s disease treatment in people. Some screened conditions were toxic, and retained fluorescence does not by itself establish prevention of neuronal death.

Technology: CNSDIP; consensus clustering and random-forest ranking of brain-activity maps

Editorial relevance 47 biology PreprintScientific article Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Consensus clustering linked drug-induced brain-activity patterns to a therapeutic reference class; a random forest trained on 363 CNS/non-CNS drugs then ranked candidates for new behavioral and cellular tests. The measured follow-up, rather than classifier accuracy alone, is the admitted result.
Test and result
Sixteen top-ranked conditions underwent follow-up; after toxicity exclusions, five compounds and one pair improved movement in tested conditions. Behavioral comparisons used n=10 and dopamine-reporter cell-count comparisons n=5. Blinded imaging counts supported preservation of identifiable reporter-positive cells for some candidates across three larval toxin models.
Limitation / still unknown
The assays are small and preclinical; not every candidate worked across every model or outcome. Five conditions showed long-term toxicity. Reporter loss or preservation can reflect neuronal phenotype rather than irreversible death or true neuroprotection. Known drug mechanisms explain some effects, and neither efficacy nor safety in people is established.

1 Nov 2026Sun Yat-sen University; State Key Laboratory of Ophthalmology; Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center, Sun Yat-sen University

Smartphone 3D reconstruction measures eyelid ptosis in a prospective multicenter study

Source check: Source checked (abstract only). Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The Digital Rulers of Ptosis system reconstructs the eye region in 3D from a 10-second smartphone video to measure marginal reflex distance 1 (MRD1) and levator function. Across 952 participants (1904 eyes) at 5 hospitals, mean absolute error against specialist measurement was 0.52 mm for MRD1 and 0.95 mm for levator function, with AUC 0.97 for diagnosing ptosis and 0.98 for identifying the surgical approach. Among 206 surgical participants (248 eyes), 3-month recurrence was 7.3%; the authors state that further studies are needed to evaluate clinical impact.

30 Jan 2026Sun Yat-sen University

Learned brain-imaging measurements agree with expert references across separate cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A CNN and YOLO-based pipeline automated region selection for a brain diffusion-imaging index. In a separate 1000-person UK Biobank test, agreement with the average of two neurologists’ measurements gave an intraclass correlation of 0.90; smaller OASIS3 and EPAD tests gave 0.80 and 0.78. These validate measurement agreement, not direct brain waste clearance or clinical benefit.

Technology: VGG-style convolutional network; YOLOv5s; dALPS

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
Development used 1000 subjects from four other cohorts, with subject-level training/validation splits. The finalized pipeline was tested against averaged expert measurements in 1000 UKB subjects and 200 each from OASIS3 and EPAD.
Limitation / still unknown

Abstract and discussion quote ICC 0.95, while specific independent-test Results give0.90/0.80/0.78; this entry follows those explicit test results. No causal genetic, medication or disease mechanism, treatment guidance or outcome benefit is represented. Supplementary methods were not independently inspected. 23 Jun 2025Sun Yat-sen University

Machine learning identifies disordered twist-angle sequences with strongly reduced thermal conductivity in multilayer graphene

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Bayesian optimization and a CNN, coupled to non-equilibrium molecular dynamics, selected two-angle disordered twisted multilayer graphene structures with substantially lower simulated cross-plane thermal conductivity than aligned and periodic stacks. The optimized 14-layer sequence reached 0.095 W m−1 K−1 versus 0.512 W m−1 K−1 in aligned graphene in the reported model; phonon participation ratios and dynamical structure factors support strong partial phonon localization.

30 May 2023Sun Yat-sen University; Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University

Deep learning on endoscopic ultrasound images stratifies gastric GIST risk

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Models built on 1320 endoscopic ultrasound images from two hospitals were tested on 656 images from external cohorts. The four-category risk model had 74.50% accuracy and 55.00% sensitivity on external tumors, and the two-category model for very low-risk GISTs reached 86.25% accuracy and 94.44% sensitivity. The study is retrospective and image-level with modest four-class sensitivity.

Technology: Deep-learning classifier on endoscopic ultrasound images for GIST risk category
