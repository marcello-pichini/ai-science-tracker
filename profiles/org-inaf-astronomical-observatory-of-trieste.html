21 Oct 2020INAF Astronomical Observatory of Trieste; INAF Astronomical Observatory of Padova; Las Campanas Observatory; Sapienza University of Rome; other QUBRICS collaboration institutions

The QUBRICS machine-learning survey spectroscopically identifies 224 new bright high-redshift quasars

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The QUBRICS survey applied a machine-learning selection pipeline to southern optical and infrared catalogs and followed candidates spectroscopically. The paper reports 224 newly identified bright quasars at z≥2.5, including 15 at z>4, and provides a large southern sample for cosmological observations.

Technology: QUBRICS machine-learning photometric selection with Canonical Correlation Analysis redshift regression

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine-learning selection ranked photometric sources for follow-up; the team obtained and analyzed optical/infrared spectra to identify and measure redshifts. The paper reports the cumulative survey sample, not 224 objects newly found by a single autonomous run.
Test and result
The QUBRICS survey applied a machine-learning selection pipeline to southern optical and infrared catalogs and followed candidates spectroscopically.
Limitation / still unknown
The 224 are cumulative QUBRICS discoveries across its pilot and follow-up publications. The paper estimates a 68% success rate for the current selection; contaminants include lower-redshift quasars/AGN. Five objects in the sample had already appeared in literature and were independently selected, so the total is a survey result, not 224 exclusive first publications in this paper.
