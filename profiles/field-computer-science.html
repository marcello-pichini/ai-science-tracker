5 Oct 2026Anthropic; Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Claude discovers algorithms that refute the 3SUM and APSP time-complexity hypotheses

Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents. The single underlying technique computes selected entries of thin matrix products more efficiently, and known reductions yield the main algorithmic consequences. The paper says an internal Anthropic Claude research model discovered the algorithm autonomously; the human authors then understood, simplified, strengthened and extended it. One paper-level theoretical-computer-science result is counted.

Technology: Internal Anthropic Claude research model (specific model version undisclosed)

17 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude optimizes more than 30 open models for biology

In just under four weeks, Claude optimized over 30 models for structure, protein design, and genomics. They were on average four times faster with a small accuracy loss, and nearly twice as fast with identical outputs. It also created a low-memory mode for systems over 10,000 tokens on a single GPU node. Design results are in silico only, and comparisons are internal to Anthropic.

Technology: Claude (in Claude Science)

1 May 2026Massachusetts Institute of Technology; ETH Zurich; Stanford University

New Zarankiewicz numbers and bounds from OpenEvolve search

Using the open-source OpenEvolve LLM-driven evolutionary framework with a custom reward, the authors find exact values Z(11,21,3,3)=116, Z(11,22,3,3)=121 and Z(12,22,3,3)=132, and lower bounds for 41 further parameter sets, at under $30 per case. Caveats: arXiv preprint not peer reviewed; a separate NeurIPS 2026 listing by overlapping authors reports different counts.

Technology: OpenEvolve (LLM-guided evolutionary search, AlphaEvolve-style)

25 Mar 2026University of London; University of Warwick; University of Oxford; London Institute for Mathematical Sciences

Transformer-guided genetic search finds six new champion linear error-correcting codes over F₈

A transformer trained to predict minimum Hamming distance, paired with a genetic algorithm and exact Brouwer–Zimmermann distance checks, found at least six previously unknown champion generalized toric codes over F₈ at dimensions 18, 22 and 38 (length 49). The article reports more than 500 champion codes in its F₈ search and up to a twofold reduction in exact distance evaluations versus random search. The six records at the three newly improved parameter settings are one paper-level coding-theory result.

Technology: Transformer model adapted from GPT-2, coupled with a genetic algorithm

28 Feb 2026Stanford University; Anthropic

Claude's Cycles: a Hamiltonian cycle decomposition found by Claude

Knuth's note reports that Claude Opus 4.6 found an explicit decomposition of the arcs of the m^3-vertex digraph into three directed Hamiltonian cycles for odd m, an open problem Knuth posed. Knuth's proof of the construction was reportedly formally verified in Lean by Kim Morrison. Caveat: sources disagree on the even case, which an independent arXiv paper (2603.24708) and a GPT-5.4 Pro proof treat differently.

Technology: Claude Opus 4.6

23 Jan 2026University of Bonn; Google DeepMind; University of Manitoba

Co-FunSearch finds adversarial instances raising the Best-Fit bin packing bound

A human-LLM pipeline (Co-FunSearch) finds adversarial instances for heuristics, raising the lower bound for Best-Fit bin packing in the random-order model from 1.3 to 1.5. The authors prove the 3/2 ratio analytically and report a search-found instance scoring 1.497 over 10,000 random shuffles against Packingsolver optima. Caveat: arXiv preprint; human analysis supplies the final proofs.

Technology: Co-FunSearch (FunSearch-based LLM program search)

7 Jun 2023Google DeepMind

AlphaDev: reinforcement learning discovers faster sorting routines

Working at the assembly-instruction level, the system found sorting routines up to 70% faster for sequences of 3–5 elements and about 1.7% faster for sequences over 250,000 elements; they were adopted in the LLVM libc++ standard library. A hashing algorithm 30% faster was added to Abseil. The largest gains are for short sequences.

Technology: AlphaDev (reinforcement learning)
