5 Oct 2026Anthropic; Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Claude discovers algorithms that refute the 3SUM and APSP time-complexity hypotheses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents. The single underlying technique computes selected entries of thin matrix products more efficiently, and known reductions yield the main algorithmic consequences. The paper says an internal Anthropic Claude research model discovered the algorithm autonomously; the human authors then understood, simplified, strengthened and extended it. One paper-level theoretical-computer-science result is counted.

Technology: Internal Anthropic Claude research model (specific model version undisclosed)

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 73 computer science Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The paper states that Claude was tasked with checking and improving cryptographic constructions but instead developed the new thin-matrix-product algorithm, first for average-case and then worst-case problems, in a 16-million-output-token session with no human input. The authors subsequently worked to understand, simplify, strengthen and extend the algorithm. Anthropic later used an internal model to Lean-certify specified main results.
Test and result
Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an arXiv preprint as of the checked record. The stated time bounds are for polynomially bounded integer inputs; real-valued variants use Las Vegas randomization and different bounds. Lean certification covers the explicitly enumerated theorems, not every statement or presentation detail in the 76-page paper. The authors take responsibility for the paper and developed important extensions/consequences after the initial AI-discovered algorithm.

17 Sep 2026Anthropic

Claude optimizes more than 30 open models for biology

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In just under four weeks, Claude optimized over 30 models for structure, protein design, and genomics. They were on average four times faster with a small accuracy loss, and nearly twice as fast with identical outputs. It also created a low-memory mode for systems over 10,000 tokens on a single GPU node. Design results are in silico only, and comparisons are internal to Anthropic.

Technology: Claude (in Claude Science)

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Claude wrote custom FlashPairformer kernels, optimized more than 30 models, built a low-memory Big mode, and ran autonomous protein binder design.
Test and result
Accelerated models were checked against their downstream tasks (for example structure prediction) and FlashPairformer kernels were compared with cuEquivariance (2.7-2.9x faster on triangle attention); design results are in silico with internal comparisons.
Limitation / still unknown
Design results have no wet-lab validation, large viral capsid predictions were incorrect, ColabFold kernels were not benchmarked, and the model is not reproducible.

25 Mar 2026University of London; University of Warwick; University of Oxford; London Institute for Mathematical Sciences

Transformer-guided genetic search finds six new champion linear error-correcting codes over F₈

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A transformer trained to predict minimum Hamming distance, paired with a genetic algorithm and exact Brouwer–Zimmermann distance checks, found at least six previously unknown champion generalized toric codes over F₈ at dimensions 18, 22 and 38 (length 49). The article reports more than 500 champion codes in its F₈ search and up to a twofold reduction in exact distance evaluations versus random search. The six records at the three newly improved parameter settings are one paper-level coding-theory result.

Technology: Transformer model adapted from GPT-2, coupled with a genetic algorithm

Editorial relevance 47 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The transformer predicts generalized toric codes’ minimum Hamming distances and guides genetic search toward promising generator matrices. Candidate champions are then checked with the Brouwer–Zimmermann algorithm and compared with known bounds; the paper reports six previously unknown champion codes at F₈ dimensions 18, 22 and 38.
Test and result
A transformer trained to predict minimum Hamming distance, paired with a genetic algorithm and exact Brouwer–Zimmermann distance checks, found at least six previously unknown champion generalized toric codes over F₈ at dimensions 18, 22 and 38 (length 49).
Limitation / still unknown
The codes are newly discovered mathematical constructions in the generalized toric family, with minimum distances verified computationally using established exact algorithms and compared to known records. The reported >500 total includes known/previous champion codes, while six at dimensions 18, 22 and 38 are identified as novel; these are not six separate catalog entries. The method searches a restricted family and does not establish optimal codes over all linear codes or quantum codes.

Claude's Cycles: a Hamiltonian cycle decomposition found by Claude

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

23 Jan 2026University of Bonn; Google DeepMind; University of Manitoba

Co-FunSearch finds adversarial instances raising the Best-Fit bin packing bound

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A human-LLM pipeline (Co-FunSearch) finds adversarial instances for heuristics, raising the lower bound for Best-Fit bin packing in the random-order model from 1.3 to 1.5. The authors prove the 3/2 ratio analytically and report a search-found instance scoring 1.497 over 10,000 random shuffles against Packingsolver optima. Caveat: arXiv preprint; human analysis supplies the final proofs.

7 Jun 2023Google DeepMind

AlphaDev: reinforcement learning discovers faster sorting routines

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Working at the assembly-instruction level, the system found sorting routines up to 70% faster for sequences of 3–5 elements and about 1.7% faster for sequences over 250,000 elements; they were adopted in the LLVM libc++ standard library. A hashing algorithm 30% faster was added to Abseil. The largest gains are for short sequences.

Technology: AlphaDev (reinforcement learning)

Family AlphaDev: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AlphaDev, an AlphaZero-based reinforcement learning agent with a transformer encoder, built assembly programs instruction by instruction, rewarded for correctness and latency; the team translated results to C++.
Test and result
AlphaDev found sort 3 and sort 5 routines with fewer instructions than human benchmarks, matched sort 4, and had lower latency on variable sorts. Libc++ versions were up to 70% faster for five elements and about 1.7% faster above 250,000 elements.
Limitation / still unknown
Hashing result (30% faster) not seen in the text read. Search is at assembly level; the paper reports optimality proof only for sort 3 (17 instructions). Results cover tested CPUs: ARMv8, Intel Skylake, AMD Zen 2.
