27 Oct 2026Shanghai AI Laboratory; Fudan University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; University of Science and Technology of China; Tsinghua University

Unchanged agent instructions could appear to improve during skill selection

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 16 tuned instruction sets, held-out task success averaged only 0.1 percentage points above the starting instructions. In replay tests, the same acceptance logic often selected apparent improvements even when every candidate kept the original instructions.

Technology: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GPT-6 Luna; model-driven skill optimizers

Family GPT-6: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned agents performed each task under original and edited natural-language instructions. Their repeated scores supplied the null replay and the held-out comparison.
Test and result
The authors tested three optimizers on three benchmarks. Their no-change replay draws task-success probabilities from a fitted beta-binomial posterior, simulates each candidate as another run of the seed instructions, and applies the original acceptance rule. Small development sets had at most 30 tasks.
Limitation / still unknown
The no-change result concerns the studied acceptance rules and resampling design. An aggregate near-zero test result does not establish equivalence of every skill or model. The null replay assumes episodes are independent conditional on each task’s success probability; its prior is fitted using seed development and test episodes, so the replay is a model-based diagnostic rather than a newly executed unchanged-instruction optimization trial.

27 Oct 2026Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology

Seller message framing tracked purchases in live persuasion games

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A retrospective analysis of 9,337 classifiable messages from one AI seller in 725 GLEE persuasion games found that buyers purchased after 65.8% of loyalty-framed messages and 30.9% of care-framed messages. The association persisted when the study adjusted for product quality and game round.

Technology: Online Thompson sampling bandit; language-model message generation

Editorial relevance 40 computer science Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
An online Thompson sampling bandit fitted framing success probabilities during play and supplied the framing for the language model that wrote the analyzed seller messages.
Test and result
Among 9,337 lexicon-classifiable outgoing messages in 725 live persuasion competition games, buyer response was 65.8% for loyalty-framed versus 30.9% for care-framed messages. Game-clustered, quality- and round-adjusted odds ratio was 3.98 (95% CI 3.31–4.79), or 2.18 (1.78–2.67) for pitches only.
Limitation / still unknown
The agent chose framing adaptively and labels came from a lexicon. Opponents included humans and AI agents, and the aggregate does not identify a human-only effect. Repeat opponents could not be accounted for, and framing was not randomized. About half of care-labelled messages were honest recommendations not to buy, compared with 6–17% for other frames, so the raw purchase-rate contrast also reflects message intent. The pitches-only restriction reduces this difference but is itself a lexical approximation.
