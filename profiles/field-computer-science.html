23 Oct 2026Johns Hopkins University; Imperial College London

Small neuron sets shifted cognitive-impairment answers in three audio models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers screened eight audio-language models on recordings and transcripts from existing cognitive-impairment datasets. In three of five models that met the analysis criteria, disabling small neuron sets shifted the models' answers more than disabling matched random sets. Separately, fitted readouts from other neurons recovered similar classification information. The experiments distinguish influence over the model's answer from where class information is represented. They do not establish clinical diagnostic performance.

Technology: Qwen3-Omni-30B; MiniCPM-o-4.5; Kimi-Audio; activation ablation

Editorial relevance 53 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Intervening on learned decoder activations changed output tendencies; trained probes separately measured how widely class information was recoverable.
Test and result
The study analyzed existing Pitt and PROCESS-2 materials. It separated the samples used to identify neuron sets from those used for evaluation, compared ablation with matched-size, matched-layer and activity-matched controls, and evaluated fitted readouts from selected, random and all available neurons.
Limitation / still unknown
The effects apply to selected models, corpora and prompt conditions. The work does not establish clinical use or generalized diagnostic performance, and some exploratory small-readout differences did not survive correction.

22 Oct 2026The University of Hong Kong

A trained transformer approximated Heavy-Ball updates without examples of them

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A theoretical and numerical study found that a simplified transformer for linear regression can shift from imitating gradient descent to approximating the known Heavy-Ball method. It first learned gradient-descent trajectories and was then trained against the true regression weights, without Heavy-Ball trajectories as supervision. The proof concerns later steps under specific initialization, parameter and high-dimensional data assumptions. The result appeared in arXiv v1 on October 22.

Technology: Linear-attention transformer; trajectory-level supervision

Editorial relevance 53 computer science Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A transformer trained in two stages produces the trajectory being characterized; no examples from the Heavy-Ball update rule were supplied as supervision.
Test and result
The analysis proves approximate Heavy-Ball behavior in later steps of a trained regression model under stated assumptions. Numerical experiments compare the transformer trained in two stages with gradient descent, Heavy-Ball and single-stage transformer training.
Limitation / still unknown
The result concerns a simplified transformer and controlled linear regression. It does not establish the same training dynamics for frontier language models or arbitrary optimization problems.

20 Oct 2026University of California, Irvine

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a 96-state SmolLM2 evaluation, giving a response controller only two learned sparse features and the governing policy raised the best attainable average loss by 0.1166 and 0.1082 for two separately trained autoencoders, compared with access to the full state. Two human raters scored responses used in the calculation. The reported differences concern a fixed set of scored responses and a composite quality measure.

Technology: SmolLM2; sparse autoencoders

Two separately trained sparse autoencoders encoded SmolLM2 activations. Restricting the controller to two selected binary feature indicators made different response states indistinguishable to it, allowing exact calculation of the information cost under the fixed outcome table.
Test and result

The calculations concern finite scored tables and are not a tested policy's future error rate. The composite score is not an unsafe-event rate. Two selected features do not establish general information loss across sparse autoencoder interfaces.

20 Oct 2026PAIR Systems; INSAIT, Sofia University

Classifier training concentrated Qwen3 hallucination signals in middle layers

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers fitted linear probes at different stages of classifier-head fine-tuning and found that hallucination labels became readable in a band of Qwen3's upper-middle layers before appearing across later layers. Combining the later layers gave no improvement over a single-layer probe in held-out comparisons. Removing 31% of the layers above the signal-forming band and a stabilization margin preserved detection scores within seed variability in Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B.

20 Oct 2026University of Edinburgh; National Taiwan University

Audio and text share neurons that influence recognition in three models

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In three audio-language models, researchers found neurons associated with comparable audio and text concepts. Adjusting their activity using text-derived information changed audio recognition more than matched random neurons in 42 of 72 model–attribute combinations. Disabling the identified neurons affected the target task before the other tested tasks in 10 of 12 model–task combinations. Effects differed across models and covered a limited set of acoustic attributes.

19 Oct 2026University of Michigan; Jinesis Lab, University of Toronto & Vector Institute; University of Oxford; EuroSafeAI; Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems

Consistency training weakens answer cues while a cue-following circuit stays active

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers applied four consistency-training objectives to Gemma-2-2B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The trained models followed the training cue less often but also tended to discount correct suggestions, especially when cues appeared before the answer choices. Replacing activations in the known cue circuit with their uncued values corrected 59.8% to 91.0% of remaining cue-induced errors, compared with at most 12.1% for random attention heads in the same layers. This arXiv v1 preprint tests particular multiple-choice prompts in two models, with weaker transfer to unfamiliar cues and some circuit results based on one seed.

5 Oct 2026Anthropic; Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Claude discovers algorithms that refute the 3SUM and APSP time-complexity hypotheses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Alman and Vassilevska Williams present deterministic algorithms for 3SUM on polynomial-size integers in O(n^1.9992) time and all-pairs shortest paths on directed graphs with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995) time, the first polynomial improvements over the textbook exponents. The single underlying technique computes selected entries of thin matrix products more efficiently, and known reductions yield the main algorithmic consequences. The paper says an internal Anthropic Claude research model discovered the algorithm autonomously; the human authors then understood, simplified, strengthened and extended it. One paper-level theoretical-computer-science result is counted.

Technology: Internal Anthropic Claude research model (specific model version undisclosed)
