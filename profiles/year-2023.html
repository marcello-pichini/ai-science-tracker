30 Dec 2023Shandong University; Shandong First Medical University

MRI edema grades improve retrospective neural-network prognosis for Guillain–Barré syndrome

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A two-institution retrospective study used a shallow neural network to estimate residual symptoms six months after discharge in 354 Guillain–Barré syndrome patients. Adding radiologist-assigned lumbosacral edema grades to clinical variables increased the source-reported external-cohort AUC from 0.84 to 0.97. The model includes a muscle-strength score measured at discharge, so it is not an admission-only forecast. External testing used 118 patients from a second institution; treatment benefit and prospective deployment were not tested.

28 Dec 2023Kyushu University; Tokyo Women’s Medical University

Learned PET-like SPECT correction improves some regional scores but can erase true perfusion defects

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A learned image-translation model was evaluated against PET in forty held-out cardiac-imaging patients. It reduced some inferior-wall SPECT scoring discrepancies, but also produced overcorrection that erased a true defect in an illustrated case. The result supports a limited retrospective imaging validation with explicit failure modes, without proof of improved clinical diagnosis or safe replacement of standard imaging.

Technology: Adversarial cycle-consistent SPECT-to-PET image translation with a U-Net generator

Editorial relevance 40 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The learned adversarial translation transformed non-attenuation-corrected SPECT images into PET-like representations, enabling the represented patient-level comparison of regional attenuation artifacts and correction failures against an imaging reference.
Test and result
Seventy-one patients had matched SPECT/PET examinations. Thirty-one supplied image-level training/validation data; forty different patients supplied the clinical test set. Two radiologists and one cardiologist performed blinded, randomized-order consensus scoring against PET. Regional inferior-wall overscoring was reduced in the translated images, while the source documented an example of a real perfusion defect disappearing after correction.
Limitation / still unknown
Internal training/validation images were randomly split within thirty-one patients, rather than a demonstrated patient-separated validation split. Training SPECT/PET scans could be separated by up to three years; test scans were separated by roughly forty-six days on average. Single-center selection, small and biased clinical subgroups, consensus readers and reference-scan temporal differences limit generalization. Some image-error metrics worsened and reduced significance against PET is not proof of equivalence. True-defect erasure prevents any claim of uniformly safe correction. No clinical outcome, radiation-dose benefit or replacement utility was established.
