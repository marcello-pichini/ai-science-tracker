11 Apr 2026University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf

Neural segmentation reveals persistent postsynaptic-density enlargement after potentiation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In hippocampal slice neurons, StarDist neural-network segmentation identified postsynaptic densities for tracking over 16 hours. After CaMKII photoactivation, postsynaptic densities and spine heads enlarged rapidly; density enlargement persisted at 14 hours while spine-head expansion lasted about 6 hours. The original result was publicly posted on April 11, 2026, followed by a peer-reviewed version on November 20. The journal analysis included 6 activated and 5 control slice cultures, with thousands of nested synaptic measurements. Models were trained on a small manually annotated tile set and visually checked; held-out segmentation accuracy and independent replication were not reported.

Technology: StarDist convolutional neural network for star-convex image segmentation, adapted to PSD microscopy

Editorial relevance 53 biology PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Custom StarDist neural-network models segmented PSD microscopy, followed by Mastodon temporal tracking and fluorescence-based quantification of PSD/spine changes. Learned segmentation contributed to the structural time course; separate electrophysiological pathway results were not produced by this model.
Test and result
StarDist-based segmentation identified PSDs in activated and control hippocampal-slice microscopy time series, with 6 activated and 5 control slice cultures and 2,948/2,953 PSD objects in the reported comparison. The model-supported imaging showed PSD enlargement remained significant at 14 hours, whereas spine-head expansion lasted about 6 hours.
Limitation / still unknown
Only a small manually annotated tile set was used for model training; predictions were inspected/retrained, but a held-out segmentation-accuracy metric is not reported. PSD counts are repeated objects nested within 11 slice cultures, not independent biological replicates. No independent replication is reported.

30 May 2025University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf; Heidelberg University Hospital; German Cancer Research Center

A human-in-the-loop classifier reduces non-tumor-cell gallery review

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A self-supervised image encoder and clinician-updated SVM were evaluated on additional blood-image data from 10 metastatic breast-cancer patients. Their proposed gallery contained higher fractions of expert-identified circulating tumor cells than CellSearch galleries, while each system found cells the other missed. This does not establish complete sensitivity or clinical benefit.

Technology: DINO self-supervised XCiT encoder, SVM and cluster-targeted human-in-the-loop labeling

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The trained encoder represented cell images and the SVM prioritized candidates after targeted human relabeling; the qualifying result is the additional-patient gallery/reference comparison, not a generic sampling benchmark.
Test and result
The 90-patient archive supplied 60 patients for self-supervised training, 20 for downstream development/testing, and 10 additional patients for final comparison. In those 10, candidate-gallery positive predictive values ranged from .479 to .878 for the final HiL pipeline versus .094 to .365 for CellSearch events. Actual CTC counts were similar overall, but CellSearch-only cells averaged 7 per patient and HiL-only cells averaged 6.
Limitation / still unknown
One expert reviewed both galleries, with blinding not stated; these labels are not an independent molecular ground truth. Selection of the uncertain development cluster used test-set performance before new examples were labeled, so development-test improvement is not untouched confirmation. The separate final 10-person comparison is small and selected; each pipeline missed cells found by the other, and complete CTC sensitivity is unknown. Higher gallery PPV does not establish improved diagnosis, survival or treatment selection. This extends prior learned cell-image representation work, rather than independently discovering a new encoder.

22 Dec 2023University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf; Jung Diagnostics

Neural uncertainty flags difficult dopamine transporter scans

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Residual-network ensembles classified dopamine-transporter SPECT and flagged uncertain decisions. A separate 645-scan Parkinson progression dataset showed 98.0% binary classification accuracy against clinical diagnosis; the uncertainty module flagged 92.3% of classification errors. Performance varied across camera datasets. This retrospective evaluation does not establish improved patient management or reliable exclusion of neurodegeneration in individual borderline scans.

Technology: Residual convolutional neural-network ensembles

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Separate high-sensitivity and high-specificity learned ensembles flag disagreement around an independently generated image classification.
Test and result
Development on 1,250 scans, separate 490-scan development test, 640-scan camera test, and 645-scan external clinical-reference test.
Limitation / still unknown
Prospective clinical utility remains unestablished; reference labels differ across cohorts, camera shifts affect performance, and uncertainty is binary rather than a calibrated probability.
