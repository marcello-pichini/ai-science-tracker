AlphaFold-guided tests link bacterial channel tails to temperature gating

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint combined AlphaFold2-Multimer predictions, molecular dynamics and electrophysiology to study a sodium channel from the marine bacterium Ruegeria pomeroyi. Predicted tail conformations suggested contacts that stabilize channel opening and reduce temperature sensitivity; targeted substitutions altered temperature responses in the predicted contexts. The study argues that the tail modulates gating rather than serving as a temperature sensor itself. Predictions across more than 800 bacterial sequences suggest broader conservation, but those channels were not all experimentally tested. The result concerns bacterial-channel biophysics, not a demonstrated mechanism in human neurons.

AlphaFold-assisted study links WFS1 dysfunction to GnRH-neuron developmental defects

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An integrated human-genetics, structural-modeling and experimental study identified WFS1 as a candidate modifier of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. AlphaFold 3 models supported simulations of rare WFS1 variants, including altered protein motions that were not captured by a simple benign/damaging prediction. Cell assays found impaired protein behavior and ER-stress activation; zebrafish knockdown disrupted GnRH-neuron architecture and reduced isl1a expression, with partial pharmacological rescue. These results support a developmental mechanism in model systems, not proof that WFS1 alone causes the human disorder or that a treatment benefits patients.

AI-designed bacterial anti-defense proteins enable phage infection and plasmid retention

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: RFdiffusion-based de novo binder design and experimental screening produced synthetic proteins that inhibited three bacterial defense systems: Thoeris and Avs1 in Bacillus subtilis assays, and DdmDE in Vibrio cholerae. Five designed proteins inhibited Thoeris and nine inhibited Avs1 in the reported screens. An engineered SBSphiJ phage carrying both anti-Thoeris and anti-Avs1 proteins replicated in B. subtilis cells expressing both defenses. In native V. cholerae O1 El Tor, two of four tested DdmDE binders improved plasmid resistance to clearance.

Technology: RFdiffusion de novo binder design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold2/3 structure-guided candidate scoring

9 May 2026Istanbul University

AlphaFold homodimer predictions link truncation to different interface outcomes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The May 9, 2026 preprint version compared 5,168 canonical/truncated human homodimer prediction pairs using AlphaFold-Multimer structures and interface-confidence scores.
