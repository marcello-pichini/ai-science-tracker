5 Nov 2026Texas A&M AgriLife Research; and Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis

AlphaFold-Multimer predicts 2,895 interactions among human mitochondrial proteins

Texas A&M AgriLife Research screened 630,003 protein pairs from about 1,100 human mitochondrial proteins with AlphaFold-Multimer and identified 2,895 likely interactions, more than half not previously reported, giving 85 uncharacterized proteins their first probable partners. The paper appeared in Nature Communications on 25 August 2026 and the institutional release on 5 November. Lab experiments in yeast and human cells confirmed several of the partnerships; most predictions remain untested, and the disease links are potential targets, not established ones. Predictions are shared through the MitoMatch web tool.

Technology: AlphaFold-Multimer

10 Jun 2026Weizmann Institute of Science; École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL)

AI-designed bacterial anti-defense proteins enable phage infection and plasmid retention

RFdiffusion-based de novo binder design and experimental screening produced synthetic proteins that inhibited three bacterial defense systems: Thoeris and Avs1 in Bacillus subtilis assays, and DdmDE in Vibrio cholerae. Five designed proteins inhibited Thoeris and nine inhibited Avs1 in the reported screens. An engineered SBSphiJ phage carrying both anti-Thoeris and anti-Avs1 proteins replicated in B. subtilis cells expressing both defenses. In native V. cholerae O1 El Tor, two of four tested DdmDE binders improved plasmid resistance to clearance.

Technology: RFdiffusion de novo binder design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold2/3 structure-guided candidate scoring

28 Mar 2026KAIST; University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

AI-designed protein family binds and senses small molecules, including cortisol

The study combines trRosetta-based scaffold hallucination and Rosetta design with ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold structural filtering to create NTF2-like binders for six small-molecule targets. An optimized cortisol binder had a measured dissociation constant of 68 nM. A purified-protein, split-luciferase cortisol sensor showed an estimated response EC50 of 72 nM; this is a sensor response measurement, not the binding-affinity value or a clinical detection limit. The sensor also responded to cortisone and 17-alpha-hydroxyprogesterone, so specificity needs improvement. The March 2026 peer-reviewed paper is the source of record.

Technology: trRosetta-based hallucination; Rosetta; ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN sequence design; AlphaFold structural filtering

23 Apr 2025University of California San Diego; collaborating biomedical research institutions

AlphaFold structure analysis helps reveal PHGDH transcriptional activity linked to Alzheimer’s amyloid pathology

AlphaFold-based structural analysis highlighted a helix-helix-turn-helix subdomain in PHGDH that resembles a TALE homeodomain, prompting tests of a previously unrecognized DNA-binding and transcriptional function. Experiments supported PHGDH binding to promoters and activation of IKKα and HMGB1 in astrocytes, independently of its metabolic enzyme activity. In mouse models and human brain organoids, PHGDH expression promoted amyloid pathology; a blood-brain-barrier-permeable inhibitor of its transcriptional function reduced amyloid pathology and improved measured behavioral deficits in mice.

Technology: AlphaFold structure prediction, followed by structural comparison of the predicted PHGDH fold

GET and interaction assays link a leukemia-risk PAX5 variant to stronger NR2C2 binding

11 Dec 2024Max Planck Institute of Molecular Cell Biology and Genetics; Center for Systems Biology Dresden; TU Dresden Cluster of Excellence Physics of Life

PICNIC machine learning predictions revealed previously poorly characterized human proteins in cellular condensates

The PICNIC gradient-boosting model ranked proteins for membership in biomolecular condensates using sequence-pattern and AlphaFold2-derived structural features. To test its predictions, researchers expressed 24 poorly characterized human proteins in U2OS cells: 21 formed mesoscale fluorescent foci, and 18 met the paper’s high-confidence condensate threshold. The experiments mapped examples to cytoplasmic bodies, nuclear bodies, nucleoli or P-bodies; for example, TYW5 and RAD51AP1 showed distinct localization patterns. Across the tested set, the authors found condensate localization was not limited to highly disordered proteins.

Technology: PICNIC, a gradient-boosting machine-learning classifier using protein sequence co-occurrence patterns, sequence complexity/disorder features and AlphaFold2-derived structural features

Deep learning identifies previously unreported protein interactions in human bacterial pathogens

A RoseTTAFold2-Lite pipeline combined residue coevolution, deep-learning complex prediction and AlphaFold modelling to screen protein pairs across 19 human bacterial pathogens. The PA4105–PA4106 structural similarity to proteins in 3-thiaglutamate biosynthesis and the WbpG–HisF2 result provide functional hypotheses, not established native-pathogen mechanisms.
