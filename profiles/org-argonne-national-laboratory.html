New combinations of additives for LNMO batteries found with machine learning

A model trained on data for 28 additives predicted the performance of 125 new combinations and identified formulations that outperformed the initial ones in tests. The study concerns high-voltage LNMO batteries; the Argonne announcement, accessed via Newswise, does not quantify the improvement or describe industrial-scale validation.

Technology: Machine learning on molecular features of additives

17 Feb 2025Argonne National Laboratory; University of Chicago

Polybot: an autonomous lab finds processing recipes for conductive polymer films

An AI-driven automated laboratory at Argonne used importance-guided Bayesian optimization to explore a seven-dimensional processing space for electronic polymer thin films. The campaign produced scale-up recipes for transparent conductive films with average conductivity above 4,500 S/cm, and feature-importance analysis identified key processing factors. The number of experiments and the baseline comparison were not checked.

Technology: Importance-guided Bayesian optimization with automated synthesis and characterization

15 Jul 2024Argonne National Laboratory; University of Chicago; University of Illinois Chicago

AI-classified XPCS links local relaxation episodes with bulk stress changes in a colloidal glass

In rheo-XPCS measurements of a sheared silica-nanoparticle glass, an unsupervised convolutional autoencoder and K-means clustering grouped two-time correlation patterns by relative relaxation rate. Changes in the frequencies of slow-dynamics classes tracked non-monotonic shear-stress relaxation, while some later local slow-dynamics episodes appeared without a corresponding change in the bulk stress signal. This is a measured microscopic-to-macroscopic relationship in the study’s model colloidal system.

Technology: AI-NERD: convolutional autoencoder for XPCS correlation images with K-means clustering in the learned latent space

5 Oct 2022Argonne National Laboratory

Machine learning and synthesis clarify metastable phases of carbon

An algorithm constructed metastable phase diagrams of carbon and identified structural features of n-diamond that were then verified experimentally. The group also synthesized predicted phases and compared structures under a microscope; the work concerns carbon only and does not establish useful properties of the new materials.

Technology: Machine learning and atomistic simulations

  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Workflow found 505 candidate carbon structures; a neural network trained on 273 phases, test error 37.1 meV/atom. Diamond-anvil laser-heating experiments on graphite with electron microscopy were used to compare predicted metastable phases, including n-diamond.
