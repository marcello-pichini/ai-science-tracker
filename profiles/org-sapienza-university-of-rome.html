1 Jul 2026Sapienza University of Rome; CNR-Nanotec Rome; INFN Sezione di Roma 1; Beihang University

Claude-assisted analytic proof establishes a+b=1 for mean-field jamming exponents

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Giorgio Parisi and Francesco Zamponi provide an analytic proof that the critical exponents a and b in the full replica-symmetry-breaking scaling solution for infinite-dimensional dense hard spheres obey a+b=1. The identity links gap, contact-force and overlap scaling relations and had previously been known numerically but not analytically. The authors report that Claude Opus 4.7 derived the core proof idea with minimal supervision, with Sonnet 4.6 helping refine it; the authors found and corrected errors in an early draft, then verified and edited the final proof.

Technology: Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7 and Claude Sonnet 4.6

After numerical investigation, Opus generated the analytic proof strategy and proof draft for the previously unproven scaling identity; Sonnet refined minor steps. The authors report 40 prompts, corrected early inconsistencies and verified/edited the published proof.
Test and result
Giorgio Parisi and Francesco Zamponi provide an analytic proof that the critical exponents a and b in the full replica-symmetry-breaking scaling solution for infinite-dimensional dense hard spheres obey a+b=1.
Limitation / still unknown
The theorem is scoped to the fullRSB scaling equations for the infinite-dimensional dense hard-sphere jamming solution; it is not a general proof about every finite-dimensional jamming system. The result is peer-reviewed and published, but proof validation and correction are attributed to the human authors.

20 Nov 2024Sapienza University of Rome; Pennsylvania State University

Deep learning resolves subtle seismic-wave changes across the 2016 Norcia earthquake cycle

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A seven-layer convolutional neural network classified raw seismic waveforms recorded near Italy’s 2016 Mw 6.5 Norcia earthquake as pre- or post-mainshock and separated time-to-failure intervals with over 90% test accuracy. The authors interpret the model’s performance, frequency dependence and null tests as evidence that local waveforms encode evolving attenuation and elastic properties consistent with progressive fault damage before rupture and postseismic recovery.

Technology: Seven-layer convolutional neural network trained on local seismic waveforms

Editorial relevance 40 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Classified the waveform signals by their temporal relation to the mainshock; controlled tests using incorrect mainshock times, distant stations and filtered frequencies helped expose subtle local seismic-wave property changes associated with the fault cycle.
Test and result
A seven-layer convolutional neural network classified raw seismic waveforms recorded near Italy’s 2016 Mw 6.5 Norcia earthquake as pre- or post-mainshock and separated time-to-failure intervals with over 90% test accuracy.
Limitation / still unknown
Authors studied one earthquake sequence and state that generalization to other tectonic settings and prospective real-time precursor detection remain untested. Related changes in elastic properties were already reported by field studies; the paper's new contribution is applying deep learning to infer them from this local waveform sequence.

27 Feb 2023Sapienza University of Rome; Sant’Andrea University Hospital

Clinical coronary CT comparison finds medium-strength learned reconstruction gives the clearest plaque images

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 51 patients, a paired comparison of fourteen reconstructions per coronary CT scan found that medium-strength deep learning reconstruction received the highest plaque-image quality ratings. High-strength reconstruction reduced noise most, but readers noted blurring of small plaque contours. The study tested image quality, without invasive angiography as a diagnostic reference or demonstrated clinical outcome benefit.

Technology: TrueFidelity deep neural network reconstruction trained on high-quality filtered-back-projection images

Editorial relevance 47 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The pretrained neural reconstruction selectively suppressed image noise in actual patient acquisitions, enabling the paired clinical test of image-quality and plaque-contour tradeoffs across reconstruction strengths.
Test and result
Prospective single-center enrollment yielded 51 patients and 714 reconstructed datasets. Each acquisition was reconstructed using filtered back-projection, ten ASiR-V strengths and three learned reconstruction strengths. Two experienced radiologists, blinded to reconstruction protocol, assessed four selected reconstructions in consensus, with randomized reading order and separated sessions. Medium-strength DLIR had the highest subjective plaque-image quality; high-strength DLIR had lower noise but did not significantly outperform maximum-strength ASiR-V on the reported objective noise, SNR and CNR comparisons.
Limitation / still unknown
One vendor and center, selected subjective comparison datasets, consensus readers without separate-reader reproducibility, and exclusion of severe motion or high heart rates limit generalization. No invasive coronary reference was routinely available, so agreement between image reconstructions does not establish diagnostic accuracy. No radiation-dose reduction, patient outcome benefit or dedicated stent subgroup result was tested.

20 Mar 2020Sapienza University of Rome; IRCCS Istituto Neurologico Mediterraneo Neuromed

Longitudinal records supported forecasts of multiple sclerosis progression

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The study used routine records from 1,624 people with multiple sclerosis to forecast transition from relapsing-remitting to secondary-progressive disease at 180, 360 and 720 days. Visit-based models had 70–100% recall but 5–10% precision without confidence filtering; the history model reached 67% recall and 42% precision at 720 days. Few transitions and false positives limit treatment use; the study does not show clinical benefit.

Technology: Recurrent neural network (LSTM); random forest, SVM, KNN and AdaBoost

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The models learned from clinical visits or longitudinal patient history to forecast disease-course transition.
Test and result
A single-center retrospective cohort of 18,574 visits from 1,624 patients. Visit models used patient-wise leave-one-group-out evaluation; the history model used a train/test split because leave-one-group-out training was computationally infeasible.
Limitation / still unknown
No independent, prospective or clinical-impact evaluation is reported unless specified in the tested field.
