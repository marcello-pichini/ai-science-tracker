Deep learning identifies previously unreported protein interactions in human bacterial pathogens

A RoseTTAFold2-Lite pipeline combined residue coevolution, deep-learning complex prediction and AlphaFold modelling to screen protein pairs across 19 human bacterial pathogens. In a validation set selected using the deep-learning pipeline, co-immunoprecipitation detected four of six predicted interactions. Three pairs lacked prior direct interaction evidence: E. coli UbiE–YcaR, P. aeruginosa PA4105–PA4106, and P. aeruginosa WbpG–HisF2. The PA4105–PA4106 structural similarity to proteins in 3-thiaglutamate biosynthesis and the WbpG–HisF2 result provide functional hypotheses, not established native-pathogen mechanisms.

Technology: RoseTTAFold2-Lite integrated with direct coupling analysis and AlphaFold complex modelling

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In a validation set selected using the deep-learning pipeline, co-immunoprecipitation detected four of six predicted interactions.
  • Still unknown: The positive interaction evidence comes from heterologous co-immunoprecipitation/pull-down assays, with two independent experiments per positive pair, rather than native-pathogen systems. The validation subset was small (four positives among six AI-selected candidates); most of the thousands of predicted interactions remain untested, and false positives and false negatives are expected. Proposed roles for PA4105–PA4106 and UbiE–YcaR remain hypotheses; this paper does not establish their physiological function or a therapeutic target.
  • AI role: RoseTTAFold2-Lite used residue–residue coevolution and predicted complex structures to rank candidate protein pairs in a proteome-wide screen. The authors selected six pairs from the final deep-learning pipeline for co-immunoprecipitation tests; four yielded positive interaction signals. Three were described as lacking previously reported direct interaction evidence, including UbiE–YcaR, PA4105–PA4106 and WbpG–HisF2. The models enabled the experimental target selection; they did not themselves establish physical interactions.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11445079/

30 May 2022Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences; Seoul National University

ColabFold: free protein folding with AlphaFold2 or RoseTTAFold

ColabFold pairs AlphaFold2 or RoseTTAFold with fast MMseqs2 homology search, running free on Google Colab. Beyond benchmark comparisons, the paper reports predicting structures for 1,762 Methanocaldococcus jannaschii proteins in 48 hours on one GPU and using predicted complexes to help determine the 120 MDa human nuclear-pore structure with cryo-electron microscopy. The proteome structures are computational predictions, not experimentally validated structures. The nuclear-pore application overlaps the separate nuclear-pore entry; this is a methodological contribution, not an additional independent biological finding.

Technology: ColabFold (AlphaFold2, RoseTTAFold, MMseqs2)

15 Jul 2021University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

RoseTTAFold: accessible prediction of protein structures

A three-track neural network (sequence, residue interactions, and 3D structure) computes a structure in about ten minutes on a single gaming computer. Baker’s group predicted hundreds of new structures, including little-known proteins in the human genome. The web server received over 4,500 requests in the previous month. The page does not state limitations.
