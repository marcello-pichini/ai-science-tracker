Machine learning reveals tropical aerosol-driven cloud expansion despite stronger rainfall

Using satellite observations of four Kīlauea volcanic outgassing episodes in 2008 and 2018, an ML counterfactual analysis found that volcanic aerosols increased cloud cover and reflected sunlight over tropical marine shallow-convective clouds, even as rainfall strengthened. Cloud-cover adjustment contributed about 65% of aerosol–cloud cooling under mixed meteorological conditions and 84–87% in humid conditions; under humid, stable conditions, a 30% increase in droplet number could correspond to up to a 50% relative increase in cloud cover. The result identifies a tropical regime in which aerosol cloud expansion is strong and its response varies with humidity and stability.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning surrogates of MODIS cloud properties, radiative fluxes, and precipitation, trained on 20 years of satellite observations and meteorology while excluding volcanic years 2008 and 2018

1 Aug 2022University of Exeter; ETH Zurich; UK Met Office; NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; University of Cambridge; University of Leeds

Machine learning isolates aerosol-driven cloud-cover increase after the Holuhraun eruption

Using MODIS satellite observations and a random-forest counterfactual trained on meteorology and non-eruption years, researchers estimated cloud responses to the 2014 Holuhraun volcanic aerosol plume. They found cloud fraction increased by about 10% across the studied region; this change contributed more to the estimated aerosol cooling than the smaller radiative effect from brighter clouds with smaller droplets. The study supplies an observational constraint on aerosol–cloud climate forcing.

Technology: Random-forest ML-MODIS surrogate trained on meteorological reanalysis and MODIS satellite data from 2001–2020, excluding the 2014 eruption year

  • Still unknown: The result concerns one volcanic natural experiment, Holuhraun in 2014, and satellite observations of a North Atlantic/Northern European region. The paper reports about a 10% cloud-cover increase and attributes the leading radiative contribution to cloud fraction, while the droplet-size effect is also present and the liquid-water-path contribution is weak.

Comparing these predictions with 2014 MODIS observations let the authors isolate cloud changes associated with the Holuhraun aerosol perturbation from meteorological co-variability and estimate the relative radiative contributions of cloud-fraction, liquid-water-path, and droplet-size changes.
