UMAP analysis of DESI spectra surfaces newly recognized inhomogeneous white dwarfs

A machine-learning-guided analysis of DESI multi-epoch spectra used UMAP to identify three systems with strongly varying spectral signatures that were proposed as new ‘double-faced’ white dwarfs with inhomogeneous surface composition. Follow-up phase-resolved time-series spectra verified the interpretation for one system; the other two remain candidates. This is one paper-level result and only the verified system is counted as confirmed.

Technology: UMAP dimensionality reduction applied to multi-epoch white-dwarf spectroscopy, alongside a neural-network spectral classifier

25 Oct 2023University of Warwick; University Hospitals Coventry and Warwickshire NHS Foundation Trust, collaborating UK hospitals and IMP Diagnostics (Portugal)

CAIMAN validates AI prescreening of typical and atypical large-bowel biopsies across UK and Portuguese cohorts

CAIMAN is a weakly supervised deep-learning model trained on pathologist-labeled whole-slide images to classify typical, atypical non-neoplastic and atypical neoplastic bowel biopsies. In three external cohorts comprising 1,536 slides from the UK and Portugal, it detected atypical biopsies with AUROC values of 0.9431, 0.9576 and 0.9636. At 99% sensitivity, it outperformed the prior IDaRS pipeline on two external cohorts, but not the third.

Technology: CAIMAN (Colorectal AI Model for Abnormality Detection), a weakly supervised deep-learning whole-slide image classifier

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: In three external cohorts comprising 1,536 slides from the UK and Portugal, it detected atypical biopsies with AUROC values of 0.9431, 0.9576 and 0.9636.
  • Still unknown: Retrospective validation on archived biopsies does not establish clinical deployment or improved patient outcomes. CAIMAN did not outperform IDaRS at 99% sensitivity in one of the three external validation cohorts; the authors call for larger multicentre validation and a clinical trial. The reported 99% sensitivity is paired with cohort-specific specificity, not a universal performance guarantee.
  • AI role: AI learned biopsy-level histological patterns from slide-level diagnostic labels, then classified whole-slide images into typical, atypical non-neoplastic or atypical neoplastic classes. The trained frozen model was evaluated on three external cohorts; the reported finding is a specific retrospective clinical diagnostic-validation result.

24 Aug 2020University of Warwick

Machine-learning validation and independent cross-checks classify 50 Kepler candidates as planets

A Gaussian-process classifier, supported by random-forest, extra-trees and multilayer-perceptron models, was applied to Kepler transit candidates. From 83 candidates meeting the study’s stringent ML and data-quality criteria, the authors newly validated 50 as planets after updated vespa false-positive checks also gave each less than a 1% false-positive probability. The result is statistical validation using Kepler pipeline and stellar data, not new telescope observations of the planets.

Technology: Gaussian process classifier, random forest, extra trees and multilayer perceptron classifiers

Relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A Gaussian-process classifier, supported by random-forest, extra-trees and multilayer-perceptron models, was applied to Kepler transit candidates.
  • Still unknown: The 50 are statistically validated candidates from archival Kepler observations, not newly observed planets. The authors caution that their method is trained and demonstrated on Kepler, that atypical/outlier candidates may be unreliable, and that ML and vespa disagree for 33 other high-ML candidates; those were not counted as validated. This entry counts one paper-level validation result, not 50 separate records.
  • AI role: Four machine-learning classifiers jointly ranked candidate transit signals against planet and false-positive scenarios. The paper required all four to cross 0.99 planet probability plus positional, stellar, binary and outlier checks; an updated vespa calculation then independently cross-checked the retained candidates.

Evidence sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10516
