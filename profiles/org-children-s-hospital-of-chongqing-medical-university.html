PROBY screening uncovers target-selective fluorescent probes for tau, BCL-2 and TDP-43

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The PROBY deep-learning pipeline screened 26,416 target-validated molecules for useful optical properties, identified 11 candidates for testing and found target-responsive probes PE859 (tau), obatoclax (BCL-2) and B3 (TDP-43). The study reports selectivity assays, cell/tissue labeling and ex-vivo tumor imaging with obatoclax. AI-guided structural modification of PE859 yielded 859-2, which enabled in-vivo two-photon and cleared-brain imaging of tau pathology in P301S transgenic mice; its measured tau-aggregate dissociation constant was 54.8 nM.

Technology: PROBY two-stage message-passing neural network pipeline (PROBY-L1 fluorescence classification and PROBY-L2 photophysical-property prediction)

AI contribution
PROBY ranked target-validated compounds by predicted fluorescence and seven optical properties, enabling selection and experimental verification of target-specific probes; the team then used AI-predicted structural modification alongside synthesis and bioassays to optimize PE859 into 859-1/859-2.

Limitation / still unknown
The PROBY hits are repurposed molecules from a target-validated library, not newly synthesized compounds. Target/probe performance is assay-specific; tau in-vivo imaging was demonstrated in a small transgenic-mouse study (n=3), not humans. This entry describes probe function and imaging results, not treatment effects or diagnostic performance in patients.
