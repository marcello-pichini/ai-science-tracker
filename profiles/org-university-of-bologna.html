AI-guided CORONA imagery search finds four archaeologically verified tells in Abu Ghraib

A fine-tuned deep-learning model trained with 1960s CORONA satellite images flagged eight previously unrecognized tell candidates in Iraq's Abu Ghraib district. Archaeologists investigated all eight; four contained surface ceramic sherds confirming archaeological remains and enabling dating. The AI suggestions led the field team to inspect locations that routine surveys would not have visited.

Technology: MANet deep convolutional segmentation network with attention, transfer learning and two-stage fine-tuning

7 Feb 2025University of Bologna; INAF; University of Milano-Bicocca; National Central University, Taiwan; University of Tokyo; INAF Istituto di Radioastronomia; CINECA

A radio survey reveals megaparsec-scale diffuse emission around a cosmic-web node

Radio U-Net prompted reanalysis of LOFAR, XMM-Newton, DESI optical/near-infrared and weak-lensing data around cluster PSZ2 G083.29−31.03. The team reports previously unnoticed diffuse radio emission extending about 5 Mpc and aligned with X-ray emission and galaxies; lensing supports a complex mass distribution with two low-mass systems on either side of the cluster. The authors propose that these systems are merging substructures in a massive cosmic-web node, while noting alternative interpretations remain possible.

Technology: Radio U-Net, a machine-learning detector for diffuse radio emission

  • Still unknown: The radio morphology and multiwavelength associations are reported observational results. The interpretation that the two faint components are subclusters merging with the central cluster is proposed rather than uniquely established; redshift/mass uncertainties and low radio resolution leave other emission classifications possible.
  • AI role: Flagged diffuse low-surface-brightness radio emission in LoTSS data that had escaped prior human analysis, motivating the multiwavelength and weak-lensing study that characterized this new extended structure.
