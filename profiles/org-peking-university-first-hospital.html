20 Oct 2026Peking University First Hospital; Siemens Healthineers

Low-field shoulder MRI with learned reconstruction agrees with surgical findings

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A paired prospective study evaluated 0.55 T shoulder MRI reconstructed with Deep Resolve Boost against conventional 1.5 T imaging. Among 25 patients with suspected rotator-cuff injury, 16 underwent arthroscopy; MRI findings showed good to perfect agreement with surgery. Different coils, hardware and reconstruction prevent attributing the system comparison solely to AI.

Technology: Deep Resolve Boost convolutional neural-network MRI reconstruction

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep Resolve Boost CNNs reconstructed the low-field images from raw measurements and noise maps, producing the images used for blinded clinical lesion evaluation and surgical-reference comparison.
Test and result
Prospectively enrolled 25 volunteers and 25 patients, each examined with both MRI systems. The 0.55 T system used a vendor-trained CNN reconstruction combining noise maps and raw sensor data; the 1.5 T comparator did not use DL. Blinded randomized readings compared image quality and lesion assessment. Sixteen patients received arthroscopy; source-reported lesion agreement was perfect for the senior reader and good to perfect for the junior reader. Both systems had closely agreeing lesion assessments.
Limitation / still unknown
Small single-center selected rotator-cuff cohort and partial surgical verification; not an unbiased sensitivity/specificity screening study. Field strength, coils, hardware and reconstruction differ simultaneously, with no same-scanner DL ablation or 1.5 T DL comparator. The study validates the combined configuration, not an isolated causal AI benefit or improved patient outcome. Vendor training data and supplementary acquisition details were not independently inspected.

4 Oct 2025Peking University First Hospital; and Peking University Health Science Center; Beijing Miyun Hospital; Beijing Friendship Hospital; collaborating Chinese hospitals

A seven-metabolite AI model detected renal-cell carcinoma across six validation cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An SVM-based screen of untargeted plasma metabolomics identified candidate renal-cell-carcinoma metabolites, which were confirmed by targeted analysis and used in the RCAID AI diagnostic model. In 1,680 participants across five hospitals, seven plasma metabolites supported AUROCs of 0.977, 0.911, 0.945 and 0.972 in internal, external, multicenter and temporal validation cohorts, respectively; validation also included late-stage and non-clear-cell RCC. The authors report six associated dysregulated pathways from multi-omic analyses.

Technology: Support-vector-machine metabolite selection and the Renal Cell Carcinoma Artificial Intelligence Detector (RCAID)

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The source states that an SVM was used to identify candidate metabolites from untargeted plasma profiles; targeted metabolomics confirmed selected metabolites. The resulting seven-feature AI-aided RCAID model was evaluated in six independent internal, external, multicenter, temporal, late-stage and non-clear-cell validation cohorts.
Test and result
An SVM-based screen of untargeted plasma metabolomics identified candidate renal-cell-carcinoma metabolites, which were confirmed by targeted analysis and used in the RCAID AI diagnostic model.
Limitation / still unknown
The study used diagnostic case-control cohorts with healthy controls, so reported discrimination does not establish population-screening performance, clinical utility, or improved outcomes. Most cases were clear-cell RCC (81%) and stage I (69%); subtype and cohort composition may affect generalizability. The abstract reports multi-omic pathway associations but does not establish causal mechanisms. Samples were collected 2019–2023; source publication date is the 2025 online-first date. Source checked; independent replication not assessed.
