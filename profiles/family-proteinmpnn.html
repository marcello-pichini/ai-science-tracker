10 Jun 2026Weizmann Institute of Science; École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL)

AI-designed bacterial anti-defense proteins enable phage infection and plasmid retention

RFdiffusion-based de novo binder design and experimental screening produced synthetic proteins that inhibited three bacterial defense systems: Thoeris and Avs1 in Bacillus subtilis assays, and DdmDE in Vibrio cholerae. Five designed proteins inhibited Thoeris and nine inhibited Avs1 in the reported screens. An engineered SBSphiJ phage carrying both anti-Thoeris and anti-Avs1 proteins replicated in B. subtilis cells expressing both defenses. In native V. cholerae O1 El Tor, two of four tested DdmDE binders improved plasmid resistance to clearance.

Technology: RFdiffusion de novo binder design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold2/3 structure-guided candidate scoring

30 Apr 2026Columbia University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Harvard University

AI-guided design enables E. coli ribosomal proteins without isoleucine

Researchers used computational protein design and generative AI to recode ribosomal proteins in E. coli so they function without isoleucine, then assembled an engineered strain that remained viable and genomically stable over more than 450 laboratory generations. The result shows a reduced amino-acid alphabet can support core translation machinery in this bacterium; the organism still uses isoleucine elsewhere.

Technology: Protein design models including ESM2, MSATr, ProteinMPNN and AfDesign

28 Mar 2026KAIST; University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

AI-designed protein family binds and senses small molecules, including cortisol

The study combines trRosetta-based scaffold hallucination and Rosetta design with ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN sequence design and AlphaFold structural filtering to create NTF2-like binders for six small-molecule targets. An optimized cortisol binder had a measured dissociation constant of 68 nM. A purified-protein, split-luciferase cortisol sensor showed an estimated response EC50 of 72 nM; this is a sensor response measurement, not the binding-affinity value or a clinical detection limit. The sensor also responded to cortisone and 17-alpha-hydroxyprogesterone, so specificity needs improvement. The March 2026 peer-reviewed paper is the source of record.

Technology: trRosetta-based hallucination; Rosetta; ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN sequence design; AlphaFold structural filtering

21 Dec 2025Weill Cornell Medicine; Macao Polytechnic University; University of Gdańsk

CycleRFdiffusion-designed TP4 is a reproducible cyclic peptide binder to TREM2

A structure-conditioned workflow combining CycleRFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and HighFold generated and screened 1,500 cyclic peptide–TREM2 complexes and prioritized four candidates. The lead TP4 showed weak but reproducible TREM2 binding in spectral-shift, microscale-thermophoresis and surface-plasmon-resonance assays. The authors report favorable plasma stability and moderate metabolic stability, presenting the work as an initial proof of concept for TREM2-targeted cyclic binders.

Technology: CycleRFdiffusion backbone design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and HighFold structural prediction/screening

GRACE designs two carbonic-anhydrase-like enzymes with measured catalytic activity

The GRACE workflow combined RFdiffusion structure generation, ProteinMPNN sequence design, CLEAN enzyme classification and solubility/structure filtering to select two carbonic-anhydrase-like sequences from 10,000 candidates. The authors report experimental expression and carbonic anhydrase activity: dCA12_2 at 400 WAU/mL and dCA23_1 at 260 WAU/mL in the reported assay.

Technology: GRACE (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and CLEAN, with computational solubility/structural filters)

19 Jun 2024École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne; University at Buffalo; University of Washington; Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Deep-learning design produced soluble analogues of membrane-protein folds with preserved binding functions

A deep-learning pipeline based on inversion of AlphaFold2 and ProteinMPNN designed soluble proteins with claudin, rhomboid-protease and GPCR-like folds. Experiments reported stable, folded soluble designs and structural agreement with target folds. The researchers grafted functional motifs, including a ghrelin-receptor epitope that bound its antibody and active/inactive A2A-receptor-like designs that differed in mini-Gs binding; a binding-site knockout served as a control.

Technology: AF2seq deep-learning backbone design with ProteinMPNN sequence design

19 Dec 2023University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

Designed proteins bind peptides with the highest affinity reported

Using RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, researchers designed proteins that bind helical peptides such as glucagon, neuropeptide Y and parathyroid hormone, with what the authors consider the highest reported affinity for a computer-generated biomolecule. They detect low concentrations of peptides in human serum and withstand heat; a parathyroid-hormone biosensor produced a 21-fold increase in bioluminescence. The press release gives no affinity values.

Technology: RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN

16 Sep 2022University of Washington Institute for Protein Design

ProteinMPNN: protein sequences designed in about one second

A neural network generates the amino-acid sequence for a given protein shape in about one second, over 200 times faster than the best previous software, without specialist tuning. A related study shows that proteins designed with ProteinMPNN fold correctly much more often, including nanoscale rings and complex assemblies. Researchers describe it as the beginning of machine learning in protein design.

Technology: ProteinMPNN
