1 Apr 2024University of Arizona; Princeton University; MMT Observatory

Spectroscopy validates machine-selected populations of obscured active galaxies

Using a sample selected by unsupervised machine learning on optical and mid-infrared photometry, the team obtained 178 MMT spectra and found the brighter class was 85% Type I active galactic nuclei (AGN), with <3% non-AGN contamination, while a fainter class was 65% Type II AGN with 15% contamination, mainly star-forming galaxies. The follow-up showed that the machine-selected classes recover redder and more obscured AGN populations than typical optical spectroscopic samples.

Technology: Unsupervised UMAP and DBSCAN photometric AGN selection pipeline

25 Oct 2023University of Arizona; University of Chicago; University of Minnesota Duluth; and collaborators

Machine-learning image search helps identify Pavo, an isolated star-forming dwarf galaxy

A search combining low-surface-brightness galaxy detection and machine-learning candidate classification identified Pavo in Dark Energy Camera Legacy Survey imaging. Follow-up Magellan imaging resolved young and old stellar populations and measured a tip-of-the-red-giant-branch distance of about 1.99 Mpc, establishing Pavo as an extremely isolated, low-mass star-forming dwarf galaxy just beyond the Local Group.

Technology: Machine-learning image candidate classifier used with low-surface-brightness galaxy search algorithms
