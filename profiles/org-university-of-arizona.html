23 Nov 2026University of Arizona; Ohio State University

Computer vision measures diverse tomato pollen responses to heat

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint fine-tuned CenterNet HourGlass104 to count and track pollen in approximately 300,000 time-lapse images from 218 tomato accessions and wild relatives. In vitro, raising temperature from 26°C to 34°C increased mean bursting at two hours from 14.9% to 83.1%, while tube-length responses varied: 72% of accessions had shorter tubes and 28% had equal or greater length. Manual-count agreement supported image measurements. These are controlled pollen-assay phenotypes, not demonstrated field fertility or yield benefits; genotype effects and assay generalization need further testing.

Technology: Transfer-learned CenterNet HourGlass104 object detector

Editorial relevance 53 biology Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned object detection classifies pollen states and tube tips, enabling time-resolved phenotyping across the accession panel.
Test and result
New in-vitro pollen time courses with at least 7 usable replicates per condition/accession; learned counts checked against manual annotations.
Limitation / still unknown
Confidence-threshold selection used count agreement; model accuracy may be optimistic. Image exclusions and varying replicate counts limit transfer. No field fertility, yield or causal genetic benefit established.

1 Apr 2024University of Arizona; Princeton University; MMT Observatory

Spectroscopy validates machine-selected populations of obscured active galaxies

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using a sample selected by unsupervised machine learning on optical and mid-infrared photometry, the team obtained 178 MMT spectra and found the brighter class was 85% Type I active galactic nuclei (AGN), with <3% non-AGN contamination, while a fainter class was 65% Type II AGN with 15% contamination, mainly star-forming galaxies. The follow-up showed that the machine-selected classes recover redder and more obscured AGN populations than typical optical spectroscopic samples.

Technology: Unsupervised UMAP and DBSCAN photometric AGN selection pipeline

Editorial relevance 53 astronomy Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The selection pipeline applied UMAP dimensionality reduction and DBSCAN clustering to optical and mid-infrared colors and morphology, with matched spectroscopy and X-ray information used to identify AGN candidate classes. Those classes defined the targets for Hectospec follow-up; the paper then used optical spectra, emission-line diagnostics, and spectral stacking to test their AGN content. The AI selected the candidate populations, while spectroscopic diagnostics established the reported classifications.
Test and result
Using a sample selected by unsupervised machine learning on optical and mid-infrared photometry, the team obtained 178 MMT spectra and found the brighter class was 85% Type I active galactic nuclei (AGN), with <3% non-AGN contamination, while a fainter class was 65% Type II AGN with 15% contamination, mainly star-forming galaxies.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a follow-up validation of two machine-selected candidate classes, not a claim that every cataloged object is confirmed. Spectroscopy recovered redshifts for 139 of 178 spectra; the fainter Type II class was limited to the brightest 75% of candidates and had 15% contamination, primarily from star-forming galaxies. The unsupervised pipeline is described in related work listed as in preparation in this paper; findings here are bounded to the spectroscopically analyzed sample.

25 Oct 2023University of Arizona; University of Chicago; University of Minnesota Duluth; and collaborators

Machine-learning image search helps identify Pavo, an isolated star-forming dwarf galaxy

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A search combining low-surface-brightness galaxy detection and machine-learning candidate classification identified Pavo in Dark Energy Camera Legacy Survey imaging. Follow-up Magellan imaging resolved young and old stellar populations and measured a tip-of-the-red-giant-branch distance of about 1.99 Mpc, establishing Pavo as an extremely isolated, low-mass star-forming dwarf galaxy just beyond the Local Group.

Technology: Machine-learning image candidate classifier used with low-surface-brightness galaxy search algorithms

Editorial relevance 53 astronomy PreprintPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Classified image-search candidates in DECaLS imaging; the search team selected Pavo for follow-up, and telescope imaging and color-magnitude analysis established its distance and galaxy properties.
Test and result
A search combining low-surface-brightness galaxy detection and machine-learning candidate classification identified Pavo in Dark Energy Camera Legacy Survey imaging.
Limitation / still unknown
The AI contributed candidate classification within a broader image-search workflow. The discovery was verified by follow-up observations and astrophysical analysis. AAS conference abstract from 2024 also describes this same Pavo discovery and is not a separate result.

24 Jan 2022University of Arizona

Recurrent networks classify cancer-bearing mouse ovary images

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A convolutional LSTM classified optical-coherence tomography of excised ovaries from sixteen mice, half from an ovarian-cancer model and half wild-type controls. Tissue pathology supported the disease labels. Across repeated leave-one-animal-out evaluations, mean AUC was 0.81; the best run reached 0.98. This is a small retrospective preclinical imaging validation, not human ovarian-cancer screening or evidence of earlier detection or improved treatment outcomes.

Technology: Convolutional long short-term memory; VGG and three-dimensional CNN comparisons

Editorial relevance 33 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The convolutional LSTM learns sequential relationships between depth-resolved OCT slices and classifies tissue from cancer-model versus wild-type animals, using pathology-supported labels.
Test and result
Existing ex-vivo ovarian OCT recordings from eight cancer-model and eight wild-type mice, with pathological verification and leave-one-animal-out testing repeated ten times. Mean convolutional-LSTM AUC .81 is distinguished from the selected peak .98.
Limitation / still unknown
Human diagnostic transfer, clinical screening, early-stage detection and benefit remain unestablished. Only sixteen animals and one held-out animal per fold limit uncertainty estimates; peak-run performance is not representative average performance.
