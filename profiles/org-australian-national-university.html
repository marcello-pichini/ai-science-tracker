Machine-learning seismicity maps a 250-km eastern edge of the subducted Yakutat microplate

An expanded machine-learning earthquake catalog and complementary receiver-function and ambient-noise images reveal a previously unrecognized, roughly 250-km linear cluster of about 1,750 earthquakes that the authors interpret as tracing the northeastern/eastern edge of the subducted Yakutat microplate beneath south-central Alaska. The inferred slab edge lies beneath the curved Denali fault; its possible role in the 2002 Mw 7.9 earthquake’s nucleation is a hypothesis, not an established cause.

Technology: Modified EQTransformer machine-learning seismic phase picker, REAL phase associator, and NonLinLoc locator

  • Tested: An expanded machine-learning earthquake catalog and complementary receiver-function and ambient-noise images reveal a previously unrecognized, roughly 250-km linear cluster of about 1,750 earthquakes that the authors interpret as tracing the northeastern/eastern edge of the subducted Yakutat microplate beneath south-central Alaska.
  • Still unknown: This is a localized tectonic interpretation from data collected between 2018 and 2021, not prospective earthquake prediction. The 64,870-event catalog reports average location uncertainties of about 5.0 km latitude, 5.2 km longitude and 8.6 km depth; the authors support the slab-edge interpretation with seismicity, receiver functions, tremor distribution and ambient-noise tomography. They propose—but do not establish—that the slab edge influenced nucleation of the 2002 Denali earthquake and the position of volcanic fields.
  • AI role: The ML phase picker helped construct a denser earthquake catalog from permanent and temporary station records. The 64,870-event catalog exposed the previously unseen long linear earthquake cluster; receiver-function and ambient-noise tomography supplied independent complementary images used to interpret its relationship to the Yakutat slab edge.
