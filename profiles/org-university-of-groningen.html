4 Sep 2025University of Groningen; University of Vienna

Machine learning reveals broad and previously uncharacterized fungal polyketide-synthase activity

A genome-mining and cell-free enzyme study characterized fungal type III polyketide synthases (T3PKSs), revealing broad and varied substrate/product profiles. An ML model trained on 341 enzyme–substrate pairs predicted substrate specificity and guided tests that found two fungal enzymes, AcaePKS and PansPKS, active on all ten tested substrates, including non-natural substrates absent from training data.

Technology: Feedforward neural-network multilayer perceptron trained on protein-language-model embeddings and substrate fingerprints

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: An ML model trained on 341 enzyme–substrate pairs predicted substrate specificity and guided tests that found two fungal enzymes, AcaePKS and PansPKS, active on all ten tested substrates, including non-natural substrates absent from training data.

4 Jun 2025University of Groningen; Pisa, KU Leuven, and University of Southern Denmark

Dead Sea Scrolls redated with AI and radiocarbon

The Enoch tool dated the writing of 135 manuscripts with about 79% agreement with palaeographers and roughly 30-year precision, following new radiocarbon dating; many scrolls appear older than expected. The 79% measures agreement with palaeographers, not accuracy against known dates; the announcement gives no details on training data.

Technology: Enoch (palaeographic dating model)

Relevance 60 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: The Enoch dates for 135 manuscripts, following new radiocarbon dating, were compared with palaeographers' judgments, with about 79% agreement.
  • Still unknown: The source states the 79% measures agreement with palaeographers rather than accuracy against known dates, that about one in five datings disagreed, and that training-data details are not given.
  • AI role: Enoch, a writing-style model trained on radiocarbon-dated samples, dated the manuscripts.

13 Feb 2025University of Groningen

CNN selection leads to spectroscopic confirmation of a Euclid strong lens

Five convolutional models ranked Euclid early-release images, followed by human visual grading. Spectroscopy and lens modeling support EUCL J081705.61+702348.8 as a strong lens, with foreground redshift 0.335 and lensed source redshift 1.475. The 97 visually graded candidates are not 97 confirmed lenses, and model ranking alone did not establish the lens.

Technology: Ensemble of five convolutional neural networks

21 Apr 2021University of Groningen

Two scribes in the Great Isaiah Scroll: new evidence from handwriting

Independent analyses of character shapes distinguish two groups across the scroll’s 54 columns, consistent with two scribes alternating around the manuscript’s midpoint. This is statistical evidence from one artifact; the writers’ identities are unknown, and other explanations for the differences are not entirely ruled out.

Technology: Pattern recognition and Kohonen maps
