4 Sep 2025University of Groningen; University of Vienna

Machine learning reveals broad and previously uncharacterized fungal polyketide-synthase activity

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A genome-mining and cell-free enzyme study characterized fungal type III polyketide synthases (T3PKSs), revealing broad and varied substrate/product profiles. An ML model trained on 341 enzyme–substrate pairs predicted substrate specificity and guided tests that found two fungal enzymes, AcaePKS and PansPKS, active on all ten tested substrates, including non-natural substrates absent from training data.

Technology: Feedforward neural-network multilayer perceptron trained on protein-language-model embeddings and substrate fingerprints

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model learned fungal T3PKS enzyme–substrate activity from measured data, was tested on unseen substrates, and predicted candidate promiscuous fungal enzymes. Guided experiments confirmed broad substrate activity for AcaePKS and PansPKS, including substrate pairs outside training data; cell-free assays and LC–MS characterized enzyme products.
Test and result
An ML model trained on 341 enzyme–substrate pairs predicted substrate specificity and guided tests that found two fungal enzymes, AcaePKS and PansPKS, active on all ten tested substrates, including non-natural substrates absent from training data.
Limitation / still unknown
The enzyme products were characterized in vitro; the study examines product scope and biocatalytic potential, not native ecological roles or medical efficacy. Two candidate proteins expressed poorly, and the model's transfer accuracy was lower on non-fungal enzyme classes. The reported five enzyme sample includes only three well-expressing candidates, of which two showed the broadest tested activity.

4 Jun 2025University of Groningen; Pisa, KU Leuven, and University of Southern Denmark

Dead Sea Scrolls redated with AI and radiocarbon

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The Enoch tool dated the writing of 135 manuscripts with about 79% agreement with palaeographers and roughly 30-year precision, following new radiocarbon dating; many scrolls appear older than expected. The 79% measures agreement with palaeographers, not accuracy against known dates; the announcement gives no details on training data.

Technology: Enoch (palaeographic dating model)

Editorial relevance 60 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Enoch, a writing-style model trained on radiocarbon-dated samples, dated the manuscripts.
Test and result
The Enoch dates for 135 manuscripts, following new radiocarbon dating, were compared with palaeographers' judgments, with about 79% agreement.
Limitation / still unknown
The source states the 79% measures agreement with palaeographers rather than accuracy against known dates, that about one in five datings disagreed, and that training-data details are not given.

13 Feb 2025University of Groningen

CNN selection leads to spectroscopic confirmation of a Euclid strong lens

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Five convolutional models ranked Euclid early-release images, followed by human visual grading. Spectroscopy and lens modeling support EUCL J081705.61+702348.8 as a strong lens, with foreground redshift 0.335 and lensed source redshift 1.475. The 97 visually graded candidates are not 97 confirmed lenses, and model ranking alone did not establish the lens.

Technology: Ensemble of five convolutional neural networks

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CNN ensemble ranked candidates for human grading and spectroscopic follow-up.
Test and result
Primary v1 and journal paper report spectroscopically supported lens with redshifts and lens modeling.
Limitation / still unknown
Earlier object reporting and aliases remain open; human visual grading and spectroscopy contributed to confirmation.

21 Apr 2021University of Groningen

Two scribes in the Great Isaiah Scroll: new evidence from handwriting

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Independent analyses of character shapes distinguish two groups across the scroll’s 54 columns, consistent with two scribes alternating around the manuscript’s midpoint. This is statistical evidence from one artifact; the writers’ identities are unknown, and other explanations for the differences are not entirely ruled out.

Technology: Pattern recognition and Kohonen maps

Editorial relevance 60 humanities Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
