15 May 2026Université Grenoble Alpes; Institut de Physique du Globe de Paris; University of Tokyo; Service des dangers naturels, Valais

Unsupervised learning separates rockfall and glacier-sliding precursors before the Blatten collapse

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Unsupervised clustering of 20 days of seismic data before the 28 May 2025 Blatten glacier collapse separated rockfall signals from events associated with basal glacier sliding. Sliding-related event counts accelerated approximately exponentially beginning about five days before collapse, with further acceleration about 1.5 days beforehand; the authors interpret this pattern as consistent with nucleation-like slip weakening.

Technology: Deep scattering network, independent component analysis and hierarchical clustering (unsupervised machine learning)

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The unsupervised representation and clustering explored continuous single-station seismic records without event templates, separating rockfall, glacier-sliding and cultural-noise signatures. This made the evolving sliding-related event population and its acceleration measurable in the 20-day record.
Test and result
Unsupervised clustering of 20 days of seismic data before the 28 May 2025 Blatten glacier collapse separated rockfall signals from events associated with basal glacier sliding.
Limitation / still unknown
The finding is a retrospective analysis of one event, relying primarily on a single seismic station 5.3 km from the site. The authors describe the physical mechanism as consistent with nucleation and hypothesize that rockfalls contributed to instability; these interpretations require study of other events. The inferred slip-rate proxy has high absolute uncertainty, and the approach is not an operational early-warning system.

2 Jul 2021Université Grenoble Alpes; CNRS

Single-station CNN analysis adds 273 visually confirmed earthquakes to the L’Aquila near-fault catalog

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A two-stage 1D CNN pipeline applied to continuous three-component seismic waveforms from one L’Aquila station identified 273 previously uncataloged local earthquake events during a one-month 2009 foreshock period. The study reports that 84% were assigned magnitude below 1 and that all 273 new detections were visually confirmed; the expanded catalog contained roughly three times as many new detections as the pre-existing catalog had events for that period.

Technology: Two-stage one-dimensional convolutional neural network (CNN) pipeline

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The detector CNN scanned continuous raw three-component waveform windows for earthquake signals; a second CNN classified distance and magnitude, enabling the researchers to find and characterize uncataloged events from a single station. The CNN pipeline generated the new event detections; authors then visually checked them.
Test and result
A two-stage 1D CNN pipeline applied to continuous three-component seismic waveforms from one L’Aquila station identified 273 previously uncataloged local earthquake events during a one-month 2009 foreshock period.
Limitation / still unknown
The analysis covers one month in February 2009 from one station. The authors report 273 new local detections and six nonlocal events among 369 windows classified as local; all 273 new detections were visually confirmed. The pipeline does not provide exact event locations, and transfer to other regions was not established. Magnitudes are coarse classes, not precise estimates.
