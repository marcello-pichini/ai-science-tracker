Self-supervised learning identifies two previously unreported seismic sequences during the Mayotte submarine eruption

A SimSiam self-supervised representation model with t-SNE and DBSCAN clustering explored two months of continuous seismological records from near the Fani Maoré submarine volcano. Researchers identified two previously undocumented multi-day sequences in October–November 2019, each beginning with increased volcano-tectonic events and followed by tremor-like, hydroacoustic or mixed signals. The paper describes the sequences as potentially associated with ongoing eruptive activity.

Technology: SimSiam self-supervised learning with t-SNE dimensionality reduction and DBSCAN clustering

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A SimSiam self-supervised representation model with t-SNE and DBSCAN clustering explored two months of continuous seismological records from near the Fani Maoré submarine volcano.
  • Still unknown: The event data are from two months in 2019 and a nearby ocean-bottom seismometer; the study does not establish exact event locations or independently prove that each sequence was eruptive. Authors state that instrumental noise cannot be ruled out for some high-energy signals, and describe the volcanic association as potential. It is a peer-reviewed 2025 issue paper published online 2024-10-10.
  • AI role: The self-supervised model constructed signal representations from full continuous seismograms; clustering surfaced two coherent, previously undocumented temporal event sequences that the researchers then interpreted using expert signal review. This avoids treating the result as a detector-only tool release.
