20 Mar 2026CMS Collaboration

ParticleNet enables first hadron-collider W-boson mass measurement from hadronic decays

Using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton data, CMS reports the first measurement of the W-boson mass in an all-jets final state at a hadron collider: 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV. The result comes from unfolded groomed jet-mass distributions for boosted hadronic W+jets events. The mass-decorrelated ParticleNet graph neural network tagger provides the main result by selecting the W-jet sample and suppressing quark/gluon multijet background; a non-neural N₂ tagger is used as a cross-check.

Technology: ParticleNet mass-decorrelated graph neural network jet tagger

The N₂ substructure observable is a separate cross-check.

25 Feb 2026CMS Collaboration; CERN Large Hadron Collider

First observation of single-top-quark production with a W and Z boson at CMS

CMS reports the first observation of single-top-quark production in association with a W and Z boson in proton-proton collisions. A Particle Transformer classifier and improved reconstruction separated the rare tWZ signal from major backgrounds in three- and four-lepton events. Combining 13 and 13.6 TeV data (200 fb⁻¹), the measured signal has 5.8σ observed significance (3.5σ expected) and cross sections of 248 ± 52 fb at 13 TeV and 242 ± 77 fb at 13.6 TeV. The result agrees with the Standard Model within 2.3σ.

Technology: Particle Transformer (ParT) multiclass classifier

Relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: CMS reports the first observation of single-top-quark production in association with a W and Z boson in proton-proton collisions.
  • Still unknown: This is the first observation of the Standard-Model tWZ production process, not evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. A 2024 CMS analysis had reported earlier evidence at 3.4σ; the 2026 paper adds 13.6 TeV Run 3 data and improved reconstruction/ML analysis to reach observation. The 5.8σ result combines 13 and 13.6 TeV datasets. Cross sections and signal strength are consistent with the Standard Model within the reported uncertainties.
  • AI role: CMS trained four ParT models, split by three- versus four-lepton signal region and 13 versus 13.6 TeV samples. In the three-lepton region, a four-node classifier distinguished tWZ, ttZ and other backgrounds; in the four-lepton region, a binary classifier separated tWZ from backgrounds. Its classifier-score categories entered the statistical signal extraction. The paper reports a substantial sensitivity gain versus the previous analysis: expected significance on the reused 13 TeV data rose from 1.4σ to 3.0σ, contributing to the first observation when combined with 13.6 TeV data.

Evidence sources: https://cms-results.web.cern.ch/cms-results/public-results/publications/TOP-24-009/ · https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19080

26 Jul 2023CMS Collaboration; CERN

CMS makes the first hadron-collider observation of boosted Z→cc̄ decay

In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider. The measured signal strength relative to the Standard Model prediction is 1.00 with combined uncertainty +0.19/−0.17. A deep neural network (DeepDoubleX) identified large-radius jets from charm-quark pairs; the paper also sets a 95% confidence-level upper limit of 47 times the Standard Model prediction on boosted H→cc̄ production.

Technology: DeepDoubleX deep neural network for double-charm jet tagging

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider.
  • Still unknown: The first-observation claim is specifically for Z→cc̄ in association with jets at high transverse momentum at a hadron collider; it is not the first observation of Z decay generally. The neural tagger is an important analysis tool, while the observation derives from the full CMS event selection and likelihood fit. In the same paper, boosted H→cc̄ was not observed; only an upper limit is reported.
  • AI role: The neural network processed secondary-vertex and particle-flow candidate features to classify boosted two-prong jets as charm versus light flavor and charm versus bottom. Its charm tagging separated the Z→cc̄ signal from dominant multijet backgrounds and supported the first observation in the boosted high-transverse-momentum regime.
