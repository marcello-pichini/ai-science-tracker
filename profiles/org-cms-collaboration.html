20 Mar 2026CMS Collaboration

ParticleNet enables first hadron-collider W-boson mass measurement from hadronic decays

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton data, CMS reports the first measurement of the W-boson mass in an all-jets final state at a hadron collider: 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV. The result comes from unfolded groomed jet-mass distributions for boosted hadronic W+jets events. The mass-decorrelated ParticleNet graph neural network tagger provides the main result by selecting the W-jet sample and suppressing quark/gluon multijet background; a non-neural N₂ tagger is used as a cross-check.

Technology: ParticleNet mass-decorrelated graph neural network jet tagger

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The analysis uses ParticleNet as its main W-jet tagger. Its graph neural network treats jet constituents as an unordered particle cloud and classifies W→qq′ jets against QCD multijet backgrounds; its mass-decorrelated output defines the signal/control regions used for the primary measurement. The N₂ substructure observable is a separate cross-check.
Test and result
Using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton data, CMS reports the first measurement of the W-boson mass in an all-jets final state at a hadron collider: 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an all-jets boosted-W extraction with 0.55 GeV total uncertainty, much less precise than the CMS leptonic-channel W-mass result; the paper frames it as a first step toward future all-jets precision measurements and as a test of jet-substructure modeling. The earlier preliminary CMS result of 80.77 ± 0.57 GeV was superseded by this same paper’s published 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV result and is not counted separately. Jet tagging, QCD background estimation, unfolding, hadronization and jet-energy modeling contribute analysis dependence and uncertainties.

25 Feb 2026CMS Collaboration; CERN Large Hadron Collider

First observation of single-top-quark production with a W and Z boson at CMS

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: CMS reports the first observation of single-top-quark production in association with a W and Z boson in proton-proton collisions. A Particle Transformer classifier and improved reconstruction separated the rare tWZ signal from major backgrounds in three- and four-lepton events. Combining 13 and 13.6 TeV data (200 fb⁻¹), the measured signal has 5.8σ observed significance (3.5σ expected) and cross sections of 248 ± 52 fb at 13 TeV and 242 ± 77 fb at 13.6 TeV. The result agrees with the Standard Model within 2.3σ.

Technology: Particle Transformer (ParT) multiclass classifier

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CMS trained four ParT models, split by three- versus four-lepton signal region and 13 versus 13.6 TeV samples. In the three-lepton region, a four-node classifier distinguished tWZ, ttZ and other backgrounds; in the four-lepton region, a binary classifier separated tWZ from backgrounds. Its classifier-score categories entered the statistical signal extraction. The paper reports a substantial sensitivity gain versus the previous analysis: expected significance on the reused 13 TeV data rose from 1.4σ to 3.0σ, contributing to the first observation when combined with 13.6 TeV data.
Test and result
CMS reports the first observation of single-top-quark production in association with a W and Z boson in proton-proton collisions.
Limitation / still unknown
This is the first observation of the Standard-Model tWZ production process, not evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. A 2024 CMS analysis had reported earlier evidence at 3.4σ; the 2026 paper adds 13.6 TeV Run 3 data and improved reconstruction/ML analysis to reach observation. The 5.8σ result combines 13 and 13.6 TeV datasets. Cross sections and signal strength are consistent with the Standard Model within the reported uncertainties.

26 Jul 2023CMS Collaboration; CERN

CMS makes the first hadron-collider observation of boosted Z→cc̄ decay

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider. The measured signal strength relative to the Standard Model prediction is 1.00 with combined uncertainty +0.19/−0.17. A deep neural network (DeepDoubleX) identified large-radius jets from charm-quark pairs; the paper also sets a 95% confidence-level upper limit of 47 times the Standard Model prediction on boosted H→cc̄ production.

Technology: DeepDoubleX deep neural network for double-charm jet tagging

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The neural network processed secondary-vertex and particle-flow candidate features to classify boosted two-prong jets as charm versus light flavor and charm versus bottom. Its charm tagging separated the Z→cc̄ signal from dominant multijet backgrounds and supported the first observation in the boosted high-transverse-momentum regime.
Test and result
In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider.
Limitation / still unknown
The first-observation claim is specifically for Z→cc̄ in association with jets at high transverse momentum at a hadron collider; it is not the first observation of Z decay generally. The neural tagger is an important analysis tool, while the observation derives from the full CMS event selection and likelihood fit. In the same paper, boosted H→cc̄ was not observed; only an upper limit is reported.
