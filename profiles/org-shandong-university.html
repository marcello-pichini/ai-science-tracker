1 Oct 2026Shandong University; Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Jining Medical University

Deep-learning prioritization of metagenomic products yielded cytotoxic compounds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: MetasynBGC used gene-cluster-family-guided metagenomic mining and a Chemprop graph neural network to prioritize predicted natural-product structures for synthesis. Six selected NRPS-derived compounds showed in-vitro cytotoxic activity across seven cancer cell lines, with reported IC50 values as low as 38.04 μM; compounds D and E showed cell-line-selective activity. These are in-vitro results and do not establish in-vivo efficacy or therapeutic benefit.

Technology: Chemprop graph neural network used to prioritize predicted biosynthetic products by antitumor activity

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A Chemprop graph neural network scored PRISM-predicted biosynthetic product structures for antitumor potential; the authors jointly used predicted scores and structural feasibility to prioritize compounds for synthesis and cytotoxicity testing.
Test and result
Six synthesized NRPS-derived compounds were tested in vitro across seven cancer cell lines; the paper reports IC50 values as low as 38.04 μM and selective activity for compounds D and E.
Limitation / still unknown
No in-vivo efficacy or clinical therapeutic benefit is established. The reported potency is limited to the tested in-vitro cancer cell-line assays.

30 Sep 2026Shandong University; National Astronomical Observatories, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Yunding Technology

WDNet-guided DESI follow-up confirms 75 new white dwarfs and 7 cataclysmic variables

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: WDNet detected candidates in DESI Legacy Surveys DR9 images. The researchers cross-matched 18,242 photometric candidates with DESI DR1 spectra, obtaining 719 candidate spectra; manual visual inspection of those spectra identified 75 new white dwarfs and 7 new cataclysmic variables. These spectroscopic classifications, not the much larger image-candidate list, are the reported verified sample.

Technology: WDNet, a deep-learning object-detection network based on YOLOv8 with multi-scale feature fusion

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
WDNet detected and localized white-dwarf candidates in DESI Legacy Surveys photometric images, thereby prioritizing objects for DESI spectral cross-match and manual spectral inspection. Human inspection of DESI spectra supplied the classification confirmation; the neural network did not itself confirm the new stellar classes.
Test and result
WDNet detected candidates in DESI Legacy Surveys DR9 images.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors confirmed 75 new WDs and 7 new CVs among the 719 candidates with DESI spectra, and explicitly note substantial contamination among remaining candidates and selection effects in the spectroscopic subsample. No atmosphere-model fitting was done for all identifications; the authors recommend further high-S/N spectroscopic modeling. The article's abstract/results/conclusion give inconsistent counts for the full detected/candidate catalog (18,242, 61,624, and 63,214 are all reported in different sections); this entry relies only on the consistent 719 spectral matches and 75+7 inspected confirmations. The full photometric catalog is not counted as confirmed discoveries.

12 Feb 2026Shandong University; National Astronomical Observatories, Chinese Academy of Sciences

Deep learning identifies 216 previously unrecognized Green Pea galaxies in DESI Legacy Survey imaging

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A MobileMamba-ECA image classifier searched DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys data and surfaced 775 Green Pea galaxy candidates. Cross-matching with DESI DR1 spectroscopy established 216 as previously unreported Green Pea galaxies: although spectra existed, the standard DESI pipeline had classified them only as generic galaxies. The study’s remaining 511 photometric-only objects are follow-up candidates and are not included in the confirmed count.

Technology: MobileMamba-ECA deep-learning classifier (Mamba state-space backbone with efficient channel attention)

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Classified multiband DESI Legacy Survey images to prioritize compact Green Pea galaxy sources from an 843,592-object parent sample; this enabled spectral cross-identification and recognition of 216 previously unreported examples.
Test and result
A MobileMamba-ECA image classifier searched DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys data and surfaced 775 Green Pea galaxy candidates.
Limitation / still unknown
The 216 newly identified galaxies have archival spectra and were classified with emission-line and BPT diagnostics; they were not all newly observed. The 511 sources without archival spectral coverage remain candidates and are excluded from this finding. The result is a newly recognized population/sample, not a claim that the galaxies themselves formed recently.

20 Mar 2025Shandong University; Shanghai Zelixir Biotech; Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences

CataPro-assisted enzyme mining identifies a high-activity Sphingobium enzyme for vanillin production

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: CataPro, a deep-learning model using pretrained protein-language representations and molecular fingerprints to predict enzyme kinetics, was combined with sequence and structure-based filtering to screen candidate enzymes for converting 4-vinylguaiacol to vanillin. The team experimentally identified Sphingobium sp. CSO (SsCSO), whose measured activity was 19.53 times that of the initial enzyme CSO2, and then used CataPro-guided engineering to increase SsCSO activity a further 3.34-fold.

Technology: CataPro

Editorial relevance 53 biology Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CataPro predicted kinetic parameters to prioritize candidate carotenoid-cleavage-oxygenase family enzymes and rank variants; researchers combined its predictions with BLAST, structural similarity and laboratory enzyme assays. It materially narrowed the enzyme search and guided mutations, while experiments established measured activity.
Test and result
The team experimentally identified Sphingobium sp.
Limitation / still unknown
The reported 19.53× and 3.34× figures are relative activities for the tested 4-vinylguaiacol oxidation assay; they do not establish industrial-scale yield or process performance. Model predictions were used alongside conventional sequence/structure methods, not as the sole discovery method.

5 Feb 2025Shandong University; and collaborating institutions

Latent diffusion designs antimicrobial peptides active against fungi and drug-resistant bacteria

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A latent-diffusion and molecular-dynamics pipeline generated and filtered peptide sequences; 40 synthesized candidates were tested, and 25 met the study's MIC threshold for antibacterial or antifungal activity. AMP-29 showed activity against Candida glabrata and efficacy in a murine skin-infection model, while AMP-24 showed in-vitro Gram-negative activity and efficacy in murine Acinetobacter baumannii skin and lung infection models.

Technology: Latent diffusion model coupled to molecular dynamics and peptide filtering/ranking

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The diffusion model generated a diverse peptide sequence set, and computational filtering selected 40 candidates for chemical synthesis and experimental antimicrobial testing; the selected peptides included leads taken into mouse infection models.
Test and result
A latent-diffusion and molecular-dynamics pipeline generated and filtered peptide sequences; 40 synthesized candidates were tested, and 25 met the study's MIC threshold for antibacterial or antifungal activity.
Limitation / still unknown
Twenty-five of 40 tested peptides met the authors' stated MIC ≤200 μM activity threshold, which includes a broad range of potency. The murine efficacy reports concern selected peptides and infection models, not human treatment. Some fungal peptides showed hemolysis; favorable safety cannot be generalized to the whole set.
