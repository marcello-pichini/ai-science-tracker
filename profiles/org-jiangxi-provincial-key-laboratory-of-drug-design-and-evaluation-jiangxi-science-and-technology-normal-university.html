23 Jul 2024Jiangxi Provincial Key Laboratory of Drug Design and Evaluation, Jiangxi Science and Technology Normal University; Jiangxi University of Traditional Chinese Medicine Cancer Research Center

Machine-learning-assisted screening yields an experimentally active FtsZ inhibitor candidate

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fused machine-learning and virtual-screening workflow narrowed compounds from three libraries to five candidate FtsZ inhibitors for bioactivity evaluation. The compound T3995 inhibited Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 6538 and Bacillus subtilis ATCC 9732 in reported assays, with MIC values of 32 and 2 μg/mL, respectively. Morphology and FtsZ-polymerization assays supported FtsZ-related antibacterial activity.

Technology: ROC-guided machine-learning selection combined with virtual screening, clustering and binding-model analysis

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
ROC-guided ML filtered candidates from three libraries; virtual screening of acquired compounds followed, then clustering and binding-model analysis prioritized five hits. Researchers performed growth, time-kill, cell-morphology, FtsZ-polymerization and hemolysis assays, including testing T3995.
Test and result
The compound T3995 inhibited Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 6538 and Bacillus subtilis ATCC 9732 in reported assays, with MIC values of 32 and 2 μg/mL, respectively.
Limitation / still unknown
Evidence is early in-vitro antibacterial activity and indirect support for an FtsZ-related mechanism; the abstract does not report direct target-binding measurements or in-vivo efficacy. Hemolysis was reported as safe at or below 16 μg/mL, while the MIC against S. aureus was 32 μg/mL, so this does not establish a therapeutic safety margin. The paper’s 2025 issue date follows its 2024 online publication date; the source-publication date here is the documented first online publication (23 July 2024).
