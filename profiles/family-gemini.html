Grower-guided AI reduces nitrogen use in two wheat field pilots

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A preprint coupled TabPFN nitrogen-response recommendations with Gemini 3 Pro schedule adjustments reviewed by an agronomist. In 2025–26 commercial field trials at two sites, treatments used 24.1% and 12.5% less nitrogen than standard practice, with no statistically significant yield differences; four replicate plots per treatment and no equivalence test limit any claim of preserved yield. Earlier 2024–25 static recommendations lowered grain protein at all five sites, significantly at several; one in-season LLM error was caught by agronomist review. These small within-study pilots do not establish general farming benefit or reduced emissions.

Technology: TabPFN tabular foundation model; Gemini 3 Pro language model; agronomist-reviewed grower-in-the-loop scheduling

20 Nov 2026Utrecht University; University of Genoa

Transformer-assisted bibliometric analysis maps changes in Italian computer science

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A study combined learned co-authorship and text representations with topic clustering to analyze a roster of 1,229 Italian computer-science scholars and publication records from 1980–2025. It identified three model-derived periods spanning foundational work, computer vision/machine learning, and later applied AI/health informatics. An ancillary Genoa pilot introduced 15 recommended researcher pairs, with four joint projects reported within six months. The topic patterns depend on metadata and analytical thresholds, 44.2% of paper abstracts were missing, and the small uncontrolled pilot does not establish a causal collaboration benefit.

Technology: Node2vec, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer, BERTopic/HDBSCAN, Falcon and Gemini topic-label generation

Language models flag spiritual pain in palliative nursing notes with many false positives

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Gemini 2.5 Flash and Microsoft Copilot detected all 56 expert-labeled spiritual-pain notes in a selected 500-note palliative-care sample. Positive predictive values were only 34% and 25%, respectively. The retrospective study identifies substantial over-detection rather than validating an autonomous diagnostic or clinical screening workflow.

Technology: Gemini 2.5 Flash; Microsoft Copilot language-model service

Family Gemini: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Pretrained language models applied prompted spiritual-pain definitions to free-text nursing notes, producing classifications compared with independent expert annotations; error analysis characterized their misinterpretations.
Test and result
From 6,858 nursing records in the last three weeks of life of 109 patients, keyword filtering retained 1,808 records and 500 were sampled. Two researchers annotated spiritual pain with consensus resolution. Japanese few-shot prompts supplied definitions/examples to Gemini 2.5 Flash and Copilot. Both detected all 56 reference-positive notes; specificity was 0.75/0.61 and positive predictive value 0.34/0.25. Gemini false positives frequently confused declining physical function and delirium with spiritual pain.
Limitation / still unknown
Selected single-hospital Japanese records, multiple notes per patient and keyword enrichment limit generalization and apparent sensitivity. Reference is retrospective note interpretation, not direct patient assessment. Copilot underlying model version was not specified. No unfiltered-record screening, prospective clinical utility, improved care or autonomous diagnosis was established; human verification remains necessary.

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.
