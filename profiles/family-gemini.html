Google's Empirical Research Assistance (ERA), a Gemini-based tree-search system, reaches expert-level performance on six benchmarks spanning genomics, public health, satellite imagery, neuroscience, time-series forecasting and mathematics. Its state-level U.S. hospital admission forecasts for flu, COVID-19 and RSV rank at or near the top of CDC leaderboards. Results are computational and checked against public benchmarks and leaderboards, with no wet-lab validation; exact benchmark scores were not visible in the sources read.

Gemini derives an exact analytic solution for the cosmic-string gravitational-radiation power integral

A Gemini Deep Think system, guided by tree search and numerical feedback, derived closed-form spectral coefficients for the integral determining gravitational radiation power from cosmic-string loops, resolving the paper’s stated open calculation. The authors compare the expressions against direct numerical integration over ranges of loop harmonic and geometry parameters, finding close agreement. This is a theoretical/computational result, not a new astronomical detection.

Technology: Google Gemini Deep Think integrated with tree search, executable Python evaluation and high-precision numerical feedback

Gemini Deep Think on research problems in mathematics, physics, and computer science

The Aletheia mathematical agent scored up to 90% on IMO-ProofBench Advanced and independently solved four open Erdős questions; work with experts covered 18 research problems, including a disproof of a submodular optimization conjecture. Its score on doctoral-level problems falls to about 38–46%; no result is claimed as a breakthrough, and the papers were submitted to journals.

Technology: Gemini Deep Think, Aletheia agent

AlphaEvolve: an agent for designing algorithms

An evolutionary agent based on Gemini and automated evaluators. It recovered an average of 0.7% of Google's computing resources, sped up a Gemini training kernel by 23%, and found an algorithm using 48 multiplications for 4×4 complex matrices. Across more than 50 open math problems, it matched the state of the art in about 75% and improved it in about 20%.
