20 Oct 2026Tsinghua University; University College London

Deep GraphCast features align with where cyclones are headed

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An analysis of 205 tropical cyclones found that deeper GraphCast layers carried features that aligned better with cyclone position and pressure structure six hours ahead than at the input time. Researchers identified those features using fitted sparse autoencoders and compared them with historical cyclone tracks and pressure fields.

Technology: GraphCast, sparse autoencoders

Family GraphCast: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 climate PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Sparse autoencoders learned features from GraphCast activations, allowing the researchers to compare feature patterns with distinct cyclone attributes at matching and later times.
Test and result
The retrospective analysis compared feature alignment at the input time and six hours ahead over 2,115 time steps, across 16 processing layers and five separate sparse decompositions of layer 8.
Limitation / still unknown
Feature alignment does not demonstrate that GraphCast uses a particular feature causally or that the feature improves operational forecasts.

17 Dec 2025NOAA

NOAA puts three AI-based global models into operation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: AIGFS (retrained from GraphCast) uses up to 99.7% less computing than GFS (a 16-day forecast in about 40 minutes); AIGEFS has skill comparable to GEFS with 9% of the computing; and HGEFS (62 hybrid members) outperforms GEFS and AIGEFS on most metrics. Cyclone intensity worsened in AIGFS v1.0; the announcement gives no numerical scores.

Technology: AIGFS, AIGEFS, and HGEFS (GraphCast-derived)

Family GraphCast: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 40 climate Press release Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: Test context unspecified in annotation. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AI global models: AIGFS forecasts with up to 99.7% less compute; AIGEFS is a 31-member AI ensemble with 9% of the GEFS computing; HGEFS combines 31 GEFS and 31 AIGEFS members.
Test and result
The release describes initial or early testing and claims skill comparable to GEFS and better ensemble performance, but gives no metrics, verification datasets or time periods.
Limitation / still unknown
The release states that AIGFS v1.0 degraded tropical cyclone intensity forecasts, that AIGEFS ensemble spread is still being improved, and that HGEFS hurricane intensity is being improved.

GraphCast: global 10-day weather forecasts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: It outperforms ECMWF’s HRES system on over 90% of 1,380 variables and test forecast lead times, and produces a 10-day forecast in under a minute on a single TPU. Comparison is limited to deterministic HRES; training uses ERA5 data.

Technology: GraphCast (graph neural network)

Family GraphCast: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 climate Blog postPublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A machine-learning model trained directly on reanalysis data.
Test and result
10-day forecasts of hundreds of variables at 0.25° in under a minute; outperforms the most accurate operational deterministic systems on 90% of 1,380 verification targets; supports better tropical-cyclone, atmospheric-river and extreme-temperature prediction.
Limitation / still unknown
Comparison with ensemble systems is not described in the abstract.
