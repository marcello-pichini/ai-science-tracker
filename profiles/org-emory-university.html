23 Oct 2026Emory University

Simulations estimate how solvents affect excited biphenyl’s relaxation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using learned MACE potentials, simulations estimated excited-state torsional relaxation in unsubstituted biphenyl across nine solvents. Mean times to complete about 63% of the relaxation broadly increased with viscosity in six aprotic solvents, from 0.254 to 3.519 picoseconds. Three protic solvents fell below that trend. Earlier experiments reported a qualitatively similar pattern for a substituted molecule.

Technology: MACE interatomic potential; PULSES multi-agent workflow

Editorial relevance 40 chemistry PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Agent-trained MACE interatomic potentials supplied the excited-state solute forces for trajectory ensembles of unsubstituted biphenyl.
Test and result
The analysis used 48 trajectories per solvent. Held-out reference geometries gave force-prediction R² of 0.9721 on the excited-state surface. The solvent trend was evaluated from simulated threshold-crossing times.
Limitation / still unknown
The relationship rests on a single-surface simulation and a classical solvent representation. Relaxation times for this specific molecule were not independently measured in the study.

31 Jul 2025Emory University

Physics-tailored machine learning reveals unexpected non-reciprocal forces in dusty plasmas

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural network built with physical constraints was trained on 3D particle-tracking data from a laboratory dusty plasma to infer the interaction forces between particles. The method describes the non-reciprocal forces more precisely than before and shows that some common theoretical assumptions about them are inaccurate. The authors checked their inferred force laws against laboratory experiments, which strengthens the claim, though the result comes from a single experimental system.

Technology: Physics-tailored neural network for inferring force laws from particle trajectories

Editorial relevance 67 physics Press releasePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A physics-informed neural network learned the non-reciprocal force laws from tracking data.
Test and result
A physics-constrained neural network fitted to 3D particle-tracking data from a laboratory dusty plasma inferred the interaction forces; particle masses were inferred two independent ways and the force laws were checked against laboratory experiments.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract states no explicit limitations; the result comes from a single experimental system.

23 Sep 2021Emory University; Council for Scientific and Industrial Research; North-West University; University of Pretoria

Random forests map South African particulate pollution patterns

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random-forest analysis combined ground stations, satellite data and environmental predictors to estimate daily particulate pollution around Gauteng during 2014–2018. It mapped persistent hotspots and seasonal differences, with lower estimated concentrations in much of the region after 2016. The comparison does not establish a policy effect.

Technology: Random forest exposure regression

Editorial relevance 33 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned tree ensembles infer particulate concentrations from satellite, meteorological, land-use and socioeconomic inputs; the field result is the resulting bounded regional exposure pattern.
Test and result
The model used 9,853 station-day observations from 20 monitors and 40 predictors. Random ten-fold cross-validation gave R² .80 and RMSE 9.40 µg/m³. Predicted winter concentrations were highest; Johannesburg and surrounding areas showed little clear change between 2014–2015 and 2016–2018 while much of the domain decreased.
Limitation / still unknown
Station-day random folds do not demonstrate transfer to unseen monitoring sites or future years. The model underestimates very high concentrations; top-quintile CV R² was .69. Satellite missingness and correction introduce additional uncertainty. A before/after-policy indicator is itself a model predictor, and the comparison has no causal control or counterfactual. Maps are modeled estimates, not complete ground measurements. No individual health benefit is measured.

23 Jun 2020Emory University; Indiana University Purdue University Indianapolis; Mentor Graphics India; K. N. Toosi University of Technology

COVID-Net and CoroNet show weaker performance on external chest X-ray data

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The v1 paper externally evaluates the pretrained COVID-Net and CoroNet chest-radiograph classifiers on three pre-pandemic public datasets and an Emory RT-PCR cohort. It finds markedly degraded generalization, including high false positives on pre-COVID images and poor COVID-Net sensitivity/precision on the institutional cohort; this is a specific retrospective diagnostic-validation result, not evidence of clinical benefit.

Technology: The authors apply two already-trained deep-learning CXR classifiers, COVID-Net and CoroNet, to independent public and institutional datasets; the models provide the COVID-19 classi

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The authors apply two already-trained deep-learning CXR classifiers, COVID-Net and CoroNet, to independent public and institutional datasets; the models provide the COVID-19 classifications under evaluation.
Test and result
The Results report COVID-Net false-positive rates of 55.3% on CheXpert and 23.4% on MIMIC-CXR, and on Emory 61.4% sensitivity, 0.54 F1 and 0.49 precision; CoroNet has a 93.7% false-negative rate on Emory. The central result is external generalization failure.
Limitation / still unknown
Evaluation is retrospective and uses datasets with different label mechanisms; the paper notes PCR positivity and radiographic findings are not identical. No clinical benefit was tested. arXiv metadata did not provide an affiliation, so the record states that the affiliation was not provided.
