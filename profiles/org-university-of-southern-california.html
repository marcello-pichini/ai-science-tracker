23 Nov 2026University of Southern California; Simons Foundation

Learned autism profiles show different clinical and genetic associations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A November 23, 2026 preprint used DDRTree to learn three clinical profiles and continuous scores from 14 measures in 6,355 autistic children in SPARK, then projected the framework into 2,717 children in the Simons Simplex Collection. The authors report preserved clinical and medication-use patterns and preliminary differences in common- and rare-variant associations. These are retrospective, researcher-tuned dimensions, not established diagnostic categories or causal genetic mechanisms; some genetic results differed between cohorts and require larger-sample validation.

Technology: DDRTree in Monocle 2; modified k-nearest-neighbor projection; MissForest

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
DDRTree learned a branching representation of age/sex-adjusted clinical measures and continuous profile scores; modified nearest-neighbor projection transferred the learned framework to the SSC cohort for comparison with clinical and genetic features.
Test and result
Retrospective discovery in 6,355 SPARK participants and projection into 2,717 SSC participants, with clinical/service/medication associations and common- and rare-genetic-variant analyses. DDRTree parameters were refined against clinical observations; external projection used imputed/harmonized measures.
Limitation / still unknown
The proposed profiles are not validated diagnostic categories, treatment-selection rules or causal genetic explanations. Genetic analyses were preliminary and some findings did not reproduce in SSC; cohort size, imputation and phenotype harmonization limit inference.

26 Oct 2026University of Southern California; OpenSciLab

Coding agents continued editing after repeated refactoring requests

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across 3,000 refactoring turns on 60 Java codebases, coding agents made edits on 2,546 turns even though each follow-up supplied no new requirement. Some runs resumed editing after a no-change turn. The study observed this behavior in Codex and Claude Code across five models.

Technology: Codex with GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4-mini or GPT-5.5; Claude Code with Opus 4.8 or Sonnet 4.6

Family Claude: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The trained coding agents decided whether to edit after repeated nonspecific developer prompts, yielding directly observable behavior across sessions.
Test and result
Each agent received up to ten nonspecific refactoring requests in a persistent session. The study measured diffs over 50 compact reference implementations and 10 highly graded object-oriented assignments.
Limitation / still unknown
The starting projects were selected for quality, but not every individual edit was independently evaluated for usefulness. The finding measures continued modification under repeated requests, not code degradation in production.

21 Oct 2026University of Southern California

Routine questions led AI agents to disclose sensitive inferences in simulation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A simulated GPT-5.6 Sol agent revealed restricted conclusions in 85 of 1,200 benign queries designed to be answerable from one document. The study tested synthetic documents and confidentiality controls, showing that an ordinary question can cause an agent to combine information into a restricted inference.

Technology: GPT-5.6 Sol and studied agent harnesses

Family GPT-5: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Language-model agents including GPT-5.6 Sol process synthetic documents; researchers compare aggregate disclosure frequencies under confidentiality controls.
Test and result
Researchers measured aggregate disclosures across controlled synthetic queries, reporting a 7.08% rate with a 95% interval of 3.75–10.67% for GPT-5.6 Sol across the tested safeguard and document conditions.
Limitation / still unknown
These are controlled outcomes on synthetic material and specified access settings. The rate does not estimate real-world disclosure frequency.

22 Jan 2023University of Southern California; California Institute of Technology; University of Southern California Southern California Earthquake Center

CNN-augmented focal mechanisms reveal stress-linked faulting changes around major southern California earthquakes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: CNN phase and first-motion pickers supplied additional waveform polarities and arrivals for a 297,478-event southern California focal-mechanism catalog, about 40% larger than the prior catalog. The authors used the expanded catalog to observe systematic post-mainshock faulting-style changes spatially correlated with estimated static stress perturbations from the 2010 El Mayor–Cucapah and 2019 Ridgecrest earthquakes, including changes more than 100 km from the epicentres.

Technology: Convolutional neural networks for seismic phase arrival and first-motion polarity picking

Editorial relevance 53 physics Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Extracted additional phase arrivals and P-wave first-motion polarities from noisy seismograms; combined with existing observations in HASH focal-mechanism solutions to increase catalog coverage and resolution for small earthquakes.
Test and result
CNN phase and first-motion pickers supplied additional waveform polarities and arrivals for a 297,478-event southern California focal-mechanism catalog, about 40% larger than the prior catalog.
Limitation / still unknown
Catalog quality varies with location and station coverage; the authors report uncertainty in some solutions and frame stress processes as associations/inferences. The catalog provides improved observations, not a claim of earthquake predictability.

30 Nov 2022University of Southern California; University of California, Berkeley; University of Washington

Concentrated poverty, ambient air pollution, and child cognitive development

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a national infant cohort, random-forest mediation models estimated that about one-third of the association between neighborhood poverty and age-four cognitive scores was statistically mediated by air-quality disparities. This observational analysis does not establish that pollution caused the cognitive differences.

Technology: Supervised random forests within causal mediation estimated pollutant-specific mediation of poverty-associated cognitive differences.

Editorial relevance 53 multidisciplinary Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Supervised random forests within causal mediation estimated pollutant-specific mediation of poverty-associated cognitive differences.
Test and result
The study matched a national infant cohort to more than 50 pollutants and performed mediation and sensitivity analyses using supervised random forests.
Limitation / still unknown
Observational mediation estimates rely on measured covariates and do not prove causal effects of poverty or pollutants.
