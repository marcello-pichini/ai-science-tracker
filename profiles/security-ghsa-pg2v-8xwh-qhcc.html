SSRF in optional Tlon Urbit authentication

OpenClaw · AI involvement attributed to Endor Labs AI SAST engine

Finding and attribution

The optional Tlon/Urbit extension accepts a configurable base URL and uses it in an outbound authentication request. A party able to influence that configured URL can cause the gateway to request attacker-chosen hosts, including internal addresses.

Product: OpenClaw. AI system: Endor Labs AI SAST engine. Team / organization: Endor Labs.

CVE identifiers: Not established in the cited evidence.

AI and human contribution (source account): Endor Labs’ case-specific report describes its AI SAST engine’s trace for this finding and the researcher’s subsequent validation with a proof of concept. This supports AI-assisted discovery with human validation; the specific model is not named. The six-case article was updated after initial publication, so the exact first public date of its current attribution text is not established.

Human involvement is only described where the source account establishes it; unknown involvement does not mean none. Vendor confirmation of a flaw does not confirm every claim about AI contribution.

Discovery context: The upstream security advisory documents the public disclosure and fix. The sources do not establish whether defenders knew of the issue or whether it was exploited before disclosure.

Remediation status: fix documented. OpenClaw 2026.2.14 fixes the issue by validating/normalizing the URL and blocking private/internal hosts by default; private-network access is opt-in via channels.tlon.allowPrivateNetwork.

A source-documented fix does not establish installed patches or current safety. Source checking does not independently reproduce the finding or establish observed exploitation.
