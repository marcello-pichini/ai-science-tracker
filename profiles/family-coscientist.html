19 May 2026Google DeepMind and Stanford

Co-Scientist: repurposed drugs against liver fibrosis

In Gary Peltz's Stanford lab, two of the three existing drugs proposed by Co-Scientist blocked fibrosis and promoted regeneration in human liver cells, while Peltz's own two choices showed no benefit. The anticancer drug vorinostat blocked 91% of a damage response that can lead to scarring. Cell experiments only; published in Advanced Science.

Co-Scientist and ALS: hypotheses about RNA-based mechanisms

19 May 2026Google DeepMind and University of Cambridge

Co-Scientist and molecular switches in severe infectious diseases

Clare Bryant (Cambridge) used Co-Scientist, which ranked hypotheses and pointed to a protein she had not considered down to individual amino acids, to study molecular switches behind diseases such as sepsis. The lab expects to build cell lines with those mutations in about six months instead of two or three years; the hypotheses are not yet validated.

19 May 2026Google DeepMind and University of Edinburgh

Co-Scientist and mechanisms of metabolic steatohepatitis (MASH)

Filippo Menolascina (Edinburgh) used Co-Scientist to search the literature for combinations of MASH therapies. The tool proposed the NLRP3 inflammasome as a link between inflammation and metabolism, and the post says the hypothesis was later tested experimentally. No numbers are given; the drug combinations were suggested for testing, not demonstrated.

19 May 2026Google DeepMind

Co-Scientist: genetic factors to rejuvenate cells

Omar Abudayyeh and Jonathan Gootenberg used Co-Scientist to search tens of thousands of papers, obtaining over 20 proposed genetic factors for reversing cellular aging, a couple of which were validated in the lab, and reducing analysis of genetic-screening data from six months to a few days. The post gives no sample size or peer review status.

19 Feb 2025Google Research

AI co-scientist: a multi-agent system for generating hypotheses

A system based on Gemini 2.0 that generates hypotheses and research proposals. In the lab, three drugs it suggested for repurposing against acute myeloid leukemia reduced cancer-cell viability, and all suggested treatments for liver fibrosis showed activity. Expert assessments covered only 11 targets; fibrosis results awaited a Stanford report.

Technology: AI co-scientist (Gemini 2.0, multi-agent)
