2 Jul 2024University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill; University of Missouri

FRASE-bot screening identifies a small-molecule ligand that selectively kills CIB1-dependent cancer cells

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: FRASE-bot used a neural-network nativeness score to triage protein–ligand fragments, then used the retained structural features to seed virtual screening for Calcium and Integrin Binding Protein 1 (CIB1). Of 56 purchased virtual-screen hits, 29 showed dose-dependent CIB1 displacement; compound UNC10245380 (UNC5380) had a TR-FRET displacement IC50 of 8.0 ± 2.5 μM and selectively killed CIB1-depletion-sensitive triple-negative breast cancer cells in the reported assays, with no detected killing in an insensitive line.

Technology: FRASE-bot: fragment-in-structural-environment screening with a neural-network nativeness scoring model

Editorial relevance 53 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The neural network triaged fragments by predicted native-ligand pose likelihood; selected fragments informed pharmacophore construction and virtual screening. The resulting purchased compounds were then tested in TR-FRET binding-displacement and cell-death assays. The model did not perform the wet-lab validation.
Test and result
Of 56 purchased virtual-screen hits, 29 showed dose-dependent CIB1 displacement; compound UNC10245380 (UNC5380) had a TR-FRET displacement IC50 of 8.0 ± 2.5 μM and selectively killed CIB1-depletion-sensitive triple-negative breast cancer cells in the reported assays, with no detected killing in an insensitive line.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors describe UNC10245380 as a starting chemical probe/lead, not a drug. Binding was inferred from peptide displacement in TR-FRET and a docking pose; the pose is not a solved small-molecule complex. Selective cell killing was in vitro at 30 μM, with no animal or clinical evidence. The earlier Research Square manuscript is the same underlying paper/result and is not counted separately.

12 Oct 2021University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill; Carnegie Mellon University

Machine learning guides automated synthesis of 19F MRI copolymer agents

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An automated continuous-flow polymer platform synthesized 397 copolymer compositions in a six-variable space, in iterative cycles guided by machine learning. Exploring under 0.9% of the compositional space, the models identified more than ten copolymers that outperformed state-of-the-art 19F MRI agents. Validation is experimental across hundreds of real syntheses, but the imaging performance claims are limited to the compositions tested.

Technology: Machine learning models guiding automated flow copolymer synthesis

31 Jul 2020University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill; Collaborations Pharmaceuticals (United States); RTI International

Synergistic drug combinations and machine learning for drug repurposing in chordoma.

Source check: Needs source check. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Bayesian models trained on prior chordoma-cell screening data ranked compounds for in-vitro testing; AZD2014 inhibited U-CH1 and U-CH2 cells (IC50 0.35 and 0.61 μM), though maximal U-CH2 inhibition was limited. Separately tested drug-pair synergy is not attributed to AI. No animal or clinical efficacy is shown.

Technology: Bayesian machine-learning inhibition models

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Bayesian machine-learning inhibition models trained on prior chordoma-cell screening data ranked compounds from an NIH NCATS asset set for in-vitro testing; the drug-combination experiments were a separate study strategy.
Test and result
Indexed primary Results report AZD2014 IC50 of 0.35 μM in U-CH1 and 0.61 μM in U-CH2, with limited maximal U-CH2 inhibition. Afatinib/palbociclib were known-inhibitor comparators; drug combinations were a separate study strategy. Replicate details were not independently assessed.
Limitation / still unknown
Cell-line activity does not establish animal or clinical efficacy. Complete model-validation and replicate methods were not independently reviewed.
