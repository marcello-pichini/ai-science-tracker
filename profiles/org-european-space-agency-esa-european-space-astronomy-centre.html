Machine-learning selection and Gaia astrometry confirm a 62-Jupiter-mass brown dwarf orbiting G 15-6

A machine-learning search of Gaia DR3 two-body astrometric orbits flagged G 15-6 as a likely low-mass companion system. Combining Gaia’s astrometric orbit with archival radial-velocity measurements resolved the inclination ambiguity and yielded a companion mass of 62.43 (+6.16/−5.44) Jupiter masses, firmly in the brown-dwarf range. The authors identify it as the first independently validated brown-dwarf companion selected by their ML method. The radial-velocity data predate the study; the new result is the Gaia orbit and AI-guided identification that resolve the companion mass, not a newly observed companion in 2025.

Technology: Semi-supervised anomaly detection, extreme gradient boosting, and random-forest classifiers applied to Gaia DR3 astrometric-orbit solutions

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A machine-learning search of Gaia DR3 two-body astrometric orbits flagged G 15-6 as a likely low-mass companion system.
  • Still unknown: The source study is an ML-assisted target-identification and Gaia-orbit analysis, not a 2025 direct detection. Archival radial-velocity observations already established binary motion but did not constrain the inclination enough to prove a substellar mass; Gaia astrometry supplies that missing orbital constraint. The wider candidate list includes false-positive Gaia solutions and unresolved stellar/substellar cases, which are not counted as confirmed companions here.
  • AI role: The machine-learning pipeline ranked Gaia DR3 two-body systems for likely low-mass companions and selected G 15-6 as a brown-dwarf candidate. The Gaia astrometric orbit, combined with the independent archival radial-velocity orbit, resolved the inclination and established the companion’s mass; the models used only the Gaia orbital solutions for their candidate selection.
