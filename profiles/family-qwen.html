20 Nov 2026City University Malaysia

Vocabulary app with Qwen tutoring shows a short-term score advantage in eight classes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A twelve-week teaching study randomized four matched pairs of university tutorial classes to a vocabulary application or non-adaptive word lists. The application bundled Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct examples, wav2vec2.0 pronunciation feedback and rule-based review scheduling. Among 92 completers from 100 allocated students, class-adjusted posttest scores favored the application by 6.6 points on a 100-point test (95% CI 1.3–11.9). This is a prospective comparison of the complete instructional package, not an isolated effect of AI. Eight classes, repeated test items, complete-case analysis and no delayed-retention measure limit generalization.

Technology: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; wav2vec 2.0 speech model; VocabPilot EDU 2.1.4

Family Qwen: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 humanities Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A fixed named Qwen instruction model generated target-word examples/explanations, with output checks and instructor audits; a local wav2vec2.0 acoustic model supplied pronunciation practice feedback. The scheduling/recommendation rules themselves are not learned AI.
Test and result
March–June 2025 twelve-week application use after matched-pair class allocation, followed by week 12 testing. 100 students allocated, 92 analyzed (46 per condition); eight classes with four per arm. A class-random-intercept model estimated 6.6 points adjusted advantage, 95% CI 1.3–11.9, p=.022.
Limitation / still unknown
The complete package includes deterministic scheduling, mobile delivery, language-model dialogue and speech feedback; the contribution of each component was not isolated. Same-item pre/post testing, only eight clusters, attrition and self-reported control study time constrain inference. Long-term retention and transfer to broader language performance were not tested.

11 Nov 2026Independent research

An unstated country representation guides Qwen’s capital answers

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a controlled two-step geography task, researchers changed a representation of a country that was never named in the prompt. At the city token, the intervention redirected Qwen2.5-7B to the paired country’s capital in 79% of 1,038 pairs. The country was also readable later in the prompt, where the intervention had no effect at the tested layers. This experiment is reported in the November 11 preprint version.

Technology: Qwen2.5-7B, cross-fitted country subspaces

Family Qwen: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Country subspaces were estimated on one half of the pretrained Qwen2.5-7B city examples, then used in a controlled intervention on matched held-out city examples.
Test and result
The preregistered intervention compared held-out matched city pairs against random and copied-embedding subspaces, whole-state replacement, and same-country pairs.
Limitation / still unknown
The test concerns one model and a constrained task on cities the model already answered correctly. Other tasks and weak latent signals remain unestablished.

27 Oct 2026FAIR, Meta Superintelligence Labs

Distilled language models retained a watermark-linked weight signal

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In controlled comparisons of models trained on matching clean or watermarked examples, researchers measured a watermark-linked component in the distilled models’ parameter changes. Its detectable signature persisted across some later model modifications tested in the study.

Technology: Fine-tuned Qwen language models; learned Qwen2.5-3B measurement probe

Matched learned student models allowed the authors to isolate a component of weight change associated with the watermark without changing the training task.
Test and result
The main comparison held the training task, prompts, initialization and recipe fixed between the clean and watermarked models. The authors measured changes in weights separately from output-level detections.
Limitation / still unknown
The represented observation is limited to tested models, watermark schemes and math distillation settings. The measured component is defined relative to a paired task-only change.

26 Oct 2026CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security; German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence; University of Technology Nuremberg; Inria

AI reviewers favored confident wording in matched research papers

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a 504-paper experiment, all six model reviewers more often gave higher scores to rewrites with confident wording. Claude Sonnet 4.6 favored the confident version in 40% of pairs and the less confident version in 6%, with 54% tied. Researchers checked text similarity and wording shifts before comparing evaluations.

Technology: Claude Sonnet 4.6; GPT-5.5; Gemini; Gemma; Qwen; GLM

Test and result

25 Oct 2026Google

Language models flagged missing evidence less often when an unsupported premise was usable

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In paired arithmetic tasks, making an unsupported premise usable reduced missing-evidence flags by 8–30 percentage points across four tested language models. The source support was unchanged. Some answers may reflect conditional reasoning because the instructions did not require rejection of premises supplied in the question.

Technology: Gemma and Qwen language models

Family Qwen: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 53 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Four pretrained Gemma and Qwen language models answered mathematically matched prompts with equally unsupported premises that varied in usability.
Test and result
The controlled factorial design varied actionability and the mention of relevant entities on GSM8K. All 16 primary paired contrasts survived Holm adjustment.
Limitation / still unknown
The selected tasks and instructions limit how broadly the result applies. It does not show that all answers accept unsupported statements as facts. Pattern-based scoring was revised after a 40-response audit; residual condition-dependent scoring error was not bounded by that small check.

23 Oct 2026Seoul School of Integrated Sciences and Technologies; Liaoning Mechanical & Electrical College of Technology

Language models trusted incorrect reassurances more than incorrect warnings in a defensive audit

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In tests of a prerecorded defensive audit dataset, incorrect reassurances moved language-model verdicts toward agreement more than incorrect warnings did. A prespecified check on seven additional model configurations found a mean asymmetry of 0.16, with a 95% interval of 0.10–0.24. This measures verdict behavior under controlled evaluation conditions.

Technology: DeepSeek; Qwen; GLM; Kimi audit models

21 Oct 2026The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications

Matching final answers masked different forgetting patterns during fine-tuning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers recovered 100% final performance on an old generation task across several replay budgets for a Qwen model. The same training runs nevertheless followed different trajectories in the probability assigned to correct old answers. The finding came from evaluating checkpoints throughout training as well as the final model.

Technology: Qwen and Mistral language-model fine-tuning

Family Qwen: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Fine-tuned Qwen and Mistral models enabled direct measurement of task outputs and loss at multiple checkpoints under matched training protocols.
Test and result
The study varied old-task replay budgets at matched training settings and separately measured old-task generation at the endpoint and negative log-likelihood across training checkpoints.
Limitation / still unknown
The result is specific to these models, old-task response tests and replay schedules. Checkpoint behavior cannot establish whether knowledge was removed or merely harder to elicit. The qualified original Qwen retained-response bank contained only 17 examples; its 100% endpoint is not broad preservation of old-task knowledge.

9 Oct 2025University of California, Los Angeles; University of Alabama at Birmingham

Multimodal language models extract seizure semiology and support patient-held-out seizure classification

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An October 2025 preprint analyzed 90 video-EEG-confirmed events from 29 patients at one epilepsy-monitoring unit using vision-language and audio-language models to extract clinical features. With leave-one-patient-out testing, KNN using automated features achieved F1 and AUC of 0.76, below the classifier using expert features. Automated feature extraction itself had mean F1 of 0.51 against expert annotations, and the small retrospective cohort does not establish clinical utility or external generalization. A later journal version was dated November 2026; the detailed results here describe the accessible preprint.

Technology: InternVL vision-language models and Qwen audio/vision-language models with downstream KNN and other classifiers

Vision-language and audio-language models extract clinical seizure semiology from patient video/audio; classifier evaluation shows moderate patient-held-out discrimination between video-EEG-confirmed epileptic and nonepileptic events.
