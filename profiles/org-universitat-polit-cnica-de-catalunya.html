Spectroscopic follow-up revises Gaia machine-learning white-dwarf classifications and reveals a large magnetic and carbon-rich population

Follow-up spectra for 255 Gaia white dwarfs were compared with machine-learning classifications from low-resolution Gaia spectra. The study reports that only 5 of 112 objects classified as massive DBs were genuine DB white dwarfs; most were magnetic white dwarfs or warm DQ stars. Its spectroscopy also catalogued 63 magnetic white dwarfs and 29 warm DQs in the selected sample, while confirming high classification accuracy for major spectral types within the classifier’s training set.

Technology: Random-forest spectral classifier applied to Gaia low-resolution spectra

  • Still unknown: The result is a follow-up characterization of a selected 500-pc sample, not a complete census of white dwarfs. It corrects a specific machine-learning-labelled ‘massive DB’ subpopulation; reported counts should not be extrapolated to Gaia’s full white-dwarf population.
  • AI role: The classifier assigned initial white-dwarf spectral types and selected objects of interest; astronomers obtained Gran Telescopio Canarias medium-resolution spectra and visually inspected them to determine the objects’ types and magnetic features.
