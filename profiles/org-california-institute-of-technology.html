4 Aug 2026California Institute of Technology; University of California, Los Angeles; AITHYRA / Austrian Academy of Sciences

Machine learning identifies the first enzyme for phenazine–thiol conjugation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A genome-informed machine-learning workflow (ML-CITO) used contrastive learning over protein-language-model representations and genomic context, starting from 14 known phenazine-modifying sequences, to identify PTC. Biochemical characterization showed PTC catalyzes glutathione-dependent phenazine modification, a reaction previously assumed to occur non-enzymatically.

Technology: ML-CITO (genome-context-informed data augmentation plus protein-language-space contrastive learning)

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model ranked candidate phenazine-interacting proteins from sparse known examples; the team then expressed and biochemically tested the candidate enzyme. AI nomination was a direct step in discovering the previously unknown enzymatic reaction.
Test and result
A genome-informed machine-learning workflow (ML-CITO) used contrastive learning over protein-language-model representations and genomic context, starting from 14 known phenazine-modifying sequences, to identify PTC.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper reports that deleting the PTC gene did not cause a strong fitness disadvantage under tested conditions. The demonstrated chemistry and substrate context should not be generalized to other phenazine reactions.

16 Jan 2025California Institute of Technology; University of California, Berkeley; Elegen Corp

Active learning identifies a protoglobin variant with 99% yield and cis-selective cyclopropanation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An iterative machine-learning campaign engineered the protoglobin from Pyrobaculum arsenaticum (ParPgb) for non-native cyclopropanation of 4-vinylanisole. After two model-guided rounds following an initial library screen, variant MPFDY gave 99% total cyclopropane yield and a 14:1 cis:trans ratio in the reported assay, compared with a parent variant that produced about 40% yield and favored trans product. The result demonstrates a specific sequence combination with improved yield and stereoselectivity in this enzyme scaffold.

Technology: ALDE (Active Learning-assisted Directed Evolution), using a bootstrapped deep-neural-network ensemble and Thompson sampling

Editorial relevance 47 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
ALDE trained on measured sequence–yield data, quantified model uncertainty, and selected variants across two iterative rounds in a five-residue, 3.2-million-sequence design space. Experimental screening measured product yield and stereoselectivity; the model-guided variants included MPFDY, whose combined mutations improved the targeted cis-product objective where tested single mutations and their naive recombinations did not.
Test and result
After two model-guided rounds following an initial library screen, variant MPFDY gave 99% total cyclopropane yield and a 14:1 cis:trans ratio in the reported assay, compared with a parent variant that produced about 40% yield and favored trans product.
Limitation / still unknown
Cyclopropanation is an established non-native enzyme reaction class; the study's distinct result is the ALDE-guided ParPgb sequence and its measured product yield/selectivity. The 99% yield and 14:1 ratio are study assay results, not evidence of industrial process readiness. The study is a single enzyme-engineering campaign.

20 Jul 2022California Institute of Technology; University of Connecticut

Interpretable machine learning discovers ultrapermeable polymer membranes for gas separation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Multitask machine-learning models trained on experimental gas permeability data screened over 9 million hypothetical polymers. They identified hundreds of never-before-seen ultrapermeable membranes, with molecular dynamics simulations confirming the predicted permeabilities of the top candidates. The validation is computational rather than fabricated membranes, so the candidates remain unproven experimentally.

Technology: Multitask machine learning with model interpretation, plus molecular dynamics

8 Jul 2022California Institute of Technology

Semi-supervised learning selects a lithium thioborate for vacancy engineering

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Semi-supervised learning screened lithium-containing structures and selected Li3BS3 for experimental characterization. The parent material was a poor room-temperature conductor. Researchers then introduced vacancies by Si substitution and amorphized the substituted material; the paper reports approximately four orders of magnitude higher ionic conductivity for a-Li2.95B0.95Si0.05S3. The model selected the framework, while human-led defect engineering produced the high-conductivity sample. This does not establish that pristine Li3BS3 is superionic.

Technology: Semi-supervised learning; agglomerative clustering

Editorial relevance 60 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Semi-supervised learning used literature-derived conductivity labels and structural descriptors to rank lithium-containing structures and select Li3BS3 for experimental characterization.
Test and result
The study selected Li3BS3 for characterization and reports increased ionic conductivity after Si substitution and amorphization, with the combined vacancy-engineering treatment improving conductivity by roughly four orders of magnitude.
Limitation / still unknown
The ML model selected a framework candidate; vacancy engineering and synthesis were subsequent researcher-led steps. The primary page was checked through indexed full-text results because direct article opening returned 403.

5 Apr 2022California Institute of Technology; Cornell University

Learned phase models identify synergistic photoanode mixtures

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Learning from measured diffraction and photocurrent data identified Bi-Cu-V oxide phase mixtures whose photoactivity exceeded an additive non-interacting-phase model. BiVO4-like phases showed beneficial associations with copper-vanadate phases. This is an experimentally grounded composition-property result; the underlying electronic, catalytic and morphological mechanisms remain unresolved, and device-level solar-fuel efficiency was not established.

19 Aug 2021California Institute of Technology

Machine learning-assisted directed evolution with in silico combinatorial screening

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A path-independent machine-learning protocol screens full combinatorial protein libraries in silico, with training-set design that avoids near-zero-fitness variants. On a four-site epistatic fitness landscape the optimized protocol reached the global fitness maximum up to 81-fold more often than single-step greedy optimization. Caveat: validation is on a combinatorial fitness landscape dataset, not a new wet-lab evolution campaign.

8 Apr 2021California Institute of Technology; IPAC; Zwicky Transient Facility collaboration

Deep learning finds comet C/2020 T2 in a single ZTF image

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The Tails EfficientDet model identified the extended object C/2020 T2 in one Zwicky Transient Facility Twilight Survey image on 2020 October 7. Follow-up orbital work confirmed it as a long-period comet. The study reports this as the first comet discovery assisted by deep learning; it also recovered a previously known comet, which is not counted as a new discovery here.

Technology: Tails, an EfficientDet-based deep-learning comet detector

Editorial relevance 53 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Detected the comet’s extended coma/tail morphology in a single survey image and surfaced C/2020 T2 for discovery/follow-up; this enabled detection without requiring multi-epoch orbit linking at the initial search stage.
Test and result
The Tails EfficientDet model identified the extended object C/2020 T2 in one Zwicky Transient Facility Twilight Survey image on 2020 October 7.
Limitation / still unknown
The model surfaced the object, while human/standard follow-up established its orbit and comet classification. The entry counts only the newly discovered C/2020 T2, not recovery of P/2016 J3/P/2021 A3 or the detector’s performance metrics.

10 Jul 2020California Institute of Technology

An attention-based neural network generates functional secretion signal peptides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An attention-based Transformer trained on signal-peptide data from Swiss-Prot generated candidate secretion sequences. Experimental testing in an industrial Bacillus subtilis strain found that model-generated signal peptides directed secreted enzyme activity competitive with industrially used signal peptides. The result establishes functional candidates in the reported host and assay; it does not establish broad performance across proteins or production hosts. The primary institutional record gives the article publication date as 2020-07-10.
