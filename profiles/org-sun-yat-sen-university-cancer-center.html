24 Nov 2026Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center; Sun Yat-sen University School of Computer Science and Engineering; Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center

Deep learning scores Claudin18.2 staining in gastric cancer slides across clones

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A pipeline that scores Claudin18.2 immunohistochemistry on whole-slide images used a LoRA-adapted UNI pathology foundation model for tumour recognition, adapter-based staining-intensity classification and two rounds of active learning. Agreement with pathologist-consensus 2/3+ scoring was ICC 0.849 on 195 internal test slides and 0.894 on 135 prospectively collected slides, and ICC 0.792-0.827 for three other antibody clones tested without retraining. In a non-randomised reader study, AI assistance improved every pathologist's agreement with the reference standard and shortened review time. The authors state the study is single-centre with limited prospective and cross-clone cohorts, uses expert consensus rather than absolute ground truth, may propagate errors across modules, and provides no treatment-response or outcome data; at the 50% cutoff the retrospective AUC was lower than one pathologist's.

Technology: UNI pathology foundation model (ViT-L/16) adapted with LoRA and inserted adapters, plus active learning, for tumour recognition and staining-intensity scoring.

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Foundation-model-based tumour recognition and staining-intensity modules produce the slide-level Claudin18.2 score.
Test and result
Slide-level agreement with pathologist consensus on 195 internal test slides and 135 prospectively collected slides, cross-clone evaluation of three further antibody clones, and a six-pathologist reader study.
Limitation / still unknown
Single centre; limited prospective and cross-clone cohorts; reader study not randomised; no therapy-response or outcome data; the paper does not state whether the model was frozen before prospective enrolment, so this is treated as evaluation on collected slides rather than prospective workflow use.

23 Oct 2026Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center; State Key Laboratory of Oncology in South China

Survival machine learning estimates metastasis-directed radiotherapy benefit in NPC

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In 1083 patients from two centers with nasopharyngeal carcinoma and exclusive distant metastasis, target trial emulation estimated a 4.2-month gain in 3-year progression-free survival restricted mean time with consolidative metastasis-directed radiotherapy after chemoimmunotherapy. A gradient boosting machine reached 3-year PFS AUROC 0.875 internally and 0.877 in a geographically external cohort, and a T-learner estimated absolute risk reductions of 21.6%, 14.7% and 5.7% by risk group. The analysis is retrospective and observational.

Technology: Eight survival machine learning algorithms (gradient boosting best) and T-learner treatment effect estimation

3 Mar 2026Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center; and collaborating hospitals/cohorts

Prospective multicentre study combines EV protein biomarkers and machine learning for preclinical oesophageal cancer detection

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The BarFlare assay and an interpretable multi-criteria decision-making classification-fusion machine-learning framework combined extracellular-vesicle SCC and MMP13 biomarkers with clinical factors. The model was evaluated in prospective multicentre diagnostic cohorts and two population cohorts; the longitudinal cohort contained baseline samples from 30 people who later developed oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma, with a reported median lead time of 34.9 months and AUC 0.864. The paper also reports external diagnostic validation in two cohorts.

Technology: Multi-criteria decision-making Classification Fusion (MCF) machine-learning framework; 289 candidate feature-selector/classifier pairs were compared and three base models fused

Editorial relevance 67 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The MCF framework integrated newly identified EV biomarkers with clinical variables, selected and fused machine-learning classifiers, and produced the risk score evaluated in diagnostic and prospective population cohorts. BarFlare is the high-sensitivity EV assay platform, not the AI model.
Test and result
The BarFlare assay and an interpretable multi-criteria decision-making classification-fusion machine-learning framework combined extracellular-vesicle SCC and MMP13 biomarkers with clinical factors.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a prospective observational diagnostic study, not an intervention trial or proof that screening improves outcomes. The longitudinal preclinical cohort had 30 incident cases and matched controls; performance estimates require external replication in larger populations. The source reports an association and lead time, not guaranteed individual prediction. Primary publisher full text and abstract checked; no independent replication assessed.

13 Jan 2026Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center; Sun Yat-sen University; Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital; Johns Hopkins University; Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center

Deep-learning histopathology identifies tertiary lymphoid structure features linked to stage II colorectal cancer relapse

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: SurvFinder analyzed 6,950 H&E slides from 1,604 stage II colorectal cancer patients across four cohorts and identified tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) as prognostic tissue features. Its multi-view model integrated TLS morphology and spatial location; explainable analysis highlighted TLS maturity and position near the tumor periphery as features associated with relapse-free survival and adjuvant-chemotherapy outcome patterns across the retrospective datasets.

Technology: SurvFinder, a multi-component deep-learning pipeline: WSINet for prognosis-associated tissue features, SegNet for TLS segmentation/subtyping, and MVNet for multi-view risk prediction

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep learning autonomously identified prognosis-associated tissue features in whole-slide images, segmented and subtyped TLSs, and integrated morphological and spatial features for risk prediction. Explainable-AI attribution identified TLS location near the tumor margin and TLS maturity state as important prognostic features.
Test and result
SurvFinder analyzed 6,950 H&E slides from 1,604 stage II colorectal cancer patients across four cohorts and identified tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) as prognostic tissue features.
Limitation / still unknown
This was a retrospective analysis of patients enrolled in 2012–2018; results do not establish prospective clinical utility. Associations between predicted risk, TLS features and adjuvant-chemotherapy outcomes are observational and do not prove treatment benefit or that TLS features causally determine relapse. The authors identify absence of prospective validation and real-world deployment as limitations.

29 Apr 2025Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center

Ultrasound deep learning predicts advanced HCC response to HAIC combination therapy

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: AE-3DNet, a 3D deep learning model with spatiotemporal attention on pretreatment contrast-enhanced ultrasound cine, was developed in 326 patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (243 for development with fivefold cross-validation, the rest external). It outperformed 3DNet and a time-intensity curve model, with AUC 0.84 and 0.85 in internal and external cohorts. Predicted response survival curves resembled actual outcomes. Dual-center retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data; the external cohort is small.

Technology: 3D deep learning (AE-3DNet with spatiotemporal attention modules) on pretreatment contrast-enhanced ultrasound cine images

30 Aug 2023Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center; State Key Laboratory of Oncology in South China

AI-aided MRI detects local recurrence of nasopharyngeal carcinoma in a multicenter cohort

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A 2.5D convolutional neural network and an nnU-Net contouring model were developed in a study of 6,916 patients at five hospitals. AUCs were 0.92 internally and 0.88 externally, with sensitivity of 79.5% and 74.3% and specificity of 91.0% and 92.8%. Sensitivity was comparable to MRI readers but lower than PET/CT, and in a randomized prospective cohort of 1,248 patients the AI group had higher sensitivity (78.6% vs 67.3%) that was not significant (P = 0.23).

Technology: 2.5D convolutional neural network for recurrence diagnosis and nnU-Net contouring model on MRI

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: prospective or independent check. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A CNN predicted and a nnU-Net visualized recurrence on MRI, used as an AI pre-inspector for radiologists.
Test and result
Performance was compared with MRI and PET/CT in validation cohorts and in a randomized prospective cohort of 1,248 patients.
Limitation / still unknown
A significant benefit is unproven; the authors estimate 3,943 patients per arm would be needed. Abstract-based; full text not reviewed.

Machine learning selects a nine-lncRNA signature for nasopharyngeal cancer metastasis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Nine lncRNAs were identified by microarray profiling and RT-qPCR validation, then selected with a machine-learning method in a training cohort of 177 patients. The signature separated high- and low-risk groups for distant metastasis-free survival in a Guangzhou internal cohort (n = 177) and a Guilin external cohort (n = 150), outperformed anatomy-based metrics, and correlated with immune activity confirmed by digital pathology.

Technology: Machine-learning feature selection of a nine-lncRNA prognostic signature
