7 Aug 2026University of Oxford; UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology; UK Met Office

Data-driven equations reveal rainfall-linked North Atlantic decadal variability

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using SINDy equation discovery on monthly observational indices from 1950–2022, researchers derived three linked low-order equations for Atlantic Multidecadal Variability, the North Atlantic Oscillation and North Atlantic precipitation. The model recovered a distinct roughly 20-year component and highlighted precipitation-linked terms as robust components of the learned dynamics; tests against the independent 1900–1939 record found skill for statistics of the variability indices and rainfall. The equations generate hypotheses about ocean-atmosphere feedbacks; they do not establish a single causal mechanism or guarantee forecasts.

Technology: Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) equation-discovery algorithm

Editorial relevance 53 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Searched candidate dynamical models against observational time series and selected sparse, interpretable coupled equations; the researchers analyzed robust equation terms and compared the resulting model against a historical holdout period.
Test and result
Using SINDy equation discovery on monthly observational indices from 1950–2022, researchers derived three linked low-order equations for Atlantic Multidecadal Variability, the North Atlantic Oscillation and North Atlantic precipitation.
Limitation / still unknown
The learned equations are compact models/hypotheses from observational associations. The paper and Oxford source note that proposed mechanisms need further evaluation; reported hindcast skill is not prospective climate prediction validation.

The levers of political persuasion with conversational AI

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In three large experiments with 76,977 participants, 19 LLMs argued 707 British political positions in dialogue. 8 Jun 2021University of Oxford; University of Coimbra; University of North Carolina at Greensboro; University of Algarve; Gorongosa National Park

Satellite-image clustering guides discovery of four late Miocene fossil sites in Mozambique’s Urema Rift

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Unsupervised k-means clustering of seven Landsat 8 spectral bands highlighted a small set of priority areas for paleontological field survey in Gorongosa National Park. The 2018 campaign documented four new late Miocene fossil localities; three lay within the model’s target cluster and the fourth was less than 90 m away in the same gully system. Together, the sites expose the first reported estuarine coastal-forest fossil setting in the East African Rift System, filling a regional paleoenvironmental gap.

Technology: Unsupervised k-means clustering of Landsat 8 imagery

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The authors clustered seven spectral bands without pre-labeled land-cover classes, compared clusters with known fossil localities, and selected the cluster containing most known sites as a predictive target for field prospecting. This decision-support workflow directed the 2018 field survey that documented the new localities; a random forest was separately used to estimate spectral-band importance.
Test and result
Unsupervised k-means clustering of seven Landsat 8 spectral bands highlighted a small set of priority areas for paleontological field survey in Gorongosa National Park.
Limitation / still unknown
This was a pilot study in a 36 km² area and the model had notable false positives: reported precision was 55.56%, despite 84.6% overall accuracy. Three of four new localities overlapped the selected cluster; the fourth was less than 90 m away and was found by surveying the same gully system, so model localization for that site is indirect. The findings are four new fossil localities, not four new hominin taxa; the reported estuarine coastal-forest interpretation is a paleoenvironmental inference.

6 Aug 2020University of Oxford; University of California, San Diego

Machine learning derives new idealized ocean-eddy momentum and heat-flux closures

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Relevance-vector-machine sparse regression applied to high-resolution idealized ocean simulations produced compact momentum and temperature-flux closures for coarse grids. In the authors’ simulated setup, the inferred momentum terms include energy transfers absent from then-current closures; the parameterizations improved selected statistics when implemented in an idealized 30-km ocean model. This is a model-derived closure result, not an observation of a previously unknown ocean process.

Technology: Relevance vector machine (RVM) sparse Bayesian regression; physically constrained convolutional neural network used as a comparison

Editorial relevance 40 climate Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
RVM selected compact closed-form terms from candidate functions of simulated velocity, vorticity and deformation fields to infer subgrid momentum and temperature forcing. The authors report the expressions captured energy pathways omitted from existing parameterizations. A physically constrained CNN was also trained and evaluated, but the interpretable closure result comes from the RVM.
Test and result
Relevance-vector-machine sparse regression applied to high-resolution idealized ocean simulations produced compact momentum and temperature-flux closures for coarse grids.
Limitation / still unknown
Derived from idealized, eddy-resolving double-gyre simulations and tested in an idealized coarse-resolution shallow-water model, not validated against observational ocean data or a global coupled climate model. The inferred tensor structure relates to earlier Reynolds-stress and deformation-based theories; the paper’s stated novelty is the additional data-derived terms/energy pathways and resulting parameterizations, not discovery of a new observed ocean phenomenon.
