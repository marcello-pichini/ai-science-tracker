7 Aug 2026University of Oxford; UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology; UK Met Office

Data-driven equations reveal rainfall-linked North Atlantic decadal variability

Using SINDy equation discovery on monthly observational indices from 1950–2022, researchers derived three linked low-order equations for Atlantic Multidecadal Variability, the North Atlantic Oscillation and North Atlantic precipitation. The model recovered a distinct roughly 20-year component and highlighted precipitation-linked terms as robust components of the learned dynamics; tests against the independent 1900–1939 record found skill for statistics of the variability indices and rainfall. The equations generate hypotheses about ocean-atmosphere feedbacks; they do not establish a single causal mechanism or guarantee forecasts.

Technology: Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) equation-discovery algorithm

4 Dec 2025University of Oxford; UK AI Security Institute; London School of Economics; Stanford University; MIT

The levers of political persuasion with conversational AI

In three large experiments with 76,977 participants, 19 LLMs argued 707 British political positions in dialogue. Persuasiveness was driven mainly by information density, while tailoring to user traits added little, and post-training for persuasion raised effectiveness by up to 50%. The most persuasive models also produced the most inaccurate claims. Caveat: the persuasion effects are measured in survey experiments, and accuracy was tested on a subset of claims.

Technology: 19 open and closed LLMs (including GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, Grok-3)

5 Mar 2025University of Oxford; 119-author international team

Canopy traits across tropical forests mapped with deep learning and satellite data

Field data from more than 1,800 vegetation plots, combined with satellite, terrain, climate and soil data and convolutional neural networks, predicted variation in 13 tree functional traits. Tropical American forests show about 40% greater functional richness than African and Asian forests, and African forests show 32% higher functional divergence than American forests. The authors caution that accuracy depends on data quality and coverage, and that AI should support rather than replace field sampling.

Technology: Convolutional neural networks combined with satellite spectral data

18 Jun 2024University of Oxford; University Medical Center Göttingen and collaborating European research institutions

A machine-learning plasma panel classifies some REM sleep behavior disorder participants years before motor Parkinson diagnosis

Researchers developed a targeted multiplex mass-spectrometry assay for eight plasma proteins and applied machine learning to distinguish Parkinson disease from healthy controls and classify people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD). In the reported longitudinal iRBD cohorts, the model classified 79% of participants up to seven years before motor Parkinson disease or dementia with Lewy bodies onset. The work connects a targeted protein panel to a pre-motor disease signal in measured blood samples.

Technology: Machine-learning classifier using recursive feature elimination on targeted plasma proteomic measurements

25 Apr 2022University of Oxford; Belfast Health and Social Care Trust; University of Leeds

Swarm learning predicts colorectal cancer mutations from histopathology slides

Swarm learning, which trains models across partner sites without moving patient data, was applied to over 5,000 patients to predict BRAF status and microsatellite instability from H&E colorectal cancer slides. Swarm-trained models outperformed most locally trained models and matched models trained on merged datasets, and were validated on two independent UK datasets. Caveat: the outcome is a research-grade prediction from slides, not a clinical test.

Technology: Swarm learning with deep learning on histopathology images

27 Oct 2021University of Oxford; Harvard Medical School

EVE: evolutionary model predicts disease-variant pathogenicity without labels

EVE, an unsupervised deep generative model trained on evolutionary sequence variation across about 140,000 species, predicted pathogenicity for more than 36 million variants in 3,219 disease genes and classified over 256,000 variants of unknown significance. The authors report it outperforms methods trained on labelled disease data and performs on par with high-throughput experimental predictions. Caveat: the authors state EVE is not a diagnostic test.

Technology: EVE (deep generative evolutionary model, variational autoencoder)

8 Jun 2021University of Oxford; University of Coimbra; University of North Carolina at Greensboro; University of Algarve; Gorongosa National Park

Satellite-image clustering guides discovery of four late Miocene fossil sites in Mozambique’s Urema Rift

Unsupervised k-means clustering of seven Landsat 8 spectral bands highlighted a small set of priority areas for paleontological field survey in Gorongosa National Park. The 2018 campaign documented four new late Miocene fossil localities; three lay within the model’s target cluster and the fourth was less than 90 m away in the same gully system. Together, the sites expose the first reported estuarine coastal-forest fossil setting in the East African Rift System, filling a regional paleoenvironmental gap.

Technology: Unsupervised k-means clustering of Landsat 8 imagery

Relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Unsupervised k-means clustering of seven Landsat 8 spectral bands highlighted a small set of priority areas for paleontological field survey in Gorongosa National Park.
  • Still unknown: This was a pilot study in a 36 km² area and the model had notable false positives: reported precision was 55.56%, despite 84.6% overall accuracy. Three of four new localities overlapped the selected cluster; the fourth was less than 90 m away and was found by surveying the same gully system, so model localization for that site is indirect. The findings are four new fossil localities, not four new hominin taxa; the reported estuarine coastal-forest interpretation is a paleoenvironmental inference.
  • AI role: The authors clustered seven spectral bands without pre-labeled land-cover classes, compared clusters with known fossil localities, and selected the cluster containing most known sites as a predictive target for field prospecting. This decision-support workflow directed the 2018 field survey that documented the new localities; a random forest was separately used to estimate spectral-band importance.

6 Aug 2020University of Oxford; University of California, San Diego

Machine learning derives new idealized ocean-eddy momentum and heat-flux closures

Relevance-vector-machine sparse regression applied to high-resolution idealized ocean simulations produced compact momentum and temperature-flux closures for coarse grids. In the authors’ simulated setup, the inferred momentum terms include energy transfers absent from then-current closures; the parameterizations improved selected statistics when implemented in an idealized 30-km ocean model. This is a model-derived closure result, not an observation of a previously unknown ocean process.

Technology: Relevance vector machine (RVM) sparse Bayesian regression; physically constrained convolutional neural network used as a comparison

Relevance 40 climate Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Relevance-vector-machine sparse regression applied to high-resolution idealized ocean simulations produced compact momentum and temperature-flux closures for coarse grids.
  • Still unknown: Derived from idealized, eddy-resolving double-gyre simulations and tested in an idealized coarse-resolution shallow-water model, not validated against observational ocean data or a global coupled climate model. The inferred tensor structure relates to earlier Reynolds-stress and deformation-based theories; the paper’s stated novelty is the additional data-derived terms/energy pathways and resulting parameterizations, not discovery of a new observed ocean phenomenon.
  • AI role: RVM selected compact closed-form terms from candidate functions of simulated velocity, vorticity and deformation fields to infer subgrid momentum and temperature forcing. The authors report the expressions captured energy pathways omitted from existing parameterizations. A physically constrained CNN was also trained and evaluated, but the interpretable closure result comes from the RVM.

6 Mar 2020University of Oxford; University of Zurich

KCML links olfactory-receptor perturbations to multicellular organization and colorectal-cancer signaling

Knowledge- and Context-driven Machine Learning (KCML) analyzed perturbation phenotypes across molecular, cellular and population scales. In colorectal-cancer cells, it associated depletion of olfactory receptors and TGFβ/WNT pathway genes with abnormal multicellular organization. The authors integrated the predictions with patient data and found olfactory-receptor expression correlated with WNT/TGFβ signatures and worse outcomes in higher-grade tumors.

Technology: KCML, weakly supervised machine-learning framework using support-vector-machine classifiers trained on gene-ontology annotations and high-throughput perturbation phenotypes
