6 May 2025University of Texas at Austin; University of Houston

A dense nodal array and machine learning reveal a blind fault zone beneath the San Fernando Valley

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning workflow analyzed one month of recordings from a 140-station nodal array and located 62 earthquakes, including 36 not in the regional catalog. Their distribution exposes a previously unidentified 5.3 × 4 km blind fault zone about 5 km beneath the southern San Fernando Valley; the small-event cluster is rare in the regional catalog despite a 2014 Mb 4.4 earthquake in the zone.

Technology: Previously developed machine-learning seismic phase picker, fine-tuned on similar waveforms, with two-step event association

Editorial relevance 47 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model detected weak earthquake signals and picked seismic phases in high-noise recordings, enabling the expanded local event catalog from which the blind fault-zone cluster was interpreted.
Test and result
A machine-learning workflow analyzed one month of recordings from a 140-station nodal array and located 62 earthquakes, including 36 not in the regional catalog.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a one-month temporary-array study reported as an arXiv preprint. The fault-zone dimensions and location are inferred from a small cluster of newly detected microearthquakes, not from direct imaging or a long-term catalog; it informs hazard mapping but does not predict future earthquakes.

7 Mar 2024University of Texas at Austin

Machine learning guides engineering of an alkaloid-biosynthesis enzyme

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers developed MutComputeX, a structure-based residual neural network for assessing amino-acid substitutions in protein–ligand environments, and used it with a 4′-O-methylnorbelladine biosensor to engineer the plant methyltransferase Nb4OMT in Escherichia coli. The paper reports engineered variants with 60% higher product titer, twofold higher catalytic activity, threefold lower off-product regioisomer formation and 5 °C higher thermostability; ligand-aware predictions helped identify the beneficial A53M substitution. These enzyme and product results do not demonstrate complete microbial production of galantamine.

Technology: MutComputeX structure-based residual neural network for protein–ligand mutation design

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MutComputeX predicted sequence substitutions in a modeled protein–ligand microenvironment and guided selection of Nb4OMT variants for biosensor screening and enzyme assays; ligand-aware predictions supported A53M.
Test and result
MutComputeX-guided, manually curated Nb4OMT variants were assayed using a 4NB biosensor and biochemical methods. The article reports 60% higher product titer, twofold higher catalytic activity, threefold lower off-product formation and 5 °C higher thermostability for engineered variants.
Limitation / still unknown
The result concerns a pathway enzyme and branchpoint intermediate in the reported microbial/biochemical systems; full pathway production of galantamine and industrial manufacturing were not demonstrated.

21 Aug 2023University of Texas at Austin; Dell Medical School; Columbia University; New York Genome Center

Deep-learning knee imaging phenotypes reveal osteoarthritis genetic and fracture associations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using knee DXA scans from the UK Biobank, a ResNet-101 model identified 1,931 more probable knee-osteoarthritis cases than health-record coding and a U-Net segmentation model estimated minimum joint-space width (mJSW). In 29,257 participants, the quantitative mJSW phenotype had substantially higher SNP heritability than the binary phenotypes and yielded 18 genome-wide-significant loci, compared with one for the image-based binary classifier and six for ICD-10 coding; only one mJSW locus was already significant in a large prior knee-OA GWAS. The AI-derived mJSW phenotype was also associated with fracture history in an epidemiological analysis, a signal not seen with the binary case labels.

Technology: ResNet-101 convolutional neural network for knee-OA classification and U-Net image segmentation for joint-space measurement

Editorial relevance 53 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The neural networks converted routine knee DXA scans into two disease phenotypes: a case–control OA classifier and a quantitative minimum joint-space-width measurement. The researchers used these AI-derived phenotypes in GWAS and downstream epidemiological association analyses, increasing statistical power and revealing genetic and fracture associations that were not significant with ICD-10 case coding or the binary classifier.
Test and result
Using knee DXA scans from the UK Biobank, a ResNet-101 model identified 1,931 more probable knee-osteoarthritis cases than health-record coding and a U-Net segmentation model estimated minimum joint-space width (mJSW).
Limitation / still unknown
The GWAS used 29,257 quality-controlled UK Biobank participants with knee DXA images, all from the analyzed white-British ancestry subset; ancestry transferability is unestablished. DXA images were non-weight-bearing. The 18 loci are genome-wide associations for a model-derived quantitative endophenotype, not 18 proven causal OA genes; the paper notes only one also reached significance in a previous large knee-OA GWAS and many loci overlap broad anthropometric associations. The fracture result is observational and does not show that OA or joint-space narrowing causes fractures. The medRxiv preprint DOI 10.1101/2023.03.07.23286909 is an earlier version of this same study and is not counted separately.

1 May 2023University of Texas at Austin

An MRI decoder turns brain activity into text

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A noninvasive decoder reconstructs continuous text from fMRI that closely, and sometimes precisely, matches the original meaning in about half of trials (after dozens of hours of training with podcasts; it also works on silent videos). Published in Nature Neuroscience; it works only with cooperative participants and is not word-for-word transcription.

Technology: Semantic decoder from fMRI

Editorial relevance 80 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A decoder trained on fMRI recordings of cortical semantic representations generated word sequences matching the meaning of brain activity.
Test and result
A non-invasive fMRI decoder generated continuous text that conveyed the meaning of perceived speech, imagined speech and silent videos; the press release says it matched the original meaning in about half of trials and works only with cooperative participants.
Limitation / still unknown
The abstract does not report participant numbers or semantic accuracy measures and states no explicit limitations; the press release says it is not word-for-word transcription.

29 Mar 2023University of Texas at Austin; and Ames National Laboratory

Machine-learning-guided calculations predict four rare-earth-free magnetic Fe–Co–Si compounds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A crystal-graph neural network screened 357,480 hypothetical Fe–Co–Si structures; adaptive genetic search and density-functional calculations then identified four predicted ternary structures with calculated saturation polarization ≥1.0 T, easy-axis anisotropy ≥1.0 MJ/m³, and Curie temperature >840 K. Their calculated energies lie within 70 meV/atom of the ternary convex hull, supporting but not proving experimental accessibility. The paper proposes the structures for synthesis; it does not experimentally demonstrate them.

Technology: Crystal graph convolutional neural network (CGCNN) with an adaptive genetic algorithm

Editorial relevance 47 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
CGCNN models rapidly screened hypothetical structures and predicted formation energy and magnetic polarization; first-principles results were fed back to train improved models across successive generations, while an adaptive genetic algorithm searched lower-energy structures. ML materially narrowed the space for the final density-functional magnetic-property calculations.
Test and result
A crystal-graph neural network screened 357,480 hypothetical Fe–Co–Si structures; adaptive genetic search and density-functional calculations then identified four predicted ternary structures with calculated saturation polarization ≥1.0 T, easy-axis anisotropy ≥1.0 MJ/m³, and Curie temperature >840 K.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a theoretical, ML-guided first-principles prediction; the paper proposes the structures for synthesis and does not report experimental synthesis. The abstract describes four compounds; the results section mentions five structures meeting initial thresholds, of which four are dynamically stable. Report the four final candidates and their calculated properties only. The work establishes neither actual permanent-magnet performance nor synthesizability.
