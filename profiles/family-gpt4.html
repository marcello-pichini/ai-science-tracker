26 Sep 2026University Medical Center Mainz

Simplified medical reports improve comprehension in a randomized reader study

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Mainz study randomly assigned 576 adult participants to an original or GPT-4-simplified version of one of five German medical documents. Among 439 completed comprehension questionnaires, simplified texts generally produced more correct answers, with the pathology comparison not statistically significant. This measures immediate understanding of selected, researcher-checked texts, not improved treatment adherence or health outcomes. Single-site recruitment, incomplete questionnaires and only one text pair per document type limit generalization.

Technology: GPT-4 text simplification

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
GPT-4 rewrites physician-authored medical reports into lay language; those generated texts form the experimental intervention used to test readers’ immediate comprehension.
Test and result
Randomized assignment to original or pre-generated simplified German medical reports, followed by immediate multiple-choice comprehension questions; 576 participants, with 439 completed comprehension questionnaires.
Limitation / still unknown
Generalization beyond five selected text pairs and a single German clinical department, long-term retention, adherence and clinical outcomes remain unestablished. Education/health-literacy measures were not collected; results include incomplete questionnaires. Output generation settings are described inconsistently as configured parameters and default web sessions; the represented result concerns the actual selected texts.

21 Sep 2026Tsinghua University; Peking University

GPT-4o-assisted literature screening nominates low-lithium LiMoOBr₄ conductor

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A literature-mining workflow using GPT-4o extracted values from 465 papers; a graph-neural-network-based pipeline screened 69,920 oxyhalide structures. Ab initio molecular-dynamics simulations for LiMoOBr₄ report a lithium content of 2.28 mol kg⁻¹, a 300 K extrapolated ionic conductivity of 13.53 mS cm⁻¹ and a 0.22 eV activation energy. The reported material properties are computational predictions, not experimental measurements.

Technology: GPT-4o literature extraction; diffusion-model and graph-neural-network screening

23 Feb 2026University of California, Berkeley (Yaghi group)

LFAST: an LLM agent drives robotic synthesis of the new COF-2000 framework

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A GPT-4o-based agent (LFAST) screened reaction conditions over a 96-condition matrix and refined them after each round, raising the crystallinity index of a reference covalent organic framework by 350% and yielding COF-2000, a previously unreported material with higher structural order. The reported validation is crystallinity measurement of the synthesized products; the authors do not pursue applications. Publication date is taken from secondary reports of the JACS paper and should be checked against the journal page.

Technology: LFAST (LLM For Accelerated Synthesis Technique), GPT-4o agent with robotic experimentation

4 Dec 2025University of Oxford; UK AI Security Institute; London School of Economics; Stanford University; MIT

The levers of political persuasion with conversational AI

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In three large experiments with 76,977 participants, 19 LLMs argued 707 British political positions in dialogue. Persuasiveness was driven mainly by information density, while tailoring to user traits added little, and post-training for persuasion raised effectiveness by up to 50%. The most persuasive models also produced the most inaccurate claims. Caveat: the persuasion effects are measured in survey experiments, and accuracy was tested on a subset of claims.

Technology: 19 open and closed LLMs (including GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, Grok-3)

27 Jun 2025University of British Columbia

Language-model analysis identifies themes of living with chronic sinus disease

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: ChatGPT-4 assisted thematic analysis of artwork interviews from 16 adults with chronic rhinosinusitis. Researchers repeated the analysis independently and identified six themes, including emotional burden, adaptation and difficulties navigating healthcare. These small, single-centre qualitative findings do not establish population prevalence, causal effects or freedom from human and model bias.

Technology: ChatGPT-4 (source reported); large language model

13 Sep 2024MIT Sloan School of Management; Cornell University; American University

Conversations with an AI chatbot durably reduce conspiracy beliefs

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Across experiments with more than 2,000 participants, three dialogues with GPT-4 reduced belief in the targeted conspiracy theory by about 20%, and the effect persisted for two months and spilled over to unrelated conspiracies. A professional fact-checker rated 99.2% of 128 sampled chatbot claims as true. Caveats: lab setting, the chatbot did not reduce belief in true conspiracies, and most participants still held the hoax belief.

Technology: GPT-4 (DebunkBot dialogue system)

ChemCrow: a language-model agent with chemistry tools

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An agent with 18 tools and GPT-4 planned and carried out the synthesis of DEET and three thiourea organocatalysts on a cloud platform, and guided discovery of a chromophore absorbing at 336 nm against a 369 nm target; evaluated on 14 cases by four expert chemists. Hallucinations, limited tasks and dual-use risks; 12 of 18 tools were released.
