Paper2Agent integrates perturbation datasets to prioritize GPR137 at a psoriasis risk locus

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Paper2Agent-generated agents used AlphaGenome to prioritize GPR137 as the top predicted gene affected by psoriasis-associated variant rs887314 in CD4+ T cells, then integrated that prediction with existing MPRA-coupled scCRISPRi and CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq data. A cross-screen correlation of gene-expression signatures supported GPR137 as the probable causal gene under stimulated conditions (Spearman rho 0.613 at 8 h and 0.630 at 48 h; FDR < 0.05), but not at rest. The paper presents this as activation-dependent computational evidence.

Technology: Paper2Agent-generated AlphaGenome, MPRA-coupled scCRISPRi and CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq agents, coordinated through an AI co-scientist

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The AlphaGenome paper agent scored and ranked candidate genes at rs887314. Under human-in-the-loop supervision, the AI co-scientist inspected two other paper agents' datasets, proposed ten validation strategies, and the researcher selected signature-correlation analysis. The resulting cross-screen, cross-modality analysis found significant concordance for GPR137 only under stimulated conditions.
Test and result
Paper2Agent-generated agents used AlphaGenome to prioritize GPR137 as the top predicted gene affected by psoriasis-associated variant rs887314 in CD4+ T cells, then integrated that prediction with existing MPRA-coupled scCRISPRi and CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq data.
Limitation / still unknown
The analysis reuses existing published datasets and does not report new wet-lab experiments for this psoriasis case. AlphaGenome supplied a model prediction; cross-dataset signature correlation supports, but does not establish, causal biology. The GPR137 concordance was significant only after stimulation, and the authors describe it as probable/activation-dependent. A human researcher selected which AI-proposed strategy to run. Primary paper source checked; no independent replication assessed.

AlphaGenome Atlas: predictive map of human DNA variation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A precomputed database of predicted molecular effects for about 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome, generated by AlphaGenome. Researchers can access it free through a portal and API. The predictions are a research tool, do not replace experiments, and are not validated for clinical use.

Technology: AlphaGenome, AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI)

Family AlphaGenome: profile · models page

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Announcement only · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AlphaGenome predicted variant effects across cell types and tissues; these were precomputed for about 9 billion variants and combined with AlphaMissense into a single score.
Test and result
Validation described is the company's own testing of the AlphaGenome Variant Impact score on pathogenicity benchmarks, plus case studies: a DNM1 variant prioritized with GREGoR and 22% more non-coding associations in UK Biobank.
Limitation / still unknown
The page does not detail how individual predictions were checked; AlphaGenome is not validated or approved for clinical use.

25 Jun 2025Google DeepMind

AlphaGenome: predicting the effects of DNA variants

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The model reads sequences up to 1 million base pairs and predicts thousands of gene-regulation properties at single-letter resolution. It outperformed the best external models in 22 of 24 sequence evaluations and matched or outperformed the best in 24 of 26 variant evaluations. Stated limits: distant regulatory elements; not validated for personal genomes or clinical use.

Technology: AlphaGenome

Test and result
AlphaGenome was evaluated on sequence and variant-effect benchmarks against external models, outperforming in 22 of 24 and matching or exceeding in 24 of 26 evaluations, plus a TAL1 case study.
