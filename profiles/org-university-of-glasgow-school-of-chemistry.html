11 Nov 2021University of Glasgow School of Chemistry

Neural-network-guided autonomous experiments reveal an unreported TosMIC reaction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A reactivity-seeking neural network coupled to an autonomous reaction platform navigated unknown chemical mixtures using real-time analytical data. Among the reactions it identified, follow-up characterization revealed an unreported photochemical reaction and a new reactivity mode for p-toluenesulfonylmethyl isocyanide (TosMIC): six equivalents of TosMIC underwent a multistep single-substrate cascade yielding a trimeric product in 47% unoptimized yield and forming five C–C bonds.

Technology: Reactivity-seeking neural network and junction-tree neural network integrated with an autonomous robotic synthesis and analytics platform

Editorial relevance 60 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A convolutional neural network classified reaction signatures from online NMR/IR/analytical data and a junction-tree neural network encoded reagent structures to help prioritize new experiments. The closed-loop system surfaced reactions; chemists then manually investigated and characterized selected outcomes, including the TosMIC trimer-forming cascade.

Limitation / still unknown
The source describes several found reactions, some previously known; this entry is limited to the distinct unreported TosMIC reactivity result. The reported 47% yield was unoptimized. The platform identified candidate reactions, while follow-up analyses and manual chemical investigation established the product and mechanism; no broad claim of fully autonomous mechanistic proof is made.
