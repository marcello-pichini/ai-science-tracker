29 Jun 2026Technical University of Munich; Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics; Technical University of Denmark; HOLISMOKES Collaboration

Deep-learning lens search leads to first confirmed galaxy-scale lensed superluminous supernova

The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821. Follow-up imaging resolved multiple supernova images and spectroscopy classified it as a Type I superluminous supernova at z=2.008 ± 0.001; the second deflector galaxy was measured spectroscopically at z=0.375 ± 0.001. This is the first confirmed galaxy-scale strongly lensed supernova with expected time delays of days to weeks, offering a new system for time-delay cosmography.

Technology: Deep-learning Pan-STARRS 3π lens-candidate classification and transient-alert cross-matching pipeline

2 Sep 2025Technical University of Munich; Helmholtz Munich; Ludwig-Maximilians University of Munich; University of Melbourne; University Medical Center Göttingen; Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences; German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases

Machine learning and tear-fluid proteomics identify a six-protein ALS signature

In discovery samples from 49 people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and 54 controls, mass spectrometry detected 876 tear-fluid proteins, of which 106 differed in abundance. Biostatistical and machine-learning analyses produced a six-protein signature (CRYM, PFKL, CAPZA2, ALDH16A1, SERPINC1 and HP). In an independent cohort of 51 ALS cases and 52 controls, Western blots replicated significant group differences for SERPINC1 and HP; their combination had the best reported in-silico discrimination.

Technology: Machine-learning models applied to data-independent acquisition mass-spectrometry proteomics and biomarker-panel discrimination

  • Still unknown: This is an exploratory biomarker study with modest discovery and validation cohorts, not a clinically validated diagnostic assay. The article reports that only SERPINC1 and HP differences replicated significantly across cohorts; the six-protein signature is a candidate and its best discriminatory performance was evaluated in silico. It does not establish ALS-specific causality or clinical utility.
  • AI role: Machine learning contributed to identification of ALS-associated differential proteins and pathways and evaluated discrimination by the derived signature. The selected tear-fluid proteins were then assayed in an independent patient/control cohort by Western blot; only SERPINC1 and HP differences are explicitly reported as replicated across cohorts.
