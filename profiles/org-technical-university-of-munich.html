29 Jun 2026Technical University of Munich; Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics; Technical University of Denmark; HOLISMOKES Collaboration

Deep-learning lens search leads to first confirmed galaxy-scale lensed superluminous supernova

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821. Follow-up imaging resolved multiple supernova images and spectroscopy classified it as a Type I superluminous supernova at z=2.008 ± 0.001; the second deflector galaxy was measured spectroscopically at z=0.375 ± 0.001. This is the first confirmed galaxy-scale strongly lensed supernova with expected time delays of days to weeks, offering a new system for time-delay cosmography.

Technology: Deep-learning Pan-STARRS 3π lens-candidate classification and transient-alert cross-matching pipeline

Editorial relevance 60 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The discovery followed a workflow that first classified all 3 billion Pan-STARRS 3π sources with deep learning to select high-quality strong-lens candidates, then continuously cross-matched those positions to ZTF alerts. The team matched the 2025 transient to the preselected lens candidate and triggered imaging and spectroscopic follow-up, which established the lensed SLSN result.
Test and result
The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821.
Limitation / still unknown
The redshift, SN type and lensing are based on follow-up observations; time delays are predicted from image separations and were still awaiting high-cadence measurement in this paper. The authors note that the SN’s brightness and resolvability introduce selection effects. An independent paper analyzed the same transient; the discovery is counted once here, not as separate detections by each follow-up team.

2 Sep 2025Technical University of Munich; Helmholtz Munich; Ludwig-Maximilians University of Munich; University of Melbourne; University Medical Center Göttingen; Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences; German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases

Machine learning and tear-fluid proteomics identify a six-protein ALS signature

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In discovery samples from 49 people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and 54 controls, mass spectrometry detected 876 tear-fluid proteins, of which 106 differed in abundance. Biostatistical and machine-learning analyses produced a six-protein signature (CRYM, PFKL, CAPZA2, ALDH16A1, SERPINC1 and HP). In an independent cohort of 51 ALS cases and 52 controls, Western blots replicated significant group differences for SERPINC1 and HP; their combination had the best reported in-silico discrimination.

Technology: Machine-learning models applied to data-independent acquisition mass-spectrometry proteomics and biomarker-panel discrimination

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine learning contributed to identification of ALS-associated differential proteins and pathways and evaluated discrimination by the derived signature. The selected tear-fluid proteins were then assayed in an independent patient/control cohort by Western blot; only SERPINC1 and HP differences are explicitly reported as replicated across cohorts.
Test and result
In an independent cohort of 51 ALS cases and 52 controls, Western blots replicated significant group differences for SERPINC1 and HP; their combination had the best reported in-silico discrimination.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an exploratory biomarker study with modest discovery and validation cohorts, not a clinically validated diagnostic assay. The article reports that only SERPINC1 and HP differences replicated significantly across cohorts; the six-protein signature is a candidate and its best discriminatory performance was evaluated in silico. It does not establish ALS-specific causality or clinical utility.
