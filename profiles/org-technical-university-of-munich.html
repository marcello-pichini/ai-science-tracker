A low-rank probe recovered motion-state information from frozen 3D features

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fitted rank-eight probe on frozen π³X features reached 91.67% balanced accuracy when classifying whether token pairs shared a motion state. Shuffling training labels reduced balanced accuracy to 50.34%. The result is specific to this probe and these recordings.

Technology: π³X; fitted low-rank motion probe

Editorial relevance 47 computer science PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Matched and shuffled motion labels trained low-rank quadratic probes on frozen π³X visual tokens.
Test and result
The test compared probes with matched or within-frame shuffled targets on YouTube-VOS validation pairs.
Limitation / still unknown
Semantic content and object identity can contribute to the signal. The test does not establish an intrinsic motion basis.

21 Oct 2026Technical University of Munich

Learned decoding validates a photonic receiver on a recorded transition trace

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Six learned decoders were fine-tuned on 1,502 recorded transitions from a phase-aware photonic receiver. Their voting ensemble classified 98.31% of 1,363 transitions in the immediately subsequent held-out trace. This is a specific retrospective physical-readout validation under the tested drift conditions, without a matched superiority comparison against the separate analytical decoder.

Technology: XGBoost; LightGBM; CatBoost; random forest; Extra-Trees; neural network

Editorial relevance 33 physics PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned classifiers map measured receiver power changes plus the preceding state to the next codeword, enabling the reported held-out physical readout evaluation.
Test and result
Physics-based synthetic pretraining followed by recorded-trace fine-tuning and an immediately subsequent experimental test trace. Learned features comprise normalized output-power differences and the preceding decoded state.
Limitation / still unknown
Back-to-back traces may share drift and hardware conditions. Sequential inference requires a previous-state reference and limited drift. No independently acquired locked-model deployment, energy, latency, network benefit or unrestricted phase-noise robustness was established. The receiver design and separate 96.19% analytical result are not credited to AI.

29 Jun 2026Technical University of Munich; Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics; Technical University of Denmark; HOLISMOKES Collaboration

Deep-learning lens search leads to first confirmed galaxy-scale lensed superluminous supernova

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821. Follow-up imaging resolved multiple supernova images and spectroscopy classified it as a Type I superluminous supernova at z=2.008 ± 0.001; the second deflector galaxy was measured spectroscopically at z=0.375 ± 0.001. This is the first confirmed galaxy-scale strongly lensed supernova with expected time delays of days to weeks, offering a new system for time-delay cosmography.

Technology: Deep-learning Pan-STARRS 3π lens-candidate classification and transient-alert cross-matching pipeline

Editorial relevance 60 astronomy Published articlePreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The discovery followed a workflow that first classified all 3 billion Pan-STARRS 3π sources with deep learning to select high-quality strong-lens candidates, then continuously cross-matched those positions to ZTF alerts. The team matched the 2025 transient to the preselected lens candidate and triggered imaging and spectroscopic follow-up, which established the lensed SLSN result.
Test and result
The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821.
Limitation / still unknown
The redshift, SN type and lensing are based on follow-up observations; time delays are predicted from image separations and were still awaiting high-cadence measurement in this paper. The authors note that the SN’s brightness and resolvability introduce selection effects. An independent paper analyzed the same transient; the discovery is counted once here, not as separate detections by each follow-up team.

2 Sep 2025Technical University of Munich; Helmholtz Munich; Ludwig-Maximilians University of Munich; University of Melbourne; University Medical Center Göttingen; Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences; German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases

Machine learning and tear-fluid proteomics identify a six-protein ALS signature

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In discovery samples from 49 people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and 54 controls, mass spectrometry detected 876 tear-fluid proteins, of which 106 differed in abundance. Biostatistical and machine-learning analyses produced a six-protein signature (CRYM, PFKL, CAPZA2, ALDH16A1, SERPINC1 and HP). In an independent cohort of 51 ALS cases and 52 controls, Western blots replicated significant group differences for SERPINC1 and HP; their combination had the best reported in-silico discrimination.

Technology: Machine-learning models applied to data-independent acquisition mass-spectrometry proteomics and biomarker-panel discrimination

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine learning contributed to identification of ALS-associated differential proteins and pathways and evaluated discrimination by the derived signature. The selected tear-fluid proteins were then assayed in an independent patient/control cohort by Western blot; only SERPINC1 and HP differences are explicitly reported as replicated across cohorts.
Test and result
In an independent cohort of 51 ALS cases and 52 controls, Western blots replicated significant group differences for SERPINC1 and HP; their combination had the best reported in-silico discrimination.
Limitation / still unknown
This is an exploratory biomarker study with modest discovery and validation cohorts, not a clinically validated diagnostic assay. The article reports that only SERPINC1 and HP differences replicated significantly across cohorts; the six-protein signature is a candidate and its best discriminatory performance was evaluated in silico. It does not establish ALS-specific causality or clinical utility.

30 Apr 2024Technical University of Munich

GAN augmentation improves retrospective metabolomic prediction of incident myocardial infarction

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The study used KORA S4 and F4 data from 1,454 participants without prior myocardial infarction, including 78 incident cases, to model seven-year risk from clinical and metabolomic variables. A GAN generated minority-class samples for an MLP predictor, and the combined approach improved reported accuracy by about two percentage points over comparison models. This is retrospective prediction in an imbalanced observational cohort, not evidence of causation or clinical utility.

Technology: Generative adversarial network data augmentation and multilayer perceptron prediction

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

Test and result
The GAN-augmented MLP was evaluated on KORA cohort data for seven-year incident myocardial infarction prediction; the study reports 78 cases among 1,454 participants.
Limitation / still unknown
The retrospective cohort is imbalanced and the review found no independent prospective validation or evidence of clinical utility.

24 Dec 2023Technical University of Munich; Saarland University Medical Center; University Hospital Schleswig-Holstein; Robert Bosch Hospital

Random forests link CT features to catheter-measured endpoints in CTEPH

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random forests combined CT measurements and qualitative findings to classify catheter-measured hemodynamic endpoints in patients with operable chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension. Retrospective internal testing reported AUC 0.82 for a pulmonary-pressure category and 0.74 for a pulmonary-artery oxygen-saturation category. Replacement of invasive testing or improved management was not demonstrated.

Technology: Random forest

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Random forests learned combinations of CT-derived measurements and qualitative imaging findings to classify catheter-referenced hemodynamic categories; feature filtering characterized the model-relevant signals.
Test and result
Single-center retrospective cohort of 127 operable CTEPH patients from 2015–2020 with CT and preoperative right-heart catheterization. Pulmonary pressure was available in 127 patients and pulmonary-artery oxygen saturation in 55. Random forests used normalized quantitative and qualitative CT features, evaluated through a source-described threefold random-sampling procedure. Reported endpoint-classification AUCs were 0.82 and 0.74.
Limitation / still unknown
Small selected surgical cohort, including only 55 patients for the oxygen endpoint; no external model validation. Exact normalization/filtering nesting and hyperparameter selection were incompletely specified. CT-feature associations are observational, and feature importance is not causation. Benefits for patient management and replacement of catheterization remain untested.

28 May 2022Technical University of Munich; Klinikum rechts der Isar

Neural phase-label flickering tracks atypical courses in a small surgical-video study

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers found that unstable phase labels from a pretrained surgical-video network coincided with complicated or atypical courses in fifteen recorded operations. The observed pattern supplied an exploratory alert signal beyond ordinary phase-recognition accuracy. Flicker measures and thresholds were developed on the same videos, so the result does not establish an independently validated complication detector or better clinical documentation.

Technology: TeCNO convolutional temporal surgical-phase recognition with post-hoc flicker analysis

Editorial relevance 33 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The neural network generated time-ordered surgical-phase predictions; instability in those learned predictions exposed the represented association with atypical observed operative courses. The reporting interface itself is not treated as a scientific discovery.
Test and result
TeCNO was trained on 52 surgical videos and applied to fifteen further cholecystectomy videos, independently phase-annotated by a highly experienced surgeon. Investigators then reviewed unstable label clusters and derived flicker thresholds on those same fifteen cases. The source reports that threshold rules identified five of six apparently divergent courses; one gallbladder perforation produced no substantial flicker increase.
Limitation / still unknown
Post-hoc pattern recognition, same-cohort cutoff derivation and a very small event count create substantial optimism and selection risk. Independent blinded complication adjudication and external alert validation were not established. Phase-annotation agreement, report generation and exploratory event association do not demonstrate complete or safer operative documentation, real-time deployment, improved outcomes or reliable detection of specific complication types.
