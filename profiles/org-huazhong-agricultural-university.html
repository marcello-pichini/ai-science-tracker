KPPepGen generates experimentally active pathogen-specific antimicrobial peptides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: KPPepGen combines Gene Ontology and pathogen-knowledge-graph prompts with a diffusion model to generate pathogen-conditioned peptide sequences. The paper reports valid peptide generation for 56 pathogens and wet-lab evaluation of nine novel peptides for Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus, reporting antimicrobial activity and low cytotoxicity. Its Magainin 2 optimization result is a separate model-comparison metric, not a clinical outcome.

Technology: KPPepGen knowledge-aware prompt diffusion model

AI-guided promoter editing raises maize ZmVTE4 expression and kernel alpha-tocopherol

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Basenji2-based deep-learning models and editing-plasticity analysis guided CRISPR editing of maize ZmVTE4, a gene involved in vitamin E biosynthesis. Researchers isolated a previously unobserved four-base deletion in the gene’s 5′ untranslated region; the edited allele significantly increased ZmVTE4 expression and α-tocopherol content in kernels. A separate promoter-variant assay supported the four-base deletion, rather than a co-occurring 82-base deletion, as the key change associated with increased reporter activity.

Technology: Basenji2-long and Basenji2-3K-NAM sequence-to-expression deep-learning models with editing-plasticity analysis

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Deep-learning models predicted plant gene expression from regulatory DNA and used interpretability/occlusion and in-silico editing to identify promoter/5′UTR sequences likely to alter expression. The editing-plasticity predictions guided CRISPR-Cas9 targeting of ZmVTE4; the resulting four-base 5′UTR deletion was experimentally linked to increased gene expression and maize-kernel α-tocopherol. This is a measured crop-specific molecular/metabolite outcome, not merely an in-silico editing proposal.
Test and result
A separate promoter-variant assay supported the four-base deletion, rather than a co-occurring 82-base deletion, as the key change associated with increased reporter activity.
Limitation / still unknown
The demonstrated editing result concerns one ZmVTE4 allele and maize material in the reported experiments; it does not establish effects on yield, nutritional outcomes in consumers, or performance across environments and genetic backgrounds. The four-base deletion is newly engineered and was not found in the 721 accessions examined, rather than a naturally observed allele. The paper reports limitations in prediction for rice and tomato and incomplete modeling of environmental influences; claims here are restricted to the maize ZmVTE4 test.
