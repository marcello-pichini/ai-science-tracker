21 Oct 2026Ruhr University Bochum; RWTH Aachen University

Carbides became rounder and occupied more of a tempered steel

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers used a trained image classifier to separate carbide particles from the surrounding high-speed steel in electron micrographs. After isothermal holding, the measured carbide volume fraction was 9.9%, up from 8.3% in directly quenched reference samples, and mean particle circularity rose from 0.881 to 0.914.

Technology: Trainable Weka Segmentation

Editorial relevance 47 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Trainable Weka Segmentation fit a classifier from manually labeled microscopy regions and separated carbide from matrix, enabling particle-area and shape measurements.
Test and result
Microscopy with learned segmentation measured about 1,600 carbides per condition. The stereological measurement error reported for each volume fraction was ±0.4 percentage points.
Limitation / still unknown
The population-level differences apply to the tested alloy and thermal conditions. They do not distinguish growth of existing particles from nucleation of new ones or prove an epitaxial growth mechanism. Carbide rounding during holding had been reported in earlier high-speed-steel research; the represented contribution is quantitative measurement in this alloy.

30 May 2026Ruhr University Bochum

Gaussian-process analysis identifies active Au–Ir–Rh regions in a measured electrocatalyst library

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An autonomous electrochemical platform measured 966 areas across Au–Ir–Rh alloy libraries and used Gaussian-process active learning to map hydrogen-evolution activity. A noise-aware model, rerun retrospectively on the completed dataset, recovered the activity trend after 140 sampled areas and identified high-activity regions near Au30Ir20Rh50 and Au10Ir35Rh55. The approximately 15% measurement requirement is a retrospective result, and the tested library does not establish long-term catalyst stability or broader performance. This record describes the 30 May 2026 arXiv preprint; publisher-deposited metadata confirms a same-title journal version dated 20 November 2026.

Technology: Multi-output Gaussian-process regression and cost-aware active learning for SECCM

Editorial relevance 40 materials Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Active learning guides measurement selection during autonomous electrochemical screening and predicts composition–activity trends from partial measurements; it is directly linked to finding high-activity Au–Ir–Rh compositions.
Test and result
Autonomous measurements of 966 areas supplied ground truth; a noise-aware multi-output Gaussian-process campaign was rerun retrospectively, with activity-map predictions assessed after 140 sampled areas.
Limitation / still unknown
Long-term catalyst stability, external replication and transfer beyond the tested alloy library and conditions are not established by the reviewed material. Final journal full text was inaccessible; its deposited abstract matches the represented result.

30 May 2021Ruhr University Bochum; Technische Hochschule Georg Agricola

Learned OCT texture analysis distinguishes sampled brain-metastasis tissue from healthy tissue

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A support-vector classifier separated OCT images of sampled brain metastases and healthy brain tissue using histology-derived labels. The analysis retained fourteen tissue samples from a twenty-patient collection. Reported image-level accuracy is exploratory: cross-validation split B-scans, without demonstrated separation of tissue samples or patients, and does not establish surgical diagnostic performance.

Technology: Support-vector machines on OCT texture features with PCA dimensionality reduction

Editorial relevance 33 biology Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The SVM combined learned boundaries over multifeature OCT texture measurements to discriminate histology-labeled sampled tissue categories, supplying a specific ex-vivo diagnostic feasibility result. PCA alone is not treated as the enabling AI contribution.
Test and result
Twenty patients contributed twenty-two tissue samples; histologic purity criteria retained ten tumor and four healthy samples. An experienced neuropathologist assigned tissue-composition reference labels. Texture features from 14,336 OCT B-scans were reduced with PCA and classified using grid-tuned SVMs with tenfold cross-validation. The source reports up to 96.83% image-level discrimination of pooled vital/necrotic tumor versus healthy tissue.
Limitation / still unknown
B-scans from the same tissue specimen are correlated, and the described allocation does not establish specimen-level or patient-level separation between folds. Normalization/PCA isolation and nested tuning are not established. Purity-selected specimens and small sample counts constrain interpretation; thousands of images do not substitute for independent patients. No intraoperative clinical deployment, margin-level ground truth, recurrence reduction or survival benefit was demonstrated.
