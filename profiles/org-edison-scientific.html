Kosmos: an AI scientist for autonomous discovery

Each run reads about 1,500 papers and executes 42,000 lines of analysis code; outside scientists judged 79.4% of its conclusions accurate. Of seven discoveries, three reproduce results already obtained by humans and four are new. Limitations include spurious correlations, a cost of about $200 per run, and a six-month work gain estimated by beta users.

Technology: Kosmos (agents with a structured world model)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: prospective or independent check
  • Tested: Independent scientists judged 79.4% of Kosmos's conclusions accurate; three discoveries reproduced findings from preprints or unpublished manuscripts that Kosmos did not access at runtime; the four new discoveries are wet-lab validation in progress, with one checked in an independent scRNA-seq dataset.
  • Still unknown: The authors state that Kosmos chases statistically significant but irrelevant findings, that about one in five statements is not accurate, and that the human time-saving estimates are intrinsically suspect.
  • AI role: An AI scientist runs about 12-hour cycles of data analysis and literature search (about 1,500 papers and 42,000 lines of code per run), linked by a structured world model, and generates traceable reports; humans set the objectives.

Evidence sources: https://advances.edisonscientific.com/research/announcing-kosmos/ · https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02824
