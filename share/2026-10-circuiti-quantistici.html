Claude designs more efficient quantum circuits

Claude designed circuits for eight basic operations (chemistry, optimization, physical simulation, and number theory), assessed by an automated verifier. In several cases they outperform the best published circuits; for example, a point addition used 17 times fewer gates at the cost of 12% more qubits. Key identities were checked with simulation and Lean; a memory shortcut in the decoder fails about once in 10,000 times.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: An automated evaluator checked each circuit for correctness and scored qubits times gates; results were compared with published designs, divsteps was checked by simulation and Lean, and decoder memory was measured over 403 million simulated decodings.
  • Still unknown: The decoder memory shortcut fails about once in 10,000 runs; gains apply to individual primitives, not a full fault-tolerant machine; Claude was conservative and needed prompts to broaden its search.
  • AI role: Claude wrote circuits as Python code and revised them in a closed loop with evaluator feedback, with no human input between rounds, and reconstructed branch information to reduce memory.
