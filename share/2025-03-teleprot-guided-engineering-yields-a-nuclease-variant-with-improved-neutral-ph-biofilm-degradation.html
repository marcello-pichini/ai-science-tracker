TeleProt-guided engineering yields a nuclease variant with improved neutral-pH biofilm degradation

A machine-learning-guided design and high-throughput screening campaign produced a Bacillus licheniformis NucB variant with 19-fold higher measured specific activity than wild type at pH 7. At 1 μM in a laboratory biofilm assay at neutral pH, the selected variant degraded 71% ± 2% of the biofilm versus 13% ± 3% for wild-type NucB.

Technology: TeleProt machine-learning-guided protein-library design framework; best-performing ML3 variant selected by model-based optimization with a deep neural network (MBO-DNN)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A machine-learning-guided design and high-throughput screening campaign produced a Bacillus licheniformis NucB variant with 19-fold higher measured specific activity than wild type at pH 7.
  • Still unknown: The evidence is from enzyme and in-vitro B. licheniformis biofilm assays; it does not show wound healing, animal efficacy or clinical benefit. At the 1 μM dose, the paper reports the stated biofilm comparison; conditions and estimates are study-specific. This is engineering of a known nuclease, not discovery of a naturally new enzyme or organismal mechanism.
  • AI role: TeleProt combined sequence-evolutionary information with experimental genotype–phenotype data to propose diverse NucB variants for high-throughput microfluidic activity screening. The selected ML-guided variant was then purified and assessed in a lower-throughput nuclease assay and a neutral-pH biofilm degradation assay.

Evidence sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2025.101236
