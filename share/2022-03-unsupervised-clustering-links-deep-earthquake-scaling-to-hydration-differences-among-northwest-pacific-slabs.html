Unsupervised clustering links deep-earthquake scaling to hydration differences among northwest Pacific slabs

K-means clustering of Japan Meteorological Agency deep-earthquake catalogs separated the northwest Pacific slab segments; Gutenberg–Richter analysis found a magnitude 3.7–3.8 kink in Honshu and Izu seismicity, with b values falling from about 1.4–1.7 to 0.6–0.7. The authors infer that small-event excess and an approximately kilometre-scale metastable-olivine-wedge rim may reflect deep hydration and enhanced transformational faulting.

Technology: Unsupervised machine-learning clustering (K-means, checked against spectral clustering and Gaussian mixture models)
