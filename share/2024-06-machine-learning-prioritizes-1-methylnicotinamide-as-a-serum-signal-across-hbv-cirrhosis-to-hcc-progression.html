Machine learning prioritizes 1-methylnicotinamide as a serum signal across HBV cirrhosis-to-HCC progression

Untargeted serum metabolomics from 69 participants, including eight patients followed from HBV-related cirrhosis to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), was analyzed with LASSO and random forest to prioritize metabolites. 1-methylnicotinamide (1-MNAM) declined across healthy, cirrhosis and HCC groups and was lower at the HCC than cirrhosis stage in the eight progressors. Targeted mass spectrometry in a separate cohort of 25 healthy controls and 25 early-stage HCC patients confirmed lower 1-MNAM in HCC (AUC 0.954 for HCC versus healthy controls).

Technology: LASSO regression and random forest classifier applied to serum metabolomics

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Targeted mass spectrometry in a separate cohort of 25 healthy controls and 25 early-stage HCC patients confirmed lower 1-MNAM in HCC (AUC 0.954 for HCC versus healthy controls).
  • Still unknown: The longitudinal transition signal comes from eight HBV-cirrhosis patients who progressed during follow-up. The independent targeted cohort compared 25 early-HCC patients with 25 healthy controls; it did not validate prediction of progression among cirrhosis patients. The authors describe the overall sample as small and call for larger prospective validation. 1-MNAM is a known endogenous metabolite; this record concerns the study-specific association and selection result, not discovery of a new compound, causal mechanism, or clinical test.
  • AI role: The authors used LASSO and random forest to select ten candidate metabolites distinguishing cirrhosis from HCC, then identified 1-MNAM as the sole candidate with a consistent decline across healthy, cirrhosis and HCC groups. The algorithms prioritized the measured metabolite; targeted mass spectrometry in a separate cohort provided assay follow-up.
