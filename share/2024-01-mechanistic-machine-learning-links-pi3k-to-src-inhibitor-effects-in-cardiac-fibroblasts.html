Mechanistic machine learning links PI3K to Src-inhibitor effects in cardiac fibroblasts

LogiMML, trained on high-content images of human cardiac fibroblasts, predicted that Src inhibition suppresses actin–myosin stress fibers through PI3K signaling. Follow-up PI3K inhibition reduced stress-fiber formation and procollagen I production in the fibroblasts, supporting the proposed pathway mechanism.

Technology: LogiMML, a logic-based mechanistic network coupled to ridge regression

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: LogiMML, trained on high-content images of human cardiac fibroblasts, predicted that Src inhibition suppresses actin–myosin stress fibers through PI3K signaling.
  • Still unknown: The validation is in vitro in primary human ventricular cardiac fibroblasts; it supports a pathway contribution to measured cell phenotypes, not therapeutic efficacy in an animal model or patients. The model's PI3K effect is described as partial.
  • AI role: LogiMML fitted the relationship between high-content microscopy phenotypes and perturbations to an experimentally informed fibroblast signaling network, then predicted pathway mechanisms for pirfenidone and the Src inhibitor WH-4-023. Researchers tested the PI3K component of the Src-inhibitor prediction with PI3K inhibition in human cardiac fibroblasts.
