Sequential model-based optimization discovers two previously unreported chemical reactions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A programmable robotic synthesis platform used inline HPLC, Raman or NMR feedback and closed-loop optimization to explore selected reaction spaces. In a tosMIC campaign, sequential model-based optimization with a Gaussian-process surrogate identified a reaction between toluenesulphonylmethyl isocyanide and benzylidenemalononitrile; subsequent optimization increased yield from the 27% initial condition to 49% (22 percentage points) over 50 reactions. The article also reports a second previously unreported reaction and an exploratory trifluoromethylation campaign that discovered new molecules.

Technology: ChemputationOptimizer, a sensor-integrated reaction engine with sequential model-based optimization (Gaussian-process surrogate); the platform also exposes random search, design-of-experiments, Bayesian-optimization and genetic-algorithm routines

AI contribution
For the tosMIC and phloroglucinol explorations, machine-guided combinatorial search proposed reaction parameters and inline spectroscopic feedback supplied product-yield measurements; a Gaussian-process sequential-model-based optimizer guided the reaction-space exploration and follow-up optimization. Human chemists defined reaction space and experimental constraints, and performed/characterized the experiments.

Limitation / still unknown
The platform's reported yield gains are specific to these selected reaction spaces and objectives, not general chemistry performance. The tosMIC and phloroglucinol campaigns are reported as previously unreported reactions; product characterization and complete procedure details are in the article/supplement. The system includes several optimization algorithms and the authors note random search can be a strong baseline; the reported tosMIC optimization used SMBO with a Gaussian-process surrogate. No claim is made that the reactions are industrially useful or broadly generalizable. Primary source checked; no independent replication assessed.
