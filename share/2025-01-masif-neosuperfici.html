MaSIF designs binders for protein–drug complexes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: MaSIF designed proteins that recognize three complexes containing progesterone, venetoclax, or actinonin, then validated them in the lab. A venetoclax-inducible system activated the antitumor activity of CAR T cells in vitro; this is a proof of concept without clinical validation.

Technology: MaSIF (deep learning on molecular surfaces)

Editorial relevance 67 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MaSIF generated surface fingerprints and searched for complementary protein surfaces to design binders.
Test and result
MaSIF-designed binders were validated in the lab against three drug-bound complexes, and a venetoclax-inducible system switched on CAR-T tumor-killing activity in vitro.
Limitation / still unknown
The source states the CAR-T work is in vitro only, with no clinical validation, and binding sites are hard to predict.
