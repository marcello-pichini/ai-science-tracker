CellOracle-guided zebrafish perturbations identify axial-mesoderm effects of noto and lhx1a loss

The CellOracle machine-learning workflow used single-cell gene-regulatory networks to prioritize transcription factors for perturbation in zebrafish development. CRISPR/Cas9 tests confirmed a model-predicted, previously unreported increase in prechordal-plate differentiation after loss of noto, alongside the known loss of notochord. Model-prioritized lhx1a loss also inhibited axial-mesoderm differentiation: single-cell RNA-seq found expansion of early notochord cells and reductions in later notochord and prechordal-plate populations; orthogonal RNA assays supported reduced notochord/prechordal markers.

Technology: CellOracle, a machine-learning gene-regulatory-network model with in-silico transcription-factor perturbation

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: CRISPR/Cas9 tests confirmed a model-predicted, previously unreported increase in prechordal-plate differentiation after loss of noto, alongside the known loss of notochord.
  • Still unknown: Evidence is from zebrafish crispants during embryonic development, not stable mutants or mammalian/human systems; the authors report no gross morphological change despite transcriptomic/cell-population effects. The CellOracle model depends on inferred networks and makes cell-state-shift predictions, not direct causal proof of all inferred edges. A later unreviewed 2024 preprint raises implementation and benchmarking concerns, particularly about its use of ATAC-seq for distal regulatory interactions and comparisons with other GRN tools; this record relies only on the specific perturbation predictions tested experimentally and does not adopt the paper's broader benchmarking claims.
  • AI role: The model built context-specific networks from single-cell data, simulated transcription-factor loss, and prioritized noto and lhx1a for experimental follow-up. Researchers used CRISPR/Cas9 crispants, single-cell RNA-seq and marker assays to test the predicted zebrafish cell-fate effects; the prechordal-plate increase after noto loss was reported as previously unrecognized.
