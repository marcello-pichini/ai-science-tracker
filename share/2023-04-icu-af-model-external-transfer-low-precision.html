External ICU tests expose low positive predictive value for an AF model

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A CatBoost model trained on Amsterdam ICU records reached ROC areas of0.77 in MIMIC-IV and0.84 at Ghent when transferred directly. In the respective imbalanced evaluation sets, positive predictive values were0.07 and0.02. Local retraining improved some metrics, but these retrospective tests do not establish a clinically useful alert.

Technology: CatBoost gradient boosting

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned CatBoost classifiers use12-hour clinical-feature windows ending before recorded AF and are tested for hospital transfer; Powershap selects features.
Test and result
Development used18163 admissions, including2000 with nursing-chart AF. Matched AF/no-AF pairs stayed together in an80:20 split, and unmatched controls formed the imbalanced test set. External evaluation covered archived MIMIC-IV and Ghent cohorts; reported direct-transfer precision is specific to those sampled event/control constructions.
Limitation / still unknown
Nursing-chart timing may lag actual arrhythmia; prior AF history was unavailable, so this is not securely new-onset AF. Admission-level splitting does not establish isolation of repeated people. External feature availability influenced the common feature set. What the source calls recalibration includes local model retraining, not merely a probability adjustment. Calibration/ROC metrics do not prove a calibrated patient-specific AF risk or clinical benefit; event-aligned retrospective windows are not live alarms. Online supplementary procedures were not independently read.
