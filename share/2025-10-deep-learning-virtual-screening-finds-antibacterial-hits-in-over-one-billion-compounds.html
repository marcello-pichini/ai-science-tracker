Deep-learning virtual screening finds antibacterial hits in over one billion compounds

Genentech and collaborators trained the deep-learning model GNEprop on high-throughput screening data and screened more than 1.4 billion synthetically accessible molecules. In follow-up experiments, 82 selected compounds showed antibacterial activity against the sensitized E. coli ΔtolC strain; the paper reports a 90-fold higher hit rate than the training screen and describes chemically diverse hits, some with activity beyond that strain and validated biological targets.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In follow-up experiments, 82 selected compounds showed antibacterial activity against the sensitized E. coli ΔtolC strain; the paper reports a 90-fold higher hit rate than the training screen and describes chemically diverse hits, some with activity beyond that strain and validated biological targets.
  • Still unknown: The 82 confirmed hits were tested for single-dose activity against the sensitized E. coli ΔtolC screening strain; this is not evidence that all 82 work against wild-type bacteria or in infections. The 90-fold improvement refers to hit rate relative to the HTS training experiment. Some additional compounds were selected for dose-response testing; do not imply all virtual hits were validated. A publisher correction fixed a coauthor’s misspelled first name and did not change results.
  • AI role: GNEprop was trained on approximately two million small molecules screened against E. coli ΔtolC and used to rank a much larger synthetically accessible library for experimental prioritization. The follow-up assays confirmed antibacterial activity for 82 compounds; biological characterization also investigated specific targets.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02814-6
