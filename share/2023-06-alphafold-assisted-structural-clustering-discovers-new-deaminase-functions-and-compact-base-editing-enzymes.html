AlphaFold-assisted structural clustering discovers new deaminase functions and compact base-editing enzymes

The team used AlphaFold2 structure predictions to cluster the deaminase family and found that most proteins in a DddA-like clade were not double-stranded DNA deaminases. The work characterized smaller single-strand-specific deaminases, developed a compact cytidine deaminase suitable for packaging a base editor into one AAV, and demonstrated robust editing by a clade member in soybean.

Technology: AlphaFold2-assisted structure-based protein-family clustering

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The team used AlphaFold2 structure predictions to cluster the deaminase family and found that most proteins in a DddA-like clade were not double-stranded DNA deaminases.
  • Still unknown: This paper is an AI-assisted protein-discovery and engineering study; experimental base editing demonstrates the utility of selected deaminases, while therapeutic application remains prospective. The study was followed by a 2024 correction/erratum; the record references the original research article, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.041, not the erratum.
  • AI role: AlphaFold2 predicted protein structures that enabled structure-similarity clustering of a full deaminase family. The clustering guided discovery of functional diversity and selection of enzymes, which the researchers characterized experimentally and used in cell, animal and plant base-editing tests.
