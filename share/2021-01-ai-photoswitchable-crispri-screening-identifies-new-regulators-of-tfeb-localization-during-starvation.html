AI-photoswitchable CRISPRi screening identifies new regulators of TFEB localization during starvation

An image-classification-guided pooled CRISPRi screen mapped factors that affect TFEB movement from nucleus to cytosol during prolonged starvation. The screen identified multiple candidate genes, and follow-up assays independently validated 21 of 64 neural-network-called hits; experiments on CREB5 further linked it to TFEB retention and starvation-associated autophagy/lysosomal protein expression.

Technology: AI-photoswitchable screening (AI-PS), using SVM classifiers and a convolutional neural network

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: The screen identified multiple candidate genes, and follow-up assays independently validated 21 of 64 neural-network-called hits; experiments on CREB5 further linked it to TFEB retention and starvation-associated autophagy/lysosomal protein expression.
  • Still unknown: Only 21 of 64 neural-network-called TFEB hits were independently validated in secondary assays (about 32%); candidate effectors should not be treated as all confirmed. Results use cultured U2OS cells and prolonged starvation, and do not establish a physiological role in an organism. CREB5 received targeted follow-up but the overall screen has a modest validation rate.
  • AI role: SVM and CNN models classified single-cell images by subcellular protein localization; the system photoactivated cells with the target phenotype for FACS isolation and gRNA sequencing in pooled CRISPRi screening. AI enabled the genome-scale genotype-to-subcellular-phenotype screen.

Evidence sources: https://rupress.org/jcb/article/220/2/e202006180/211694/Image-based-pooled-whole-genome-CRISPRi-screening
