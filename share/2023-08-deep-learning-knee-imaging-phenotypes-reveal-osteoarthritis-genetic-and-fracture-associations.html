Deep-learning knee imaging phenotypes reveal osteoarthritis genetic and fracture associations

Using knee DXA scans from the UK Biobank, a ResNet-101 model identified 1,931 more probable knee-osteoarthritis cases than health-record coding and a U-Net segmentation model estimated minimum joint-space width (mJSW). In 29,257 participants, the quantitative mJSW phenotype had substantially higher SNP heritability than the binary phenotypes and yielded 18 genome-wide-significant loci, compared with one for the image-based binary classifier and six for ICD-10 coding; only one mJSW locus was already significant in a large prior knee-OA GWAS. The AI-derived mJSW phenotype was also associated with fracture history in an epidemiological analysis, a signal not seen with the binary case labels.

Technology: ResNet-101 convolutional neural network for knee-OA classification and U-Net image segmentation for joint-space measurement

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Using knee DXA scans from the UK Biobank, a ResNet-101 model identified 1,931 more probable knee-osteoarthritis cases than health-record coding and a U-Net segmentation model estimated minimum joint-space width (mJSW).
  • Still unknown: The GWAS used 29,257 quality-controlled UK Biobank participants with knee DXA images, all from the analyzed white-British ancestry subset; ancestry transferability is unestablished. DXA images were non-weight-bearing. The 18 loci are genome-wide associations for a model-derived quantitative endophenotype, not 18 proven causal OA genes; the paper notes only one also reached significance in a previous large knee-OA GWAS and many loci overlap broad anthropometric associations. The fracture result is observational and does not show that OA or joint-space narrowing causes fractures. The medRxiv preprint DOI 10.1101/2023.03.07.23286909 is an earlier version of this same study and is not counted separately.
  • AI role: The neural networks converted routine knee DXA scans into two disease phenotypes: a case–control OA classifier and a quantitative minimum joint-space-width measurement. The researchers used these AI-derived phenotypes in GWAS and downstream epidemiological association analyses, increasing statistical power and revealing genetic and fracture associations that were not significant with ICD-10 case coding or the binary classifier.
