Bayesian optimization finds mild solvent-free mechanochemical conditions for cyclopropanation and epoxidation

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A Bayesian-optimization-guided mechanochemical study identified conditions for the established Johnson–Corey–Chaykovsky reaction using KOH in place of hazardous NaH, under air-equilibrated conditions and with no solvent or liquid-assisted grinding agent. In the chalcone benchmark, one optimized condition reached cyclopropanation completion in five minutes; the study also demonstrated cyclopropanation and epoxidation across multiple substrates, including yields of 45–75% for four additional substrates.

Technology: Bayesian optimization implemented with BoTorch/PyTorch, including multi-objective optimization of product yield and process mass intensity

AI contribution
The BO models selected among six reaction variables (base identity/loading, time, milling frequency, liquid-assisted grinding agent and loading) using experimental yields and process-mass-intensity outcomes. Researchers ran the experiments, performed synthetic substrate-scope tests and used time-resolved in-situ X-ray diffraction to study milling effects.
Test and result
A Bayesian-optimization-guided mechanochemical study identified conditions for the established Johnson–Corey–Chaykovsky reaction using KOH in place of hazardous NaH, under air-equilibrated conditions and with no solvent or liquid-assisted grinding agent.
Limitation / still unknown
The Johnson–Corey–Chaykovsky transformation itself is established chemistry; the finding is a measured, safer and faster mechanochemical process window rather than discovery of a new reaction class. A 5-minute completion time and other yields apply to the tested substrates/conditions; some prior solvent-minimized conditions could not be reproduced. The scale and substrate scope remain those reported in the paper.
