AI-generated virtual spatial proteomics reveals lung-cancer tumor–immune niches associated with immunotherapy response

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: HEX, a pathology-image AI model, predicts spatial expression of 40 protein biomarkers from routine H&E slides. In retrospective NSCLC cohorts, combining HEX-derived virtual spatial proteomics with histology improved prognosis prediction and, in a separate 148-patient immune-checkpoint-inhibitor cohort, improved response prediction over H&E-only and virtual-proteomics-only models. The authors interpreted virtual maps as showing co-localized T-helper and cytotoxic T-cell niches among responders and immunosuppressive macrophage/neutrophil aggregates among non-responders.

Technology: HEX (H&E to protein expression), using a pathology foundation-model backbone; MICA (multimodal integration via co-attention)

Editorial relevance 53 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
HEX predicts expression of 40 proteins and produces spatially resolved virtual proteomics from H&E images; MICA fuses the original image with AI-derived maps for outcome prediction. Researchers used predicted marker distributions to interpret therapy-associated tumor–immune spatial patterns. These are retrospective model analyses with cohort-based clinical associations, not prospective treatment evidence.
Test and result
In retrospective NSCLC cohorts, combining HEX-derived virtual spatial proteomics with histology improved prognosis prediction and, in a separate 148-patient immune-checkpoint-inhibitor cohort, improved response prediction over H&E-only and virtual-proteomics-only models.
Limitation / still unknown
Retrospective observational cohorts; predictive associations do not establish causal mechanisms or improved patient outcomes from model-guided treatment. The 148-patient immunotherapy cohort is separate from the prognosis cohorts. The paper itself says extensive clinical validation remains necessary; do not describe the finding as a clinically validated diagnostic or prospective treatment-selection tool.
