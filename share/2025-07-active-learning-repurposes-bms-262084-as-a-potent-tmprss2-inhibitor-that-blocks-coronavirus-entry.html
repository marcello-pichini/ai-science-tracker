Active learning repurposes BMS-262084 as a potent TMPRSS2 inhibitor that blocks coronavirus entry

A molecular-dynamics and active-learning workflow prioritized the existing compound BMS-262084 as a nanomolar inhibitor of human TMPRSS2 (IC50 1.82 nM). Cell assays reported inhibition of SARS-CoV-2 variants and other coronavirus entry into Calu-3 lung cells.

Technology: Molecular-dynamics-informed active learning with a target-specific scoring function

  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Cell assays reported inhibition of SARS-CoV-2 variants and other coronavirus entry into Calu-3 lung cells.
  • Still unknown: BMS-262084 is a known small molecule repurposed here for TMPRSS2; the discovery claim is its target/antiviral activity under tested conditions. Evidence is biochemical and cell-based, not clinical efficacy or safety. Assay concentrations, viral variants and cell systems limit generalization.
  • AI role: The active-learning workflow used receptor ensembles and target-specific features to iteratively prioritize compounds for testing; researchers measured purified-enzyme inhibition and viral-entry effects in cell assays. This is AI-assisted repurposing of an existing compound, not de novo molecule design.
