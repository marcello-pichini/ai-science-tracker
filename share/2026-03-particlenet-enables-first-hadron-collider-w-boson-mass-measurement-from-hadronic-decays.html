ParticleNet enables first hadron-collider W-boson mass measurement from hadronic decays

Using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton data, CMS reports the first measurement of the W-boson mass in an all-jets final state at a hadron collider: 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV. The result comes from unfolded groomed jet-mass distributions for boosted hadronic W+jets events. The mass-decorrelated ParticleNet graph neural network tagger provides the main result by selecting the W-jet sample and suppressing quark/gluon multijet background; a non-neural N₂ tagger is used as a cross-check.

Technology: ParticleNet mass-decorrelated graph neural network jet tagger

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton data, CMS reports the first measurement of the W-boson mass in an all-jets final state at a hadron collider: 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV.
  • Still unknown: This is an all-jets boosted-W extraction with 0.55 GeV total uncertainty, much less precise than the CMS leptonic-channel W-mass result; the paper frames it as a first step toward future all-jets precision measurements and as a test of jet-substructure modeling. The earlier preliminary CMS result of 80.77 ± 0.57 GeV was superseded by this same paper’s published 80.83 ± 0.55 GeV result and is not counted separately. Jet tagging, QCD background estimation, unfolding, hadronization and jet-energy modeling contribute analysis dependence and uncertainties.
  • AI role: The analysis uses ParticleNet as its main W-jet tagger. Its graph neural network treats jet constituents as an unordered particle cloud and classifies W→qq′ jets against QCD multijet backgrounds; its mass-decorrelated output defines the signal/control regions used for the primary measurement. The N₂ substructure observable is a separate cross-check.
