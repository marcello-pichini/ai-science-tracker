CycleRFdiffusion-designed TP4 is a reproducible cyclic peptide binder to TREM2

A structure-conditioned workflow combining CycleRFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and HighFold generated and screened 1,500 cyclic peptide–TREM2 complexes and prioritized four candidates. The lead TP4 showed weak but reproducible TREM2 binding in spectral-shift, microscale-thermophoresis and surface-plasmon-resonance assays. The authors report favorable plasma stability and moderate metabolic stability, presenting the work as an initial proof of concept for TREM2-targeted cyclic binders.

Technology: CycleRFdiffusion backbone design, ProteinMPNN sequence design and HighFold structural prediction/screening

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The lead TP4 showed weak but reproducible TREM2 binding in spectral-shift, microscale-thermophoresis and surface-plasmon-resonance assays.
  • Still unknown: The only reported lead, TP4, is described as a weak binder. The evidence is biophysical binding and stability profiling, not demonstrated modulation of TREM2 signaling, microglial function, disease pathology or therapeutic efficacy. Treat this as proof-of-concept binder discovery.
  • AI role: The AI pipeline generated and ranked 1,500 candidate cyclic peptide–TREM2 complexes based on structure, sequence and predicted energetics, from which four were selected for experiments; TP4 was then assessed with multiple orthogonal binding assays.
