20 Oct 2026Harvard University; Duke Kunshan University; Duke University

Manifold learning tests electrolyte categories against paired clinical measurements

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural ECG model classified potassium, calcium and magnesium categories using paired laboratory measurements in MIMIC-IV. In the study cohort of8599 patients, potassium macro-F1 was0.61, calcium0.50 and magnesium0.40. This is retrospective clinical-reference evaluation, not a validated replacement for laboratory testing.

Technology: MINN; ResNet18; learned manifold-source network; metadata fusion

Editorial relevance 33 medicine PreprintPreprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned waveform features, task-aware graph-source inference and metadata fusion enable the source-specific laboratory-referenced category classification.
Test and result
The study paired18037 ECG recordings with nearby serum measurements and used patient-level time-based partitions. A learned source network and ECG encoder were jointly fitted with a graph residual objective.
Limitation / still unknown
One-center archived data, imbalanced categories and up-to-one-hour pairing differences limit transportability. Medication/demographic metadata contribute to prediction, especially for calcium/magnesium; the result is not ECG-only inference. Preprocessing can truncate late waveform content. No prospective independent clinical validation, diagnostic deployment or patient benefit was established.
