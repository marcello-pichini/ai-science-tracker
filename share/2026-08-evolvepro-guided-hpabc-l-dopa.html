Machine learning and directed evolution improved an L-Dopa-producing monooxygenase

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Zero-shot protein models prioritized HpaBC substitutions, which were experimentally assayed with a L-Dopa-responsive GFP biosensor. EVOLVEpro then combined experimentally validated substitutions, including directed-evolution-derived variants, and selected higher-order designs. The best reported combined variants produced up to 57–58 times the wild-type fluorescence signal under the stated assay conditions. This is a biosensor proxy for L-Dopa production, not a purified-enzyme kinetic measurement; the work also builds on prior directed-evolution mutations and does not establish clinical benefit.

Technology: MutCompute, ProteinMPNN, ESM protein language models and EVOLVEpro combinatorial design

AI contribution
MutCompute, ProteinMPNN and ESM prioritized single substitutions for experimental testing; EVOLVEpro combined tested mutations and iteratively selected higher-order variants, including designs incorporating directed-evolution results.
Test and result
329 model-predicted variants were assayed; 19 showed at least 1.25-fold biosensor signal. EVOLVEpro-selected combined variants yielded up to 57–58-fold normalized fluorescence relative to wild type under the reported assay conditions.
Limitation / still unknown
Fluorescence from a DopA/GFP biosensor is an indirect L-Dopa-production proxy, not a purified-enzyme catalytic constant. The work includes directed-evolution-derived mutations; G295T had prior directed-evolution support. No human therapeutic efficacy is established.
