Machine-intelligence design yields selective synthetic COX-1 inhibitors from a marine natural-product template

Using Marinopyrrole A as a template, an automated design workflow generated and synthesized new natural-product-inspired molecules; target-prediction software pointed to cyclooxygenases. In biochemical assays, synthetic mimetics 2 and 2a inhibited COX-1 at 0.10 and 0.16 μM, respectively, and compound 2a was crystallographically observed bound to COX-1. Human platelet assays also showed inhibition.

Technology: DOGS rule-based de novo molecule construction and CATS molecular-similarity ranking, coupled with SPiDER self-organizing-map machine-learning target prediction

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In biochemical assays, synthetic mimetics 2 and 2a inhibited COX-1 at 0.10 and 0.16 μM, respectively, and compound 2a was crystallographically observed bound to COX-1.
  • Still unknown: The study tests a small number of designed molecules; automated structure generation was rule-based and similarity-driven rather than a trained generative neural model. The COX-1 effects were biochemical/platelet assays, not clinical effects; the platelet IC50s for 2, 2a, 2b were 9, 13 and 56 nM, but COX-2 inhibition occurred at higher concentrations and selectivity varies by assay. Do not call these approved drugs or imply therapeutic benefit.
  • AI role: DOGS algorithmically generated natural-product-inspired structures and suggested synthetic routes; CATS ranked candidates by pharmacophore similarity. SPiDER's cascaded self-organizing-map models predicted molecular target families, including COX. Experimental target assays confirmed the previously unrecognized COX-1 activity of selected synthetic compounds, with crystallographic confirmation for compound 2a.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8373093/
