Machine-learned multiscale simulations reveal lipid-dependent RAS organization at membranes

The MuMMI machine-learning multiscale simulation ensemble found that local membrane lipid fingerprints correlate with wild-type KRAS clustering and orientation, with many simulated RAS–RAS interfaces appearing nonspecific. Complementary liposome and diffusion experiments supported lipid-dependent RAS adhesion and dynamics, motivating the authors' conclusion that lipids can mediate RAS multimer organization.

Technology: MuMMI (Multiscale Machine-learned Modeling Infrastructure)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Complementary liposome and diffusion experiments supported lipid-dependent RAS adhesion and dynamics, motivating the authors' conclusion that lipids can mediate RAS multimer organization.
  • Still unknown: Most specific orientation and interface conclusions are simulation-derived; experimental comparisons provide validation of selected membrane adhesion/diffusion behaviors, not direct demonstration of these mechanisms in intact cells or cancer outcomes. Authors describe absolute diffusion values as semiquantitative and dependent on methods/force fields.
  • AI role: Machine learning selects diverse local membrane patches from a macroscale lipid model to launch and couple atomistic/coarse-grained simulations, producing an ensemble of more than 100,000 simulations. The resulting simulations identified lipid-composition relationships with RAS orientation and multimer organization; selected predictions were compared with surface-plasmon resonance, fluorescence-correlation and other biophysical experiments.

Evidence sources: https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/119/1/e2113297119.full.pdf
