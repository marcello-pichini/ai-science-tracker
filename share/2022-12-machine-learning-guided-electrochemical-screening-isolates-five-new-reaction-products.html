Machine-learning-guided electrochemical screening isolates five new reaction products

A machine-learning-guided, chemist-in-the-loop screen predicted electrochemical reactivity for 38,865 potential partners with 1,4-dicyanobenzene (DCB). Automated tests of 20 prioritized, previously untested partners yielded DCB conversion in 16; five produced an isolable major product judged of synthetic or mechanistic interest. The reported examples include a silyl-enol-ether coupling and electrochemical benzylic arylation of benzyltrimethylsilane with DCB, plus three mechanistically unexpected products.

Technology: Machine-learning-guided reaction-competency classifier using a DFT-informed molecular representation

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Automated tests of 20 prioritized, previously untested partners yielded DCB conversion in 16; five produced an isolable major product judged of synthetic or mechanistic interest.
  • Still unknown: This is an early reaction-discovery demonstration under one fixed DCB electrolysis system, not a general reaction predictor or optimized synthetic platform. Seven of the 16 DCB-converting selected reactions gave complex mixtures without an appreciable single isolated product; five yielded isolable major products, reported at low yields and without method optimization. DCB conversion alone does not establish a product identity, and the three mechanistically ambiguous outcomes require further study.
  • AI role: The model screened a 38,865-member virtual set for reaction competency and, with chemist selection, prioritized 20 new partners for automated electrolysis. The experiments measured DCB conversion, then product analysis isolated and characterized five major products from the selected set.
