1 Jul 2026Nanjing Tech University; State Key Laboratory of Materials-Oriented Chemical Engineering, Nanjing Tech University

Machine learning guided a PET-binding module into a reusable whole-cell catalyst

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An HMM and supervised PET hydrolase model screened sequence candidates and prioritized the PET-binding module tCBM13-1. Experiments found greater PET adsorption and retention than the CBM2 comparator; when displayed with FAST-PETase on E. coli, the system increased PET-film depolymerization by about 43% and retained over 64% of initial activity after ten cycles. These are laboratory assays, not evidence of industrial-scale recycling.

Technology: Profile-HMM homology search combined with supervised PET hydrolase machine-learning model PETML

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A supervised PET hydrolase machine-learning model within an HMM-based sequence-mining workflow prioritized tCBM13-1 from candidate modules for experimental adsorption and whole-cell depolymerization tests.
Test and result
The authors experimentally measured tCBM13-1 adsorption and retention on PET and tested whole-cell co-display with FAST-PETase. The publisher abstract reports about 43% greater PET-film depolymerization (2793 μg/(d·cm²)) and over 64% residual activity after ten cycles.
Limitation / still unknown
Results are laboratory PET assays; they do not establish industrial-scale recycling, environmental deployment, or lifecycle benefit.
