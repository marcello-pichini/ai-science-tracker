Machine learning prioritizes ruthenium complexes with increased MRSA antibacterial hit rates

Researchers trained machine-learning models on 288 modularly synthesized ruthenium arene Schiff-base complexes and used them to prioritize 54 new compounds for antibacterial-activity testing. The model-selected set had a 53.7% hit rate against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, compared with 9.4% in the original library, an approximately 5.7-fold enrichment.

Technology: Machine-learning activity-prediction models trained on ruthenium arene Schiff-base complex structures and antibacterial assay data

  • AI role: Models were trained using assay data from 288 modularly synthesized ruthenium complexes and used to predict activity for a new set of 54 compounds.
