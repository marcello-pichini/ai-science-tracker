Alignment-free discovery of conserved RNA structures in the human genome

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: RLMHelix combines RNA-language-model dependency maps with thermodynamic pairing probabilities to rank candidate conserved structures. Its genome scan reported 15,821 candidates, two-thirds absent from four earlier screens. The paper compared predicted structures with existing public icSHAPE datasets and sequence-alignment evidence; these retrospective analyses and exploratory pull-downs do not establish prospective or separate-team validation.

Technology: RNA language model RLMHelix with thermodynamic structure scoring

Editorial relevance 60 biology Preprint Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
RLMHelix combines RNA-language-model dependency maps with thermodynamic base-pair probabilities, scores helices in single sequences, then tests candidates against alignments; distillation enables genome-scale scans
Test and result
The preprint compared predicted structures against public icSHAPE datasets GSE145805 and GSE74353, including measured RNA cores from five cell lines, and tested conservation/covariation using retrieved alignments. These are existing-data evaluations; pull-down experiments are described as exploratory.
Limitation / still unknown
The checked source does not report prospective testing before new outcomes or a separate research team's follow-up confirmation; public probing datasets and same-paper exploratory assays support retrospective/benchmark evidence only.
