Tree-based machine learning nominates Parabacteroides merdae and N-acetyl-D-mannosamine as ulcerative-colitis alleviators in a mouse model

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The researchers combined personalized gut-microbiome community metabolic models from human iHMP data with tree-based machine-learning feature selection to identify microbial strains and metabolites associated with ulcerative-colitis samples. Parabacteroides merdae ATCC 43,184 and N-acetyl-D-mannosamine were among the most predictive features; the strain was also the main modeled microbial contributor to the metabolite. In a DSS-induced mouse colitis experiment, oral administration of the bacterium or metabolite improved reported colitis measures.

Technology: Tree-based supervised classifiers including random forest, gradient-boosted regression trees, XGBoost, XGBoost-random forest and LightGBM, combined with individualized genome-scale microbiome community models

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Personalized community metabolic models estimated strain-level contributions to secreted metabolites in human iHMP samples. Tree-based classifiers selected discriminative strain and metabolite features; XGBRF feature attribution ranked P. merdae ATCC 43,184 and N-acetyl-D-mannosamine highly, motivating the mouse validation. The AI analysis nominated these targets; it did not establish human therapeutic efficacy.
Test and result
In a DSS-induced mouse colitis experiment, oral administration of the bacterium or metabolite improved reported colitis measures.
Limitation / still unknown
The human data are observational microbiome samples, while intervention was tested in a DSS-induced mouse model, not in people. The animal groups contained eight mice each; findings do not establish treatment efficacy or safety in human ulcerative colitis. Metabolite-production associations came from community-model simulations and were not direct evidence of individual strain production in each person. The model provides candidate prioritization, not a causal human mechanism.
