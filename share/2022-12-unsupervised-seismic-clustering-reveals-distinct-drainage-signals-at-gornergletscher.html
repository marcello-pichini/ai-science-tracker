Unsupervised seismic clustering reveals distinct drainage signals at Gornergletscher

An unsupervised analysis of five weeks of seismic records from Gornergletscher, Switzerland, separated cryoseismic signals into clusters associated with the 2007 Gornersee lake flood and with afternoon/evening meltwater flow. One high-energy, broadband noise cluster began about two days before the reported onset of subglacial flooding; the authors suggest it captures the earlier initiation of englacial and subglacial drainage and improves the flood chronology.

Technology: SpecUFEx unsupervised feature extraction: non-negative matrix factorization, hidden Markov modeling, principal component analysis, and k-means clustering

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: An unsupervised analysis of five weeks of seismic records from Gornergletscher, Switzerland, separated cryoseismic signals into clusters associated with the 2007 Gornersee lake flood and with afternoon/evening meltwater flow.
  • Still unknown: The analysis covers five weeks at one glacier using the 2007 flood record. The inferred drainage start precedes lake-level evidence by about two days, but the source cannot definitively exclude ice-bed slip, till deformation, or small fractures as explanations for some spectral features. The study interprets cluster timing and spectra as hydrologic signals; it does not directly image subglacial drainage pathways.
  • AI role: The unsupervised learning pipeline reduced seismic spectrograms to fingerprints and grouped similar continuous-noise and icequake signals without labels. Comparing the learned clusters with lake level, GPS, temperature, and event timing exposed distinct clusters associated with lake-flood drainage and daily meltwater flow, which supported the revised drainage chronology.
