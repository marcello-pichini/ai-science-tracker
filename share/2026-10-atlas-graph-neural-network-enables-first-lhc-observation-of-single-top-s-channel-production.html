ATLAS graph neural network enables first LHC observation of single-top s-channel production

Using 140 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton collisions, ATLAS reports the first observation of single-top-quark s-channel production at the LHC, with 5.1σ observed (5.2σ expected) significance. The measured cross-section is 10.8 ± 1.5(stat) ± 1.6(syst) pb, consistent with the Standard Model. A graph neural network discriminant separates the signal from top-pair, W+jets and t-channel single-top backgrounds; the analysis also uses broadened event acceptance, improved b tagging and refined modeling.

Technology: Event-level graph neural network; ATLAS GN2 graph-transformer b-jet tagger

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Using 140 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton–proton collisions, ATLAS reports the first observation of single-top-quark s-channel production at the LHC, with 5.1σ observed (5.2σ expected) significance.
  • Still unknown: This is an ATLAS preprint submitted to PRL as of the checked date; prior ATLAS analyses had reported evidence, and the Tevatron had already observed the process in a combined CDF+DØ result. The new claim is first observation at the LHC and by one experiment. The cross-section has about 20% total uncertainty and agrees with the Standard Model; the network is one of several improvements contributing to sensitivity, not the sole cause.
  • AI role: The GNN uses event kinematics to distinguish s-channel signal from major backgrounds, with its score distribution used in the likelihood fit. GN2 improves b-jet identification; the paper attributes increased sensitivity jointly to the GNN, broader acceptance, improved b tagging, calibrations and theory modeling.
