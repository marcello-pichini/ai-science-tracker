Proteomic profiling and machine learning identified an externally validated diagnostic panel and severity markers for infective endocarditis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The study profiled plasma and excised vegetation samples from 238 infective-endocarditis patients and 100 controls, then used an ensemble machine-learning pipeline to select a 10-protein plasma diagnostic panel. The paper reports AUCs of 0.97 in the held-out discovery test set and 0.98 and 0.94 in two external plasma cohorts. Integrating proteomic and clinical features also produced a prognostic model for severe heart-failure status (abstract AUC 0.87). LRG1 and NDUFB4 were associated with infection severity in the cohort analyses.

Technology: Ensemble machine-learning diagnostic and prognostic models; the diagnostic workflow compared six algorithms and used recursive feature elimination with cross-validation to select a 10-protein panel

AI contribution
Machine-learning feature-importance analysis and recursive feature elimination selected a 10-protein plasma combination for distinguishing infective endocarditis from non-endocarditis samples. The models were evaluated in a held-out discovery cohort and two external plasma cohorts. A separate model combined proteomic and clinical features to classify severe heart-failure status.
Test and result
The paper reports AUCs of 0.97 in the held-out discovery test set and 0.98 and 0.94 in two external plasma cohorts.
Limitation / still unknown
The study is an observational proteomic cohort analysis; it does not demonstrate that use of the panel improves patient outcomes or can replace current diagnostic criteria. The development comparison included 238 IE patients and 100 controls, so case-control separation may not reflect performance in an unselected real-world diagnostic population. External evaluation used two additional plasma cohorts (combined n=328); the prognostic endpoint and biomarkers are associations, not causal mechanisms. The paper's proposed NET-related therapeutic implications are hypothesis-generating and are not counted as demonstrated treatment benefit.
