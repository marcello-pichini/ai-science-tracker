Generative AI designs and experimentally validates an ultrathin broadband ENZ absorber

A generative deep neural network searched multilayer indium-tin-oxide/aluminum-doped-zinc-oxide designs and selected an epsilon-near-zero absorber stack. After the researchers merged two similar adjacent layers for fabrication, the approximately 159-nm device experimentally maintained more than 98% absorption over a 1,077-nm near-infrared bandwidth at 49° p-polarized illumination. The modeled design predicted 1,688 nm bandwidth; the experiment’s detector cutoff limited measurements above 2,600 nm.

Technology: GLONet generative deep neural network, trained in simulation loop with differentiable transfer-matrix solver TMM-Fast

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A generative deep neural network searched multilayer indium-tin-oxide/aluminum-doped-zinc-oxide designs and selected an epsilon-near-zero absorber stack.
  • Still unknown: The demonstrated result is a fabricated device under p-polarized illumination at a 49° incidence angle using a prism-coupled ENZ mode; it is not an angle-independent or application-qualified absorber. Measurement above 2,600 nm was limited by the detector, so the 1,688-nm bandwidth is a simulation result and the measured bandwidth is 1,077 nm. The network-produced four-layer design was manually simplified by merging two similar layers before fabrication. Earlier work by the same group used neural networks to design homogeneous ITO stacks; this paper reports a distinct heterogeneous ITO/AZO design with experimental validation. No commercial application is demonstrated.
  • AI role: The GLONet maps random latent vectors to multilayer designs, optimizing per-layer refractive/material parameters and thickness using differentiable optical simulations to maximize broadband absorption. The authors generated 10,000 candidate stacks and selected a high-performing design for experimental fabrication and measurement. A manual layer-merging step simplified the four-layer design to three layers before fabrication.
