Three spectroscopically confirmed z≈6 quasars found through contrastive learning

A self-supervised contrastive-learning search of Dark Energy Survey imaging isolated a group of 11 sources that included seven known z≈6 quasars. Follow-up Gemini-South and archival NTT spectroscopy confirmed three additional quasars at redshifts 5.94–6.07. One source, J0109−5424, shows evidence of a foreground object and is a lensed-quasar candidate, but its multiple-image status requires high-resolution imaging.

Technology: Self-supervised contrastive-learning neural network trained on Dark Energy Survey imaging data

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A self-supervised contrastive-learning search of Dark Energy Survey imaging isolated a group of 11 sources that included seven known z≈6 quasars.
  • Still unknown: The three quasar identifications are spectroscopically confirmed, but the possible gravitational lens interpretation of J0109−5424 remains unconfirmed pending high-resolution imaging. The reported 91% efficiency refers to ten quasars among the eleven-source island, including seven previously known objects; three of the four previously unclassified targets were confirmed quasars. It is not a universal completeness estimate.
  • AI role: Grouped sources into a learned embedding-space island that prioritized four unclassified candidates; spectroscopy confirmed three as quasars, including one source missed by conventional color selection because foreground light contaminated the photometry.
