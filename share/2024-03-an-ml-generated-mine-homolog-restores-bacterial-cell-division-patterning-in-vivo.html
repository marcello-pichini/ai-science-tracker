An ML-generated MinE homolog restores bacterial cell division patterning in vivo

A machine-learning-generated MinE homolog, synMinEv25, restored Min oscillations and wild-type-like cell growth and morphology when substituted for the native minE gene in an E. coli strain lacking minDE. Researchers generated variants with a multiple-sequence-alignment variational autoencoder, used AlphaFold2-based structural scoring to select candidates, and screened them in synthetic-cell mimics before in-vivo testing. The validated variant is a distant homolog with less than 50% sequence identity to E. coli MinE.

Technology: Multiple-sequence-alignment variational autoencoder (MSA-VAE), AlphaFold2 Multimer, and a structure-based divide-and-conquer scoring pipeline

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Researchers generated variants with a multiple-sequence-alignment variational autoencoder, used AlphaFold2-based structural scoring to select candidates, and screened them in synthetic-cell mimics before in-vivo testing.
  • Still unknown: This is a proof-of-principle result in one engineered E. coli MinDE system, not a general demonstration that generated proteins can replace natural genes. The paper notes weak correlation between several in-silico scores and individual measured protein properties; it reports one best-performing variant and does not establish applications outside this assay.
  • AI role: The MSA-VAE generated MinE homolog sequences; structure-based scoring estimated membrane binding, MinD complex formation and dimerization to prioritize 48 candidates from 4,000 generated sequences. Cell-free synthetic-cell assays identified 14 variants with Min oscillations, and researchers tested these in E. coli; synMinEv25 restored oscillations, growth and morphology. AI generated and prioritized candidates, while wet-lab experiments established function.
