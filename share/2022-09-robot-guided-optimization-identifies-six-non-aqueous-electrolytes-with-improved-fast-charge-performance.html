Robot-guided optimization identifies six non-aqueous electrolytes with improved fast-charge performance

A closed-loop platform paired Clio, an automated electrolyte-formulation and conductivity-testing robot, with Dragonfly Bayesian optimization. In 42 experiments over two workdays, it selected six non-aqueous electrolyte formulations from a LiPF₆ and EC/EMC/DMC design space. The team tested these formulations in 220 mAh graphite‖NMC pouch cells; all showed improved fast-charging capability relative to a baseline electrolyte selected in advance from the same design space. The search reached its reported target about six times faster than random search using the same automated setup.

Technology: Dragonfly Bayesian optimization coupled to the Clio robotic electrolyte experimentation platform

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A closed-loop platform paired Clio, an automated electrolyte-formulation and conductivity-testing robot, with Dragonfly Bayesian optimization.
  • Still unknown: This is a search within a defined design space of LiPF₆ in EC/EMC/DMC solvents, not a claim that the constituent chemicals or all six electrolyte recipes were wholly unprecedented. The optimization target was bulk ionic conductivity, with fast-charge cell performance as a subsequent validation. Pouch-cell tests used nominal 220 mAh graphite‖LiNi0.5Mn0.3Co0.2O2 cells and a baseline chosen from the same design space; results do not establish commercial-scale manufacturing or long-term fleet performance. The paper compares optimization time with random search in the same automated experiment.
  • AI role: Dragonfly selected electrolyte formulations for experiments and used measurement feedback from Clio to adapt subsequent selections. The closed-loop system optimized measured ionic conductivity over a non-aqueous single-salt/ternary-solvent search space; selected formulations were then assembled and evaluated by the researchers in pouch cells.
