Instantaneous voice-synthesis neuroprosthesis for a man with ALS

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A brain-computer interface decoding 256 microelectrodes in the ventral precentral gyrus synthesized the participant's intended voice causally, with a reported latency of about 10 ms per decoding step. Listeners understood almost 60% of synthesized words, compared with 4% when he was not using the device, and he could modulate intonation and sing short melodies. Caveat: a single participant with ALS, within the BrainGate2 trial, and the authors describe the technology as early stage.

Technology: Deep neural network speech decoder for intracortical recordings

Test and result
A single participant with ALS used a neural decoder on 256 microelectrodes with closed-loop audio feedback to produce synthesized voice in near real time, and could modulate intonation and sing short melodies.
Limitation / still unknown
