Machine learning isolates aerosol-driven cloud-cover increase after the Holuhraun eruption

Using MODIS satellite observations and a random-forest counterfactual trained on meteorology and non-eruption years, researchers estimated cloud responses to the 2014 Holuhraun volcanic aerosol plume. They found cloud fraction increased by about 10% across the studied region; this change contributed more to the estimated aerosol cooling than the smaller radiative effect from brighter clouds with smaller droplets. The study supplies an observational constraint on aerosol–cloud climate forcing.

Technology: Random-forest ML-MODIS surrogate trained on meteorological reanalysis and MODIS satellite data from 2001–2020, excluding the 2014 eruption year

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Using MODIS satellite observations and a random-forest counterfactual trained on meteorology and non-eruption years, researchers estimated cloud responses to the 2014 Holuhraun volcanic aerosol plume.
  • Still unknown: The result concerns one volcanic natural experiment, Holuhraun in 2014, and satellite observations of a North Atlantic/Northern European region. The cloud response is estimated by comparing observations with an ML counterfactual and is not a randomized intervention; it depends on model and MODIS retrieval assumptions. The paper reports about a 10% cloud-cover increase and attributes the leading radiative contribution to cloud fraction, while the droplet-size effect is also present and the liquid-water-path contribution is weak.
  • AI role: The random forest estimated the cloud properties expected under observed meteorological conditions without volcanic aerosol, creating a counterfactual baseline. Comparing these predictions with 2014 MODIS observations let the authors isolate cloud changes associated with the Holuhraun aerosol perturbation from meteorological co-variability and estimate the relative radiative contributions of cloud-fraction, liquid-water-path, and droplet-size changes.
