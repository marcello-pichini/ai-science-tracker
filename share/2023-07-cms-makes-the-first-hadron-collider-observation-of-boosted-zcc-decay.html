CMS makes the first hadron-collider observation of boosted Z→cc̄ decay

In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider. The measured signal strength relative to the Standard Model prediction is 1.00 with combined uncertainty +0.19/−0.17. A deep neural network (DeepDoubleX) identified large-radius jets from charm-quark pairs; the paper also sets a 95% confidence-level upper limit of 47 times the Standard Model prediction on boosted H→cc̄ production.

Technology: DeepDoubleX deep neural network for double-charm jet tagging

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV proton-proton data, CMS observed boosted Z→cc̄ production in association with jets for the first time at a hadron collider.
  • Still unknown: The first-observation claim is specifically for Z→cc̄ in association with jets at high transverse momentum at a hadron collider; it is not the first observation of Z decay generally. The neural tagger is an important analysis tool, while the observation derives from the full CMS event selection and likelihood fit. In the same paper, boosted H→cc̄ was not observed; only an upper limit is reported.
  • AI role: The neural network processed secondary-vertex and particle-flow candidate features to classify boosted two-prong jets as charm versus light flavor and charm versus bottom. Its charm tagging separated the Z→cc̄ signal from dominant multijet backgrounds and supported the first observation in the boosted high-transverse-momentum regime.
