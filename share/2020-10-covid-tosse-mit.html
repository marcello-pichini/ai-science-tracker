A model analyzes coughs to detect COVID-19 infections

A model trained on volunteer recordings recognized 98.5% of coughs associated with confirmed infections in the test reported by MIT. This is an early result on data collected online, with health status also self-reported; it is not a diagnosis or prospective clinical validation.

Technology: Neural networks for audio

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Model trained on 4,256 subjects and tested on 1,064 held-out subjects from web-collected cough recordings, with results validated against official COVID-19 test outcomes; sensitivity 98.5% and specificity 94.2% overall, but 83.2% specificity in asymptomatic subjects.
  • Still unknown: Not a diagnosis for symptomatic people and does not separate Covid from flu or asthma; status was self-reported; false-positive results for healthy participants not reported; app would need approval and a larger, more diverse dataset.
  • AI role: Neural networks trained on cough biomarkers (vocal cords, sentiment in speech, lung performance) combined with a muscular-degradation estimate to flag Covid-positive coughs.

Evidence sources: https://news.mit.edu/2020/covid-19-cough-cellphone-detection-1029 · https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1109/OJEMB.2020.3026928&resultType=core&format=json
