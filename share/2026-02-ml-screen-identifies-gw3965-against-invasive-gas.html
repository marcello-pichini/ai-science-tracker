Machine learning screening identifies GW3965 activity against invasive group A Streptococcus

A neural network trained on measured growth responses from 2,560 compounds screened 6,111 FDA-evaluated compounds for activity against multidrug-resistant emm92 group A Streptococcus. Of nine selected compounds tested, GW3965 reduced bacterial survival by 99% at an MIC of 6.25 μM. The authors also report improved wound closure in an infected human skin-equivalent model and reduced lesion area and bacterial burden in mice. This is preclinical evidence and does not establish efficacy or safety in people. The peer-reviewed journal article followed on 2026-05-30 and reports the same core GW3965 result.

Technology: Deep neural network trained on compound SMILES and measured bacterial growth responses; model screened an FDA-evaluated compound library

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The model was trained on 2,560 compounds and screened 6,111. Nine selected compounds were validated; GW3965 had MIC 6.25 μM and reduced viable emm92 iGAS by 99%. In a human skin-equivalent model infected tissues treated with GW3965 showed wound closure; in a mouse SSTI model 30 mg/kg reduced lesion size and day-6 wound bacterial burden by about 2 logs on average.
  • Still unknown: Results come from in-vitro assays and animal/tissue models; the preprint reports no synergy with ampicillin. Human clinical efficacy, dosing, safety and mechanism against GAS remain unestablished.
  • AI role: A neural network learned structure–growth-inhibition patterns from measured compound screening data, prioritized GW3965 for follow-up, and the selected candidate was experimentally tested in bacterial, human skin-equivalent, and mouse infection models.
