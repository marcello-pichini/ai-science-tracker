Machine learning prioritizes a protective Arabidopsis leaf fungus that suppresses oomycete infection

Four classifiers (random forest, support-vector machine, logistic regression and multilayer perceptron) used six years of natural Arabidopsis leaf-microbiome data to prioritize taxa associated with Albugo infection status. Follow-up plant assays found that selected health-associated microbes reduced infection; Cystofilobasidium macerans OTU126 gave the strongest individual protection, reducing Albugo infection by an average of 73%. In a 15-member synthetic leaf community, adding this fungus increased the reported infection reduction to 40.25%, compared with 19.36% for the community alone.

Technology: Random forest, support-vector machine, logistic regression and multilayer perceptron classifiers with cross-validated feature selection

  • Tested: Follow-up plant assays found that selected health-associated microbes reduced infection; Cystofilobasidium macerans OTU126 gave the strongest individual protection, reducing Albugo infection by an average of 73%.
  • Still unknown: This is a controlled Arabidopsis–Albugo plant-pathogen model, not a field crop efficacy trial. ML features are associations with infection status; they do not establish the microbes caused naturally observed health status. Isolates were matched to predicted OTUs using marker sequences, which the authors note do not fully establish strain-level functional identity. Genomic terpene-cluster hypotheses were not directly tested. No commercial or field-ready biocontrol performance is established.
  • AI role: Models classified infected versus uninfected plants from microbial community profiles and prioritized disease-associated and health-associated taxa for follow-up. Researchers then tested selected cultured isolates in planta and tested Cystofilobasidium in a synthetic community.
