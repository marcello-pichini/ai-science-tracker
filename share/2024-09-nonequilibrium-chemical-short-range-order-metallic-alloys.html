Machine learning simulations reveal nonequilibrium short-range order in metallic alloys

A moment-tensor machine-learning interatomic potential enabled large-scale simulations of CrCoNi processing. The authors report that thermomechanical deformation and solidification can generate steady-state chemical short-range order outside the equilibrium-annealing spectrum, and propose an ordering-bias mechanism. This is a simulation-based computational finding, not direct experimental validation of the predicted states in manufactured alloys. The result and AI role were already present in arXiv v1; journal publication followed in 2025.

Technology: Moment-tensor machine-learning interatomic potential; E(3)-equivariant graph neural network for local chemical-motif identification

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The primary v1 reports molecular-dynamics and nonequilibrium Monte Carlo simulations of CrCoNi thermomechanical deformation and solidification; these simulations yield steady-state SRO states outside the equilibrium-annealing spectrum.
  • Still unknown: This is a simulation-based result, not direct experimental confirmation of every predicted state or industrial deployment. Broad prior-art screening is bounded; independent reproduction is not established.
  • AI role: A moment-tensor machine-learning interatomic potential (TS-f) supplies energies and forces for simulations; an E(3)-equivariant graph neural network separately identifies local motifs and supports SRO quantification.
