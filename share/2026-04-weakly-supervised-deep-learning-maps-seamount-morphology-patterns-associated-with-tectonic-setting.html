Weakly supervised deep learning maps seamount morphology patterns associated with tectonic setting

A weakly supervised deep-learning analysis combining satellite-derived gravity and bathymetry produced a global seamount inventory and reported systematic morphology differences: broad edifices along fast-spreading ridges and steeper forms near intraplate hotspots. The authors interpret these differences as reflecting geodynamic controls on volcanic construction. Many detections rely on indirect gravity/bathymetry data rather than shipborne mapping, so individual feature identifications and causal interpretations require further validation.

Technology: Weakly supervised deep-learning detector with vision foundation model, iterative pseudo-label refinement, and elliptical Gaussian fitting
