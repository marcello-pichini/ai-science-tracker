BIONIC links the natural product NP329 to yeast cell-wall biogenesis and β-1,6-glucan synthesis

The BIONIC graph-convolutional network integrated yeast interaction datasets and predicted essential-gene chemical sensitivities to the uncharacterized compound NP329. Experimental screens supported four BIONIC-predicted sensitive genes in the cell-wall biogenesis process; follow-up tests showed related steroidal alkaloid jervine reduced β-1,6-glucan and KRE6/SKN1 mutations conferred resistance, supporting a pathway-level connection for this compound family.

Technology: BIONIC (Biological Network Integration using Convolutions), a graph-convolutional deep-learning network-integration method

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: Experimental screens supported four BIONIC-predicted sensitive genes in the cell-wall biogenesis process; follow-up tests showed related steroidal alkaloid jervine reduced β-1,6-glucan and KRE6/SKN1 mutations conferred resistance, supporting a pathway-level connection for this compound family.
  • Still unknown: BIONIC predicted chemical–genetic sensitivity, not direct molecular binding. NP329 is pseudojervine; jervine is a related compound tested in follow-up. The authors report jervine reduced β-1,6-glucan and KRE6/SKN1 mutations conferred resistance, implicating but not biochemically proving target engagement. Yeast-specific findings.
  • AI role: BIONIC combined yeast protein-interaction, coexpression and genetic-interaction network features to predict essential-gene chemical sensitivities. For NP329 it predicted four of six experimentally sensitive essential genes in the glycosylation/cell-wall biogenesis process, helping identify this cellular process for follow-up. The later jervine experiments are supportive chemical-family follow-up, not direct proof that BIONIC predicted the jervine interaction.
