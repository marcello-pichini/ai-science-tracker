AlphaCD maps experimentally measured catalytic properties across cytidine deaminases and supports a high-fidelity editor

The researchers measured catalytic efficiency, editing windows, motif preferences and off-target activity for 1,100 APOBEC-like cytidine deaminases fused to nCas9 in HEK293T cells, then trained AlphaCD to predict these properties. They applied it to 21,335 UniProt deaminases and experimentally checked a 28-protein subsample. As an application, the team used AlphaCD to identify a high-efficiency deaminase and alanine scanning to produce the E100A variant, which retained editing activity while reducing off-target activity; cell experiments tested editing at endogenous loci and a PCSK9 splice site.

Technology: AlphaCD, a machine-learning model trained on sequence, predicted structure and related protein features

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The researchers measured catalytic efficiency, editing windows, motif preferences and off-target activity for 1,100 APOBEC-like cytidine deaminases fused to nCas9 in HEK293T cells, then trained AlphaCD to predict these properties.
  • Still unknown: The large functional dataset was measured in HEK293T reporter assays. The 21,335-protein landscape is model-predicted, with a 28-protein validation subsample. The engineered E100A editor was tested in cells; these experiments do not establish therapeutic safety, in-vivo efficacy or clinical benefit. This is distinct from the earlier AlphaFold2 structural-clustering papers: the named discovery and assay here concern the 1,100-enzyme dataset, AlphaCD predictions and A0A2R2Z4E4 E100A variant, not CD0208^P52A or the 2023 DddA-like clade findings.
  • AI role: AlphaCD learned experimentally measured activity, editing-window, motif and off-target properties across 1,100 deaminases, predicted properties for 21,335 database sequences, and was applied to select a high-efficiency candidate for subsequent alanine-scanning engineering. The authors report the model's predictions validated on 28 sampled deaminases; the E100A mutation itself was experimentally produced by alanine scanning, not generated by the model.
