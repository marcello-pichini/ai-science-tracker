Blood DNA methylation machine learning signatures classified brain insulin resistance across three cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning framework applied to blood DNA-methylation profiles identified 540 CpG sites that classified brain insulin resistance in adults without type 2 diabetes. In the 167-person discovery cohort, the selected methylation signature was tested in two separate replication cohorts of 33 and 24 participants, with reported classification accuracy of 83–94%. The CpGs mapped to 445 genes enriched for neuronal-development and axonogenesis annotations; 98 sites showed significant blood–brain methylation correlation.

Technology: Supervised machine-learning framework using blood DNA-methylation profiles; a multi-fold elastic-net approach was described in the associated institutional and conference materials

AI contribution
The supervised model selected a 540-CpG methylation signature from peripheral blood measurements to classify participants with and without brain insulin resistance, and the signature was assessed in two replication cohorts. The model-derived CpG set was then used for differential-methylation, gene-annotation and blood–brain correlation analyses.
Test and result
In the 167-person discovery cohort, the selected methylation signature was tested in two separate replication cohorts of 33 and 24 participants, with reported classification accuracy of 83–94%.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a biomarker-classification study, not a prospective clinical screening or intervention study. The discovery cohort had 167 participants and the replication cohorts were small (n=33 and n=24); replication accuracy is reported as 83–94%, and larger independent cohorts are needed to establish transportability. Blood–brain methylation correlations at 98 sites support a proxy relationship but do not show that peripheral methylation causes brain insulin resistance. The authors describe potential future diagnostic use rather than demonstrated clinical utility.
