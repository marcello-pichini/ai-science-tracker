Machine-learning classification shows rigidity-corrected moment release is similar across earthquake depths

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Random-forest classification of source-time-function characteristics for 3,675 global Mw > 5.5 earthquakes showed that depth-dependent rigidity explains much of the differences in rupture duration, length and stress drop between shallow, intermediate and deep earthquakes. Once medium rigidity is corrected, the analyzed events exhibit similar moment-release processes, supporting a constant-strain-drop interpretation across depths and host-rock types.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning classification and feature-importance analysis

AI contribution
Compared source-time-function features across depth classes and identified surrounding-medium rigidity as the principal explanatory feature, enabling the rigidity-corrected cross-depth comparison.
Test and result
Random-forest classification of source-time-function characteristics for 3,675 global Mw > 5.5 earthquakes showed that depth-dependent rigidity explains much of the differences in rupture duration, length and stress drop between shallow, intermediate and deep earthquakes.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a global observational analysis of 3,675 moderate-to-large earthquakes. Its constant-strain-drop conclusion is a supported scaling interpretation, not proof that every earthquake rupture mechanism is identical; depth class and source-time-function estimates have sampling limitations.
