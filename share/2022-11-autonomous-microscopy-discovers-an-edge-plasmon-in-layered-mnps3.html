Autonomous microscopy discovers an edge plasmon in layered MnPS₃

Deep-kernel-learning-guided STEM-EELS measurements identified a low-energy edge-localized plasmon response in the layered antiferromagnet MnPS₃, distinct from the higher-energy bulk plasmon response. The system used local structure to predict spectra and uncertainty, then autonomously selected where to measure based on a physics-defined objective. After 10–25 spectra, about 1% of the image-space points, it localized the edge mode to the flake boundary.

Technology: Deep kernel learning (feed-forward neural-network feature embedding with a Gaussian-process kernel and physics-based acquisition/scalarizer)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Deep-kernel-learning-guided STEM-EELS measurements identified a low-energy edge-localized plasmon response in the layered antiferromagnet MnPS₃, distinct from the higher-energy bulk plasmon response.
  • Still unknown: The paper demonstrates the mode in the reported exfoliated MnPS₃ flake and STEM-EELS setup. The physical search objective was supplied by researchers (a spectral feature below the bulk-plasmon peak), so the AI did not independently formulate the scientific question. The authors report that the edge response varied with local boundary shape and present this as a proof-of-concept discovery workflow, not a complete theory of MnPS₃ plasmon behavior.
  • AI role: The model learned a structure-to-electron-energy-loss-spectrum relationship from sequential measurements and used its predicted response and uncertainty to choose subsequent STEM probe locations. A user-specified spectral scalarizer prioritized a low-energy feature relative to the bulk plasmon; the closed-loop instrument then discovered and mapped an edge-localized response without pretraining on MnPS₃ sample data.
