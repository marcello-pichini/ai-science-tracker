DinoTracker: AI classifies dinosaur footprints

An unsupervised model trained on nearly 2,000 fossil footprints and millions of simulated variations identifies eight features of variation and agrees with experts about 90% of the time. Some tracks over 200 million years old resemble bird feet, which could suggest bird origins tens of millions of years earlier. The interpretation is open: they could be early dinosaurs whose feet happened to look similar.

Technology: DinoTracker (unsupervised model)

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: An unsupervised variational autoencoder was trained on 1,974 fossil footprints; after labeling, its groupings agreed with published expert identifications in 80-93% of cases (the press release says about 90%).
  • Still unknown: The interpretation is open: the tracks could be early dinosaurs whose feet resembled birds' feet; the study states no limitations section.
  • AI role: An unsupervised disentangled variational autoencoder found eight shape-variation features without prior labels, and predicted which dinosaur made each track.
