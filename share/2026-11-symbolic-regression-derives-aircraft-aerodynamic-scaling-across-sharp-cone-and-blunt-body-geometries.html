Symbolic regression derives aircraft aerodynamic scaling across sharp-cone and blunt-body geometries

A Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR) analysis of hypersonic simulation data derived an interpretable non-power-product scaling relation for axial-force coefficient across sharp-cone and blunt-body configurations. It combines angle of attack, Mach number and Reynolds number through a dominant term and a viscous correction; the relation has a shared functional form but geometry-specific fitted constants. The authors interpret the constants as reflecting differences in shock/base drag, friction drag and pressure drag. This is a new model-derived scaling result from simulations, not a wind-tunnel or flight measurement.

Technology: Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR), a progressive symbolic-regression framework for dimensionless physical relations

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A Chain of Symbolic Regression (CoSR) analysis of hypersonic simulation data derived an interpretable non-power-product scaling relation for axial-force coefficient across sharp-cone and blunt-body configurations.
  • Still unknown: The aerodynamic relation was inferred from hypersonic simulation data for two configurations (sharp cone and blunt body), not independently measured flight or wind-tunnel data. It is a parameterized model over the analyzed configuration/data range; external validation and broader geometry coverage are not established in the source. The simpler dominant component is linked to known hypersonic invariance, while the learned viscous correction and geometry-specific scaling provide the paper’s new modeled relationship. Do not treat the authors’ drag-component interpretations as direct measurements.
  • AI role: CoSR progressively searched for interpretable dimensionless groups and combined their relationships instead of fitting one end-to-end expression. Applied to hypersonic simulation data, it identified a dominant angle-of-attack/Mach term and a Reynolds-number-dependent viscous correction, then fitted geometry-specific constants for two body shapes. Earlier canonical-law and fluid examples in the same paper are framework validations and are not counted separately here.
