AlphaDev: reinforcement learning discovers faster sorting routines

Working at the assembly-instruction level, the system found sorting routines up to 70% faster for sequences of 3–5 elements and about 1.7% faster for sequences over 250,000 elements; they were adopted in the LLVM libc++ standard library. A hashing algorithm 30% faster was added to Abseil. The largest gains are for short sequences.

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: AlphaDev found sort 3 and sort 5 routines with fewer instructions than human benchmarks, matched sort 4, and had lower latency on variable sorts. Libc++ versions were up to 70% faster for five elements and about 1.7% faster above 250,000 elements.
  • Still unknown: Hashing result (30% faster) not seen in the text read. Search is at assembly level; the paper reports optimality proof only for sort 3 (17 instructions). Results cover tested CPUs: ARMv8, Intel Skylake, AMD Zen 2.
  • AI role: AlphaDev, an AlphaZero-based reinforcement learning agent with a transformer encoder, built assembly programs instruction by instruction, rewarded for correctness and latency; the team translated results to C++.
