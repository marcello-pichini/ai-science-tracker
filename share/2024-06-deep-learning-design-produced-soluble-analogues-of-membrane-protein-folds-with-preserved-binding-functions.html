Deep-learning design produced soluble analogues of membrane-protein folds with preserved binding functions

A deep-learning pipeline based on inversion of AlphaFold2 and ProteinMPNN designed soluble proteins with claudin, rhomboid-protease and GPCR-like folds. Experiments reported stable, folded soluble designs and structural agreement with target folds. The researchers grafted functional motifs, including a ghrelin-receptor epitope that bound its antibody and active/inactive A2A-receptor-like designs that differed in mini-Gs binding; a binding-site knockout served as a control.

Technology: AF2seq deep-learning backbone design with ProteinMPNN sequence design

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Experiments reported stable, folded soluble designs and structural agreement with target folds.
  • Still unknown: This is a protein-design proof of concept with in-vitro biophysical and binding assays; the analogues are not native membrane receptors and do not demonstrate signaling, drug activity or therapeutic efficacy. The GPCR analogues tested binding to mini-Gs in conformation-designed constructs. The separate live-catalog record on designed proteins modulating dopamine D1 receptor signaling reports a different binder/target and signaling result; this paper reports soluble receptor-fold analogues and binding assays, not D1 signaling modulation.
  • AI role: The deep-learning design pipeline generated soluble analogues of complex membrane-protein folds and the scaffolds into which native functional motifs were grafted. The study tested solubility, thermal stability, structural accuracy and selected binding functions experimentally, including conformation-specific A2A-receptor-like constructs.
