Machine-learning-selected α-MoO3 and ReS2 deliver high measured responsivity in extreme-ultraviolet detectors

A cross-spectral machine-learning model trained on 1,927 visible/UV photodetector records screened materials for extreme-ultraviolet response and selected α-MoO3 and ReS2 for device validation. At the Australian Synchrotron, the α-MoO3 detector showed measured responsivity of about 50–160 A/W depending on photon energy and bias (about 60–70 A/W near 13.5 nm); ReS2 reached about 20 A/W at 91.8 eV and 1 mV, close to the model estimate. The α-MoO3 device retained repeatable current response through 100 bias cycles under high-flux exposure.

Technology: Extremely Randomized Trees cross-spectral response prediction model

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A cross-spectral machine-learning model trained on 1,927 visible/UV photodetector records screened materials for extreme-ultraviolet response and selected α-MoO3 and ReS2 for device validation.
  • Still unknown: The reported measurements are for the fabricated devices and stated synchrotron energies, biases and exposure conditions; they do not establish equivalent responsivity across all EUV wavelengths or device architectures. The paper reports α-MoO3 responsivity ranging about 50–160 A/W across test conditions, while near 13.5 nm it reports about 60–70 A/W; ReS2 was tested at a specified energy/bias. The authors' silicon comparison varies with the comparison conditions and metric (the abstract gives about 225-fold, while the device discussion reports up to about 800-fold); this record therefore states the direct responsivity measurements without treating either ratio as universal. The material screening result is a small experimental validation set, not an exhaustive search.
  • AI role: The model learned relations between material/device descriptors and detector response across wavelength ranges from a 1,927-record dataset of visible and ultraviolet photoresponse measurements. It screened candidate materials for EUV responsivity and selected α-MoO3 and ReS2 for fabrication and synchrotron testing. The AI contribution was transferring response patterns across spectral domains to prioritize materials; experiments then measured the EUV detector performance.
