RetroChimera: predicting small-molecule synthesis

Published in Nature, the method combines a Transformer model (R-SMILES 2) and a template-based graph model (NeuralLoc) through a learned vote. In blind tests with expert chemists, 90% of complete routes were accepted, compared with 20–50% for the component models or a baseline, and nine of ten difficult targets were solved. Evaluation covered only ten targets; one wrong prediction invalidates an entire route.

Technology: RetroChimera (Transformer and template-based model)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Blind tests by PhD-level chemists rated individual steps and complete routes for ten difficult targets: 90% of RetroChimera routes were accepted, and nine of ten targets were solved, versus 20-50% for the component models or baseline.
  • Still unknown: The route-level evaluation covers only ten targets; one incorrect prediction invalidates a route; NeuralLoc is constrained on reactions outside its template library, and R-SMILES 2 may hallucinate.
  • AI role: An ensemble of a Transformer (R-SMILES 2) predicting precursors and a graph neural network (NeuralLoc) selecting templates, combined by a learned rank-dependent vote.

Evidence sources: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/improving-synthesis-prediction-of-small-molecules-at-scale-with-retrochimera/
