Deep learning designs abiotic miniproteins that improve antisense-oligonucleotide nuclear delivery

A generator–predictor–optimizer deep-learning workflow trained on quantitative delivery assays was used to design abiotic Mach miniproteins. Twelve selected predicted constructs were synthesized/tested; the strongest library construct improved phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer (PMO) delivery nearly 20-fold over unconjugated PMO in the standardized HeLa assay, while the authors describe the best designs as up to 50-fold more effective than prior polypeptide-based variants. PMO–Mach3/4 also produced dose-dependent reporter-protein restoration in skeletal muscle, diaphragm and heart in transgenic mice after a single intravenous dose.

Technology: Deep-learning generator–predictor–optimizer workflow: nested-LSTM sequence generator, CNN activity predictor on residue chemical fingerprints, and evolutionary optimization

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: A generator–predictor–optimizer deep-learning workflow trained on quantitative delivery assays was used to design abiotic Mach miniproteins.
  • Still unknown: The paper reports improved delivery in a standardized HeLa reporter assay and transgenic mouse reporter model, not therapeutic correction of a disease. Only selected designs were synthesized (12 from hundreds of predicted sequences); mouse experiments used an EGFP reporter transgene and short seven-day follow-up. The article separately states the library's highest construct was nearly 20-fold over PMO alone and characterizes the designs as 50-fold better than previously known polypeptide variants; these denominators are not interchangeable. Safety evidence is limited to reported assays/doses and short follow-up.
  • AI role: The model generated seed cell-penetrating sequences, predicted quantitative nuclear-delivery activity from peptide fingerprints, and iteratively optimized sequences for higher predicted activity subject to length, charge, similarity and solubility constraints. The team synthesized and experimentally assayed selected model-designed peptides; this linked the AI design to measured enhanced PMO delivery and animal reporter results.
