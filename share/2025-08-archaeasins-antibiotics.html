Archaeasins: deep learning finds antibiotic candidates in the archaeal proteome

The APEX 1.1 deep learning platform scanned 233 archaeal proteomes and flagged 12,623 candidate antimicrobial molecules. Of 80 synthesized candidates, 93% showed in vitro activity against key pathogens, and three selected compounds reduced a drug-resistant bacterium in animal models after a single dose. Candidate and synthesis counts differ between press coverage and secondary reports, and the mouse details come from press coverage rather than the full text, so the paper's own figures should be checked.

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: 80 top-ranked APEX-predicted peptides were synthesized and tested by broth microdilution MIC assays, then in mouse skin-abscess and thigh-infection models (six mice per group).
  • Still unknown: Authors state the model is sequence-based without structural information, pseudo-MIC labels may add noise, screening used only reviewed UniProt sequences, the thigh model did not prevent infection, efficacy declined between days 2 and 4 in the skin model, and only female mice were used.
  • AI role: APEX 1.1, a bacterial-strain-specific antimicrobial activity predictor, scored 193,331,608 peptides from the archaeome and selected candidates for synthesis.
