Machine-learning multi-omics links gut metabolites to GPCR targets and tau changes in Alzheimer’s patient-derived neurons

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A machine-learning and multi-omics framework screened 1.09 million pairs across 408 human GPCRs and 335 gut microbial metabolites. It prioritized orphan receptor GPR84 and the metabolite triacanthine, which activated GPR84 in a cell assay. In Alzheimer’s patient-derived iPSC neurons, agmatine reduced C3AR and tau markers, while phenethylamine reduced phosphorylated and total tau in the reported experiments.

Technology: Extra Trees machine-learning model integrated with molecular docking, multi-omics and Mendelian-randomization analyses

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The ML model ranked predicted metabolite–GPCR associations over a large search space; multi-omics and genetic analyses prioritized Alzheimer’s-related receptors and metabolites. These predictions guided follow-up assays, including triacanthine activation of GPR84 and testing agmatine and phenethylamine in patient-derived neurons.
Test and result
It prioritized orphan receptor GPR84 and the metabolite triacanthine, which activated GPR84 in a cell assay.
Limitation / still unknown
The metabolite–receptor screen is computational and many pairs remain predictions. Receptor activation was tested in a cell assay and tau-marker effects in patient-derived neurons; these experiments do not demonstrate clinical benefit, in-vivo efficacy or that altered tau markers translate to disease modification. Mendelian-randomization results and model scores are not proof of causality.
