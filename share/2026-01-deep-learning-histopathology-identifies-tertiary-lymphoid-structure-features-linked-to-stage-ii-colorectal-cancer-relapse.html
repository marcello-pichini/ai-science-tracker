Deep-learning histopathology identifies tertiary lymphoid structure features linked to stage II colorectal cancer relapse

SurvFinder analyzed 6,950 H&E slides from 1,604 stage II colorectal cancer patients across four cohorts and identified tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) as prognostic tissue features. Its multi-view model integrated TLS morphology and spatial location; explainable analysis highlighted TLS maturity and position near the tumor periphery as features associated with relapse-free survival and adjuvant-chemotherapy outcome patterns across the retrospective datasets.

Technology: SurvFinder, a multi-component deep-learning pipeline: WSINet for prognosis-associated tissue features, SegNet for TLS segmentation/subtyping, and MVNet for multi-view risk prediction

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: SurvFinder analyzed 6,950 H&E slides from 1,604 stage II colorectal cancer patients across four cohorts and identified tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) as prognostic tissue features.
  • Still unknown: This was a retrospective analysis of patients enrolled in 2012–2018; results do not establish prospective clinical utility. Associations between predicted risk, TLS features and adjuvant-chemotherapy outcomes are observational and do not prove treatment benefit or that TLS features causally determine relapse. The authors identify absence of prospective validation and real-world deployment as limitations.
  • AI role: Deep learning autonomously identified prognosis-associated tissue features in whole-slide images, segmented and subtyped TLSs, and integrated morphological and spatial features for risk prediction. Explainable-AI attribution identified TLS location near the tumor margin and TLS maturity state as important prognostic features.

Evidence sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41532433/
