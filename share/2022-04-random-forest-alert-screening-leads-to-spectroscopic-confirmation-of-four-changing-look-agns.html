Random-forest alert screening leads to spectroscopic confirmation of four changing-look AGNs

A hierarchical random-forest classifier in the ALeRCE broker applied to Zwicky Transient Facility alert-stream light curves helped select candidate changing-look active galactic nuclei from objects previously classified as type 2. New spectra confirmed significant changes in broad Balmer emission in four of six observed candidates, establishing new type 2-to-type 1 transitions. The other two observed objects had only tentative evidence and are not counted as confirmed transitions.

Technology: ALeRCE light-curve classifier (hierarchical imbalanced random forest)

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: A hierarchical random-forest classifier in the ALeRCE broker applied to Zwicky Transient Facility alert-stream light curves helped select candidate changing-look active galactic nuclei from objects previously classified as type 2.
  • Still unknown: Only six of the 30 visually retained candidates received new spectra in this paper, selected partly for observability; four showed clear >3σ broad-line changes and two were tentative. Thus the reported ≥66% success rate is a small, selected follow-up sample, not an unbiased prevalence estimate. The authors note possible 30–50% misclassification in the parent type-2 sample and that the ZTF alert threshold makes the method incomplete.
  • AI role: The ALeRCE random-forest classifier used variability features from ZTF light curves and ZTF/AllWISE photometric colors to classify alert sources as AGN-like. Its classifications were cross-matched against a pre-existing spectroscopic type-2 AGN sample to produce candidates with current type-1-like variability; this AI-assisted shortlist enabled follow-up spectroscopy that confirmed the transitions.
