AcRanker-guided discovery of AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 anti-CRISPR proteins

AcRanker, an XGBoost ranking model using amino-acid sequence features, ranked proteins from prophage regions in self-targeting Streptococcus genomes. Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.

Technology: AcRanker, an XGBoost pairwise ranking model trained on amino-acid composition and grouped di- and tri-mer features

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Experimental testing identified two previously unknown type II-A anti-CRISPRs: AcrIIA20 (ML1), which strongly inhibited Streptococcus iniae Cas9 and weakly inhibited S. pyogenes Cas9, and AcrIIA21 (ML8), which inhibited S. pyogenes, S. aureus and S. iniae Cas9 with lower potency.
  • Still unknown: The authors selected 10 candidates for follow-up, successfully purified nine and reported three validated candidates across their experiments, including ML3/AcrIIA12, which had also been identified separately as an LmoCas9 inhibitor during the study. AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21 were roughly tenfold less potent against SpyCas9 than the established AcrIIA4 control; tested potency and host range are assay-specific. The 2025 Evo paper reports distinct generated proteins EvoAcr1–5 and separate assays; shared anti-CRISPR function does not make these identical discoveries.
  • AI role: The model ranked candidate proteins from predicted prophage regions for anti-CRISPR likelihood; the researchers selected candidates from those rankings and biochemically tested them. It directly prioritized the proteins later named AcrIIA20 and AcrIIA21, whose Cas9 inhibition was measured in vitro.
