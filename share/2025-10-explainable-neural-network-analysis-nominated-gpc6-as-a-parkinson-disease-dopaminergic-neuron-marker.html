Explainable neural-network analysis nominated GPC6 as a Parkinson disease dopaminergic-neuron marker

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An explainable machine-learning workflow applied to four post-mortem midbrain single-nucleus RNA-sequencing datasets identified cell-type-specific gene signatures that distinguished Parkinson disease from control cells across datasets (mean balanced accuracy 0.92) and nominated ten candidate genes. GPC6 emerged as a marker in dopaminergic neurons; rare GPC6 variants were enriched among Parkinson disease cases in analyses spanning three case-control cohorts.

Technology: Explainable neural-network classifiers with LIME feature attribution applied to single-nucleus RNA-sequencing data

Editorial relevance 53 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The neural-network classifiers identified cell-type-specific genes important for distinguishing Parkinson disease from control nuclei across four datasets; LIME feature attribution prioritized candidate genes. The researchers then used external rare-variant case-control analyses to validate GPC6 enrichment. The model nominates markers and candidates; it does not establish that GPC6 causes Parkinson disease.
Test and result
GPC6 emerged as a marker in dopaminergic neurons; rare GPC6 variants were enriched among Parkinson disease cases in analyses spanning three case-control cohorts.
Limitation / still unknown
The expression analysis used post-mortem datasets and the rare-variant association is not a functional perturbation or proof of causality. GPC6 remains a candidate marker/association requiring further validation. The reported balanced accuracy is cell-level classifier performance, not a clinical diagnostic test. Source checked; independent replication beyond the reported cohorts was not assessed.
