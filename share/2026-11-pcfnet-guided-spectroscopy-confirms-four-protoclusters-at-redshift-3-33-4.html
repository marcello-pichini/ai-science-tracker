PCFNet-guided spectroscopy confirms four protoclusters at redshift 3.3–3.4

Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution. Twenty-three spectroscopically identified members are largely on the star-forming main sequence. Model-weighted estimates put star-formation-rate density above cosmic averages, while metallicity differences are marginal in two systems and consistent with the field in the others.

Technology: PCFNet point-cloud deep-learning protocluster finder

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Subaru/MOIRCS spectroscopy of four PCFNet-selected candidate regions confirmed four protoclusters at z = 3.316, 3.403, 3.408 and 3.425, across three regions, with galaxy-density peaks more than five times the uniform distribution.
  • Still unknown: This is a small follow-up sample: four of 121 PCFNet candidates were targeted, and the parent catalogue is trained on simulated galaxies. Four protoclusters are confirmed across three of four observed regions; a fourth observed candidate region had no significant redshift peak. One of the confirmed regions had previously been reported as a photometric candidate, so this is a spectroscopic confirmation/characterization result rather than four wholly unprecedented structures. The halo masses are inferred from member stellar masses, not direct lensing measurements. The elevated SFR densities depend on membership-probability weighting and are sensitive to assumed geometry, volume and methodology. Metallicity excesses are only 1.4–2 sigma in two systems; two others are statistically consistent with the field. The authors’ proposed gas-inflow/recycling explanation is interpretive.
  • AI role: PCFNet used broad-band optical photometry and galaxy positions to assign protocluster-membership probabilities and identify candidate regions. The model outputs determined which regions received Subaru/MOIRCS follow-up and prioritized slit targets; their probabilities also informed member selection and the authors’ volume/SFR-density estimator. Spectroscopy then tested those predictions, finding a 40% member detection rate among high-probability galaxies and confirming three of four observed regions.
