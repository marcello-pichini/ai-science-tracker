AI-assisted hippocampal atlas maps Alzheimer-associated cellular changes around amyloid plaques

In a six-donor postmortem hippocampal study, neural cell segmentation and Spatial-ID annotation helped map Alzheimer-associated cell distributions and gene-expression changes. Immunofluorescence supported the inferred accumulation patterns of microglia and astrocytes around amyloid-beta plaques. These are observational associations, not causal effects or clinical benefit. This record describes the December 7, 2023 preprint; the later Neuron paper was published online March 31, 2025 with an expanded cohort, whose counts and final methods are not pooled here.

Technology: StereoCell/ESPANet cell segmentation; Spatial-ID deep neural network and graph convolutional network

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Spatial transcriptomics and single-nucleus sequencing in three AD and three control donor brains; immunofluorescence checked the plaque-associated glial distribution pattern within the same study.
  • Still unknown: Postmortem observational results do not establish disease causation. No independent replication was assessed. Preprint methods retain an unfinished training-epoch placeholder. The reviewed claims and AI architectures refer to the primary preprint, not authenticated final journal methods; candidate blood biomarkers and diagnostic efficacy are outside this record's represented claim.
  • AI role: Trained neural segmentation delineated cells; Spatial-ID used a DNN and GCN to assign spatial cell types, enabling plaque-associated cell-distribution analysis.
