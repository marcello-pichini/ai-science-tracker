Deep learning extends the mapped extent of a North Anatolian Fault slow-slip event

A deep convolutional autoencoder trained on synthetic deformation and atmospheric-noise time series recovered millimeter-scale ground motion from real InSAR without being given the fault location. Applied to COSMO-SkyMed observations of the 2013 North Anatolian Fault slow-slip event, it detected about 2 mm of slip beyond the previously identified segment and revised the estimated along-strike event length from about 10 km to 15–20 km. The same paper used the approach to resolve transient millimeter-scale subsidence at California’s Coso geothermal field. This is one paper-level finding enabled by the model’s extraction of deformation signals from noisy satellite data.

Technology: Deep convolutional autoencoder trained on synthetic InSAR time series

  • Tested: A deep convolutional autoencoder trained on synthetic deformation and atmospheric-noise time series recovered millimeter-scale ground motion from real InSAR without being given the fault location.
  • Still unknown: The newly resolved extension is a reanalysis of an existing 2013 InSAR event, not a newly occurring earthquake. The paper’s reported extension is model-derived; the original expert analysis had detected the larger slow-slip segment, while the autoencoder identifies an additional lower-amplitude portion. The model assumes atmospheric noise characteristics represented by its synthetic training data and omits some noise sources, including ionospheric perturbations. Coso results are a separate application within the same paper and are not counted separately.
  • AI role: The deep autoencoder separated persistent ground deformation patterns from atmospheric noise in real InSAR data without prior fault-location information, resolving the additional 2 mm slow-slip segment; the model was trained on synthetic data and was not fine-tuned on the real case.
