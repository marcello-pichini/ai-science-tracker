Single-cell imaging and machine learning reveal a Cdk2–COP9 interaction that induces a senescence-like immune state in Drosophila cells

A synthetic genetic-interaction and cell-morphology map covering more than 6,800 Drosophila genes supported machine-learning-based gene-function assignments across 47 modules. In follow-up, Cytoclass single-cell classification revealed a state-specific interaction between Cdk2 and COP9 signalosome knockdown: co-depletion arrested cell-cycle progression and produced a senescence-like phenotype with increased antimicrobial-peptide expression in cultured cells and hemocytes, including without bacterial infection.

Technology: Single-cell morphology classifier ensemble and machine-learning analysis of genetic-interaction profiles (Cytoclass; support-vector machines and random forest)

  • AI role: The authors generated single-cell morphology and synthetic genetic-interaction profiles, used machine-learning-based function discovery to assign functions to genes in 47 modules, and applied Cytoclass state classification to reveal interactions hidden in bulk-average profiles. The state-specific Cdk2–COP9 interaction was followed up by double-RNAi assays, single-cell transcriptomics, beta-galactosidase and antimicrobial-peptide measurements.
