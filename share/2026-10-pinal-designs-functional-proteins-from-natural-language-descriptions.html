Pinal designs functional proteins from natural-language descriptions

The 16-billion-parameter Pinal model generated protein candidates from plain-language function descriptions. In four reported design tasks—fluorescent protein, PET hydrolase, alcohol dehydrogenase and metabolic H-protein—the selected designs displayed the intended function in laboratory tests; the best H-protein raised product titre 1.7-fold relative to native E. coli H-protein in a reconstituted CO2-fixation pathway.

Technology: Pinal, a 16-billion-parameter protein foundation model with text-to-structure (T2struct) and text-conditioned sequence design (SaProt-T) components

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In four reported design tasks—fluorescent protein, PET hydrolase, alcohol dehydrogenase and metabolic H-protein—the selected designs displayed the intended function in laboratory tests; the best H-protein raised product titre 1.7-fold relative to native E. coli H-protein in a reconstituted CO2-fixation pathway.
  • Still unknown: This is a primary-paper proof of concept with selected designs, not a broad hit-rate or industrial-performance demonstration. Experimental denominators vary substantially by task; extended data reports one active PETase among 94 screened and a fluorescent design selected from 29. The H-protein titre comparison was in a reconstituted pathway, not an organism or commercial process. No independent replication or therapeutic result is claimed.
  • AI role: Pinal maps natural-language function descriptions to candidate protein structures and sequences. The team filtered and ranked model outputs, selected candidates for synthesis, and tested intended functions. The paper reports lab-observed fluorescence, PET-hydrolysis/esterase activity, alcohol-dehydrogenase catalysis and H-protein activity; the latter improved titre in a reconstituted multi-enzyme pathway.
