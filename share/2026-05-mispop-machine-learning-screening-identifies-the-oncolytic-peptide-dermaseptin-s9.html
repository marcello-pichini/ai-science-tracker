MISPOP machine learning screening identifies the oncolytic peptide Dermaseptin-S9

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The MISPOP ensemble screened a natural peptide library of 1,033 sequences, prioritized 16 oncolytic-peptide candidates and selected five for synthesis and testing. Dermaseptin-S9 showed membrane disruption and immunogenic-cell-death readouts in vitro and over 92% tumor-growth inhibition in a B16F10 melanoma mouse model.

Technology: MISPOP mechanism-informed ensemble combining XGBoost, deep neural networks and transfer learning with physicochemical priors

Editorial relevance 60 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The machine-learning ensemble integrated three predictors and mechanism-related physicochemical features to prioritize candidate oncolytic peptides from a natural sequence library. Five shortlisted peptides were synthesized and evaluated; Dermaseptin-S9 was tested in cell-based membrane-disruption/ICD assays and a mouse melanoma model.
Test and result
The MISPOP ensemble screened a natural peptide library of 1,033 sequences, prioritized 16 oncolytic-peptide candidates and selected five for synthesis and testing.
Limitation / still unknown
The active peptide is a natural sequence rather than a new AI-designed molecule. Findings are preclinical: three tumor cell lines and a B16F10 mouse melanoma model (n=5 per group shown in article figures); they do not establish human efficacy or absence of toxicity beyond the tested model/doses. Only five of 16 model-prioritized candidates were synthesized and evaluated.
