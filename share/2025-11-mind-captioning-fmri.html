Mind captioning: text descriptions of viewed videos decoded from fMRI

A language-model pipeline mapped fMRI activity from six participants to semantic signatures of 2,180 captioned videos and generated text matching them. The generated descriptions identified the correct video among 100 candidates about 50% of the time, against a chance level of about 1%, and the method worked for recalled clips and without the brain's language areas. Caveats: small participant group, needs extensive per-person data and cooperative subjects, and the authors note it cannot read private thoughts.

Technology: Deep language model semantic-feature decoder with text generation search

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: Descriptions generated from brain activity induced by videos captured the viewed content and generalized to recalled content, even without relying on the canonical language network.
  • Still unknown: Use with people who have aphasia is described as a potential, not tested in the abstract.
  • AI role: Linear decoders map brain activity to semantic features from a deep language model; candidate text is optimized to match them.
