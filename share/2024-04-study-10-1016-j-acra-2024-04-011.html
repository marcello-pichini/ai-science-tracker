25 Apr 2024National Cancer Institute; National Institutes of Health

Automated prostate-extension grading shows a sensitivity–specificity tradeoff against pathology

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A cascaded segmentation and random-forest pipeline was evaluated against surgical pathology in a retrospective prostate-cancer cohort. In 127 held-out patients, one threshold produced lower sensitivity but higher specificity than the radiologist comparison. This is a bounded clinical-validation finding, without established superiority, improved surgical decisions or independent replication of the reused segmentation model.

Technology: Reused 3D U-Net/residual segmentation pipeline with random-forest extraprostatic-extension grading

Editorial relevance 33 biology Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned segmentation extracted lesion/capsule features and a trained random forest mapped them to extension grades; comparison with actual surgical pathology supplies the distinct clinical-validation result. This extends a previously developed segmentation workflow rather than constituting independent replication of it.
Test and result
The source split 634 prostatectomy patients into 507 development and 127 test patients. Fixed segmentation models produced prostate/lesion masks; geometric features trained random forests with repeated cross-validation. At EPE≥1, test sensitivity/specificity were 0.67/0.73 versus 0.81/0.62 for the radiologist, using pathology as reference. Four-grade balanced accuracy was limited, and individual grade AUCs ranged 0.55–0.70.
Limitation / still unknown
This retrospective analysis used prospectively recorded radiologist assessments, without prospective AI-model locking. Development grades partly incorporated pathology, while comparison grades came from the radiologist. Reused segmentation models and overlapping institutional recruitment periods mean the full pipeline is not established as wholly unfamiliar with test patients. No paired clinical reader-assistance or treatment-benefit trial was performed, and no general superiority is claimed.
