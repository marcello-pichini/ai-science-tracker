Satellite model maps collapse and surviving Acropora corals in Belize

A supervised Random Tree classifier on satellite images predicted a drop of over 90% in live Acropora cover at Belize from 2011 to 2023 across 25 km². Field searches in June 2025 found live or recently dead colonies at 28 of 42 predicted sites and live colonies at 16 previously undocumented locations. The field check was a search for survivors at sites the model flagged, not a blind test.

Technology: Random Tree supervised classification of satellite imagery (machine learning)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A Random Tree classifier's predicted over-90% decline in Acropora cover (2011-2023) was compared with field data from 2012-2025, and field searches at 42 predicted sites found live or recently dead colonies at 28 of them.
  • Still unknown: The abstract states no limitations; the search was a targeted check of flagged sites rather than a blind test, and 14 of 42 predicted sites had no colonies found.
  • AI role: A supervised Random Tree classifier identified Acropora in satellite images and generated the cover predictions that guided the field searches.
