AlphaQubit: a neural decoder for quantum error correction

A Transformer-based decoder trained on data from a 49-qubit Sycamore processor made 6% fewer errors than tensor-network methods and 30% fewer than correlated matching in the largest experiments. It is too slow for real-time error correction on fast superconducting processors.

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: On Sycamore data, logical error per round was 2.901e-2 (distance 3) and 2.748e-2 (distance 5), versus 3.028e-2 and 2.915e-2 for a tensor-network decoder. Simulations to distance 11 (241 qubits); correlated matching was 1.25 to 1.4 times worse.
  • Still unknown: Throughput is slower than the 1 microsecond per round target. Training at distance 11 needs more data. Accuracy beyond distance 11 and logical computations remain future work. Larger-distance results are simulated.
  • AI role: A recurrent transformer neural network decodes surface-code syndromes to predict logical errors; it is the method under test.
