Deep learning enables multi-site bone marrow adiposity genetics in UK Biobank

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Deep learning measured bone marrow fat fraction at four skeletal sites in UK Biobank MRI data; after quality control, 48,608 participants contributed to genome-wide analyses. In the white-participant meta-GWAS, the study reported 67, 147, 134 and 174 independent significant variants for femoral head, total hip, femoral diaphysis and spine, mapping to 54, 90, 43 and 100 genes, respectively. Analyses also identified site- and sex-specific genetic associations and added signals in multi-ancestry analyses.

Technology: Previously validated deep-learning models for MRI segmentation and bone marrow fat-fraction measurement

AI contribution
The study applied deep-learning models to segment four skeletal regions and derive bone marrow fat-fraction phenotypes at UK Biobank scale. Those AI-derived measurements were the phenotypes used in the study's subsequent genetic association analyses; the paper's genetic findings are not themselves outputs of a generative model.
Test and result
Deep learning measured bone marrow fat fraction at four skeletal sites in UK Biobank MRI data; after quality control, 48,608 participants contributed to genome-wide analyses.
Limitation / still unknown
The GWAS, transcriptome-wide and colocalization results are statistical associations and candidate gene mappings, not proof of causal mechanisms. Ancestry subgroup sizes differ, and results do not establish clinical utility or disease prevention. The deep-learning measurement method was established in an earlier study; this record concerns the distinct population-scale phenotype and genetic findings. A 2025 author correction fixed a misspelling of AKNA as ANKA; it did not alter the reported analysis or findings.
