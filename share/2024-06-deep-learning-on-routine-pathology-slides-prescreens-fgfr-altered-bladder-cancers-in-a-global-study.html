Deep learning on routine pathology slides prescreens FGFR-altered bladder cancers in a global study

A deep-learning device analyzed routine H&E whole-slide images to estimate whether advanced urothelial tumors carried selected FGFR2/FGFR3 alterations used for erdafitinib eligibility. In a separate 350-slide retrospective validation set, the device achieved 88.7% sensitivity and 31.8% specificity; it was also prospectively integrated into a non-interventional study spanning 89 sites in nine countries. The paper projected a 28.7% reduction in molecular tests assuming 15% biomarker prevalence; that estimate was simulated, not a measured population-level reduction.

Technology: FGFR Device, a multi-instance-learning attention model with a convolutional neural-network backbone

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: prospective or independent check
  • Tested: In a separate 350-slide retrospective validation set, the device achieved 88.7% sensitivity and 31.8% specificity; it was also prospectively integrated into a non-interventional study spanning 89 sites in nine countries.
  • Still unknown: The 350-slide retrospective validation set was deliberately enriched (150 FGFR-positive and 200 negative; 43% prevalence), and the device fell slightly short of the prespecified 90% sensitivity target. Prospective real-time workflow validation covered only 17 samples in one month (15 predictions; two quality-control failures); production deployment reported 24 samples, with only 20 receiving molecular testing. The 28.7% test reduction is a simulation assuming 15% prevalence, not an observed clinical outcome. This is prescreening support, not a stand-alone diagnosis or evidence of patient benefit.
  • AI role: Trained on more than 3,000 urothelial-cancer slides with molecular test labels, the deep-learning model inferred selected FGFR alteration status from H&E images and returned a likelihood for prescreening before confirmatory molecular testing. The device was prospectively integrated into a non-interventional study workflow; molecular tests remained the reference standard.
