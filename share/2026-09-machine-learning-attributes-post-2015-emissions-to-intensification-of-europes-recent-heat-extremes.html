Machine learning attributes post-2015 emissions to intensification of Europe’s recent heat extremes

Stanford University; Boston University; University of Auckland; Colorado State University · 22 Sep 2026 · climate

Machine learning attributes post-2015 emissions to intensification of Europe’s recent heat extremes

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology. It found >95% probability that anthropogenic emissions since the 2015 Paris Agreement intensified Europe’s summer temperature extremes in 2021–2025; for the June 2025 heatwave, regional mean temperature is estimated to have been 0.34 °C lower under 2015 cumulative emissions, with >99% probability.

Technology: Generative diffusion model for spatially explicit temperature fields

Editorial relevance 53 climate Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The model learned climate-model relationships between meteorological conditions, cumulative CO₂ emissions and daily temperature fields, then generated conditional counterfactual temperature distributions to attribute the incremental post-2015 emissions effect to recent European heat extremes.
Test and result
A diffusion model trained on ten CMIP6 climate models estimated event-specific temperature changes conditional on observed meteorology.
Limitation / still unknown
This event-specific attribution is conditioned on observed large-scale meteorology and trained on ten CMIP6 models; it is not a direct experiment that removed emissions. The paper emphasizes uncertainty from internal variability/model spread and reports robust regional-mean effects, while grid-cell-level changes are more limited. The June 2025 value is a median modeled counterfactual estimate.
