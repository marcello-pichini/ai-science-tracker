WeatherNext Cyclones: forecasting cyclones one day earlier

Three-day forecasts match what earlier models achieved at two days: about 100 km trajectory error and 11 knots intensity error, compared with ECMWF-ENS and HWRF. The work is with the National Hurricane Center, CIRA, and the Met Office. The comparison uses historical 2023–2024 cyclones; why results are good at 28 km remains an open question.

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Historical cyclones from 2023-2024 were benchmarked deterministically and probabilistically against ECMWF-ENS (track) and HWRF (intensity): three-day errors matched earlier two-day forecasts; a 2025 storm is cited only as an anecdote.
  • Still unknown: Why the model is accurate at 28 km resolution is an open question; the 'extra day' is an average dependent on the chosen benchmarks, and the evaluation window is slightly unclear.
  • AI role: A single model using Functional Generative Networks produced track, intensity and wind-structure ensembles of up to 1,000 members.
