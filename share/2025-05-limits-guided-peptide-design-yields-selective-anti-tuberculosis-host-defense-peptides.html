LIMITS-guided peptide design yields selective anti-tuberculosis host-defense peptides

The Penn State/University of Minnesota team used the Lead Informed Machine Interrogation of Therapeutic Sequences (LIMITS) workflow to train random-forest and neural-network predictors on approximately 100 host-defense peptides, using lead mutational scans and biophysical sequence features. Experimentally tested neural-network-designed MAD5.1 and MAD5.3 showed reported MICs of 5 μM against M. tuberculosis H37Rv, selectivity indices >16 against an eight-organism non-mycobacterial panel, and mammalian-cell toxicity indices >100. The study identifies peptide length as a potentially underappreciated activity determinant.

Technology: LIMITS (Lead Informed Machine Interrogation of Therapeutic Sequences), using random-forest and feed-forward neural-network predictors

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Experimentally tested neural-network-designed MAD5.1 and MAD5.3 showed reported MICs of 5 μM against M. tuberculosis H37Rv, selectivity indices >16 against an eight-organism non-mycobacterial panel, and mammalian-cell toxicity indices >100.
  • Still unknown: This is preclinical in-vitro peptide evidence, not animal efficacy or clinical treatment. The paper reports that MAD5.3 did not produce a statistically significant reduction in H37Rv viability after seven days of culture despite its MIC and selectivity/toxicity metrics; do not generalize the headline activity to every candidate or assay. MAD2.2 was moderately hemolytic. The 5 μM H37Rv MIC and high indices apply to selected peptides and stated assay conditions.
  • AI role: Lead-candidate mutational scanning informed feature selection and training on a limited ~100-peptide dataset; the models iteratively selected candidate anti-TB peptide sequences for synthesis and bacteriological testing. NN-predicted MAD5 sequences were assayed against virulent and avirulent M. tuberculosis strains and counter-screened against other bacteria and mammalian cells.
