Computer-vision and metabolomics fusion links paired serum–urine flux mismatch to early diabetic kidney disease

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: In a two-center cohort of 364 participants with type 2 diabetes and controls, targeted serum/urine metabolite measurements found a high-serum/low-urine pattern for indoxyl sulfate and related metabolites in early diabetic kidney disease (DKD), consistent with reduced tubular secretion. Computer vision quantified urine foam stability and chromaticity; Random Forest recursive feature elimination selected these image features together with metabolite ratios and clinical variables for the FluxPro-DKD classifier. It distinguished DKD from diabetes without kidney disease with AUC 0.90 in discovery and 0.85 in a geographically distinct external cohort of 82.

Technology: Computer-vision urine phenotyping and Random Forest recursive feature elimination (RF-RFE) for multimodal feature selection

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Computer vision quantified urine foam half-life and chromaticity from standardized sample images. RF-RFE selected eight predictors, including digital urine features and serum-to-urine metabolite ratios, for the fusion classifier distinguishing early DKD from type 2 diabetes without DKD. The paper reports external cohort evaluation. The biochemical interpretation of flux mismatch as reduced tubular secretory capacity is based on measured metabolite ratios and literature-supported interpretation; transporter expression was not measured.
Test and result
In a two-center cohort of 364 participants with type 2 diabetes and controls, targeted serum/urine metabolite measurements found a high-serum/low-urine pattern for indoxyl sulfate and related metabolites in early diabetic kidney disease (DKD), consistent with reduced tubular secretion.
Limitation / still unknown
The external cohort validation was cross-sectional; the paper says longitudinal follow-up for hard kidney outcomes is ongoing. Its projected three-year event rates are theoretical Monte Carlo simulations based on cross-sectional data and are not observed outcomes. The study did not measure proximal-tubule transporter expression or compare directly against KIM-1 or NGAL, and generalization beyond the two Chinese centers and population requires further validation. Metabolite flux supports, but does not prove, tubular transport dysfunction.
