CogSim helps predict fusion ignition at the NIF

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Neural networks, Bayesian inference and physics simulations helped assess, before the experiment, the NIF design that produced 3.15 MJ of fusion energy from 2.05 MJ of laser energy delivered to the target on December 5, 2022. Experts designed the experiment; AI estimated its outcome and uncertainties. This is not the energy balance of the entire facility; the study was later published in Science in 2025.

Technology: CogSim (neural networks, transfer learning and Bayesian inference)

Editorial relevance 73 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A generative model combining simulations, deep learning, experimental data and Bayesian statistics estimated the outcome and uncertainties of the NIF design before the shot.
Test and result
The CogSim team predicted the December 5, 2022 shot outcome before the experiment (about 50.2% chance of exceeding break-even), and the result of 3.15 MJ from 2.05 MJ matched the prediction after peer review and independent diagnostics.
Limitation / still unknown
The page says the approach does not guarantee success and that techniques still have much to prove; one good prediction does not validate a model, and testing on more experiments was planned for 2023.
