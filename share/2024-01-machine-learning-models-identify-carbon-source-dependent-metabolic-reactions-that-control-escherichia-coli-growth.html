Machine-learning models identify carbon-source-dependent metabolic reactions that control Escherichia coli growth

Elastic-net and multilayer-perceptron models integrated genome-wide gene-deletion data and simulated fluxes to predict beneficial and detrimental reactions for E. coli growth on 30 carbon sources. Beyond training-set examples, the authors experimentally validated model predictions about the glyoxylate shunt, pyruvate dehydrogenase, and redundant purine-biosynthesis reactions. The findings showed that effects of energy-generating pathways depend on carbon source, while biosynthetic pathways were more generally growth-promoting across the tested conditions.

Technology: Elastic-net regression and multilayer-perceptron models

  • Tested: Beyond training-set examples, the authors experimentally validated model predictions about the glyoxylate shunt, pyruvate dehydrogenase, and redundant purine-biosynthesis reactions.
  • Still unknown: Reported reaction effects are for E. coli under the tested carbon-source and genetic-perturbation conditions; they do not establish universal metabolic-control rules.   • AI role: The models combined gene-deletion phenotypes with simulated metabolic fluxes to prioritize reactions whose perturbation would benefit or harm growth across carbon conditions; selected out-of-training-set predictions were experimentally tested.
