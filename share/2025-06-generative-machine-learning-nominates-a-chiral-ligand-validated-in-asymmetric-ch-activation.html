Generative machine learning nominates a chiral ligand validated in asymmetric C–H activation

A transfer-learned chemical language model generated novel chiral amino-acid ligands for palladium-catalyzed asymmetric β-C(sp³)–H activation, while an ensemble of 30 fine-tuned deep-learning regressors predicted reaction enantioselectivity. From 490 generated ligand candidates, mechanistic and synthetic filters retained 73; the authors tested selected reactions prospectively without disclosing the model-predicted enantioselectivity to the experimentalists. One highlighted reaction using generated ligand L5 gave 94% ee, matching the model prediction of 94%.

Technology: Transfer-learned ULMFiT chemical language model (FnG) for ligand generation and a 30-model deep-learning ensemble (EnP) for enantioselectivity prediction

The selected reaction gave 94% ee, but the accessible text table names it L5 and summarizes substrate/coupling partner graphically rather than in searchable prose.
  • AI role: FnG generated candidate chiral ligands from a model fine-tuned on known ligands, while EnP ranked reactions formed by pairing generated ligands with known substrates/coupling partners. The researchers selected a small set for blinded prospective wet-lab testing; the paper reports one highlighted new ligand/reaction at 94% ee, matching prediction.
