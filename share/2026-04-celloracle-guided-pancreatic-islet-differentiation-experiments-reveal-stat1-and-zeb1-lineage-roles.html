CellOracle-guided pancreatic islet differentiation experiments reveal STAT1 and ZEB1 lineage roles

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A digital model built from 400,603 cells across 61 single-cell datasets used CellOracle gene-regulatory-network inference and in-silico transcription-factor perturbations to nominate regulators of stem-cell-derived human islet differentiation. CRISPRi experiments supported a previously unreported role for STAT1 in exocrine specification: transient STAT1 knockdown reduced exocrine markers, and JAK–STAT inhibition preserved beta-cell markers and improved glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. CRISPRi also supported a stage-dependent role for ZEB1, whose early knockdown impaired endocrine specification and later knockdown reduced off-target serotonergic cells while increasing beta-cell proportion. The date is the first preprint posting; this entry summarizes the later version reviewed in the research record, which reports the specific STAT1 and ZEB1 experiments.

Technology: CellOracle v0.12.0 within a digital model integrating single-cell transcriptomic and chromatin-accessibility data

AI contribution
The integrated cell-state model inferred regulatory networks and performed in-silico transcription-factor knockout simulations to identify candidate lineage regulators and intervention windows. These model predictions guided subsequent CRISPRi and pharmacologic perturbations in stem-cell-derived islet differentiation cultures.

It integrates existing datasets and reports laboratory validation in stem-cell-derived islet cultures; it does not establish human therapeutic benefit or mature clinical transplantation outcomes. Regulatory-model predictions remain context- and stage-dependent.
