Machine learning reveals a nitrogen-assimilation strategy in Bacteroides

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A microbiome metabolomics study used random-forest models to associate bacterial metabolite profiles with taxonomy and found a previously undescribed nitrogen-assimilation pattern in Bacteroides/Bacteroidetes. Follow-up growth experiments tested 60 Bacteroidetes strains with asparagine or glutamine as the sole nitrogen source and supported the predicted phenotype; the study also reports a distinct reduced-sulfur utilization result in B. thetaiotaomicron.

Technology: Random-forest models over metabolomics profiles

Editorial relevance 60 biology Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The models identified metabolite features that distinguished taxa and highlighted a previously undescribed Bacteroides metabolic phenotype; researchers then tested bacterial growth under defined nitrogen sources. Comparative-genomics pathways remained candidate explanations rather than proven pathways.
Test and result
Follow-up growth experiments tested 60 Bacteroidetes strains with asparagine or glutamine as the sole nitrogen source and supported the predicted phenotype; the study also reports a distinct reduced-sulfur utilization result in B. thetaiotaomicron.
Limitation / still unknown
The study's abstract reports machine-learning discovery of a new metabolism and the figure captions document follow-up culture tests; the article's full text is paywalled in this session, so the exact mechanistic pathway is not asserted. Mouse and culture evidence do not establish a human health effect.
