Physics-based differentiable imaging detects 51 Eridani b in a JWST band where standard processing did not

Archived from discovery counts: the lead researchers explicitly state that FLIP did not use AI. Its automatic-differentiation tools optimize a physics-based instrument model. A physics-based coronagraph model optimized with automatic differentiation and gradient descent recovered 51 Eridani b in JWST/NIRCam F430M images at 4.3 μm, where the published standard-processing result was a non-detection. The paper also reports two- to fifteen-fold sensitivity gains across its test observations.

Technology: FLIP (Physics-based Learning for Imaging Planets), a differentiable instrument model optimized with automatic differentiation

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A physics-based coronagraph model optimized with automatic differentiation and gradient descent recovered 51 Eridani b in JWST/NIRCam F430M images at 4.3 μm, where the published standard-processing result was a non-detection.
  • Still unknown: The planet 51 Eridani b was already discovered by ground-based imaging in 2015; the new finding is its first JWST image in the 4.3 μm carbon-dioxide band, not discovery of a previously unknown planet. Performance estimates are based on a limited set of three target systems and should not be generalized to all JWST data.
  • AI role: The researchers state that this work did not use AI, although it used computational tools also used to train AI models. FLIP optimized a physics-based telescope model using automatic differentiation.
