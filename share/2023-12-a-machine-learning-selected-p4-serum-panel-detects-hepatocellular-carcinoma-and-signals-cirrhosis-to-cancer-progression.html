A machine-learning-selected P4 serum panel detects hepatocellular carcinoma and signals cirrhosis-to-cancer progression

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A staged serum-proteomics study across 1,002 individuals used random-forest machine learning to select a four-marker panel, P4 (HABP2, CD163, AFP and PIVKA-II). In an independent validation set, the panel distinguished hepatocellular carcinoma from cirrhosis (AUC 0.979; sensitivity 0.925; specificity 0.915) and from healthy controls (AUC 0.992). In a prospective external cohort of cirrhosis patients followed for HCC, it predicted conversion with AUC 0.890 and median lead time of 11.4 months before imaging diagnosis.

Technology: Random-forest machine-learning model for serum-protein panel selection and HCC classification

AI contribution
The random-forest model evaluated serum protein panels and selected the four-marker P4 panel; researchers then tested classification in an independent validation set and assessed prediction of cirrhosis-to-HCC conversion in prospective follow-up data.
Test and result
In an independent validation set, the panel distinguished hepatocellular carcinoma from cirrhosis (AUC 0.979; sensitivity 0.925; specificity 0.915) and from healthy controls (AUC 0.992). The prospectively collected cirrhosis cohort was split into 177 training and 76 validation participants for predictive-model construction; collection timing alone does not establish a prospective locked-model evaluation.
Limitation / still unknown
The prospective cohort included 253 cirrhosis patients, with 36 developing HCC; 8 of 65 participants were false positives for conversion by the reported endpoint. This is source-reported research performance, not proof of improved survival or a clinically implemented screening test. The lead time is measured relative to imaging diagnosis in the study cohorts.
