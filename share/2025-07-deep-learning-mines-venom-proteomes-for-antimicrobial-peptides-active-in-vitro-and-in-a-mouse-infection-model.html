Deep learning mines venom proteomes for antimicrobial peptides active in vitro and in a mouse infection model

The APEX deep-learning model screened 16,123 venom proteins by generating and scoring 40,626,260 venom-encrypted peptides. After activity prediction and filtering for low similarity to known antimicrobial peptides, the authors selected 58 for experiments; 53 showed antimicrobial activity against at least one tested pathogen. Three selected peptides reduced bacterial counts after topical treatment in a mouse Acinetobacter baumannii skin-abscess model; the wolf-spider-derived Arachnoserver-5 peptide produced a reported three-log reduction versus untreated controls through day four, without significant body-weight change under the study conditions.

Technology: APEX deep-learning model for bacterial strain-specific minimum inhibitory concentration prediction

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: After activity prediction and filtering for low similarity to known antimicrobial peptides, the authors selected 58 for experiments; 53 showed antimicrobial activity against at least one tested pathogen.
  • Still unknown: Only 58 of the 386 low-similarity candidates were experimentally tested, and activity was measured against a defined panel of 11 strains; some tested peptides were cytotoxic. The mouse study was a topical single-dose skin-abscess model with six animals per group, not systemic treatment or human efficacy. The paper notes possible ion-channel activity for many candidates and calls for follow-up safety testing. A related APEX study mined archaeal proteomes (10.1038/s41564-025-02061-0); this entry is limited to the separate venom-protein source library and its distinct peptide set and experiments.
  • AI role: APEX scored 40.6 million candidate peptide fragments generated from venom proteins for predicted antimicrobial activity. The authors filtered predicted hits for sequence dissimilarity to known antimicrobial peptides, synthesized a selected subset, and experimentally tested antimicrobial activity, cytotoxicity and selected animal-model efficacy. The AI model narrowed an otherwise very large peptide search space; assays established which selected candidates were active.
