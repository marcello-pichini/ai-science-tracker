Machine-learning screening yields novel potent acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase inhibitors

NCATS researchers trained machine-learning QSAR models and applied them to a roughly 360,000-compound library. Experimental colorimetric and orthogonal fluorescence assays confirmed new AChE/BChE inhibitors; the study reports 22 AChE and 67 BChE inhibitors with IC50 below 5 μM, including novel structural series, with some submicromolar compounds.

Technology: Ensemble comparison of machine-learning QSAR classifiers (including XGBoost, random forest, SVM, neural network and naïve Bayes)

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Experimental colorimetric and orthogonal fluorescence assays confirmed new AChE/BChE inhibitors; the study reports 22 AChE and 67 BChE inhibitors with IC50 below 5 μM, including novel structural series, with some submicromolar compounds.
  • Still unknown: These are biochemical inhibitor assays and early medicinal-chemistry leads; they do not establish brain exposure, in-vivo efficacy, safety or clinical benefit. Several known compounds were newly identified as inhibitors, so the count of all assay hits should not be described as entirely novel molecules.
  • AI role: Models trained on quantitative high-throughput screening data prioritized compounds in a ~360,000-compound collection for AChE/BChE inhibition and selectivity assays. Researchers tested 2,762 high-scoring compounds in colorimetric assays and used an orthogonal fluorescence assay to confirm predicted inhibitors; subsequent structure–activity analysis identified novel scaffolds.
