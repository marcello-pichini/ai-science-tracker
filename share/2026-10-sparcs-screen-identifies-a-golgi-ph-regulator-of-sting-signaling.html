SPARCS screen identifies a Golgi-pH regulator of STING signaling

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A genome-wide imaging and CRISPR screen of about 70 million cells identified GPR89A and GPR89B (encoding GPHR) as important for maintaining Golgi acidity and early STING trafficking and activation. The study also found a previously undescribed autophagy phenotype associated with EI24 and linked it to altered autophagosome formation.

Technology: SPARCS platform with machine-learning image analysis

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Machine-learning image analysis classified phenotypes in genome-wide CRISPR screens; SPARCS recovered known autophagy genes and surfaced EI24 and GPHR-linked findings. Follow-up molecular profiling and experiments by the researchers examined these hits.
Test and result
A genome-wide imaging and CRISPR screen of about 70 million cells identified GPR89A and GPR89B (encoding GPHR) as important for maintaining Golgi acidity and early STING trafficking and activation.
Limitation / still unknown
The study used pools of at least 500 excised cells for sequencing, so it could not map individual images to individual guide RNAs. The paper describes GPHR as a regulator of STING trafficking and signaling; therapeutic implications remain untested.
