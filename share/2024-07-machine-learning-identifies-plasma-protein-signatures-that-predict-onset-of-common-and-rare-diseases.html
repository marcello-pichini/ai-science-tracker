Machine learning identifies plasma protein signatures that predict onset of common and rare diseases

Researchers used regularized machine-learning models to select sparse plasma-protein signatures for 10-year incidence across 218 diseases in 41,931 UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project participants. Five to twenty proteins improved held-out risk prediction over basic clinical information for 67 diseases and over clinical information plus routine assays for 52. The study identified TNFRSF17 as a specific predictor for future multiple myeloma and TNFRSF13B for monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance. In bone-marrow single-cell data from 11 newly diagnosed myeloma patients and three controls, four of five myeloma predictor proteins were most highly expressed by plasma cells; FCRLB and QPCT elevation was attributable to malignant plasma cells. Proteomic signatures also replicated improved prediction for five of six diseases tested in EPIC-Norfolk.

Technology: LASSO feature selection and regularized Cox machine-learning models integrating plasma proteomics and clinical covariates

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Five to twenty proteins improved held-out risk prediction over basic clinical information for 67 diseases and over clinical information plus routine assays for 52.
  • Still unknown: The disease predictions are observational risk associations, not causal mechanisms or clinical screening outcomes. Most protein signatures were not externally replicated; EPIC-Norfolk tested six diseases, with improved prediction replicated for five. The myeloma single-cell analysis involved 11 patients and three healthy controls and supports cellular expression attribution, not prospective validation of an assay. Absolute quantification with clinical-grade assays and further validation would be needed before clinical use.
  • AI role: The authors applied a three-stage machine-learning framework—feature selection, hyperparameter tuning and held-out validation—to ~2,923 proteins in UK Biobank. It selected parsimonious protein signatures for 218 future disease outcomes and identified disease-specific predictors and shared predictors. For multiple myeloma, the model-selected five-protein signature and follow-up single-cell expression analysis linked four predictors to marrow plasma cells and showed malignant-cell-associated FCRLB and QPCT expression.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03142-z
