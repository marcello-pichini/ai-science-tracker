Bayesian optimization finds high-performance hole-transport molecules for perovskite solar cells

A closed-loop workflow combined high-throughput synthesis of organic semiconductors, measured photovoltaic-device performance, molecular descriptors and Bayesian optimization to search a virtual library of about one million candidate hole-transport materials. The machine-learning-guided iterations identified high-performing molecules; after device optimization, a perovskite solar cell using a selected material reached 26.23% power-conversion efficiency (25.88% certified). The work links candidate molecular structures to measured device performance in this specific cell system.

Technology: Molecular-descriptor machine-learning models with Bayesian optimization in a high-throughput synthesis and device-testing loop

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A closed-loop workflow combined high-throughput synthesis of organic semiconductors, measured photovoltaic-device performance, molecular descriptors and Bayesian optimization to search a virtual library of about one million candidate hole-transport materials.
  • Still unknown: The 26.23% champion and certified 25.88% values followed device-specific optimization of a selected molecule; this does not establish that the molecule alone accounts for the full efficiency gain or that it is globally optimal among all possible molecules. The paper reports a materials-selection workflow for a particular p-i-n perovskite device setup, with performance also affected by other cell layers and processing. The million-member search was virtual and only a small selected subset was synthesized and measured; authors do not claim to have found the single best molecule in that space.
  • AI role: Models trained on measured data from 149 synthesized molecules linked calculated molecular descriptors to perovskite-device performance. Bayesian optimization selected new structures from a virtual library of roughly one million; high-throughput synthesis, characterization and device tests supplied feedback for two closed-loop iterations. Three promising molecules then received individual device optimization. This workflow identified hole-transport materials yielding the reported high-efficiency perovskite cells; the AI contribution was candidate selection and structure–performance modeling, with synthesis and measurements performed experimentally.
