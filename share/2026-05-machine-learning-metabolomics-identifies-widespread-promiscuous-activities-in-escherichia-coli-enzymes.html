Machine-learning metabolomics identifies widespread promiscuous activities in Escherichia coli enzymes

In vitro metabolomics of 667 purified E. coli enzymes, analyzed with a machine-learning-derived multivariate classifier, indicated previously unrecognized promiscuous activities in 11% of enzymes tested. Follow-up reconstruction assigned 22 reaction stoichiometries to 11 enzymes, with four reactions experimentally validated; examples include expanded nucleotide-substrate use by DeoA, cofactor and sugar promiscuity in NanK, and FMN dephosphorylation by CobC. Follow-up assays also found that pyrimidine-specific ribonucleoside hydrolase RihB acted on thymidine, guanosine and deoxyuridine, further extending its measured substrate range.

Technology: Machine-learning-derived multivariate classifier applied to enzyme metabolomics ion-trace data

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: In vitro metabolomics of 667 purified E. coli enzymes, analyzed with a machine-learning-derived multivariate classifier, indicated previously unrecognized promiscuous activities in 11% of enzymes tested.
  • Still unknown: The classifier operated at a 33% false-discovery rate; the 135 ion traces were putatively annotated, and only four of 22 reconstructed reaction stoichiometries were experimentally validated. Therefore the full 11% result is a set of candidate activities, not 11% independently confirmed enzyme functions. The RihB activities were prompted by a low-confidence potassium-thymidine-adduct trace and then tested directly with purified enzyme; they should be distinguished from the classifier’s higher-confidence hit set.
  • AI role: A machine-learning-derived multivariate classifier, with a reported false-discovery rate of 33%, detected unexpected changes in metabolite ion traces in the enzyme assays and helped nominate candidate promiscuous activities. The researchers then reconstructed candidate reaction stoichiometries and experimentally validated four reactions.
