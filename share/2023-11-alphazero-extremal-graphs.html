AlphaZero finds improved finite extremal-graph bounds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural network-guided AlphaZero search with a curriculum generated triangle- and 4-cycle-free graphs that improve known lower bounds for the extremal function ex(n,{C3,C4}) over finite sizes; the proceedings report AlphaZero improvements for n=64–136 and release the graph constructions. The paper also finds improvements using a separate tabu-search method, which often matches or outperforms AlphaZero. This does not solve the asymptotic Erdős conjecture. The result was present in the arXiv v1 posted 2023-11-06; the 2024 IJCAI proceedings paper is a later version.

Technology: AlphaZero reinforcement learning with neural Pairformer representation and curriculum

Editorial relevance 53 mathematics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Preprint · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AlphaZero with a neural Pairformer representation and a curriculum generated extremal graphs improving the established lower bounds for n=64–136; tabu search is a separate, non-neural method and its results are not attributed to AI.
Test and result
The paper reports released graphs with improved lower bounds for ex(n,{C3,C4}) for node counts 64–134. Incremental AlphaZero (neural network-guided Monte Carlo tree search) is explicitly reported to improve bounds for sizes 64–136 in the proceedings version; incremental tabu search also improves bounds and the authors report it can match or exceed AlphaZero. This is a finite-graph construction result, not a proof of the asymptotic Erdős conjecture.
Limitation / still unknown
The result consists of explicit finite constructions/lower bounds; the asymptotic conjecture remains open. The work does not establish that learned search outperforms strong local search.
