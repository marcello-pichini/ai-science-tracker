Unsupervised seismicity categories distinguish documented preparatory phases before three large earthquakes

A K-means framework grouped earthquake families by catalog-derived spatial, temporal, magnitude and seismo-mechanical features. Retrospective analyses found highly localized families with higher interaction and strain release before the 2023 Kahramanmaraş, 2009 L’Aquila and 2014 Iquique earthquakes; similar critical categories were absent before the 2016 Amatrice and 2024 Noto events, where no catalog-visible preparatory phase was detected. The work describes a conditional diagnostic, not a general earthquake prediction system.

Technology: Unsupervised K-means categorization of catalog-derived seismic-family features

  • Tested: A K-means framework grouped earthquake families by catalog-derived spatial, temporal, magnitude and seismo-mechanical features.
  • Still unknown: Peer-reviewed retrospective analysis of five large-earthquake sequences. Preparatory phases in Kahramanmaraş, L’Aquila and Iquique had been documented previously; this paper contributes an unsupervised family-level categorization and comparative feature characterization. The authors state that the approach works only when detectable seismic transients occur, and its use for operational forecasting remains prospective; it is not evidence of reliable prospective prediction.
  • AI role: Unsupervised machine learning grouped spatiotemporal event families from multiple seismicity features into categories without predefining the categories; the authors identified a category with highly localized, interacting seismicity and increased strain release in cases with documented preparatory phases.
