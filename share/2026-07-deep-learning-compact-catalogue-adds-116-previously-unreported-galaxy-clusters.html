Deep-learning ComPACT catalogue adds 116 previously unreported galaxy clusters

Analysis of the CNN-detected ComPACT Sunyaev–Zel’dovich catalogue identified 116 previously unreported galaxy clusters with new redshift estimates, including five massive systems (M500c > 6 × 10^14 solar masses) at z > 0.7. The authors report 158 new cluster-mass determinations and higher completeness than ACT DR5 at fixed purity, especially for massive high-redshift systems.

Technology: ComPACT deep-learning convolutional neural network catalogue, followed by optical photometric redshift and SZ mass analysis

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Analysis of the CNN-detected ComPACT Sunyaev–Zel’dovich catalogue identified 116 previously unreported galaxy clusters with new redshift estimates, including five massive systems (M500c > 6 × 10^14 solar masses) at z > 0.7.
  • Still unknown: The ComPACT catalogue contains 2,962 SZ-selected candidates; the paper confirms approximately 60% as clusters, so the full candidate list must not be treated as confirmed. Its newly reported cluster count relies on cross-matches and optical photometric-redshift significance criteria with an estimated 5% false-positive rate; most are not presented as new spectroscopic confirmations. Masses are scaling-relation estimates. The five high-mass, high-redshift systems are identified with redshift algorithms and SZ measurements, and their interpretation remains subject to catalogue selection/completeness and mass-estimation uncertainties.
  • AI role: The CNN detected SZ cluster candidates in combined ACT+Planck maps, creating the candidate catalogue analyzed here. The authors used existing catalogues and DESI Legacy Imaging Survey photometry to validate systems and estimate redshifts, and ACT+Planck/Planck Compton-y maps with SZ scaling relations to derive masses. The CNN catalogue has higher completeness than ACT DR5 at fixed purity, particularly at high mass and redshift; it is the entry point enabling these new cluster identifications and measurements.
