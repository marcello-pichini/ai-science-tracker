Latent diffusion designs antimicrobial peptides active against fungi and drug-resistant bacteria

A latent-diffusion and molecular-dynamics pipeline generated and filtered peptide sequences; 40 synthesized candidates were tested, and 25 met the study's MIC threshold for antibacterial or antifungal activity. AMP-29 showed activity against Candida glabrata and efficacy in a murine skin-infection model, while AMP-24 showed in-vitro Gram-negative activity and efficacy in murine Acinetobacter baumannii skin and lung infection models.

Technology: Latent diffusion model coupled to molecular dynamics and peptide filtering/ranking

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A latent-diffusion and molecular-dynamics pipeline generated and filtered peptide sequences; 40 synthesized candidates were tested, and 25 met the study's MIC threshold for antibacterial or antifungal activity.
  • Still unknown: Twenty-five of 40 tested peptides met the authors' stated MIC ≤200 μM activity threshold, which includes a broad range of potency. The murine efficacy reports concern selected peptides and infection models, not human treatment. Some fungal peptides showed hemolysis; favorable safety cannot be generalized to the whole set.
  • AI role: The diffusion model generated a diverse peptide sequence set, and computational filtering selected 40 candidates for chemical synthesis and experimental antimicrobial testing; the selected peptides included leads taken into mouse infection models.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11797553/
