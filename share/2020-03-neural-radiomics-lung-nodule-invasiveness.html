31 Mar 2020Taizhou University; Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center

Neural and radiomics score fusion estimates lung-nodule invasiveness

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A neural image model and radiomics SVM estimated invasive versus non-invasive or minimally invasive adenocarcinoma in surgically confirmed ground-glass lung nodules. Maximum-score fusion reached a reported AUC of 0.90 in a 127-nodule test cohort from a second center, compared with 0.83 for the neural model and 0.87 for radiomics alone. This retrospective, selected surgical cohort supports diagnostic discrimination, without establishing screening performance or clinical benefit; several fusion strategies were compared on the reported test cohort.

Technology: Recurrent residual U-Net, transfer-learned CNN, radiomics SVM and maximum-score fusion

Editorial relevance 40 medicine Published articlePublished article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A recurrent residual U-Net segments nodules, a transfer-learned CNN predicts invasiveness, and an SVM classifies selected radiomics features; their prediction scores are fused.
Test and result
373 pathology-confirmed nodules from323 patients:246 nodules/229 patients for training and validation at Taizhou;127 nodules/94 patients at Fudan for independent testing. Source reports maximum-fusion AUC0.90 (95%CI0.84–0.95) and accuracy80.3%; segmentation pretraining used257 LIDC-IDRI nodules.
Limitation / still unknown
Retrospective selected stage-I surgical nodules, multiple nodules treated independently, small cohorts and varying scanner parameters. The report compares fusion choices on the same test set, so that performance is not a separately confirmed frozen-choice estimate. No prospective clinical-utility or survival benefit.
