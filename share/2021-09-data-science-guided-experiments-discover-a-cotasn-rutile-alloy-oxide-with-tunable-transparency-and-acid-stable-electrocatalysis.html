Data-science-guided experiments discover a Co–Ta–Sn rutile alloy oxide with tunable transparency and acid-stable electrocatalysis

A high-throughput optical screen and data-science models identified a Co–Ta–Sn composition region whose optical trends indicated phase behavior beyond simple mixtures. Follow-up X-ray diffraction verified a rutile substitutional-alloy oxide, (Sn,Co,Ta)O₂. Composition tuning produced optical transparency and oxygen-evolution electrocatalytic activity with stability in strong-acid electrolyte, as reported by the authors.

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A high-throughput optical screen and data-science models identified a Co–Ta–Sn composition region whose optical trends indicated phase behavior beyond simple mixtures.
  • Still unknown: The phase inference initially came from optical data and the authors discuss potential false positives from extrinsic film morphology; the selected Co–Ta–Sn rutile phase was followed up and verified by XRD. The optical screen identifies promising regions, not all possible phases, and may miss phases without emergent optical signatures. Reported oxygen-evolution performance and acid stability are laboratory electrocatalysis results; no commercial device or scale-up is established.
  • AI role: Models analyzed high-throughput optical absorption measurements across 376,752 compositions in 108 three-cation oxide systems. They inferred candidate phase diagrams and flagged optical trends inconsistent with lower-order phase mixtures, guiding the Co–Ta–Sn follow-up composition and XRD experiments that verified a rutile alloy phase; this screening also guided catalytic characterization.
