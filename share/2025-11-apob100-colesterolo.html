The structure of the LDL protein apoB100

At the University of Missouri, Zachary Berndsen and Keith Cassidy used AlphaFold to predict the structure of apoB100, the scaffold of LDL particles, and refined it using cryo-electron microscopy data. The result is a cage-like shell with a ribbon belt. The post gives no figures such as resolution and no clinical validation.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Cryo-EM images were refined with AlphaFold2 predictions; the resulting apoB100 structure was compared with more than 200 intramolecular cross-links and showed close agreement, with subnanometre resolution in most regions.
  • Still unknown: The article states that clinical applications will take time and the structure's implications are not yet realized; it gives no resolution figures or clinical validation, and the abstract states that resolution is subnanometre only in most regions.
  • AI role: AlphaFold2 generated the initial atomic-resolution structure predictions, which were refined against cryo-EM data.
