Halicin: deep learning identifies an antibiotic

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A model trained on about 2,500 molecules screened the roughly 6,000 compounds in the Broad Institute’s Drug Repurposing Hub and identified halicin, which killed many resistant bacteria in the lab and cleared A. baumannii infections in mice within 24 hours. Cell-culture and mouse results, not human studies; the mechanism remains preliminary.

Technology: Graph neural network for predicting antibacterial activity

Editorial relevance 80 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A deep neural network predicted antibacterial activity across chemical libraries; candidates were then tested experimentally.
Test and result
Bactericidal activity against a wide range of pathogens, including M. tuberculosis and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae; halicin treated C. difficile and pan-resistant A. baumannii infections in mice. Of 23 tested predictions from over 107 million ZINC15 molecules, eight were antibacterial.
Limitation / still unknown
Human safety and efficacy are not reported in the abstract.
