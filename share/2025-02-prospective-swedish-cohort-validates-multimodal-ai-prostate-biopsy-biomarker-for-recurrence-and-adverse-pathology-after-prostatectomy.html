Prospective Swedish cohort validates multimodal AI prostate-biopsy biomarker for recurrence and adverse pathology after prostatectomy

A previously developed multimodal AI score was retrospectively applied to archived diagnostic biopsies and follow-up from a prospectively collected Swedish prostatectomy cohort. The score, derived from digitized diagnostic prostate-biopsy images plus age, PSA and clinical T-stage, was associated with post-surgical biochemical recurrence and adverse pathology. Among 143 men with evaluable data and median 8.8-year follow-up, the continuous score was associated with recurrence (subdistribution HR 2.45, 95% CI 1.77–3.38); among 88 evaluable for adverse pathology, OR was 4.85 (95% CI 2.54–10.78). Estimated 5-year recurrence was 25% in intermediate/high score versus 4% in low score.

Technology: Artera multimodal artificial-intelligence (MMAI) prostate biomarker combining deep-learning features from digitized biopsy histopathology with age, PSA and clinical T-stage

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A previously developed multimodal AI score was retrospectively applied to archived diagnostic biopsies and follow-up from a prospectively collected Swedish prostatectomy cohort.
  • Still unknown: This is retrospective external prognostic validation using specimens and follow-up from a prospectively collected cohort; it is not prospective AI-guided care, a randomized test of biomarker-guided treatment, or evidence that using the score improves outcomes. Only 143 patients had evaluable images and complete covariates, and 88 were evaluable for adverse pathology; it is a single tertiary Swedish center. The model’s earlier development setting involved men receiving radiation in North America, so this tests transport to a different population/treatment context. A 2025 conference abstract describing this cohort is an earlier presentation of the same study, not another record.
  • AI role: The locked MMAI score converted diagnostic biopsy images and clinical variables into patient-level prognostic scores. The prospective Swedish cohort tested whether that model-derived score associated with the distinct post-prostatectomy outcomes biochemical recurrence and adverse pathology; associations persisted after adjustment for standard NCCN/CAPRA clinical risk measures.
