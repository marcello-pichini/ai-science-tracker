A 3D Bioprinted Pseudo-Bone Drug Delivery Scaffold for Bone Tissue Engineering.

Source check: Needs source check. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: ANN-supported optimization selected a 3D-printed simvastatin scaffold with 20-day in-vitro release. The primary abstract reports hardness and resilience near healthy bone in a mechanically fractured human clavicle model. These release and ex-vivo mechanical endpoints do not establish living-organism healing or clinical benefit.

Technology: Artificial neural network for scaffold optimization

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A multilayer feed-forward/back-propagation artificial neural network optimized scaffold formulation using thermogelation and drug-release responses; it did not test biological healing.
Test and result
Thirty-nine scaffold formulations were evaluated with ANN-supported optimization. The selected 14% PPF/16% PF127 formulation released simvastatin in vitro for 20 days. The abstract reports hardness and resilience near healthy bone in a mechanically fractured human clavicle model; specimen and replicate counts were not assessed in this review.
Limitation / still unknown
Living-organism bone healing and patient benefit are not established by the reported in-vitro release and ex-vivo mechanical model. Specimen and replicate counts were not assessed.
