3D-HiChAT predicts embryonic enhancer hubs validated by CRISPR interference

A random-forest model integrating ATAC-seq, H3K27ac ChIP-seq and HiChIP data predicted enhancer effects across mouse trophoblast stem cells, embryonic stem cells and extraembryonic endoderm cells. CRISPRi tests supported a shared Enh14 hub regulating both Tfcp2l1 and Gli2 in embryonic stem cells, and an Enh4 hub linked to seven genes in extraembryonic endoderm; four of those seven showed expression reductions as predicted. Across 40 tested enhancer-promoter pairs, experiments yielded 12 true positives and 13 true negatives, with overall prediction accuracy 0.71.

Technology: 3D-HiChAT, an optimized Random Forest model combining chromatin accessibility, H3K27ac and 3D HiChIP connectivity features

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: CRISPRi tests supported a shared Enh14 hub regulating both Tfcp2l1 and Gli2 in embryonic stem cells, and an Enh4 hub linked to seven genes in extraembryonic endoderm; four of those seven showed expression reductions as predicted.
  • Still unknown: The experiments were performed in mouse-derived cell lines, not intact embryos or human cells. Only selected predictions were tested; the 40-pair validation had 12 true positives, 13 true negatives and an overall reported accuracy of 0.71, leaving discordant/uncertain pairs. The Enh14 and Enh4 results support specific cell-type-dependent regulatory hubs but do not establish the full genome-wide predicted map as experimentally validated or prove developmental phenotypes in vivo.
  • AI role: The model used 1D and 3D chromatin features to rank genome-wide enhancer-promoter pairs by predicted expression impact after in-silico enhancer perturbation. Researchers selected predictions for CRISPRi validation and measured target-gene expression by RT-qPCR; the paper reports specific validated multi-gene enhancer hubs and positive/negative prediction rates across 40 pairs.
