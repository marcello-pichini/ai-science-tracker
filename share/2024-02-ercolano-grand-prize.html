Vesuvius Challenge: the first Herculaneum scroll can be read

The $700,000 prize went to a submission with 11 additional columns and more than 2,000 characters (about 5% of the first scroll), using models to detect ink and automated segmentation. Papyrologists evaluated 15 anonymized columns and recovered more text from the winning submission. Segmentation remains largely manual and costly.

Technology: Ink detection with vision models and automated segmentation

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Announcement only
  • Test type: prospective or independent check
  • Tested: Papyrologists independently evaluated 15 anonymized columns, and the winning submission yielded the most text for every reviewer, meeting the stated criteria.
  • Still unknown: The comprehensive study is not yet published, only rough draft transcriptions exist, segmentation is largely manual, and about 5% of the first scroll has been read.
  • AI role: Models detected ink in CT scans (a TimeSformer-based model was strongest), and an automated tool produced the segmentation of the papyrus surfaces.
