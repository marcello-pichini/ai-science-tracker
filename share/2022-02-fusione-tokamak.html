Tokamak plasma control with reinforcement learning

A single neural network controls the magnetic coils of Lausanne’s TCV tokamak and forms plasmas in different configurations (snowflake, ITER-like shape, double droplet). Trained mainly in simulation and then tested on the real machine. Stated limitations: plasma lasts about three seconds and the machine changes from day to day.

Technology: Deep reinforcement learning

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: On the TCV tokamak the controller produced and held elongated, conventional, negative-triangularity and snowflake configurations, with accurate tracking of location, current and shape, and sustained two separate 'droplet' plasmas.
  • Still unknown: Transfer to larger or reactor-scale devices is not reported in the abstract.
  • AI role: A deep reinforcement-learning architecture learned to command the full set of control coils from high-level objectives.

Evidence sources: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04301-9
