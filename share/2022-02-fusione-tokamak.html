Tokamak plasma control with reinforcement learning

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A single neural network controls the magnetic coils of Lausanne’s TCV tokamak and forms plasmas in different configurations (snowflake, ITER-like shape, double droplet). Trained mainly in simulation and then tested on the real machine. Stated limitations: plasma lasts about three seconds and the machine changes from day to day.

Technology: Deep reinforcement learning

Editorial relevance 80 physics Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A deep reinforcement-learning architecture learned to command the full set of control coils from high-level objectives.
Test and result
On the TCV tokamak the controller produced and held elongated, conventional, negative-triangularity and snowflake configurations, with accurate tracking of location, current and shape, and sustained two separate 'droplet' plasmas.
Limitation / still unknown
Transfer to larger or reactor-scale devices is not reported in the abstract.
