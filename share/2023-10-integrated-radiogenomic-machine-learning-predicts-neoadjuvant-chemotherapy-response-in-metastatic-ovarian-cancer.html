Integrated radiogenomic machine learning predicts neoadjuvant chemotherapy response in metastatic ovarian cancer

The IRON ensemble combined baseline clinical data, CA-125, ctDNA and CT radiomics from primary and metastatic lesions to estimate tumor-volume change during neoadjuvant chemotherapy for high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma. In an independent 42-patient Barts cohort, the integrated model achieved AUC 0.78 for RECIST 1.1 response classification versus 0.47 for a clinical-only model; radiomics-containing scores significantly correlated with volume change in internal holdout. The study also measured spatial heterogeneity, with omental lesions showing better response than pelvic/ovarian disease.

Technology: IRON ensemble combining elastic net, support-vector regression and random forest

  • Tested: In an independent 42-patient Barts cohort, the integrated model achieved AUC 0.78 for RECIST 1.1 response classification versus 0.47 for a clinical-only model; radiomics-containing scores significantly correlated with volume change in internal holdout.   • Still unknown: Model analysis of two prospective observational cohorts (training n=72, internal holdout n=20, external n=42), not a decision trial. External ctDNA unavailable and imputed to training means. Sample size limits generalizability; treatment benefit from model-guided decisions was not tested.
  • AI role: Pretreatment clinical, blood and multi-lesion radiomic features predicted volumetric response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Held-out and external evaluation showed radiomics added predictive information beyond clinical variables.
