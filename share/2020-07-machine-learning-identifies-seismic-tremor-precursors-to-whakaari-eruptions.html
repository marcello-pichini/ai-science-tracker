Machine learning identifies seismic tremor precursors to Whakaari eruptions

A structured machine-learning analysis of nine years of Whakaari seismic records identified four-hour energy bursts that occurred hours to days before most of the five major eruptive episodes studied. The authors interpret the pattern as possible charging of the vent hydrothermal system by hot magmatic fluids. In cross-validation, the prototype alert model gave advance warning for four of five unseen eruption instances, including at least four hours before the 2019 eruption.

Technology: Structured machine-learning pipeline using engineered seismic time-series features, a random forest, and an alert decision model

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A structured machine-learning analysis of nine years of Whakaari seismic records identified four-hour energy bursts that occurred hours to days before most of the five major eruptive episodes studied.
  • Still unknown: This is a study of five major eruptive episodes at one volcano. The 4/5 warning result is cross-validation on this historical record, not independent operational validation; the model was described as a prototype. The ensemble alert score is not a calibrated eruption probability, and alert thresholds trade missed events against false alarms. The proposed hydrothermal charging mechanism is an interpretation of the seismic pattern.
  • AI role: The machine-learning pipeline analyzed continuous seismic tremor data, selected precursor patterns, and modeled short-term eruption alerts. Its feature analysis identified the four-hour energy-burst pattern; the proposed hydrothermal charging mechanism is the authors’ geophysical interpretation, not a direct measurement of subsurface fluid movement.
