An AI system improves a Chan–Lam coupling reaction

The system identified TEMPO as a useful additive for coupling primary sulfonamides; chemists reproduced yield improvements in 11 of 14 substrate pairs at bench scale. The work retains human supervision and specialized infrastructure; independent reproducibility and generalization remain to be checked.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Announcement only
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Over two cycles in an automated lab, yields improved for 88% of boronic acids and 83% of sulfonamides tested (mean 16.6% to 25.2%); human chemists repeated representative reactions at bench scale, with higher yields for 11 of 14 substrate pairs.
  • Still unknown: Not stated in the reviewed material
  • AI role: GPT-5.4 connected to Molecule.one Maria proposed, designed and analyzed experiments; humans steered prompts, selected proposals, made limited corrections and validated the final result.
