HiDDEN finds malignant-like cell signals in early multiple-myeloma precursor samples

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: HiDDEN refines sample-level case labels at single-cell resolution. In human bone-marrow single-cell RNA-seq data, its labels tracked manual neoplastic-cell annotations in mixed precursor samples and inferred malignant-like cells in three low-purity MGUS samples that the original manual analysis had labeled as containing only healthy cells. The inferred cells were enriched for previously described malignancy-associated gene signatures. In a separate mouse demyelination dataset, HiDDEN identified an early endothelial subpopulation with expression changes associated with angiogenesis, blood–brain-barrier breakdown and extracellular-matrix responses.

Technology: HiDDEN, a machine-learning framework for refining disease/condition labels at single-cell resolution

AI contribution
The model inferred which individual cells in case samples were affected rather than assigning the sample-level case label to all cells. This enabled the paper’s reanalysis of human myeloma precursor single-cell data and mouse demyelination single-nucleus data, yielding the specified malignancy and endothelial-population findings.
Test and result
HiDDEN refines sample-level case labels at single-cell resolution.
Limitation / still unknown
The MGUS finding is a transcriptomic inference in previously collected samples, computationally supported by known gene signatures and sample-purity estimates; the paper does not report independent pathology confirmation, clinical follow-up, or validation as a diagnostic test. The endothelial subpopulation was found in a mouse lesion model; its association with blood–brain-barrier dysfunction does not establish causality.
