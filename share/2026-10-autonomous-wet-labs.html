AI agents in the lab: aptamers and proteins designed in a closed loop

AI agents designed DNA aptamers and protein binders, which were then tested in the lab over multiple rounds. Target success rates reached 33.5% for myoglobin and 42.1% for α-thrombin (98.5% specificity). The post summarizes three arXiv papers whose peer review is unconfirmed; it also reports initial failures.

Technology: AI agents with laboratory robots

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Agents ran three design-test-iterate rounds of DNA aptamers against myoglobin and alpha-thrombin in the lab, reaching 33.5% and 42.1% hit rates with 98.5% specificity for thrombin; protein binders against FAP were reported at single-digit nanomolar affinity.
  • Still unknown: Peer review of the cited arXiv papers is unconfirmed; there are two protein targets and three rounds; initial rounds failed, and protein expression adds steps to each loop.
  • AI role: Agents designed and revised sequences across rounds and adjusted design rules after reading wet-lab data, under human oversight of protocols and robot runs.

Evidence sources: https://research.google/blog/grounding-ai-agents-in-biological-reality-building-blocks-for-autonomous-wet-labs/
