Deep-learning splice prediction links a PARP2 variant to altered protein isoforms and influenza susceptibility

By combining single-cell splicing-QTL analysis during influenza A infection with SpliceAI/Pangolin annotation and statistical fine-mapping, researchers prioritized causal splice variants. They experimentally characterized rs2297616 in PARP2: its A allele creates an alternative 5′ splice donor, adding 39 nucleotides and producing a protein isoform 13 amino acids longer. CRISPR-edited A549 cells showed the predicted splice and protein changes; the A-allele edited cells also had higher influenza A infection than G-allele cells. The variant was associated with higher viral burden in a panel of 96 lymphoblastoid cell lines.

Technology: SpliceAI and Pangolin deep-learning splice-effect predictors, integrated with single-cell scHi-HOST sQTL mapping and SuSiE statistical fine-mapping

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: They experimentally characterized rs2297616 in PARP2: its A allele creates an alternative 5′ splice donor, adding 39 nucleotides and producing a protein isoform 13 amino acids longer.
  • Still unknown: The 96-donor LCL association is observational and modest (the paper reports p=0.029 for the genotype association). Infection experiments used edited A549 lung epithelial cells, not patients or an in-vivo infection model. The same locus had been linked to alternative splicing previously, but the paper provides new evidence connecting genotype to PARP2 protein isoform expression and to influenza susceptibility. Broader viral-susceptibility implications remain unproven.
  • AI role: Deep-learning splice models prioritized variants predicted to alter donor/acceptor sites; statistical fine-mapping and sQTL association narrowed causal candidates. The AI-supported analysis nominated rs2297616; the authors then tested its splice and protein-isoform effects using reporter assays, CRISPR-edited A549 cells, sequencing and western blotting, and assessed influenza infection in edited cells.
