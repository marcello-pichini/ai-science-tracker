Machine-guided synthetic regulatory DNA drives cell-type-specific expression across cell lines and vertebrates

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: The CODA platform used the Malinois deep-learning model and sequence-search algorithms to design synthetic cis-regulatory elements (CREs). In massively parallel reporter assays, 94.1% of the synthetic sequences met the authors’ cell-type-specificity criterion, versus 73.6% of model-selected natural sequences and 40.6% of chromatin-mark-selected natural sequences; synthetic sequences also had higher measured target/off-target separation across K562, HepG2 and SK-N-SH cells. In transgenic zebrafish, two of three liver-directed and two of three neuron-directed CREs showed the intended activity. One neuron-directed sequence, synN1, retained cortical and neuronal expression in mice, including postnatal neocortical layer 6 and subplate neurons.

Technology: CODA (Computational Optimization of DNA Activity) with Malinois deep neural network and AdaLead, simulated annealing, and Fast SeqProp sequence search/design algorithms

AI contribution
Malinois predicted MPRA activity of candidate DNA sequences in K562, HepG2 and SK-N-SH cells; CODA iteratively optimized sequences for specified cell-type specificity. Researchers synthesized and experimentally measured the designs by MPRA, then tested selected CREs with reporter constructs in zebrafish and mice. The paper attributes the novel sequences and their measured expression specificity to this model-guided design loop.
Test and result
In massively parallel reporter assays, 94.1% of the synthetic sequences met the authors’ cell-type-specificity criterion, versus 73.6% of model-selected natural sequences and 40.6% of chromatin-mark-selected natural sequences; synthetic sequences also had higher measured target/off-target separation across K562, HepG2 and SK-N-SH cells.
Limitation / still unknown
The in-vitro work used three transformed human cell lines and reporter assays. In-vivo testing covered a small selected set: two of three liver-directed and two of three neuron-directed elements were active in zebrafish; mouse validation focused on neuronal synN1, with n=3 synN1 mice for postnatal cell-type analysis and n=6 for the 5-week cortical reporter assay. These are transgenic reporter results, not therapeutic delivery or evidence of clinical utility. Earlier synthetic enhancer studies existed; this paper’s counted result is its CODA/Malinois comparison against natural sequences plus selected vertebrate validation, not the general idea of AI-designed enhancers.
