Aeneas: restoration, dating, and provenance of Latin inscriptions

The model restores texts with 73% top-20 accuracy for gaps up to ten characters (58% when length is unknown), attributes provenance with 72% accuracy across 62 Roman provinces, and dates inscriptions within 13 years; it was trained on over 176,000 inscriptions. In a study with 23 historians on 60 inscriptions, results were best when parallels and predictions were used together. Published in Nature.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Corpus of 176,861 Latin inscriptions. Aeneas: 72% geographical accuracy over 62 provinces; dating within 13 years on average; restoration character error rate 40.5% with known length. 23 historians on 60 inscriptions, solo versus with Aeneas parallels and predictions.
  • Still unknown: The paper notes a small dataset, survival bias, only about 5% of inscriptions with images, and weaker results where data are scarce. Training data include earlier editors' restorations. Other languages are suggested, not tested.
  • AI role: Aeneas restored text, attributed provinces, estimated dates and retrieved parallels; historians evaluated outputs and combined them with their own knowledge.

Evidence sources: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09292-5
