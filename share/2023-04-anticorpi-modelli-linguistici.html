Language models guide improvements to seven antibodies

Two rounds of laboratory evolution, using at most 20 variants per antibody, improved the affinity of four mature antibodies by up to sevenfold and three immature ones by up to 160-fold. Biochemical and pseudovirus tests; no demonstration of therapeutic benefit in humans.

Technology: Protein language models

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Language-model-guided variants of seven antibodies were made in two lab evolution rounds (at most 20 variants per antibody) and binding was measured by biolayer interferometry; four antibodies were also checked for thermostability, polyspecificity and pseudovirus neutralization.
  • Still unknown: The authors state that affinity gains are smaller than in vivo somatic hypermutation, the method improves existing binding rather than creating new binders, and it may work less well under other selection pressures; no therapeutic benefit in humans is shown.
  • AI role: General protein language models guided affinity maturation by choosing which antibody variants to test.
