SONAR identifies 3′UTR sequence features that raise T-cell receptor protein expression and CD8-cell killing

The XGBoost-based SONAR pipeline modeled protein abundance from thousands of sequence features across human immune cells and cell lines, identifying cell-type- and activation-dependent regulatory patterns. Reporter assays found that CT-rich 3′UTR motifs increased protein output while AG-rich motifs were generally repressive. SONAR-informed synthetic 3′UTRs raised CDK4-specific T-cell-receptor protein without increasing its mRNA; engineered CD8+ T cells carrying the CT-rich constructs killed CDK4-expressing melanoma cells more effectively in a coculture assay.

Technology: SONAR, an XGBoost machine-learning pipeline using thousands of sequence features to predict protein abundance

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Reporter assays found that CT-rich 3′UTR motifs increased protein output while AG-rich motifs were generally repressive.
  • Still unknown: The target-cell killing experiment used engineered CD8+ cells in vitro and three donors; no animal or patient treatment outcome was tested. Protein abundance prediction used limited cell and immune-subset datasets, and model feature importance is not itself causal evidence. The assay supports effects for the tested synthetic 3′UTRs and CDK4-specific TCR in the reported system, not all proteins or therapeutic settings.
  • AI role: SONAR learned sequence-feature patterns associated with protein abundance in immune cells, including cell-type- and activation-dependent feature importance. Its outputs guided selection of 3′UTR motifs and synthetic sequence design; reporter assays and a CDK4-specific TCR construct then tested their effects on protein output and CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity. The model generated design hypotheses; the downstream experiments established the measured motif and T-cell results.
