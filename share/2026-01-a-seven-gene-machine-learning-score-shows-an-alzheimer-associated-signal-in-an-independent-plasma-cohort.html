A seven-gene machine-learning score shows an Alzheimer-associated signal in an independent plasma cohort

A machine-learning pipeline selected a seven-gene lactylation-related transcriptomic score (LDS) from nine Alzheimer disease cohorts. The study evaluated the signature in seven independent brain transcriptomic datasets and a single-center clinical plasma cohort of 540 participants (180 controls, 90 with amnestic mild cognitive impairment and 270 with Alzheimer disease). Plasma LDS had AUC 0.772 for Alzheimer disease; combining LDS with p-tau181 and p-tau217 had AUC 0.859. LDS scores increased across control, aMCI and Alzheimer groups and correlated with MMSE and Braak stage.

Technology: Lactylation-Derived Score (LDS), a random-forest feature-screening and partial least-squares logistic-regression model

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A machine-learning pipeline selected a seven-gene lactylation-related transcriptomic score (LDS) from nine Alzheimer disease cohorts.
  • Still unknown: The plasma validation cohort was recruited at one center and the analyses are retrospective and observational. The score does not establish lactylation as a causal mechanism, clinical benefit, or readiness for routine diagnosis; broader and prospective validation is needed. This is a transcriptomic signature and a different result from previously reported Alzheimer spectral-protein/chemical biomarker studies.
  • AI role: Machine learning selected a seven-gene expression signature from lactylation-related genes across multi-cohort brain transcriptomic data; the resulting score was measured by RT-qPCR in a separate clinical plasma cohort and evaluated alone and with plasma p-tau markers.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02305-7
