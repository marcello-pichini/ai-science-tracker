Euclid and machine-learning selection identify 31 spectroscopically confirmed quasars at redshift 6.6–7.8

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Using Euclid Wide Survey photometry over about 3,000 square degrees, the team applied several photometric selection methods, including XGBoost classifiers and probabilistic modeling, then obtained telescope spectra for candidates. Spectroscopy confirmed 31 new quasars at redshifts 6.6–7.8; 12 are at z≥7, and the paper reports EUCL J172902.75+641018.1 at z≈7.77 as the highest-redshift quasar known at publication. The sample extends quasar studies to fainter luminosities at z>7.

Technology: XGBoost machine-learning classifiers and Extreme-Deconvolution high-redshift quasar selection, alongside probabilistic spectral-energy-distribution methods

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Multiple photometric selection methods ranked high-redshift quasar candidates from Euclid colors; XGBoost estimated quasar probabilities and redshifts. Humans visually removed artifacts and prioritized follow-up, and Keck, Magellan and LBT spectra established the 31 quasar identifications.

Limitation / still unknown
Peer-reviewed Euclid Consortium study. Candidate ranking was AI-assisted, but human review and spectroscopic follow-up were essential to confirmation. The redshift record is the authors’ claim at the article’s publication date, not a claim about the present-day record. This is one paper-level sample of 31 confirmed quasars; candidate lists and the 31 individual objects are not separate entries.
