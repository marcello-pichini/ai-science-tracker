Deep learning expands the Utah FORGE DAS microearthquake catalog and resolves stimulation structure

A ResNet-based DASEventNet model applied to continuous distributed acoustic sensing from the April 2022 Utah FORGE Well 16A (78)-32 stimulation manually verified 5,749 microearthquakes absent from the Silixa STA/LTA catalog; 45 of 5,794 model-positive windows were false detections on manual review. The study reports events down to Mw −1.80 versus −1.14 for STA/LTA and a broader Stage 3 seismicity distribution consistent with fracture geometry seen in an independent deep-borehole geophone catalog. This is a local event-catalog and stimulation-geometry result, not a direct DAS-based event-location measurement.

Technology: DASEventNet (ResNet-50 convolutional neural network)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A ResNet-based DASEventNet model applied to continuous distributed acoustic sensing from the April 2022 Utah FORGE Well 16A (78)-32 stimulation manually verified 5,749 microearthquakes absent from the Silixa STA/LTA catalog; 45 of 5,794 model-positive windows were false detections on manual review.
  • Still unknown: Original JGR: Solid Earth paper reports manual review of model-positive events and comparison with conventional Silixa detections. Model predicts event presence but does not estimate event locations or magnitudes from DAS alone; timing association and location/magnitude comparison use an independent deep-borehole geophone catalog with an assumed 0.2 s matching window. Fiber placement limits direct geometry inference. Detection of cataloged events is not independent proof of every physical interpretation.
  • AI role: Trained on manually checked DAS event and noise windows, the network detected weak microearthquake waveforms in continuous seven-day DAS recordings. It expanded the catalog and enabled a denser spatiotemporal characterization of the hydraulic stimulation seismicity.
