ML-guided discovery of 5ANI passivator yields high-efficiency, stable perovskite solar cells

An interpretable machine-learning and active-learning workflow screened a virtual library of 18,264 candidate passivation molecules and guided synthesis/testing of selected candidates for perovskite solar cells. The newly identified molecule 5-(aminomethyl)nicotinonitrile hydroiodide (5ANI) yielded 0.05 cm² devices at 27.22% power-conversion efficiency (27.18% certified) and 21.4 cm² mini-modules at 23.49%. Under the reported ISOS-L-1I operating test, 5ANI devices retained 98.7% of initial efficiency after 1,200 hours.

Technology: SISSO interpretable symbolic regression and NGBoost active-learning models, coupled to DFT calculations and a Bayesian-optimization/symbolic-regression automated fabrication platform

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: An interpretable machine-learning and active-learning workflow screened a virtual library of 18,264 candidate passivation molecules and guided synthesis/testing of selected candidates for perovskite solar cells.
  • Still unknown: This is a result for a specific perovskite composition, cell architecture and passivation/fabrication protocol. The ML regression was trained on only 20 initial known passivator/device datasets; four selected candidates were experimentally synthesized/validated. The reported champion efficiency is a small-area cell result; the mini-module efficiency is lower (23.49%) and laboratory-scale. The 1,200-hour stability result follows the stated ISOS-L-1I protocol and does not establish commercial lifetime. The paper reports high reproducibility from its automated setup, not factory-scale manufacturing. This is a different study and molecule from the 2024 Bayesian-optimized hole-transport materials record (Science DOI 10.1126/science.ads0901): here AI identifies an interfacial passivator 5ANI.
  • AI role: The team used DFT descriptors and SISSO regression trained on measurements from 20 known passivator/device sets to learn structure–performance relationships. NGBoost active learning reduced the computational cost of estimating interfacial properties across an 18,264-molecule virtual library; model-guided screening selected four candidates for synthesis and experimental validation. 5ANI emerged as a passivator for which the automated device-fabrication loop optimized deposition and processing settings. The ML models guided material choice, while the closed-loop platform refined device fabrication and generated measured feedback.
