Elastic-net proteomic risk modeling and multi-cohort validation identify proteins associated with incident diabetes

An untargeted analysis of 4,955 plasma proteins in the ARIC cohort identified 140 associations with incident diabetes; 64 were significant in an internal sample and 47 (including 22 reported as novel) were associated in the external Singapore Multi-Ethnic Cohort. Elastic-net Cox modeling selected a 27-protein panel for 20-year risk prediction. In internal validation, adding those proteins to conventional risk factors including fasting glucose and HbA1c increased the C statistic from 0.720 to 0.731. Genetic-instrument analyses supported possible causal roles for SHBG, ATP1B2 and GSTA1, although ATP1B2’s genetic and observational directions differed.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: An untargeted analysis of 4,955 plasma proteins in the ARIC cohort identified 140 associations with incident diabetes; 64 were significant in an internal sample and 47 (including 22 reported as novel) were associated in the external Singapore Multi-Ethnic Cohort.
  • Still unknown: The 47-protein associations were externally tested in Singapore MEC; the elastic-net predictive model itself was internally validated in ARIC, not independently externally validated. Its incremental C statistic over covariates including fasting glucose and HbA1c was modest (0.011; P=0.04), and the protein model overestimated observed diabetes risk across internal-validation deciles. The causal claims are genetic-instrument inferences, not experimental proof; ATP1B2 had opposite directions in genetic-instrument versus observational analyses. Cohort associations do not establish clinical utility.
  • AI role: Elastic net selected a 27-protein combination from candidate proteins to improve 20-year incident-diabetes risk prediction beyond clinical and cardiometabolic covariates. The model was trained in the ARIC discovery subset and internally evaluated in a held-out ARIC subset. Separate Cox association and genetic-instrument analyses reported the externally validated protein associations; the record does not attribute those analyses to machine learning.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10090896/
