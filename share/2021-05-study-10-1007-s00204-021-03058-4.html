Selection of safe artemisinin derivatives using a machine learning-based cardiotoxicity platform and in vitro and in vivo validation.

Selection of safe artemisinin derivatives using a machine learning-based cardiotoxicity platform and in vitro and in vivo validation.

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random-forest cardiotoxicity model and additional toxicity filters reduced a 374-derivative library to seven candidates. Artemisinin B was then examined in AC16 cells and zebrafish larvae; these preclinical assays do not establish human safety.

Technology: Random-forest cardiotoxicity models

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original source Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A leave-one-out random-forest model predicted arrhythmia, cardiac failure, heart block, hypertension and myocardial infarction; DataWarrior and Pro-Tox-II added other toxicity filters.
Test and result
The screen retained 197/374 derivatives, then 17, then seven. Five were available for AC16 viability assays (three independent experiments, six parallel measurements each); one showed moderate cytotoxicity at high concentration. Artemisinin B was tested in 210 zebrafish larvae across seven groups of 30; one death at 0.5 pg/mL and pericardial edema at 1 pg/mL were reported.
Limitation / still unknown
The seven-candidate list is a computational screening outcome. The reported cell and zebrafish assays do not establish human safety or clinical cardioprotection; transcriptomic markers are indirect endpoints.
