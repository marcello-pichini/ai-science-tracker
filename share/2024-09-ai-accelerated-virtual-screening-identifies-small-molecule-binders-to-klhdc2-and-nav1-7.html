AI-accelerated virtual screening identifies small-molecule binders to KLHDC2 and Nav1.7

The OpenVS platform combined target-specific neural-network active learning with RosettaVS physics-based docking to search billion-scale compound libraries. The study reported seven low-micromolar KLHDC2 degron-pocket binders and four Naᵥ1.7 VSD4 binders. KLHDC2 hit C29 competed with a degron peptide at IC50 about 2.9 μM and its predicted pose was supported by a 2.0 Å crystal structure; the strongest Naᵥ1.7 hit, Z8739902234, had IC50 1.3 μM in whole-cell electrophysiology.

Technology: OpenVS active-learning platform with target-specific feed-forward neural-network triage, coupled to RosettaVS physics-based docking and RosettaGenFF-VS scoring

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: The study reported seven low-micromolar KLHDC2 degron-pocket binders and four Naᵥ1.7 VSD4 binders.
  • Still unknown: Early ligand-hit discovery only: binding/inhibition was measured in biochemical competition, biolayer interferometry and cell electrophysiology assays, not in animals or patients. The paper states further optimization is needed to improve KLHDC2 compounds toward nanomolar potency. KLHDC2 C29’s crystal structure is experimental support for one predicted binding pose; target modulation, therapeutic efficacy and safety were not established. The reported target hit rates apply to manually filtered, synthesized subsets, not the full billion-molecule library.
  • AI role: The target-specific neural network used docking-labeled data to guide which molecules were prioritized for additional expensive docking over iterative multi-billion-compound screens; RosettaVS/RosettaGenFF-VS performed physics-based docking and pose/affinity scoring. The AI-guided screen nominated molecules that were synthesized and experimentally tested. Experiments confirmed KLHDC2 degron displacement and a crystal structure matching the predicted pose, plus Naᵥ1.7 binding/inhibition in patch clamp. The paper attributes discovery to the combined AI-accelerated and physics-based workflow, not to a generative model alone.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-52061-7.pdf
