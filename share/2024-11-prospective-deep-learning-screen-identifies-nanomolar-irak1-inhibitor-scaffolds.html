Prospective deep-learning screen identifies nanomolar IRAK1 inhibitor scaffolds

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Ro5's prospective HydraScreen structure-based deep-learning screen ranked compounds for IRAK1 and was evaluated against a 47,000-compound robotic high-throughput screen. The study reports that 23.8% of IRAK1 hits were in HydraScreen's top 1% and identifies three nanomolar scaffolds, two described in the abstract as novel IRAK1 candidates.

Technology: HydraScreen structure-based deep-learning virtual screening, with SpectraView knowledge-graph target evaluation

Editorial relevance 53 chemistry Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
SpectraView selected IRAK1 as the study target; HydraScreen scored docked ligand-pose ensembles to rank compounds before prospective screening. Researchers compared rankings with automated high-throughput assay results from a 47,000-compound library and reported nanomolar IRAK1 hit scaffolds, two described as novel candidates.
Test and result
The study reports that 23.8% of IRAK1 hits were in HydraScreen's top 1% and identifies three nanomolar scaffolds, two described in the abstract as novel IRAK1 candidates. The new candidate experiments were conducted within the Ro5/Strateos discovery collaboration.
Limitation / still unknown
This is prospective early-stage hit identification, not drug development or disease efficacy. The assay validated kinase inhibition in vitro. Authors are affiliated with the companies that developed the tools. The abstract describes two scaffolds as novel candidates, while detailed similarity analysis indicates one of three nanomolar scaffolds had a similar known active; do not call all hits unprecedented.
