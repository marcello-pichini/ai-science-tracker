CNN-augmented focal mechanisms reveal stress-linked faulting changes around major southern California earthquakes

CNN phase and first-motion pickers supplied additional waveform polarities and arrivals for a 297,478-event southern California focal-mechanism catalog, about 40% larger than the prior catalog. The authors used the expanded catalog to observe systematic post-mainshock faulting-style changes spatially correlated with estimated static stress perturbations from the 2010 El Mayor–Cucapah and 2019 Ridgecrest earthquakes, including changes more than 100 km from the epicentres.

Technology: Convolutional neural networks for seismic phase arrival and first-motion polarity picking

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper

  • Tested: CNN phase and first-motion pickers supplied additional waveform polarities and arrivals for a 297,478-event southern California focal-mechanism catalog, about 40% larger than the prior catalog.
  • Still unknown: Catalog quality varies with location and station coverage; the authors report uncertainty in some solutions and frame stress processes as associations/inferences. The catalog provides improved observations, not a claim of earthquake predictability.
  • AI role: Extracted additional phase arrivals and P-wave first-motion polarities from noisy seismograms; combined with existing observations in HASH focal-mechanism solutions to increase catalog coverage and resolution for small earthquakes.
