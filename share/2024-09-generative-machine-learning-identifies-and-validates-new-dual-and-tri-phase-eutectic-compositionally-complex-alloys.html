Generative machine learning identifies and validates new dual- and tri-phase eutectic compositionally complex alloys

A conditional variational autoencoder generated candidate alloy compositions and an artificial neural network ranked eutectic-forming likelihood. The team experimentally validated previously unreported eutectic compositionally complex alloys across quaternary to senary systems. Reported examples include Al₁₆Cr₁₈Fe₂₄Ni₄₂ with a dual-phase FCC/BCC eutectic lamellar structure and Al₁₁.₃Nb₁₃.₇Co₃₆.₆Cr₈.₂Fe₇.₉Ni₂₂.₃ with a tri-phase FCC/BCC/Laves eutectic structure, supported by XRD, SEM and DSC.

Technology: Conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) and artificial neural network (ANN)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: A conditional variational autoencoder generated candidate alloy compositions and an artificial neural network ranked eutectic-forming likelihood.
  • Still unknown: The authors report five newly identified ECCA compositions across the studied systems; this is one paper-level result, not five records. The entry names two representative experimentally examined compositions. Validation is limited to the reported arc-melted samples and XRD/SEM/DSC characterization; no long-term mechanical performance or application testing is established. The training data were sparse and imbalanced, and dataset filtering was part of the framework.
  • AI role: A CVAE generated candidate compositions conditioned on eutectic/non-eutectic microstructure labels; an ANN trained on composition and thermodynamics-derived descriptors estimated eutectic-forming likelihood and selected generated candidates for experimental validation. Dataset preprocessing with PCA and K-means addressed class imbalance. The ML pipeline directly proposed the compositions whose eutectic structures were examined experimentally.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-024-01385-5
