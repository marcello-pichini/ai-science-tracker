1 May 2023University of Cambridge; Université Paris Cité; University of St Andrews

Learned reaction models guide improved yields for three ester hydrogenations

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Learned models of ruthenium-catalysed ester hydrogenation guided laboratory condition choices for four previously studied substrates. Three experiments gave higher alcohol yields than the corresponding literature conditions; a fourth failed despite a high predicted yield. The represented result is a bounded improvement of known reactions, rather than a new catalyst, transformation or universally accurate yield predictor.

Technology: Gaussian-process regression and comparative learned reaction models

Editorial relevance 47 materials Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Learned regressors map substrate/catalyst descriptors and reaction conditions to measured yield and guide new condition choices subsequently tested in the laboratory.
Test and result
The journal paper reports measured alcohol yields of 96%,63% and 99% compared with corresponding literature values of 3%,0% and 32%. The fourth experiment yielded below 1% despite an 84% model prediction.
Limitation / still unknown
An earlier matching preprint,10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-t1zsn, is dated January 10,2022; its full original body was not authenticated, so this record uses the documented May 1,2023 journal version and does not claim first public priority. The substrates and catalysts were known; improvements concern these conditions, not new chemical entities or general catalyst superiority. Prediction errors and high-yield-biased data limit generalization.
