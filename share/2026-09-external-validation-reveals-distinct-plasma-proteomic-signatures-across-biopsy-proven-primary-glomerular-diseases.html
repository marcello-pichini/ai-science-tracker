External validation reveals distinct plasma proteomic signatures across biopsy-proven primary glomerular diseases

Profiling 5,416 plasma proteins in Korean cohorts, the study found distinct signatures across four biopsy-proven primary glomerular diseases. An elastic-net model using 93 proteins differentiated disease subtypes and controls in discovery and external validation cohorts. It achieved AUCs above 0.8 for minimal change disease, membranous nephropathy and IgA nephropathy; the model correctly classified 14 of 15 minimal-change cases and 20 of 32 IgA-nephropathy cases, but only 3 of 14 focal-segmental glomerulosclerosis cases. Functional analysis highlighted hemostasis-related proteins in minimal change disease.

Technology: Elastic-net-regularized logistic regression using plasma proteomic features

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Profiling 5,416 plasma proteins in Korean cohorts, the study found distinct signatures across four biopsy-proven primary glomerular diseases.
  • Still unknown: The discovery cohort contained 147 participants and the external validation cohort 85; subtype-level validation samples were small. Classification was strong for minimal change disease, membranous nephropathy and IgA nephropathy but poor for focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (3/14 correctly classified). The findings support cohort-level proteomic differentiation and do not establish a clinically deployable noninvasive replacement for kidney biopsy or causal roles for the selected proteins.
  • AI role: The model used elastic-net regularization to select and combine a 93-protein plasma signature for classifying biopsy-proven primary glomerular disease subtypes and controls. The model was evaluated in an independent external validation cohort. Functional analysis of key proteins was used to characterize subtype-associated pathways, including hemostasis in minimal change disease.
