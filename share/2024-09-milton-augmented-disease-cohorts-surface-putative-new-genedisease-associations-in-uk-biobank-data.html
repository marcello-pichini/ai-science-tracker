MILTON-augmented disease cohorts surface putative new gene–disease associations in UK Biobank data

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: MILTON uses machine learning on routine clinical biomarkers, quantitative traits and plasma proteomics to predict disease phenotypes and augment case labels in UK Biobank. In a phenome-wide analysis of 484,230 genome-sequenced participants, the authors report 182 putative novel gene–disease associations that were not genome-wide significant in baseline cohorts or explained by biomarker associations; 38.9% of the 167 mappable signals were nominally supported with the same direction in FinnGen.

Technology: MILTON (Machine Learning with Phenotype Associations), an ensemble machine-learning framework for disease prediction from clinical and molecular biomarkers

Editorial relevance 53 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
MILTON predicted cases from clinical biomarkers and other quantitative traits, augmenting phenotype labels for genetic association analyses. The AI-enabled cohort enrichment yielded putative novel gene–disease signals; the study compared them with baseline analyses, orthogonal prioritization tools and FinnGen.
Test and result
In a phenome-wide analysis of 484,230 genome-sequenced participants, the authors report 182 putative novel gene–disease associations that were not genome-wide significant in baseline cohorts or explained by biomarker associations; 38.9% of the 167 mappable signals were nominally supported with the same direction in FinnGen.
Limitation / still unknown
The authors classify the 182 associations as putative novel; they are computational associations, not experimentally established causal genes. FinnGen nominal same-direction support applied to 38.92% (65/167) of the mappable putative novel PheWAS associations, while the paper also reports other aggregate exome-wide results. A separate example, APOB–thyrotoxicosis, showed FinnGen support. These signals require further biological validation.
