MILTON-augmented disease cohorts surface putative new gene–disease associations in UK Biobank data

MILTON uses machine learning on routine clinical biomarkers, quantitative traits and plasma proteomics to predict disease phenotypes and augment case labels in UK Biobank. In a phenome-wide analysis of 484,230 genome-sequenced participants, the authors report 182 putative novel gene–disease associations that were not genome-wide significant in baseline cohorts or explained by biomarker associations; 38.9% of the 167 mappable signals were nominally supported with the same direction in FinnGen.

Technology: MILTON (Machine Learning with Phenotype Associations), an ensemble machine-learning framework for disease prediction from clinical and molecular biomarkers

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: In a phenome-wide analysis of 484,230 genome-sequenced participants, the authors report 182 putative novel gene–disease associations that were not genome-wide significant in baseline cohorts or explained by biomarker associations; 38.9% of the 167 mappable signals were nominally supported with the same direction in FinnGen.
  • Still unknown: The authors classify the 182 associations as putative novel; they are computational associations, not experimentally established causal genes. FinnGen nominal same-direction support applied to 38.92% (65/167) of the mappable putative novel PheWAS associations, while the paper also reports other aggregate exome-wide results. A separate example, APOB–thyrotoxicosis, showed FinnGen support. These signals require further biological validation.
  • AI role: MILTON predicted cases from clinical biomarkers and other quantitative traits, augmenting phenotype labels for genetic association analyses. The AI-enabled cohort enrichment yielded putative novel gene–disease signals; the study compared them with baseline analyses, orthogonal prioritization tools and FinnGen.
