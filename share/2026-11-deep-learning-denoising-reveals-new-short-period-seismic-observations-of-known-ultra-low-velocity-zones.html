Deep-learning denoising reveals new short-period seismic observations of known ultra-low-velocity zones

A supervised deep-learning denoising autoencoder exposed previously undocumented short-period S-diffracted wave observations associated with several known ultra-low-velocity zones (ULVZs). It also revealed four Hawaiian-ULVZ S-diff post-cursor records at Japan F-net stations from East Pacific Rise earthquakes, providing ray paths with mean azimuth about 78° from earlier North American paths. These new cross-cutting observations add constraints on ULVZ geometry and frequency/azimuth-dependent waveforms, but the paper does not present a new ULVZ detection or a completed structural inversion.

Technology: Supervised deep-learning denoising autoencoder (DeepSeisDAE)

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A supervised deep-learning denoising autoencoder exposed previously undocumented short-period S-diffracted wave observations associated with several known ultra-low-velocity zones (ULVZs).
  • Still unknown: This paper reports new seismic observations sampling known ULVZs; it does not establish a new ULVZ structure. The Japanese post-cursors are difficult to separate from the main S-diff phases and require future 3D modelling to determine their interpretation and resolve trade-offs. Denoiser amplitude fidelity is a limitation, especially under training/application mismatch; the authors advise caution for single-station denoising. These are observational constraints, not a completed inversion of shape or composition.
  • AI role: The denoiser was trained on synthetic seismic waveforms mixed with real global noise, then used to separate signal from noise in real S-diff record sections. Its output helped researchers identify short-period post-cursor observations not reported in earlier literature, including four events recorded in Japan with nearly perpendicular ray geometry to previous North American observations of the Hawaiian ULVZ.
