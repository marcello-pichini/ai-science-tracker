Machine learning uncovers new small-molecule chloride transporters from DrugBank

After high-throughput testing 1,894 compounds and confirming 59 chloride transporters, researchers trained and evaluated machine-learning classifiers, then prioritized DrugBank compounds for wet-lab tests. Five of ten selected drugs—bazedoxifene, centhaquine, brilliant green, tamoxifen and nevanimibe—were classified by the authors as active chloride transporters, with an estimated 50–60% hit rate under their assays.

Technology: Machine-learning binary classifiers and consensus QSAR models trained on experimentally measured chloride-transport activity

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: After high-throughput testing 1,894 compounds and confirming 59 chloride transporters, researchers trained and evaluated machine-learning classifiers, then prioritized DrugBank compounds for wet-lab tests.
  • Still unknown: The paper combines a large primary high-throughput screen with a later ML-led DrugBank virtual screen; do not attribute all 59 initial hits to ML. The five drug-repurposing hits were measured in artificial liposome assays, not cells, animals or patients. The paper discusses assay artifacts: quinacrine/brilliant green interfere with the fluorescent assay, HCl salts can affect electrode readings, and evidence for brilliant green was weaker than for the other confirmed compounds. Authors report 5/10 active among selected candidates (or 7/12 including two known active compounds), not a generalizable clinical hit rate.
  • AI role: After initial experimental discovery data were collected, machine-learning models ranked DrugBank candidates for transport activity. Researchers synthesized no new candidates for the virtual screen; instead, they tested ten prioritized available compounds in liposome chloride-transport assays. Five were classed as active; results extend these compounds' known pharmacology with membrane chloride transport activity.
