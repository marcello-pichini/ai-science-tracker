Spatial glycan imaging and machine learning identify a serum N-glycan panel for HCC in cirrhosis

Spatial N-glycan imaging of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissue and paired serum identified thirteen altered glycan structures; glycoproteomics linked them to more than 50 proteins, and sixteen glycoproteins were assessed in a separate patient set. Machine-learning algorithms combining glycan/glycoprotein features with clinical variables differentiated early-stage HCC from cirrhosis in a case-control cohort of 100 HCC and 101 cirrhosis-only patients, with reported AUROC values across the algorithms of 0.88–0.97.

Technology: Machine-learning algorithms using serum N-glycan and glycoprotein features, age, sex and AFP

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Machine-learning algorithms combining glycan/glycoprotein features with clinical variables differentiated early-stage HCC from cirrhosis in a case-control cohort of 100 HCC and 101 cirrhosis-only patients, with reported AUROC values across the algorithms of 0.88–0.97.
  • Still unknown: The discovery samples included 53 HCC tissues and 23 matched sera; the 201-patient testing set was a case-control cohort, not prospective surveillance. The paper notes that sialic acid was removed before analysis, excluding an important class of glycan modification. It does not establish screening benefit or prospective clinical utility. Individual glycoforms were weak classifiers; performance came from combined feature algorithms.
  • AI role: Researchers used spatial glycan imaging and glycoproteomics to select altered tissue and matched-serum features, then used machine-learning algorithms to combine selected glycan/glycoprotein measurements with clinical variables for HCC-versus-cirrhosis classification. The source reports model performance in an independent case-control patient set.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-024-00677-7.pdf
