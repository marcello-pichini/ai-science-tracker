Bayesian optimization experimentally identifies a high-AHE Fe–Co–Ni–Ta–Ir amorphous film

An autonomous closed-loop search combining composition-spread experiments with Bayesian optimization reached a maximum anomalous Hall resistivity of 10.9 μΩ·cm in an amorphous Fe44.9Co27.9Ni12.1Ta3.3Ir11.7 thin film deposited at room temperature. This measured value exceeded the study’s target of 10 μΩ·cm and the values found in the initial random-exploration cycles.

Technology: Bayesian optimization for composition-spread experiments

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: An autonomous closed-loop search combining composition-spread experiments with Bayesian optimization reached a maximum anomalous Hall resistivity of 10.9 μΩ·cm in an amorphous Fe44.9Co27.9Ni12.1Ta3.3Ir11.7 thin film deposited at room temperature.
  • Still unknown: The 10.9 μΩ·cm maximum is the best among 234 compositions explored over 18 cycles in this search, not a global proof of the maximum over all compositions. The demonstrated film is amorphous and was measured at room temperature on thermally oxidized silicon; the study does not report a completed sensor device or independent reproduction. Although the experimental decision loop was automated, human sample transfers between instruments remained.
  • AI role: The method selects which five-element alloy composition-spread film to make next and which pair of elements to grade across it, using prior AHE measurements. The closed loop then executes deposition, multi-device fabrication and AHE measurement, feeding results into the next composition proposal; the Bayesian optimization was central to reaching the high-AHE Fe–Co–Ni–Ta–Ir composition.
