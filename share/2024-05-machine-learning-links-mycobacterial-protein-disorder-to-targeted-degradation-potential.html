Machine learning links mycobacterial protein disorder to targeted degradation potential

Researchers combined a genetic chemical-proximity platform and experimental screening of 72 Mycobacterium smegmatis protein candidates with machine learning. A Lasso model trained on measured degradation data identified N-terminal disorder propensity as associated with degradation potential and predicted candidate targets among 348 essential proteins conserved between M. smegmatis and M. tuberculosis. Experimentally induced degradation of selected essential M. smegmatis proteins inhibited bacterial growth and, for some targets, potentiated antimicrobial compounds.

Technology: Lasso regression model trained on sequence and structural protein descriptors, including AlphaFold2 confidence and predicted disorder propensity

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Researchers combined a genetic chemical-proximity platform and experimental screening of 72 Mycobacterium smegmatis protein candidates with machine learning.
  • Still unknown: Most mechanistic and growth evidence was generated in M. smegmatis, a non-pathogenic model, not in clinical treatment or an animal efficacy study. The machine-learning target predictions for the larger set of 348 essential proteins require orthogonal experimental validation. The study reports that targeted degradation of some proteins inhibits growth and potentiates antimicrobials; this does not establish a deployable drug or clinical benefit. Primary paper source checked; no independent replication assessed.
  • AI role: Machine learning connected measured degradation constants from experimentally screened protein substrates to protein sequence/structure features, identifying N-terminal disorder propensity as a predictor of degradation potential; the trained model then ranked candidate targets among essential proteins conserved between M. smegmatis and M. tuberculosis. Experiments—not the model alone—showed degradation and antibacterial phenotypes for selected targets.

Evidence sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-48506-8
