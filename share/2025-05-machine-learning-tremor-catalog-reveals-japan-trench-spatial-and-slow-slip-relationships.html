Machine-learning tremor catalog reveals Japan Trench spatial and slow-slip relationships

A machine-learning monitoring system applied to eight years of S-net seafloor records detected seven times more tectonic tremors than the previous envelope-correlation catalog. The denser catalog resolved tremor activity extending along strike and dip, tremor patterns synchronized with slow-slip events, higher tremor energy rates near large-earthquake asperities, and a positive relationship between energy rate and recurrence interval; authors interpret the latter as evidence consistent with plate-boundary frictional heterogeneity.

Technology: DiET convolutional neural network and GrASP graph-based station association, with hybrid envelope-correlation/energy-ratio hypocenter location

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: A machine-learning monitoring system applied to eight years of S-net seafloor records detected seven times more tectonic tremors than the previous envelope-correlation catalog.
  • Still unknown: The paper reports a substantial catalog expansion and interprets energy-rate patterns as suggestive of frictional heterogeneity; this is not a prospective earthquake forecast or proof of a causal friction mechanism. The abstract states prior work had already observed complementary tremor/earthquake distributions in the region, so the new contribution is the expanded catalog and improved resolution of the relationships, not the first observation of any complementary distribution.
  • AI role: The CNN classified station spectrograms as earthquake, tremor or noise; graph clustering associated station detections, enabling a higher-resolution catalog from continuous S-net data. Human researchers interpreted the spatial and temporal relationships.
