Machine-learning-guided screening discovers potent innate immune signaling modulators

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: At the University of Chicago, an active-learning pipeline iteratively selected molecules from a 139,998-compound library for in-vitro assays of NF-κB and interferon regulatory factor (IRF) signaling. After screening about 2% of the library, the study reported compounds suppressing NF-κB up to 15-fold or elevating NF-κB and IRF up to 5-fold and 6-fold, respectively. Follow-up cytokine assays found PME-4007 increased IFN-β secretion about threefold when co-delivered with the STING agonist cGAMP.

Technology: Machine-learning active-learning pipeline with variational-autoencoder molecular representations, Gaussian-process regression and Bayesian optimization

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Models were iteratively trained on screening measurements and used Bayesian optimization to select subsequent compounds for in-vitro testing. This model-guided selection led to the reported NF-κB/IRF modulators; follow-up cytokine release assays tested a subset.
Test and result
At the University of Chicago, an active-learning pipeline iteratively selected molecules from a 139,998-compound library for in-vitro assays of NF-κB and interferon regulatory factor (IRF) signaling.
Limitation / still unknown
The work reports in-vitro pathway modulation and primary-cell cytokine assays, not vaccination, immunotherapy efficacy, animal outcomes or clinical benefit. Fold changes are study-specific relative to agonist-only controls. A subset of candidates was further characterized; four tested compounds were flagged as non-viable by the cytostasis/cytotoxicity confluency mask despite strong pathway effects.
