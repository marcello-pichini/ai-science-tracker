A machine-learning plasma panel classifies some REM sleep behavior disorder participants years before motor Parkinson diagnosis

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Researchers developed a targeted multiplex mass-spectrometry assay for eight plasma proteins and applied machine learning to distinguish Parkinson disease from healthy controls and classify people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD). In the reported longitudinal iRBD cohorts, the model classified 79% of participants up to seven years before motor Parkinson disease or dementia with Lewy bodies onset. The work connects a targeted protein panel to a pre-motor disease signal in measured blood samples.

Technology: Machine-learning classifier using recursive feature elimination on targeted plasma proteomic measurements

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The classifier selected an eight-protein panel from targeted mass-spectrometry measurements, then classified motor Parkinson cases and iRBD participants; the longitudinal analysis linked panel scores with later clinical conversion.
Test and result
Researchers developed a targeted multiplex mass-spectrometry assay for eight plasma proteins and applied machine learning to distinguish Parkinson disease from healthy controls and classify people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD).
Limitation / still unknown
The discovery phase used only 10 Parkinson cases and 10 controls. Although the study includes larger targeted-assay cohorts, the longitudinal iRBD groups were small (18 and 54 participants), and the finding is not a validated screening test or proof that intervention changes outcomes. The model’s apparent early classification needs larger prospective cohorts.
