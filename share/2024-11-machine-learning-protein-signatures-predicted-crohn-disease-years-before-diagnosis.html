Machine-learning protein signatures predicted Crohn disease years before diagnosis

Regularized logistic-regression and other machine-learning analyses of 178 Olink proteins identified a 29-protein preclinical Crohn disease signature. It distinguished future cases from controls with AUC 0.85 in a Swedish discovery cohort and 0.87 in an independent preclinical cohort; a separate inception cohort produced AUC 1.0. The study also reported a less predictive ulcerative-colitis signature and found that downregulated proteins related to gut-barrier integrity and macrophage function correlated with time to Crohn diagnosis.

Technology: Regularized logistic regression and additional machine-learning methods (L1-penalized regression, elastic net, support-vector machines and random forests) applied to Olink protein measurements

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: It distinguished future cases from controls with AUC 0.85 in a Swedish discovery cohort and 0.87 in an independent preclinical cohort; a separate inception cohort produced AUC 1.0.
  • Still unknown: This is a nested case-control study using biobanked samples, not prospective screening or intervention evidence. The paper reports that the Crohn signature’s AUC fell to 0.58 when preclinical twins were compared with their healthy twin siblings, suggesting inherited/shared-environment factors affect discrimination. The ulcerative-colitis validation AUC was lower (0.67). Protein associations do not establish causal mechanisms; authors call for mechanistic studies. Source checked; independent replication beyond reported cohorts was not assessed.
  • AI role: Machine-learning feature selection/modeling produced preclinical Crohn and ulcerative-colitis protein signatures in the discovery cohort. The fitted signature was evaluated in an external population-based preclinical cohort, an inception cohort at diagnosis, and twin comparisons. The result is predictive association from cohort data; the paper says mechanistic studies are needed.

Evidence sources: https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085%2824%2905741-X/pdf
