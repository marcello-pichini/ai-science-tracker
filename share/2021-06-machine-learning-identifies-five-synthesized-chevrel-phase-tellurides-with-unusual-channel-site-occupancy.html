Machine learning identifies five synthesized Chevrel-phase tellurides with unusual channel-site occupancy

An interpretable machine-learned descriptor for decomposition enthalpy screened more than 200,000 Chevrel-phase compositions, predicting 48,501 below the study’s statistical synthesizability cutoff. Researchers selected five previously unreported tellurides for synthesis and successfully made all five; experiments also confirmed the predicted preference for intercalant cations to occupy Chevrel-phase channels, a rare motif in this family. The predicted candidate list is much larger than the experimentally attempted and confirmed subset. The date is the journal paper’s online publication; UC Davis announced it on 24 June 2021.

Technology: SIFT descriptor-generation and interpretable machine-learning model Hδ

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: An interpretable machine-learned descriptor for decomposition enthalpy screened more than 200,000 Chevrel-phase compositions, predicting 48,501 below the study’s statistical synthesizability cutoff.
  • Still unknown: The original paper abstract reports five successful syntheses among five tellurides attempted and confirms a predicted channel-site occupancy trend. The other thousands of predicted candidates were not demonstrated as synthesized in this study. The result concerns a specific molybdenum-chalcogenide family.
  • AI role: The SIFT method generated more than 560,000 candidate descriptors; an interpretable ML model trained against DFT-computed decomposition enthalpies assessed synthesizability and selected Chevrel-phase compositions for experimental follow-up.
