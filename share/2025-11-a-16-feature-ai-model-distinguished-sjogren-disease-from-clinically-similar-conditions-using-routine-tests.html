A 16-feature AI model distinguished Sjögren disease from clinically similar conditions using routine tests

The retrospective multicenter SMFIF study screened routine laboratory data and used multi-algorithm feature selection, SHAP ranking and a stacked ensemble to construct a 16-feature Sjögren disease model. Across a 34,958-patient source population from three Chinese hospitals, reported AUCs were 0.923 in testing, 0.934 in internal validation and 0.964 in a 545-person external cohort. The study also compared the model with conventional autoantibody and laboratory markers and evaluated discrimination from rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, systemic sclerosis and osteoarthritis.

Technology: SMFIF, a stacked ensemble of five selected classifiers built from multi-algorithm feature selection and SHAP-ranked routine laboratory features

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: Across a 34,958-patient source population from three Chinese hospitals, reported AUCs were 0.923 in testing, 0.934 in internal validation and 0.964 in a 545-person external cohort.
  • Still unknown: All cohorts were retrospective and drawn from three hospitals in China; the external cohort had 545 people, including 139 Sjögren cases. External calibration showed greater variability, and the authors call for prospective and multinational validation. Case groups had longer recorded symptom duration than controls and higher organ involvement, which may contribute to discrimination. The study does not show clinical impact, improved outcomes, or replacement of diagnostic criteria/clinical assessment.
  • AI role: The AI pipeline screened 70 candidate features from routine laboratory tests, selected a 16-feature set using multiple feature-selection methods and SHAP ranking, and combined five base classifiers in a stacking model. The resulting score estimated Sjögren disease probability and was evaluated on a held-out test set, internal validation set and external-hospital cohort.

Evidence sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12660861/
