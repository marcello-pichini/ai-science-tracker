EvoGradient evolves antimicrobial peptides active against resistant pathogens

EvoGradient uses an explainable deep-learning potency model and virtual sequence evolution to optimize candidate antimicrobial peptides. Applied to peptides encoded by low-abundance human oral bacteria, it virtually evolved 32 candidates; the six most effective were synthesized and tested against multidrug-resistant pathogens. The authors report activity across carbapenem-resistant E. coli, K. pneumoniae and A. baumannii and vancomycin-resistant E. faecium; pep-19-mod reduced bacterial loads by more than 95% in mouse thigh-infection models under the reported systemic and local administrations. These are preclinical findings and do not establish human efficacy or safety.

Technology: Explainable deep-learning antimicrobial-peptide potency model with gradient-based virtual sequence evolution

  • Tested: Six synthesized peptides were tested against multidrug-resistant pathogens; the authors also report pep-19-mod in-vivo testing in mouse thigh-infection models, with over 95% bacterial-load reduction under the described systemic and local administration.

  • AI role: EvoGradient predicts peptide potency and virtually modifies peptide sequences through gradient-based evolution; the workflow selected optimized candidates for synthesis and experimental testing.
