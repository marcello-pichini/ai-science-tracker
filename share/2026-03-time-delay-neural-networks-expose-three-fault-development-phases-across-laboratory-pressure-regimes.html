Time-delay neural networks expose three fault-development phases across laboratory pressure regimes

A time-delay neural network trained on multi-parameter acoustic-emission records from Alzo-granite triaxial tests at 5–40 MPa resolved three recurring phases: microcrack nucleation marked by waveform-scattering changes, fault growth with evolving spatial and magnitude distributions, and coalescence marked by peak-delay increases and b-value changes. The model also predicted stress-drop timing and size across axial-splitting and shear-localization failures. This is a laboratory result, not a natural-earthquake precursor claim.

Technology: Genetic-algorithm-optimized time-delay neural network (TDNN)

  • Still unknown: The analysis uses four laboratory experiments in Alzo granite, so results are sample- and apparatus-limited and do not establish natural-earthquake predictability. The observed phase interpretation is based on model-assisted analysis of acoustic emissions and measured mechanical data.
  • AI role: The TDNN integrated time-dependent acoustic-emission waveform features (peak delay, coda Q/scattering) and occurrence measures (event rate, Gutenberg–Richter b-value, spatial fractal dimension), dynamically weighting them to model stress/strain evolution and identify repeatable phases across pressure conditions. Leave-one-experiment-out validation tested cross-condition generalization.
