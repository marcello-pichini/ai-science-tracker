THESEUS derives resource-efficient designs for high-dimensional photonic GHZ and heralded Bell states

The interpretable THESEUS design algorithm produced a three-dimensional six-photon GHZ-state blueprint that suppresses unwanted state terms toward arbitrarily high fidelity, with lower count rates, and a heralded three-dimensional Bell-state blueprint using destructive interference to cancel vacuum contributions. The same graph-based reasoning generalized the constructions to larger photon numbers or dimensions.

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: retrospective or benchmark
  • Tested: The interpretable THESEUS design algorithm produced a three-dimensional six-photon GHZ-state blueprint that suppresses unwanted state terms toward arbitrarily high fidelity, with lower count rates, and a heralded three-dimensional Bell-state blueprint using destructive interference to cancel vacuum contributions.
  • Still unknown: These are theoretically calculated experiment blueprints, not experimentally demonstrated states in this paper. For the six-photon GHZ design, unwanted amplitudes can be reduced arbitrarily by adjusting graph weights, at the cost of lower count rates; feasibility is argued for contemporary photonics. The heralded Bell design’s estimated count rate and fidelity are modeled, not measured.
  • AI role: The authors’ AI-enabled automated design algorithm used an interpretable weighted, colored-graph representation of photonic experiments, gradient-based optimization, and topology reduction to search open experiment-design problems and expose generalizable physical concepts. The authors identify the work as explainable AI; the contribution is the algorithmically found design/concept, not merely faster benchmarking.
