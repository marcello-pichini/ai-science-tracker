CAIMAN validates AI prescreening of typical and atypical large-bowel biopsies across UK and Portuguese cohorts

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: CAIMAN is a weakly supervised deep-learning model trained on pathologist-labeled whole-slide images to classify typical, atypical non-neoplastic and atypical neoplastic bowel biopsies. In three external cohorts comprising 1,536 slides from the UK and Portugal, it detected atypical biopsies with AUROC values of 0.9431, 0.9576 and 0.9636. At 99% sensitivity, it outperformed the prior IDaRS pipeline on two external cohorts, but not the third.

Technology: CAIMAN (Colorectal AI Model for Abnormality Detection), a weakly supervised deep-learning whole-slide image classifier

Editorial relevance 47 medicine Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
AI learned biopsy-level histological patterns from slide-level diagnostic labels, then classified whole-slide images into typical, atypical non-neoplastic or atypical neoplastic classes. The trained frozen model was evaluated on three external cohorts; the reported finding is a specific retrospective clinical diagnostic-validation result.
Test and result
In three external cohorts comprising 1,536 slides from the UK and Portugal, it detected atypical biopsies with AUROC values of 0.9431, 0.9576 and 0.9636.
Limitation / still unknown
Retrospective validation on archived biopsies does not establish clinical deployment or improved patient outcomes. CAIMAN did not outperform IDaRS at 99% sensitivity in one of the three external validation cohorts; the authors call for larger multicentre validation and a clinical trial. The reported 99% sensitivity is paired with cohort-specific specificity, not a universal performance guarantee.
