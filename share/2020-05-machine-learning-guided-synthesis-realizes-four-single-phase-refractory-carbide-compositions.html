Machine-learning-guided synthesis realizes four single-phase refractory carbide compositions

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A random-forest model ranked new chromium-containing multicomponent carbide compositions by predicted entropy-forming ability. After density-functional calculations and experimental synthesis, X-ray diffraction identified four previously unreported single-phase rock-salt compositions: CrMoNbVWC₅, CrMoNbTaWC₅, CrMoTaVWC₅, and CrMoTiVWC₅. The first three were high-EFA selections; CrMoTiVWC₅ was a medium-EFA composition and required extended annealing to reach chemical homogeneity.

Technology: Random-forest models using chemical attributes, with and without CALPHAD thermodynamic features

Editorial relevance 53 materials Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The random-forest models learned entropy-forming ability from 56 previously reported carbide compositions and rapidly screened 70 new chromium-containing candidates. Model rankings guided selection of seven compositions for DFT calculations and synthesis; experimental phase outcomes agreed with predicted rankings, including four single-phase materials.
Test and result
A random-forest model ranked new chromium-containing multicomponent carbide compositions by predicted entropy-forming ability.
Limitation / still unknown
Only seven of the 70 model-screened candidates were selected for DFT and synthesis, and several tested compositions formed multiple phases. Single-phase identification used XRD and was supported by microstructure, elemental mapping and EBSD; the medium-EFA CrMoTiVWC₅ sample required extended annealing. The paper establishes synthesis and phase formation, not measured application performance or engineering readiness.
