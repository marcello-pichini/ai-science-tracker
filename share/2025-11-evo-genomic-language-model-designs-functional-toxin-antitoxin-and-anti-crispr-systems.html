Evo genomic language model designs functional toxin-antitoxin and anti-CRISPR systems

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: Evo used genomic context to generate functional bacterial defence-system components. The paper reports an AI-generated toxin and low-sequence-similarity antitoxins that neutralized it in growth assays, including antitoxins that also acted against multiple natural toxins; it also reports five generated anti-CRISPR proteins that protected bacteria against Cas9 activity in cell and phage assays. Two generated anti-CRISPR proteins lacked significant sequence similarity to known proteins. These experiments demonstrate function-guided genomic design beyond simple copying of natural sequences.

Technology: Evo 1.5, a long-context generative genomic language model trained on prokaryotic DNA sequences

AI contribution
Researchers prompted Evo 1.5 with genomic contexts that associate functionally related bacterial genes and used it to generate candidate toxin-antitoxin and anti-CRISPR sequences. Candidates were filtered computationally, synthesized/expressed and tested in bacterial growth, Cas9-protection and phage assays. AI generated candidates; the biological activity was established experimentally.
Test and result
The paper reports an AI-generated toxin and low-sequence-similarity antitoxins that neutralized it in growth assays, including antitoxins that also acted against multiple natural toxins; it also reports five generated anti-CRISPR proteins that protected bacteria against Cas9 activity in cell and phage assays.
Limitation / still unknown
This is a set of functional in-vitro bacterial assays, not evidence of therapeutic use, broad host range or ecological performance. One generated toxin had 71% sequence identity to a known RelE toxin; novelty is especially supported by low-similarity antitoxins and anti-CRISPRs, two of which lacked significant sequence identity to known proteins. Earlier Evo phage-genome design and Evo 2 regulatory-sequence work are different papers and different functional results; this row is limited to the toxin-antitoxin/anti-CRISPR findings in the cited Nature article.
