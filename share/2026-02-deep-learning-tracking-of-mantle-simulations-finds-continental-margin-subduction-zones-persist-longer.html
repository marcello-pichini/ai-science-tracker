Deep-learning tracking of mantle simulations finds continental-margin subduction zones persist longer

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A fully convolutional network segmented subduction zones in two-dimensional mantle-convection simulations using temperature, vertical velocity and fineness fields. Across the modeled runs, zones forming near continental margins were generally longer-lived and less sensitive to internal heating than oceanic zones, supporting the hypothesis that continental lithosphere can stabilize subduction during early-Earth-like conditions. This is a computational-model result rather than direct observation of ancient Earth.

Technology: Attention-based fully convolutional network with custom segmentation loss

AI contribution
Provided threshold-free, spatially contextual tracking of evolving subduction zones in simulation outputs; the resulting tracked histories enabled statistical comparison of lifetimes and sensitivity to mantle convective vigor by tectonic setting.
Test and result
A fully convolutional network segmented subduction zones in two-dimensional mantle-convection simulations using temperature, vertical velocity and fineness fields.
Limitation / still unknown
The physical inference comes from two-dimensional numerical mantle-convection models and their selected parameter space, not empirical reconstruction of Earth's early subduction history. The AI performs segmentation/tracking; the tectonic contrast is a statistical result from model outputs.
