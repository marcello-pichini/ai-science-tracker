AI-assisted work proves a Latin-square imbalance bound, with optimal examples through order 52

A collaborative LLM-plus-symbolic-computation study proved that every n×n Latin square with n ≡ 1 (mod 3) has imbalance at least 4n(n−1)/9. It introduced near-perfect permutations that attain the bound and computationally found independently verified matching examples for every such order from 4 through 52. The universal lower-bound theorem was formally checked in Lean 4.

Technology: Claude Opus 4.5 LLM agent with SageMath, Rust exhaustive solver, simulated annealing and Lean 4 verification tools

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: prospective or independent check
  • Tested: A collaborative LLM-plus-symbolic-computation study proved that every n×n Latin square with n ≡ 1 (mod 3) has imbalance at least 4n(n−1)/9.
  • Still unknown: This is an arXiv preprint (v2 dated 2026-08-13), not yet peer-reviewed in the sources checked. The universal proven theorem is a lower bound for n ≡ 1 mod 3. Matching constructions, and hence demonstrated tightness, are computationally established only for n=4,7,…,52; the paper does not prove that the bound is attained for every larger order. Lean 4 formalizes the lower-bound inequality, not the finite construction search or universal tightness.
  • AI role: The agent identified an all-even row-distance parity pattern in numerical search data, formulated and helped prove the lower bound, and used model review to catch proof errors. SageMath and a Rust solver supported symbolic analysis and enumeration; simulated annealing found matching constructions through n=52. Human strategic direction supplied the decisive pivot to the minimum-positive-imbalance question. Lean 4 formally verifies the lower-bound theorem itself.
