AI-guided experiments uncover new zeolitic imidazolate framework crystals

AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks. The campaign crystallized new structures using 10 of 48 previously untested linkers, doubling the discovery rate of random exploration (700 versus 1,400 experiments) and increasing the single-linker Zn-ZIF library by about one-third. The paper reports structure-resolved products, including eleven previously unreported crystals across its discovery campaign.

Technology: Algorithmic Iterative Reticular Synthesis (AIRES)

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: AIRES combined probabilistic machine-learning predictions with robotic synthesis, optical crystal screening and single-crystal X-ray diffraction to explore zinc zeolitic imidazolate frameworks.
  • Still unknown: This is one paper-level experimental discovery campaign. The reported efficiency comparison is against a random-exploration baseline within the study, not an independent replication. Structures were identified by single-crystal X-ray diffraction; the paper describes discovery of ZIF crystals but does not establish application performance. The campaign covered a defined single-linker Zn-ZIF chemical and reaction space, not all reticular materials.
  • AI role: AIRES used probabilistic random-forest and Gaussian-process crystallization models to guide experiment selection across linker chemistries and reaction conditions. Its greedy and batch decision rules prioritized experiments predicted to yield distinct new crystals; automated synthesis, computer-vision crystal screening and subsequent single-crystal X-ray diffraction supplied experimental outcomes for model updates and structure determination. The AI-guided campaign found structures for 10 of 48 new linkers, with twice the distinct-crystal discovery rate of random exploration.
