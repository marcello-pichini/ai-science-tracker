AI co-scientist: a multi-agent system for generating hypotheses

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A system based on Gemini 2.0 that generates hypotheses and research proposals. In the lab, three drugs it suggested for repurposing against acute myeloid leukemia reduced cancer-cell viability, and all suggested treatments for liver fibrosis showed activity. Expert assessments covered only 11 targets; fibrosis results awaited a Stanford report.

Technology: AI co-scientist (Gemini 2.0, multi-agent)

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
A Gemini-based multi-agent system generated, debated and ranked hypotheses; experts selected candidates and prioritized lab experiments.
Test and result
Elo auto-evaluation over 203 research goals; 15 expert-curated goals; blinded experts rated 11 goals (preference rank 2.36). In vitro on four AML cell lines: binimetinib, pacritinib, cerivastatin reduced viability; KIRA6 IC50 10 nM in KG-1a versus 180 nM in TK6. Seven combinations tested.
Limitation / still unknown
The paper calls validation preliminary and in vitro only. KIRA6 was far less effective in MOLM-13 and HL-60. Expert evaluation was small and subjective; Elo is not ground truth. Liver fibrosis and AMR details are summarized only.
