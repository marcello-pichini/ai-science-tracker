Machine-learning feature selection identifies student-performance factors in Nepal

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: A study analyzed 1,021 survey responses from engineering students reflecting on their first year at colleges affiliated with Tribhuvan University in Nepal. A feature-selection vote including Random Forest and AdaBoost selected 21 candidate academic, psychometric and demographic factors, triangulated with 11 focus-group themes and expert review. The reported set included adaptation, motivation, residence and travel-time factors alongside academic measures. These context-dependent selections do not establish causal effects, externally validated predictive performance or transfer beyond the sampled institution system.

Technology: Random Forest and AdaBoost within a multi-method feature-selection vote

Editorial relevance 40 humanities Published article Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: retrospective or benchmark. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
Random Forest and AdaBoost were included in a multi-method feature-selection vote used to derive the reported student-performance correlates; qualitative focus-group themes and expert review were then used to triangulate them.
Test and result
Survey data contained 1,021 valid responses; quantitative feature selection used a voting threshold of three and produced 21 features, which were triangulated with 11 focus-group themes and expert review. Fourteen selected features were academic, four psychometric and three demographic.
Limitation / still unknown
Single-context sample from IOE-affiliated Tribhuvan University colleges; qualitative collection may contain bias; the authors explicitly say the selected determinants are not causal. No external prediction-performance validation is reported.
