HydrAMP generates antimicrobial peptide analogues validated in bacterial assays

HydrAMP, a conditional variational autoencoder with antimicrobial activity classifiers, generated peptide analogues and de novo candidates. The 2022 preprint reports wet-lab confirmation of increased activity for a generated Pexiganan-based analogue; the 2023 journal version reports testing across five bacterial strains, including nine analogues of clinical prototypes and six analogues of an inactive peptide. The tested panel was small and model/human filtered; it does not establish therapeutic efficacy.

Technology: Conditional variational autoencoder with classifier-guided peptide generation and molecular-dynamics-based candidate preselection

Result, AI contribution and limits

Evidence context (descriptions follow the cited sources; test type is a catalog classification):

  • Publication status: Preprint
  • Test type: direct test
  • Tested: Generated peptide analogues were experimentally evaluated for antimicrobial activity across five bacterial strains; the journal abstract reports high activity for nine generated analogues of clinical prototypes and six analogues of an inactive peptide.
  • Still unknown: The tested panel is selected and small; these are in-vitro assays and do not establish safety, therapeutic efficacy or clinical benefit. The 2023 correction replaced a duplicated generation-statistics figure, not the wet-lab result stated here.
  • AI role: A conditional VAE generated peptide analogues and unconstrained candidates from antimicrobial activity conditions; ranking and molecular-dynamics preselection helped choose candidates for experimental validation.
