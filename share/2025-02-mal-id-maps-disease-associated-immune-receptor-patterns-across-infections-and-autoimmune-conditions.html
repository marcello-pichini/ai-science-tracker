Mal-ID maps disease-associated immune-receptor patterns across infections and autoimmune conditions

Mal-ID analyzed B-cell and T-cell receptor repertoires from 593 people, including patients with COVID-19, HIV, systemic lupus erythematosus and type-1 diabetes, influenza-vaccination recipients and healthy controls. Its combined models classified held-out immune states and highlighted disease-associated receptor patterns; interpretation identified distinct autoreactivity features in lupus and type-1 diabetes and antigen-associated receptor sequences in COVID-19. The authors also evaluated classification on independent COVID-19 cohorts.

Technology: Mal-ID (MAchine Learning for Immunological Diagnosis), an ensemble using repertoire feature models and ESM-2 protein-language-model embeddings

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: The authors also evaluated classification on independent COVID-19 cohorts.
  • Still unknown: This is a patient-sample diagnostic-validation and immune-biology study, not evidence of clinical deployment or benefit. Validation cohorts and disease-specific sample sizes are limited; external COVID-19 classification involved small cohorts and thresholds were tuned in some analyses. Additional disease-specific validation, threshold calibration, sequencing-depth assessment and interpretation alongside standard clinical assessment remain necessary. Its 2024 bioRxiv preprint is the same work, not a separate finding.
  • AI role: Three machine-learning representations of B-cell and T-cell receptor data were combined to classify immune states and prioritize disease-associated receptor sequences, gene segments and isotypes for biological interpretation.
