Deep-learning CT response score stratifies outcomes in lung-cancer immunotherapy cohorts

A deep-learning radiomic pipeline generated a pretreatment CT response score (CTRS) for advanced non-small-cell lung cancer treated with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors. In an independent real-world test cohort, high-response-score first-line ICI-monotherapy patients had longer progression-free and overall survival after adjustment for baseline covariates including PD-L1 (adjusted HR 0.46 and 0.50). In a secondary analysis of 54 participants from a prospective sasanlimab trial, the score was associated with overall survival (adjusted HR 0.33) but not progression-free survival.

Technology: Three-dimensional hierarchical convolutional neural network feature extractor combined with a Cox survival model (CT response score, CTRS)

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: In an independent real-world test cohort, high-response-score first-line ICI-monotherapy patients had longer progression-free and overall survival after adjustment for baseline covariates including PD-L1 (adjusted HR 0.46 and 0.50).
  • Still unknown: The real-world data were retrospective. Association of the score with survival among treated patients does not establish that the score predicts differential treatment benefit versus an untreated or alternative-treatment control. Clinical-trial analysis was secondary and small (n=54): PFS association was not significant, while OS association was significant. Authors say larger prospective studies are needed; no improved-care or deployment outcome is shown.
  • AI role: The model learned image features from pretreatment CT/PET-CT scans and combined them with a Cox survival model to produce an imaging-only response score. Researchers evaluated the score in a held-out real-world cohort and in a secondary analysis of a prospective clinical-trial cohort; it stratified PFS/OS in the first and OS in the latter.
