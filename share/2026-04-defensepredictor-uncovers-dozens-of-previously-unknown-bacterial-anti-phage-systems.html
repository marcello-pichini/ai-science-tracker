DefensePredictor uncovers dozens of previously unknown bacterial anti-phage systems

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: DefensePredictor, a machine-learning classifier using protein-language-model embeddings, nominated candidate defense genes across diverse E. coli strains. Of 94 predicted gene clusters selected for testing, experiments validated 42 previously unknown systems; analyses of 1,000 prokaryotic genomes also revealed thousands of uncharacterized protein clusters without homology to known systems.

Technology: DefensePredictor, gradient-boosted classification over protein-language-model embeddings and genomic context

Editorial relevance 60 biology Original sourcePaper Suggest a correction

Publication: Peer-reviewed paper · Test context: direct test. Catalog categories, not an evidence grade.

AI contribution
The machine-learning model identified likely defense proteins beyond conventional defense-island co-localization methods, guiding experimental cloning and phage-challenge validation. Validation confirmed 42 new systems among 94 tested candidates.
Test and result
Of 94 predicted gene clusters selected for testing, experiments validated 42 previously unknown systems; analyses of 1,000 prokaryotic genomes also revealed thousands of uncharacterized protein clusters without homology to known systems.
Limitation / still unknown
The 42 validated systems came from a selected subset of predictions, not all computationally proposed systems; genome-wide counts are predicted protein clusters, not experimentally tested systems. The source reports a 45% validation rate for 94 tested units.
