AlphaFold-assisted study links WFS1 dysfunction to GnRH-neuron developmental defects

Source check: Source checked. Not independent scientific confirmation.

Finding: An integrated human-genetics, structural-modeling and experimental study identified WFS1 as a candidate modifier of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. AlphaFold 3 models supported simulations of rare WFS1 variants, including altered protein motions that were not captured by a simple benign/damaging prediction. Cell assays found impaired protein behavior and ER-stress activation; zebrafish knockdown disrupted GnRH-neuron architecture and reduced isl1a expression, with partial pharmacological rescue. These results support a developmental mechanism in model systems, not proof that WFS1 alone causes the human disorder or that a treatment benefits patients.

Technology: AlphaFold 3 structural modeling; PolyPhen-2 variant prediction

AI contribution
AlphaFold 3 supplied monomeric and tetrameric WFS1 models for variant-specific membrane simulations and structural interpretation. PolyPhen-2 assisted variant assessment; experimental genetics and functional assays supplied the biological evidence. AI did not independently identify the gene from the exomes.
Test and result
Two rare heterozygous WFS1 variants in two families were examined using predicted structural models, replicated molecular-dynamics simulations and cell assays. Embryonic expression studies and zebrafish wfs1b knockdown/rescue experiments assessed GnRH-neuron development and ER-stress/isl1a changes.
Limitation / still unknown
Oligogenic inheritance and model-system results do not establish WFS1 as a sufficient human disease cause. Pharmacological rescue is partial and includes pleiotropic effects. Predicted structure/dynamics are not experimental structural confirmation; the model contribution was not isolated by ablation.
