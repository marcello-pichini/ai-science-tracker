Machine learning identifies bacterial strains and combinations that reduce pathogen colonization in Arabidopsis

Random forest and elastic-net models analyzed 136 synthetic communities, each composed of five bacterial strains, and identified strain identity as the strongest predictor of protection against Pseudomonas syringae DC3000 in Arabidopsis. Independent test communities confirmed predictive performance, and follow-up plant assays showed that Acidovorax Leaf76, Rhizobium Leaf68 and Pseudomonas Leaf15 each reduced pathogen colonization by about two orders of magnitude versus axenic controls; a Rhizobium Leaf371–Arthrobacter Leaf337 combination also reduced colonization.

Technology: Random forest and elastic-net generalized linear models (GLMNet)

  • AI role: Models predicted pathogen outcomes from strain presence or abundance across the 136 screened communities and ranked strains by feature importance. Researchers tested the leading strains and combinations in additional plant experiments, validating the model-prioritized protection results.
