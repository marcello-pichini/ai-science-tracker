AI-assisted seismic catalog links the 2018 Baige landslide-dammed lakes to increased local earthquakes

An earthquake catalog built with the PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector and cross-correlation/template matching revealed a statistically significant (over 99%) increase in small earthquakes as the second Baige landslide-dammed lake approached peak water level; rates returned toward background after breach. Coulomb-stress modeling found about 90% of the earthquakes in regions of positive modeled stress change, supporting likely triggering through direct water loading and pore-pressure diffusion. The paper presents this as evidence that earthquake–surface-hazard triggering can be bidirectional.

Technology: PhaseNet machine-learning phase detector, combined with PyOcto association and cross-correlation/template-matching methods

  • Still unknown: This is a case study of one pair of 2018 landslide-dammed lakes and nearby faults. The authors conclude the lakes likely triggered the sequence, rather than directly observing causality, and the global implication is modeled potential.
  • AI role: PhaseNet detected P- and S-wave arrivals in continuous seismic records; these detections supported a new 2014–2023 earthquake catalog used to identify and statistically test the local seismicity increase. Coulomb-stress modeling then assessed a possible physical mechanism.
