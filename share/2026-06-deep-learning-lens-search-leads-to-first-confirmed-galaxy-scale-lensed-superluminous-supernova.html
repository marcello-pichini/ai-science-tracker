Deep-learning lens search leads to first confirmed galaxy-scale lensed superluminous supernova

The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821. Follow-up imaging resolved multiple supernova images and spectroscopy classified it as a Type I superluminous supernova at z=2.008 ± 0.001; the second deflector galaxy was measured spectroscopically at z=0.375 ± 0.001. This is the first confirmed galaxy-scale strongly lensed supernova with expected time delays of days to weeks, offering a new system for time-delay cosmography.

Technology: Deep-learning Pan-STARRS 3π lens-candidate classification and transient-alert cross-matching pipeline

Evidence context (pilot; stated by the cited sources, not an editorial judgment):

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper
  • Tested: The HOLISMOKES search identified the ZTF transient SN 2025wny at the position of the previously selected lens candidate PS1 J0716+3821.
  • Still unknown: The redshift, SN type and lensing are based on follow-up observations; time delays are predicted from image separations and were still awaiting high-cadence measurement in this paper. The authors note that the SN’s brightness and resolvability introduce selection effects. An independent paper analyzed the same transient; the discovery is counted once here, not as separate detections by each follow-up team.
  • AI role: The discovery followed a workflow that first classified all 3 billion Pan-STARRS 3π sources with deep learning to select high-quality strong-lens candidates, then continuously cross-matched those positions to ZTF alerts. The team matched the 2025 transient to the preselected lens candidate and triggered imaging and spectroscopic follow-up, which established the lensed SLSN result.
