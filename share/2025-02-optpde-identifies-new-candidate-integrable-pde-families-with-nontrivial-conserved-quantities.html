OptPDE identifies new candidate integrable PDE families with nontrivial conserved quantities

Automatic differentiation optimized partial-differential-equation coefficients to increase the number of conserved quantities. The published study reports four PDE families with at least one nontrivial conserved quantity and analytically studies one new candidate, u_t = u_x^3; the work establishes candidate systems and conserved quantities, not that every generated family is fully integrable.

Technology: OptPDE coefficient optimization with the CQFinder conserved-quantity search

  • Publication status: Peer-reviewed paper

  • Tested: Automatic differentiation optimized partial-differential-equation coefficients to increase the number of conserved quantities.
  • Still unknown: The peer-reviewed source reports four PDE families with at least one nontrivial conserved quantity and analytically examines one new candidate, u_t = u_x^3. The authors explicitly state that maximizing conserved quantities does not guarantee integrability; the result is a concrete candidate system and its studied properties, not proof that every output family is integrable.
  • AI role: The authors explicitly describe OptPDE as a machine-learning approach. CQFinder computes the conserved-quantity objective from a symbolic PDE basis; OptPDE uses a sigmoid-smoothed objective and backpropagation to optimize 34 trainable PDE coefficients. They ran 5,000 random initializations and used dimensionality reduction to group the outputs into four families. This is a learned, trainable symbolic search, not simply use of an automatic-differentiation library.
