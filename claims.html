Open problems to claim

Concrete next steps left by AI discoveries in the catalog. Anyone can take one, and a public checker decides.

How it works

  1. Pick a problem below and open a claim issue on GitHub. One problem per issue. A claim lapses after 30 days without activity, so others can take it.
  2. Work with any method and any tools. Say which AI agent and model you used, if any.
  3. Open a pull request that adds your file under claims/submissions/PROBLEM-ID/. The public checker runs on it and reports the result in the pull request.
  4. A verified result beats the baseline recorded here. A maintainer then checks the literature before marking the problem solved. Credit goes to the GitHub author of the pull request.

Failed attempts stay listed with their reason, so the next person does not repeat them. 4 of 4 problems are open. List updated 27 Nov 2026. Data: claims.json. Results of AI agents on other open problem trackers are in Open problems attacked by AI agents.

More mutually orthogonal Latin squares for some order below 500

Open From the discovery GPT-6 Astra constructs mutually orthogonal Latin squares that improve lower bounds at 33 orders.

Construct more mutually orthogonal Latin squares (MOLS) of some order n below 500 than the best lower bound N(n) in the baseline table. The 2024 MOLS table, updated with the 33 orders improved by explicit constructions in arXiv 2611.16299, is the baseline.

What counts

Plain text: first line 'n k', then k squares of n rows with n symbols 0..n-1; lines starting with # are ignored. Same format as the Constructive-Codes MOLS repository.

Baseline: Best lower bound N(n) for every order 2 to 499.

  • Miller, Abel, Valkov and Fraser, Implementing the MOLS table for n up to 500, Symmetry 16(12):1678, Table 1
  • Explicit constructions improving 33 orders, arXiv 2611.16299, 12 Nov 2026

The baseline holds explicit constructions and the 2024 table. A bound that follows from a recursive construction may already be known without appearing here, so a maintainer checks the literature before marking a claim solved.

A bin packing instance where Best-Fit in random order is worse than 1.5

Open From the discovery Co-FunSearch finds adversarial instances raising the Best-Fit bin packing bound.

Find a bin packing instance whose expected number of Best-Fit bins, with the items in uniformly random order, exceeds 1.5 times the optimum. This would raise the lower bound on the absolute random-order ratio of Best-Fit, now between 1.5 and 1.7.

What counts

JSON: {"capacity": C, "items": [integer sizes], "packing": [[sizes in bin 1], ...]}. The packing certifies an upper bound on the optimum; the checker computes the exact expectation over all orders.

Baseline: Absolute random-order ratio of Best-Fit: at least 1.5, at most 1.7.

  • Nikoleit, Anand, Naredla and Röglin, lower bound 1.5 found with Co-FunSearch, arXiv 2601.16849, 23 Jan 2026
  • Dósa and Sgall (2014), upper bound 1.7 for Best-Fit, as cited in arXiv 2601.16849

The asymptotic random-order ratio is a different and harder question. With an optimum of 2 or 4 bins the ratio cannot exceed 1.5.

Attempts

  • 25 Nov 2026, @marcello-pichini with Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5): released, no improvement found. Exact evaluation by dynamic programming plus eight 10-minute local searches and structured families. Best ratios: 1.3333 with an optimum of 3 bins and 1.214 with 5. No instance exceeded 1.5.

A record linear code of length 49 over GF(8)

Open From the discovery Transformer-guided genetic search finds six new champion linear error-correcting codes over F₈.

Find a linear [49, k] code over GF(8) whose minimum distance exceeds the best known lower bound on codetables.de for that k. Generalized toric codes over GF(8) have length 49 and gave the six champion codes in the parent discovery, but any construction counts.

What counts

Plain text: 'q n k' then k rows of n field elements 0..7 (GF(8) as polynomials over GF(2) modulo x^3+x+1, bit i is the coefficient of x^i). The checker computes the minimum distance exactly with the Brouwer-Zimmermann algorithm.

Baseline: Best known lower and upper bounds for every k; 38 values of k are still open.

  • Grassl, Bounds on the minimum distance of linear codes (codetables.de), q=8, n=49, checked 26 Nov 2026

Exact verification gets slow beyond about k = 14 at this length, so a large-k submission may exceed the checker's time limit; that is a fault, not a rejection.

Attempts

  • 26 Nov 2026, @marcello-pichini with Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5): released, no improvement found. Local search over generalized toric codes for k = 3 to 14, 20 minutes per k, with exact checks of every candidate whose quick bound beat the record (93,807 across GF(8) and GF(9)). None beat the record.
