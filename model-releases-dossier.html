How to interpret the evidence

What does an AI model release count represent?

The five labels in the figure account for 202 of the 440 records, or 45.9% of this selection. One record is an entry in the underlying data. Specific versions may have their own entry, source labels can differ after reorganizations, and a model’s recorded date can refer to an announcement, publication or release.

The 81 entries dated to 2026 run through 10 November in this snapshot. The 88 entries dated to 2025 cover the full calendar year. A comparison of release pace requires matching calendar windows and consistent rules for including versions.

Why do model and research rankings differ?

The organization named on a release record plays a different role from the one named in a scientific record. The organization view counts the first affiliation in each record after label normalization. The ordering of affiliations can reflect reporting conventions, and external groups can perform research with a model that they did not release.

Both series describe coverage of this selection. Comparing productivity or scientific value requires independent outcome measures and broader coverage.

How common are model-family links in the research catalog?

The catalog links 287 of 4,270 active records to at least one family, about 6.7% in this snapshot. This measures coverage of recorded links. Research can use models that the catalog does not link to a family, and a single study can mention several families.

The family profiles show which entries support each count. To evaluate what a particular model contributed, open the underlying discovery record and the original study. Look for the role the researchers report and whether they identify a model version or only a family.
